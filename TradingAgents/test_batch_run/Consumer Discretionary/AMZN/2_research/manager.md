**Recommendation**: Overweight

**Rationale**: Weighing both sides, the bull's core argument holds up better than the bear's: AWS still generates ~$90B+ revenue at a leading 32% share with genuine AI-driven upside (Bedrock, Trainium/Inferentia), the ads business is a legitimately underappreciated high-margin growth engine, and net income of $135B+ TTM at a ~20.5x P/E is not a demanding multiple for a company with this earnings power and multiple growth vectors (cloud, ads, retail). The bear raises fair points—AWS share has been ceding ground to Azure, AI compute risks commoditization, fulfillment costs are rising, and the ad TAM expansion to off-platform brand spend is unproven—but these are largely arguments about the rate and durability of growth, not evidence of impending structural decline. The bear's strongest point is the capex/FCF concern: $3.2B TTM FCF against ~25% capex/revenue is a genuine near-term risk if the capex cycle doesn't moderate as the bull assumes, and this deserves respect and monitoring rather than dismissal. However, this is a two-sided risk (heavy AI infrastructure investment could also be the seed of the next earnings leg, similar to past AWS build-out cycles) rather than a reason to turn bearish outright. Technicals are constructive but not decisive on their own—price is consolidating in a healthy range above rising 50/200 SMAs with neutral RSI, supporting the idea that current weakness is not capitulation. Net-net, the bull's growth optionality (AWS, AI infra rental economics, ads) plus reasonable valuation outweighs the bear's margin-compression and capex-permanence concerns, which are real but not yet showing up destructively in the numbers. This tips the balance to a constructive, not maximally aggressive, stance: Overweight rather than a full conviction Buy, acknowledging the capex/FCF and margin-trajectory risks the bear surfaced as reasons to size the position with some discipline rather than go max-weight.
