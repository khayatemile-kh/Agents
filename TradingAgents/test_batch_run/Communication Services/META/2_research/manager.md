**Recommendation**: Hold

**Rationale**: Both sides landed real punches, and neither fully closes the case, so I'm not comfortable committing capital aggressively in either direction right now.

The bull's strongest point is the current profitability picture: $68.1B net income, 29.83% net margin, 34.83% operating margin, and $21.55B of free cash flow are not in dispute and represent a genuinely high-quality, cash-generative business. The forward P/E of 17.71x versus a 23.34x trailing multiple is also real and does look attractive on its face relative to other mega-cap tech comparisons the bull cited (MSFT ~30x, NVDA 50x+).

But the bear's rebuttal on the growth assumption is the more rigorous piece of analysis in this round. The bear actually did the math: hitting the forward EPS of $34.96 implied by that 17.71x multiple requires roughly 32-33% earnings/revenue growth, which the bear argues is well above the 16-22% revenue growth trends typically associated with Meta's recent run-rate. If that gap is real, the "cheap on forward earnings" argument partially unwinds — you'd be looking at a stock trading closer to 20-25x on more realistic growth, which is far less of a bargain. The bull never actually rebutted this specific arithmetic; the bull's valuation section was cut off before addressing it, so I have to weight the bear's unanswered math more heavily.

On AI, the bull's monetization thesis ($5-10B incremental revenue by 2027, 5x multiple to get $25-50B market cap upside) is directionally plausible given Meta's 3-billion-user distribution advantage and the fact that ad-recommendation AI is already showing up in margins, but the bear is right that the specific revenue figure is unsupported speculation with no unit economics behind it, and the prediction-market signal (effectively 0% for Meta on "best model") is a legitimate data point on competitive positioning that the bull only partially defused with the "monetizing attention vs. best model" argument. That distinction is reasonable but doesn't fully answer the bear's point that an inferior model could still cause user/usage migration to competitor platforms in high-value use cases like search and reasoning.

On technicals, RSI at 74 cuts both ways — the bull's "uptrends stay overbought" point is fair, but so is the bear's "momentum can diverge from and mask fundamental risk" framing. I don't think the chart evidence alone should drive a rating change either way.

Net-net: this is a high-quality business with a real, unresolved disagreement about whether the growth rate embedded in the valuation is achievable. The bear exposed a specific, quantified gap in the bull's forward-multiple argument that wasn't rebutted, which keeps me from endorsing Buy/Overweight. At the same time, the underlying profitability, margin structure, and cash generation are too strong, and the AI optionality too real (even if unquantified), to justify Underweight/Sell. This is a genuine case for balance rather than conviction in either direction, so I'm calling it a Hold.
