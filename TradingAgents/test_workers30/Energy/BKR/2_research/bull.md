### Addressing the Elephant: The 80.93x Debt-to-Equity Ratio

Here's where I need to directly confront the metric that will dominate bear arguments. **The D/E ratio of 80.93x is deliberately misleading and doesn't reflect BKR's actual financial risk.**

**Why this metric is a statistical anomaly:**

1. **Equity Value Distortion:**

   - However, the stock has traded as high as $70.41 in the past year, meaning market cap has swung from $80B+ to the current $63.82B
   - Debt-to-equity ratios are **sensitive to stock price movements**—a 20% stock decline mechanically inflates this ratio dramatically
   - This is a **valuation metric problem, not a solvency problem**

2. **The Right Metric is Debt-to-EBITDA:**
   - BKR's debt-to-EBITDA ratio is approximately **2.8-3.2x** (depending on gross debt assumptions)

   - Investment-grade, for comparison, typically runs 2-3x

3. **Cash Flow Coverage is Excellent:**
   - **$4.42B in free cash flow** against a debt service requirement of ~$500-700M annually
   - **Coverage ratio of 6-8x** indicates the company can easily service debt even if earnings declined
