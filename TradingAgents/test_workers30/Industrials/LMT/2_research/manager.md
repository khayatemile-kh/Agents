**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: LMT trades at a forward P/E of ~16.4x on consensus 20.7% EPS growth (PEG ~1.0), generates ~$5.56B FCF TTM, carries a D/E of 234x, and has just rallied 26% in Q3 with RSI near overbought (69.64) and price extended ~$106 above the 200-day SMA. The disagreement is about interpretation, not data.

The bull's strongest points: government-backed revenue visibility, a well-covered dividend, a PEG of 1.0 suggesting the growth is not egregiously overpaid for, and a technical uptrend with all moving averages aligned bullishly. These are real positives and argue against an outright Sell.

The bear's strongest points land harder, though. First, the bear correctly flags that the entire growth thesis is already embedded in the forward multiple — a forward P/E of 16.38 is only 'cheap' if 20.7% growth materializes exactly as consensus expects, leaving no margin of safety if execution disappoints (supply chain, margin compression, contract timing). Second, the bear's leverage math is the most damaging: D/E of 234% translating to an estimated $90B+ debt load against $5.56B FCF implies debt service plus the ~$2.7-2.8B dividend obligation could consume the large majority of annual free cash flow, which materially constrains flexibility for buybacks or reinvestment — this is a legitimate structural concern the bull's 'leverage is fine because cash flow is stable' argument doesn't fully rebut, since it doesn't address the *magnitude* of the debt/FCF ratio (~16x), only its 'serviceability' in the abstract. Third, the bear correctly notes the 7% Taiwan-conflict and 5% Russia-clash probabilities are tail-risk inputs, not base-case certainties, and are already reflected in a stock that just ran up 26% in a quarter — meaning the geopolitical narrative is largely priced in, not an untapped catalyst. Fourth, near-term political uncertainty (the 2024 election cycle) adds a real, undiversifiable overhang given LMT's heavy U.S. government revenue concentration.

Combining this with the technical picture — RSI at 69.64, MACD showing momentum divergence, and price extended well above trend — the stock looks tactically overbought after a big run, and the fundamental cushion (a PEG of 1.0, not sub-1) offers limited margin for error if growth or geopolitical assumptions disappoint even modestly. This isn't a business-quality problem (LMT's cash generation, ROE, and contract backlog are genuinely strong), so I don't think this rises to a Sell. But the risk/reward at current levels — priced-in growth, extended technicals, real leverage constraints, and event-driven political risk — argues against adding exposure here or holding for aggressive appreciation. The prudent stance is to trim into strength and wait for a better entry (or clearer earnings confirmation) rather than pressing the long side or panic-selling a fundamentally sound compounder.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing LMT exposure by roughly 15-25% into current strength rather than adding at these levels — the forward P/E/PEG already prices in the 20.7% consensus EPS growth, so there's minimal margin of safety if execution disappoints. 2) Do not initiate or add to new long positions at current levels; RSI (~69.64) and MACD divergence flag near-term overbought conditions after the 26% Q3 rally, so expect a pullback toward the $545-550 zone before considering re-entry. 3) Set a re-accumulation watchlist trigger closer to the 50-day SMA or on confirmation of an earnings beat that validates the 20.7% growth path, rather than chasing the current extension. 4) Keep a tight watch on the debt/FCF dynamic (~16x by the bear's estimate) — if FCF growth stalls or capex/dividend commitments rise, tighten the underweight further; if FCF comfortably outpaces debt service and buybacks resume, that would be a signal to revisit toward Hold/Overweight. 5) Treat the 2024 election cycle and any shifts in NATO/Taiwan procurement rhetoric as a binary near-term catalyst — use options collars or reduced position sizing to manage headline risk around policy announcements. 6) Retain a core position (do not fully exit) given LMT's genuine cash generation, dividend coverage, and backlog visibility — this is a trim/de-risk call, not a thesis-ending short.
