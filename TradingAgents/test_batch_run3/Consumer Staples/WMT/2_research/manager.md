The bull's defense that low equity is 'a feature not a bug' doesn't really rebut the bear's point that this leaves little cushion for refinancing risk or a downturn - it's a structural characteristic that limits flexibility, even if it hasn't caused problems historically. The bear's point about thin free cash flow margin (after capex and dividends) relative to a $735B revenue base is also a fair, data-grounded caution, even though absolute FCF is large.

On liquidity, the bull's peer comparison (Target 0.84, Costco 0.75, Amazon 0.88) is a reasonable defense that this is industry-normal, so I don't weight the current ratio issue heavily either way - it's largely a non-issue for a working-capital-efficient retailer like this, and the bear's rebuttal on this point was cut off without landing a knockout argument.

Where the bull is strongest is on the technical/momentum picture: a genuine 16% rally, moving averages in full bullish alignment, and a healthy RSI pullback with institutional-looking volume patterns. That's concrete price-action evidence that near-term momentum favors holders, not sellers. The macro backdrop (stable rates, benign inflation, resilient consumer, WMT's defensive positioning) is also a fair tailwind the bear didn't really contest.

Netting this out: the valuation/leverage concerns raised by the bear are legitimate and suggest the stock is pricing in a lot of good news already, which argues against aggressively adding at current levels. But the technical setup, defensive characteristics, and real (if modest) high-margin growth initiatives argue against an outright sell or underweight call either. This is a case where the bull's momentum argument and the bear's valuation/leverage argument roughly offset each other, landing on a balanced stance - existing holders should stay put and let the Q3 earnings catalyst clarify the picture, but new aggressive buying at a near-33x multiple with the highest leverage in the peer group is not warranted without confirmation of the growth-driver payoff (visible margin expansion from ads/marketplace, controlled interest expense).
