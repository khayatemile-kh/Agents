## 4. ENERGY CAPEX CYCLE: THE BULL'S SECRET WEAPON

### Macro Headwinds Are Priced In—But Here's the Reality

The bear will likely argue: "Fed rate hikes, oil majors are disciplined with capex, 2.0–2.5% GDP growth."

**I agree those are real.** But here's what's being *missed*:

### Deferred Capex, Not Eliminated Capex

1. **Low replacement rates:** After 2016–2020 underinvestment, oil & gas infrastructure globally is *aged*. You can't run subsea wells or deepwater fields on deferred maintenance forever.

2. **Energy security:** Geopolitical tensions (Middle East, Russia/Ukraine, South China Sea disputes) make energy self-sufficiency a strategic priority for developed nations. Capex for LNG, deepwater, and unconventional production is *structurally supported*, not discretionary.

3. **OPEC production restraint:** With OPEC+ managing production, there's a floor on capex spending by major producers to maintain/grow capacity. This isn't a collapsed capex cycle; it's a *normalized* cycle at elevated levels.

### Energy Majors' Capex Guidance (Macro Context)

Even with elevated rates:
- **Shell, BP, Equinor, TotalEnergies** have all guided to $12–18B annual capex through 2025–2026
- That's *materially higher* than 2016–2020 levels and sustains strong demand for services like BKR's

The market's prediction of "93% no rate cuts through 2026" actually *helps* BKR's capex thesis—it means operators have already *committed* to capex budgets and won't pull back mid-cycle. Rate hikes are priced in; it's a *known* headwind, not a surprise.
