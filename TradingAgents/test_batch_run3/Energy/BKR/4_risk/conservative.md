## **THE "HIGHER RATES = DISCIPLINED CAPEX" ARGUMENT IS BACKWARDS AND REVEALS A FUNDAMENTAL MISUNDERSTANDING OF THE CYCLE**

This is where I need to push back hardest. The aggressive analyst claims that higher rates actually *support* disciplined capex and that oil majors have "learned to operate profitably at higher rates." 

This is dangerously wrong.

Here's the actual dynamic: Higher rates make *all* capital deployment more expensive, including E&P capex. Oil majors aren't "redirecting capex toward superior economics"—they're *reducing absolute capex* because the hurdle rate (the minimum return required to justify a project) has risen. A subsea deepwater project that looked acceptable at 8% required return looks marginal at 12% required return. Same project, same reservoir—but much higher cost of capital kills the deal.

The aggressive analyst cites "upwardly revised guidance for 2024 capex deployment." But here's the catch: **2024 capex was already contracted for by mid-2023, before the Fed took interest rates to 5.25–5.50%.** The question isn't what E&P majors *committed to spend* in early 2023; it's what they'll *decide to spend starting in 2025* when they're planning budgets in a permanently higher rate environment.

The macro data is unambiguous: 93% of prediction markets price NO rate cuts through 2026. That means oil majors are *not* expecting relief. They're planning permanent austerity. And when E&P capex cycles turn down, BKR doesn't just see flat cash flows—it sees *sharp declines* because oilfield services is a swing business. Revenue can drop 20–30% in a downturn.
