## PILLAR 2: NET INTEREST MARGIN COMPRESSION IS FAR MORE SEVERE THAN "ALREADY PRICED IN"

The bull's argument on NIM is built on one claim: **The Fed's 93% probability of holding rates steady through 2026 locks in current margins.**

### **The Actual NIM Math JPM Faces**

Here's what the bull conveniently skipped: **JPM's NIM environment is already deteriorating, regardless of whether the Fed cuts rates.**

**The Problem:**
- Fed started cutting in September 2024 (25 bps cut on 9/18)
- Current Fed Funds Rate: **4.25%-4.50%** (down from 5.25%-5.50% peak)
- 10Y Treasury: **~3.7%-4.0% range**

**Why NIM Compresses Even If Rates Hold:**
JPM doesn't earn margins in isolation—it earns them on the **deposit/lending spread differential.** As rates normalize at lower levels:

1. **Deposit Costs Rise** (sticky upward due to competition)
   - Money market fund yields are still elevated from recent rate peaks
   - Depositors are comparing JPM's rates to money market funds and other banks
   - JPM will be forced to raise deposit rates to retain deposits
   - The bull assumes JPM can keep deposits at 0.01% yields—it can't. Deposit betas are normalized again after the SVB crisis

2. **Lending Rates Don't Reset As Fast Downward**
   - Prime rate is linked to Fed Funds (moves with Fed cuts)
   - But lending spreads are compressing as loan demand weakens in a slowing economy
   - The bull assumes lending rates stay elevated—they won't if demand slackens

3. **The Forward P/E Already Shows This Compression**
   - Bull argues: *"Forward P/E of 14.17x vs TTM 15.18x means only modest compression is priced in"*
   - **Wrong.** That 7.1% forward discount reflects NIM compression headwinds
   - But the market is *underestimating* the magnitude of that compression
   - If the Fed cuts to 3.0%-3.25% range (which Polymarket predicts is possible by 2025), NIM will compress materially—20-30 bps or more

### **The Historical Precedent**

Let's look at 2019-2020, the last time the Fed cut aggressively:
- JPM's NIM fell from 2.17% (Q4 2018) to 1.57% (Q2 2020) — a 60 basis point drop
- Net interest income fell sharply despite maintained loan volumes
- This was with a stronger economy than what we have now

**The bull is assuming 2024-2025 will be different.** On what basis? The math doesn't support that.
