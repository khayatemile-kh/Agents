### **2. CHINA IS A MIRAGE, NOT A GROWTH ENGINE**

The bull argues: "China is delivering 20.8% revenue growth with 1,550+ stores, and represents only 8-10% of revenue—massive upside!"

**I say: This narrative is dangerously misleading, and here's why:**

**First, Let's Contextualize the China Opportunity:**

Yes, DPC Dash Ltd (Domino's China master franchisee) reported 20.8% revenue growth. That sounds amazing. But let's inject some realism:

1. **China Represents ~8-10% of Revenue, But How Much of Consolidated Profit?**
   
   The bull assumes China operates at the same margin profile as the U.S. Pizza delivery in China faces:
   - **Intense local competition** (Meituan, Eleme, local QSR brands)
   - **Regulatory constraints** on delivery logistics
   - **Pricing pressure** (delivery fees capped in many cities; consumer price sensitivity)
   - **New market discount margins** (typical franchisees operate at 15-20% EBIT margins in emerging markets vs. 25-30% in mature U.S. market)
   
   **Conservative estimate:** China likely contributes 5-7% of consolidated EBITDA despite being 8-10% of revenue. It's a **high-growth, low-margin business.**

2. **20.8% Growth is Not Sustainable Long-Term:**
   
   Here's what the bull isn't saying: **Pizza delivery in China is in hyper-growth penetration mode right now.** But once China reaches saturation (which typically occurs at 1,500-2,000 stores in major urban markets), growth will normalize to 3-5% annually—the same maturation pattern we're seeing in the U.S.
   
   DPZ is adding 300+ net stores annually in China. At that pace:
   - **By 2030:** ~2,000-2,200 stores
   - **By 2032:** Market saturation in major cities; growth decelerates to 2-3%
   
   **The bull's thesis relies on 20%+ growth continuing for 3-5 years. It won't.** Pizza is a category with limited frequency (1-2x per month average order frequency). Market saturation occurs *fast*.

3. **The Bull's $750M Revenue Upside Math Is Wildly Optimistic:**
   
   The bull claims: "China could add $750M-$1B in revenue, worth $0.65/share."
   
   Let me check this math:
   - DPZ's current enterprise value: ~$11.5B market cap + $4.7B net debt = **$16.2B enterprise value**
   - Current EV/Revenue: $16.2B / $5.03B = **3.2x**
   - If China adds $750M revenue at similar margins, it's worth: $750M × 3.2x = **$2.4B enterprise value**
   - But this happens over 5 years, so NPV at 10% discount: ~$1.5B / 34.2M shares = **$0.44/share today**
   
   **Even if the bull's scenario plays out perfectly, it's worth $0.44/share, not $0.65.** And that assumes:
   - No margin compression in China (likely to occur)
   - Growth doesn't decelerate (it will)
   - DPZ maintains market share vs. local competitors (uncertain)

4. **China is Already Priced In:**
   
   Here's the thing: **Sell-side analysts already model China growth into their valuations.** DPZ's forward P/E of 16.61x assumes *some* international growth. The market isn't blind to China.
   
   What the market *is* skeptical about is whether China growth will offset maturation in the U.S. **It won't.** The math simply doesn't work at scale. DPZ will end up being a **low-single-digit global growth story**, with China adding 1-2% incremental growth as it matures.

**Real talk:** The bull is using China as a "hope story" to justify holding a stock that's fundamentally challenged in its core U.S. market. That's textbook value trap behavior.
