## SECTION 1: THE FORWARD EARNINGS "MIRACLE" IS A MIRAGE

### 1.1 Forward EPS Guidance is Historically Unreliable and Likely Inflated

Let's start with the bull's centerpiece: **31.6% forward earnings growth to $20.91 EPS.**

This is not a company guidance figure. This is a consensus *estimate* compiled from sell-side analysts—the same analysts who were massively wrong on Nike, Tapestry, and Lululemon's recent trajectories. Here's what we need to ask:

**Where is this 31.6% growth actually coming from?**

Looking at the actual quarterly data:
- Q2 2026 EPS: $4.28
- Q2 2025 EPS: $3.52
- **YoY growth: 21.6%**

But wait—let's annualize Q2 2026 performance:
- Q2 2026 annualized: $4.28 × 4 = **$17.12 EPS (TTM run rate)**

The forward estimate of $20.91 requires **a 22.1% acceleration from the current TTM run rate.** That's not organic growth—that's a *step-change improvement* that the company hasn't guided to and that contradicts sector headwinds.

**Red flag #1:** Forward consensus estimates often overshoot during periods of positive sentiment. When sentiment reverses (as it's beginning to now), estimates get slashed hard. We're likely in the early innings of a consensus downgrade cycle.

### 1.2 Q1 2026 EPS Was Only $2.45—Revealing Instability

Here's a critical data point the bull glosses over:

| Quarter | EPS | QoQ Change | YoY Change |
|---------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| Q2 2026 | $4.28 | +74.7% QoQ | +21.6% YoY |
| Q1 2026 | $2.45 | -58.0% QoQ | -30.5% YoY |
| Q4 2025 | $5.82 | +75.3% QoQ | - |
| Q3 2025 | $3.32 | -43.0% QoQ | - |

**This is not steady earnings growth—this is erratic, highly seasonal business with massive quarterly swings.** The company is riding holiday and seasonal peaks, with troughs in Q1 and Q3. 

**The bull cherry-picked Q2 2026 ($4.28) as the "proof" of acceleration, but:**
- Q1 2026 was down 30.5% YoY
- The business shows 50%+ quarterly volatility
- Annualizing off a peak quarter is exactly what killed bulls on Nike and LULU

When you strip out seasonality and look at the *trend*, Q1 2026 weakness is the more honest signal. Q2 peaks are normal for apparel.

## SECTION 2: THE DIGITAL GROWTH STORY IS OVERSTATED AND POTENTIALLY UNSUSTAINABLE

### 2.1 8% Digital Comps Growth is Being Presented Out of Context

The bull presents the 8% digital comps as "accelerating" and a "game-changer." But let me provide the actual context:

**Where is the 8% digital growth coming from?**
- Is it like-for-like (same-store) growth, or is it including new DTC store openings?
- Is it at healthy margins, or is RL discounting to drive volume?
- What's the blended comp growth (digital + retail combined)?

The data provided only says "Digital Comps Rise 8% as E-Commerce Momentum Builds." **It does not clarify whether this is:**
1. Same-store digital sales growth (harder to achieve, higher quality)
2. Or total digital revenue growth diluted by new store additions (lower quality)

This ambiguity is precisely where bulls hide. In industry parlance, when a company reports "8% digital comps" without specifying same-store, it often means total digital growth includes store expansion—which is NOT the same as organic momentum.

### 2.2 Digital Margins Claim Lacks Supporting Evidence

The bull claims: "E-commerce carries 50-70% higher gross margins than wholesale."

This is a *generalized* industry statement, not RL-specific data. Looking at RL's actual margins:

**Gross Margin Progression:**
- Q2 2026: 73.7%
- Q1 2026: 69.7%
- Q4 2025: 69.8%
- Q3 2025: 67.9%
- Q2 2025: 72.3%

The gross margin is **73.7% in Q2 2026**, which is only 1.4pp higher than Q2 2025's 72.3%. If digital was truly carrying 50-70% higher margins and growing at 8%, we should see gross margin *expansion*, not flatlining.

**Red flag: Digital growth is NOT translating to margin expansion.** This suggests either:
1. Digital growth is offset by wholesale mix pressure
2. RL is discounting on digital to drive volume
3. The margin uplift from digital is being reinvested in acquisition costs (AI, personalization)

The bull's narrative doesn't match the data.

### 2.3 AI Investment ROI is Unproven

The bull mentions: "AI investments are deepening customer engagement."

This is marketing language without financial proof. RL is investing in AI, but the data shows:
- No material margin expansion from digital
- Quarterly volatility persists despite digital initiatives
- Competitor investments in AI (Nike, LULU, Tapestry) have not prevented their recent underperformance

**AI investments are capital-intensive and have uncertain ROI.** The bull is assuming these investments will pay off, but there's zero financial evidence yet that AI-driven engagement is converting to higher customer lifetime value or margins.

In fact, if AI investments are driving the Q1 2026 weakness (-30.5% YoY EPS), then RL may be *overspending* on digital initiatives with negative near-term ROI.
