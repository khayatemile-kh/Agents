**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points, but weighing them against each other, the bear case lands more decisively on the near-term financial evidence while the bull case leans heavily on narrative and historical analogy that doesn't fully hold up under scrutiny.

The bull's strongest points: reasonable absolute valuation (20.5x P/E, PEG 1.49), genuine competitive moats in logistics/Prime/advertising, a benign macro backdrop (low recession odds, stable Fed), and the argument that heavy AWS/AI capex today could pay off later. These are real positives and shouldn't be dismissed.

However, the bear analyst effectively challenges the two pillars the bull relies on most:
1. Cash flow trajectory: FCF went from +$14.94B (Q4) to -$18.17B (Q1), driven by CapEx growing ~40% YoY while revenue growth is only ~8%. That's a genuine divergence, not noise. The bull's rebuttal ("this is like 2014-2016") is a weak analogy given the bear's counter that AMZN traded at 10-15x P/E with 15-25% growth then vs. 20.5x P/E and 8% growth now — the margin of safety and growth support for that kind of capex bet is much thinner today.
2. AWS growth deceleration and competitive parity: the bear's point that AWS growth has compressed into the mid-20s and is being matched (not beaten) by Azure, with Google Cloud undercutting on AI pricing, directly undermines the bull's core "widening moat" thesis. The bull didn't provide hard data to rebut the deceleration claim, just asserted acceleration without quarter-over-quarter growth-rate evidence.

The bear's macro read is also more nuanced: 42% probability of a worse-than-soft-landing outcome is a real tail risk the bull glossed over, and "higher for longer" rates are a legitimate headwind for enterprise capex decisions, not a clean tailwind.

On balance, the technical picture (price near support, low relative volume on the pullback) offers some near-term reassurance and argues against panic-selling, but it doesn't offset the more fundamental concern: Amazon is now burning cash quarter over quarter at the same time growth is decelerating and competitive intensity in cloud is rising. This is a real change in trend, not just noise, and needs to be watched closely over the next 1-2 quarters (Q3/Q4 earnings, AWS re:Invent) before adding meaningfully to the position.

Given the genuine cash flow deterioration and the credible challenge to the "widening moat" narrative, this isn't a "buy the dip aggressively" moment, but it's also not a broken thesis — Amazon retains real profitability, diversified high-margin advertising revenue, and a reasonable absolute valuation. The right call is a cautious, trim-biased stance: reduce conviction from what would otherwise be a buy/hold, without going as far as an outright sell, since the balance sheet is strong (net income $135B, ROE 30%, cash $101.8B) and this could well be a temporary investment cycle rather than value destruction. The prudent path is Underweight — trim some exposure and wait for confirmation in the next 1-2 earnings reports that FCF and AWS growth are stabilizing before adding back.
