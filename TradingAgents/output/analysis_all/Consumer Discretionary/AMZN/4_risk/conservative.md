First, the magnitude. We're talking about a $33 billion swing in free cash flow quarter-over-quarter. That's not noise. That's a 221% deterioration from Q4's +$14.94B to Q1's -$18.17B. The aggressive analyst tries to normalize this by annualizing Q1 numbers and claiming Amazon can support $175B in annual capex. But that's exactly my concern: *can they really?*

Let's look at the actual cash position. Yes, Amazon has $101.8B in cash. But they also just issued $52.77B in net debt in Q1 2026. Why would they need to issue debt if capex is a manageable, planned cycle? Debt issuance during a capex cycle is normal, but the *size* relative to the cash balance suggests management sees a constraint. If the capex cycle were clearly temporary and well-understood, you wouldn't see this scale of debt raising happening alongside negative free cash flow.

Second, the seasonality argument they're using is weak. Yes, Q4 is seasonally strong. But Q2 typically recovers as well—we're past that point now. And they haven't explained why capex is *growing* at 40% YoY while revenue only grows at 8%. That's a massive capital intensity increase that doesn't match with "deploying AI infrastructure." That sounds more like Amazon is building redundancy, expanding into lower-return markets, or facing margin pressure that requires capital spending to offset.

Third—and this is critical—there's no visibility into when this capex cycle normalizes. The aggressive analyst claims 12-18 months, but that's pure speculation. What if AWS competitive dynamics intensify? What if the AI infrastructure arms race requires even higher capex? Waiting for Q3 earnings isn't "missing the upside"—it's *prudent risk management* when the data is ambiguous.
