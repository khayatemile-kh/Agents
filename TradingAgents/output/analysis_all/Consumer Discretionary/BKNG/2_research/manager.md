Where I come down: the bull's cash-flow and margin quality are real and provide a floor, and the technical setup argues against pressing shorts here or exiting a long outright. But the bear's balance sheet trend and the seasonal-mirage critique of the EPS growth number are strong enough that I can't underwrite this as a high-conviction re-rating catalyst either. Rosenblatt's initiation is one data point, not confirmation of a trend, and the debt-funded buyback story is a real capital-allocation concern that deserves to be monitored rather than dismissed. This is a case where the evidence is genuinely two-sided — good business, weakening balance sheet, muddled EPS growth optics, decent but not compelling technical setup — so Hold is the intellectually honest call rather than forcing a lean either way.
