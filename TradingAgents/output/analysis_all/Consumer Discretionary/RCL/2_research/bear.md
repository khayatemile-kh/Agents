## II. THE OIL HEDGING ARGUMENT IS DANGEROUSLY NAIVE

The bull claims: *"Cruise operators have sophisticated fuel hedging programs. A 5% spot oil rally doesn't translate to a 5% margin hit overnight."*

**This is where the bull's argument breaks down.** Let me explain why:

### Hedging Doesn't Eliminate Oil Risk—It Transfers It

1. **Hedging provides protection, but it's NOT perfect.** Here's the reality:
   - Most cruise operators hedge 50-75% of fuel exposure (not 100%)
   - **RCL's specific hedging ratio is unknown from the provided data**
   - If RCL is hedged 60% of exposure, a 5% oil move = **2% unhedged margin impact** (not 0.4-0.6% as the bull suggests)
   - **Over a multi-quarter period, sustained oil volatility breaks through hedges**

2. **Hedges roll off and reset at higher prices.** Here's the operational reality the bull ignores:
   - As hedges expire, new hedges are put in place at *current* prices
   - If oil stays elevated or trends higher due to geopolitical tensions, **each rolling hedge resets at higher levels**
   - This creates a **ratchet effect** where near-term margins are protected but forward margins compress
   - The bull's "hedging provides protection" argument is only true *in the short term*

3. **Geopolitical oil shocks are persistent, not transient.** The bull assumes:
   > *"Oil price volatility is cyclical, not structural. Geopolitical tensions are temporary—oil will normalize eventually."*

   **This is wishful thinking.** Middle East tensions in 2026 are not a one-week story:
   - If tensions stem from regional conflicts, they persist for months or years
   - OPEC often uses geopolitical uncertainty to support prices
   - Oil tends to stay elevated once shocked upward, especially when anchored by supply concerns
   - **Historical precedent:** The 2022 Russia-Ukraine shock saw oil elevated for 18+ months

   RCL faces a **multi-quarter headwind, not a one-week blip**.

### The Fuel Cost Math Doesn't Add Up

The bull claims: *"Fuel represents ~8-12% of cruise operating costs. Even if unhedged, a 5% oil jump = 0.4-0.6% margin impact."*

**Let me do the math more rigorously:**
- If fuel is 10% of operating costs and unhedged, a 5% oil spike = **0.5% margin hit** (the bull is right on the unhedged number)
- BUT: If hedges are 60% effective (industry average), that means **40% of exposure is unhedged**
- **A 5% oil move on 40% unhedged exposure = 2% of fuel costs × 10% of operating costs = 0.2% margin hit** (still modest)

**HOWEVER—here's the critical miss:**

The bull's calculation ignores **second and third-order effects**:

1. **Consumer demand destruction:** Higher fuel costs → higher cruise prices → reduced demand, especially from price-sensitive consumers. This compresses *revenue*, not just margins.
2. **Competitive pricing pressure:** If RCL's competitors can absorb fuel costs better (or have more hedges), RCL may not be able to raise prices. **Pricing power assumption is unproven.**
3. **Working capital deterioration:** Higher fuel costs today mean higher cash outflows before higher-priced future cruises are booked. **Liquidity gets tighter.**
