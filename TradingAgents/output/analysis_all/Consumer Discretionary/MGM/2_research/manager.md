# MGM RESORTS INTERNATIONAL (MGM) — RESEARCH MANAGER'S VERDICT

## Adjudication of the Debate

**Where the Bull is Strongest:**
- Nevada Strip data (+3.64% YoY) is real and directionally positive
- Company remains profitable (Net Income $423.6M TTM, Q2 2026 $292.4M)
- Liquidity is adequate (Current Ratio 1.34x, $2.55B cash)
- RSI at 25.54 is genuinely extreme, historically associated with mean-reversion potential

**Where the Bull Overreaches:**
- The PEG ratio argument is the critical vulnerability. A PEG of 0.51 implied by 20x forward P/E requires ~39% forward earnings growth — the Bull never defends this number's plausibility, and the Bear's dismantling of it goes unanswered. This is not a minor gap; it's the load-bearing pillar of the valuation case, and it collapses under scrutiny.
- The Bull's read of technicals (ATR collapse = "no sellers left") is a plausible but unproven interpretation. The Bear's counter — new lows on declining volatility = capitulation continuation, not floor — is equally if not more consistent with standard technical analysis. Price is 10.2% *below* the 50-SMA, which the Bull never adequately addresses.
- The Nevada Strip data problem is fatal: the Bull cites Strip-wide gaming win growth but has **no MGM-specific revenue, market share, or margin data** to connect it to. The stock fell 5.9% post-earnings *despite* citing this same tailwind — a red flag the Bull's rebuttal never resolves. Gaming win ≠ gaming profit, and labor cost inflation is unaddressed by the Bull entirely.
- The 12.2x net-debt/EBITDA leverage is waved off as "manageable" by reclassifying capital leases as quasi-benign, but this doesn't change the leverage math or the fixed-cost burden in a slowing consumer environment.

**Where the Bear Overreaches:**
- Assumes margin compression and labor inflation without direct evidence provided in the debate (no labor cost data was actually cited — this is inference, not fact)
- The "15-25% estimate cuts" and "25-30x forward P/E" forecast is speculative, not sourced

## Net Assessment

The Bear wins this round on **evidentiary discipline**. The Bull's two central pillars — the PEG-implied growth story and the technical "capitulation = reversal" thesis — both required assumptions that were directly challenged and not adequately defended. The stock's actual price action (down 5.9% on earnings, breaking the 50-SMA, sitting barely above the 200-SMA with accelerating downside momentum) is *more consistent* with the Bear's read than the Bull's hopeful reversal narrative. Meanwhile, the disconnect between "strong Nevada data" and "stock sold off hard on earnings" was never resolved by the Bull — and that disconnect is the single most important unanswered question in this debate.

That said, the Bear's case is not airtight either — leverage is a known structural feature of this business model (not new), and the company is still generating positive net income and adequate liquidity. This is not a distressed-debt or solvency situation. The macro backdrop (59% soft landing, only 8% recession odds) is a legitimate tailwind the Bear didn't fully rebut.

This is not a "balanced" picture warranting Hold — the technical deterioration is confirmed by price action itself (broken 50-SMA, accelerating MACD downside, new lows), and the valuation support the Bull leans on rests on an earnings growth assumption that neither side could substantiate. The burden of proof was on the Bull to justify why "cheap" is really cheap, and that burden was not met.

## RECOMMENDATION: **Underweight**

**Rationale:**
1. Trim exposure rather than exit outright — the company remains profitable with adequate near-term liquidity, and outright Sell is not warranted absent confirmed margin/share deterioration (data gap acknowledged).
2. The technical setup (broken 50-SMA, negative MACD, price barely clinging to 200-SMA) argues against adding or holding full-size exposure into what looks like continued distribution, not accumulation.
3. The valuation cushion the Bull relies on (PEG 0.51) is not credible without earnings growth substantiation — this removes the primary reason to buy the dip.
4. The unresolved disconnect between "strong Nevada Strip data" and a 5.9% post-earnings decline should be treated as a warning sign until clarified in the next earnings cycle, not dismissed as noise.

**Action Plan:**
- Reduce position size now into any near-term bounce toward the 10 EMA ($42.70) or 50-SMA ($45.39) resistance zone
- Do NOT add to the position on "oversold RSI" alone — wait for confirmation of price stabilization above the 50-SMA on rising volume before considering re-entry
- Watch Q3 earnings closely for MGM-specific market share and margin data (not just Strip-wide gaming win figures) — this is the key missing variable that could flip the thesis in either direction
- Reassess if net debt/EBITDA trends improve below 10x or if forward EPS revisions stabilize rather than decline

**Key Risk to This Call:** If Q3 earnings show MGM outperforming the Strip average with stable margins, this would materially strengthen the Bull case and warrant an upgrade back to Hold/Overweight. Monitor closely.
