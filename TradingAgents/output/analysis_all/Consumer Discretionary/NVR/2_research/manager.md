**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the raw numbers—margins have compressed for six consecutive reporting periods (gross 25.5%→23.1%→21.8%; operating 18.9%→16.3%→13.9%; net 16.0%→12.9%→11.9%), Q2 operating cash flow was -$151.5M driven by a $296.8M inventory build, cash on hand fell from $1.68B to $1.14B in a single quarter, and buybacks ($373.6M in Q2, $1.83B in FY25 = 137% of FCF) continued despite negative operating cash flow. The dispute is about interpretation, and the bear's framing is more consistent with the data: the bull calls this a 'cyclical trough' and inventory build a 'forward-looking investment,' but a trough implies stabilization, and instead every margin line is still rolling over in the most recent TTM print with no sign of a floor. Critically, the bull's own recovery thesis depends on mortgage/Fed easing that the debate's own macro inputs argue against (89% probability of no cuts, 86% probability mortgage rates stay >6.75%), which undercuts the core bull assumption that built inventory will clear at good margins rather than at markdowns. The bear's point that FCF fell 18.4% YoY (from $1.345B to $1.097B) and that Q2 annualized core cash generation (~$580M) is roughly half of FY24 levels is a real deceleration, not noise—and funding buybacks out of a shrinking cash balance while operating cash flow is negative is a real liquidity/capital-allocation risk flag, not simply 'confidence.' That said, I'm not going full Sell: balance sheet leverage is still low (D/E ~32%), ROE/ROA remain elite in absolute terms (31.5%/16.5%), and valuation (16.3x trailing, 15.4x forward) isn't stretched — this is a quality operator, just one facing a demand backdrop that the bulls haven't adequately rebutted. The weight of evidence — deteriorating margins with no inflection, cash burn funding buybacks, and a macro rate environment that structurally caps the recovery catalyst the bulls need — tips this to a cautious, underweight stance rather than hold or outright sell.

**Strategic Actions**: Trim existing NVR exposure by roughly 20-30% of the position rather than adding on this pullback; do not treat the 9.8% drawdown as a buying opportunity until at least one to two quarters show margin stabilization (gross margin holding flat or improving sequentially) and operating cash flow turning positive excluding one-time inventory swings. Set a watch-list trigger to re-engage (upgrade toward Hold/Overweight) if: (1) mortgage rates decline meaningfully below 6.75% and existing-home sales/order data inflect positive, (2) the built inventory begins converting to closings without price concessions (gross margin stabilizes), and (3) buybacks are funded from positive operating cash flow rather than draining the cash balance. Until then, avoid adding to the position and consider using any near-term bounce to reduce further; keep remaining exposure sized small and monitor cash balance trend quarter-over-quarter as the key leading indicator of balance-sheet stress. Do not initiate new full-size positions; if already underweight the sector, stay underweight NVR specifically given it is trading at a premium multiple (16.3x) despite the deteriorating trend, which limits margin of safety relative to peers experiencing similar compression.
