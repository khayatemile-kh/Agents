## 1. THE "OPERATIONAL EXCELLENCE" NARRATIVE IS A MIRAGE

### Q2 Earnings: The Devil Is in the Details the Bull Ignored

Yes, WYNN beat on revenue. Yes, EPS grew 106% YoY. But let's look at what the bull conveniently **omitted**:

**Fundamental Problem #1: Margin Compression Is Accelerating, Not Stabilizing**

| Metric | FY 2024 | FY 2025 | Q2 2026 | Trend |
|--------|---------|---------|---------|-------|
| **Operating Margin** | 19.0% | 16.9% | 16.5% | **Down 250 bps in 18 months** |
| **Net Profit Margin** | 7.0% | 4.6% | 7.5% | Volatile, unsustainable |
| **Gross Margin** | 43.5% | 41.4% | 40.9% | **Down 260 bps** |

**The bull calls this "strategic investment." I call it what it is: deteriorating operational efficiency.** A 260 basis point contraction in gross margin over 18 months is not the hallmark of "excellence"—it's a canary in the coal mine signaling rising input costs that the company cannot pass through to customers.

Why? Because **luxury hospitality margins are under structural pressure**:
- Labor costs aren't normalizing; they're *permanently* elevated post-unionization
- Energy and commodity costs are sticky to the downside
- Premium positioning actually *limits* pricing power in a soft consumer environment

The bull wants us to believe this is temporary. **The data shows it's permanent.** Operating margins have not recovered to 2024 levels despite revenue growth. That tells you the business model is compressing.

**Translation:** WYNN isn't accelerating; it's recovering from a trough. And we know what happens to cyclical recoveries when rates stay elevated (89% probability of no Fed cuts) and consumer discretionary spending starts to cool.

|--------|---------|---------|---------|---------------------------|------------|
| **Net Income** | ~$105M | $66M | $140M | +112% | +15% |
| **EPS** | ~$0.95 | $0.64 | $1.32 | +106% | +18% |

**The bull is comparing to an anomalously weak Q2 2025 and calling it "momentum."** When you look at the 2-year CAGR, the picture is much less impressive—mid-teens growth that's entirely consistent with industry averages, not outperformance.

**More critically, let's look at what's driving the "beat":**

The $74M improvement in Q2 net income ($66M → $140M) comes primarily from:
1. **Revenue growth of $131M** (6.9% growth)
2. **Operating leverage assumptions** (assuming margins stabilize)
3. **Lower interest expense** (partially offset by higher debt levels)

**But the fundamental issue is this:** If revenue is only growing 6.9% YoY and gross margins are *compressing*, then the net income growth is **mostly dependent on SG&A leverage**—which is a one-time benefit, not a repeatable trend.

**The operating margin data confirms this:** It went from 15.9% in Q2 2025 to 16.5% in Q2 2026—only a 60 basis point improvement despite 6.9% revenue growth. That's weak operational leverage.
