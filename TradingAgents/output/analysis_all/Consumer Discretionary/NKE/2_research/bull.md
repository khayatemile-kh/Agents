1. **Inventory clearance is working**: The company is successfully moving excess stock without destroying margins like we'd expect if discounting were rampant.
2. **Pricing power is intact**: A 49.2% gross margin in an environment of "excess discounting" simply doesn't math. This reflects better product mix, improved supply chain efficiency, and pricing discipline.
3. **Operational excellence is returning**: You don't jump 137% in operating income by accident.

**The Bear Will Argue:** "But that Q3 was terrible—down 9.2% in revenue!"

**My Counter:** Yes, and management clearly identified the problem, took action, and course-corrected. That's *exactly* what you want from leadership. The bounce from Q3 to Q4 profitability proves the inventory issue was cyclical, not structural. Companies that can adjust quickly and recover margin are *exactly* the ones worth owning.

## **SECTION 2: VALUATION IS GENUINELY ATTRACTIVE**

Current metrics:
- **Forward P/E:** 16.69x
- **TTM P/E:** 18.12x
- **PEG Ratio:** 1.48x

Let's be clear about what this means:

1. **The forward P/E discount** (16.69x vs. 18.12x) implies the market *expects earnings to grow*. That's actually a vote of confidence baked into the valuation.
2. **The PEG ratio of 1.48x** is reasonable for a company of Nike's quality. A PEG near 1.0x typically signals an undervalued, fast-growing name. Nike's 1.48x reflects that it's fairly valued relative to growth expectations—not overpriced.
3. **Compared to premium consumer brands** (Hermès, Lululemon, Starbucks), Nike's 16.69x forward P/E is a *discount*.

The Bear Will Likely Argue: "The market is pricing in slow growth because of China and inventory headwinds—justify paying 16.69x for a slowing company."

- Inventory is cyclical, not structural.
- Nike's $7.56B cash hoard and 0.74x D/E ratio give it room to invest through this cycle.
- Historical comps: Nike has traded at 18-22x P/E in normal times. At 16.69x, we're **at a 15-25% discount to historical norms**.

**This is not expensive for a $46B revenue company with 22% ROE and a fortress balance sheet.**
