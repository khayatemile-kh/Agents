That's saying for every dollar of shareholder equity, the company has $2.46 in debt obligations. Yes, the aggressive analyst correctly notes these are predominantly capital lease obligations (97% of the $5.42B total debt), but capital leases are *still debt*. They're *still* fixed obligations that must be serviced.

The working capital swing into negative territory—from +$623.7M in Q3 2025 to -$374.3M in Q2 2026—is not "efficient inventory management." That's a $1 billion deterioration in nine months. The aggressive analyst dismisses this because "the company has $680.7M in quarterly operating cash flow." But here's the critical flaw in that logic: operating cash flow *includes* working capital changes. Once you normalize for the working capital deterioration, the underlying cash generation is under pressure.

Look at the sequential operating cash flow trend: Q4 2025 was $425.8M, Q1 2026 was $651.4M, Q2 2026 was $680.7M. Yes, Q2 looks strong, but that strength is partially attributable to the working capital swing. The company is squeezing suppliers and deferring payments. That's not a sustainable advantage—that's a ticking timer on supplier relationships and payment pressure.
