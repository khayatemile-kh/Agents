| **Book Value Per Share** | $2.64 | $1.73 | -$0.91 | **-34.5%** ⚠️ |

**This is not a feature—it's a bug.**

Shareholder equity has been **slashed by nearly 40% in just one year**. The bulls frame this as "opportunistic capital allocation," but let me translate: **CMG is liquidating the balance sheet to fund buybacks at inflated prices.**

Here's the math the bulls won't show you:

**CMG's Buyback Program (TTM):** $2.83B  
**Free Cash Flow (TTM):** $1.12B  
**Shortfall:** $1.71B

**Where is this $1.71B coming from?**
- Debt increases (adding leverage to an already-stressed balance sheet)
- Working capital deterioration (burning cash to maintain the illusion of strength)
- Asset sales or lease restructuring (one-time financing gimmicks)

This is **financial engineering masquerading as capital allocation strategy.** McDonald's does buybacks. But McDonald's maintains healthy equity ratios. CMG is systematically destroying its balance sheet.

The bulls might say, "Fast-casual restaurants typically run negative working capital." **That's true, but not to this extent.** A swing from +$624M to -$374M in 12 months is not "normal"—it's a warning sign.

## II. THE PROMOTIONAL DEPENDENCY TRAP: THIS IS WEAKNESS, NOT STRENGTH

### A. Deconstructing the "Record BOGO" Narrative

The bulls celebrate "record BOGO sales" as evidence of brand strength. I see it differently: **It's evidence that customers won't pay full price.**

Here's what the data actually shows:

**Q2 2026 Revenue Growth: +9.5% YoY**

On the surface, this looks solid. But let's dig deeper:

- **BOGO promotions were "record" levels** — meaning unprecedented discounting
- **Comparable store sales growth had to overcome margin compression from discounting** — suggesting organic, full-price traffic growth was likely **weak or negative**
- **The company had to offer buy-one-get-one deals to achieve single-digit YoY growth** — this is not aspirational

**Compare this to a truly strong business:** A company with pricing power can grow 9-10% without crushing margins with promotions.

### B. The Margin Expansion "Illusion"

The bulls point to Q2 net margin of 11.4% vs. Q1 of 7.4% and claim this proves promotions "drive operating leverage." Let's be precise about what actually happened:

**Q2 2026 Margin Drivers:**
1. **Gross margin: 25.2%** — This is healthy but not extraordinary; it's been this range historically
2. **Unusual items benefit: $30.2M** — This is the hidden lever; mostly impairments that reduced expenses
3. **Operating deleverage: Marketing, labor, and other SG&A were likely higher** in absolute terms

The 400bp sequential margin improvement from Q1 to Q2 is primarily a **seasonal benefit and one-time items**, not a sustainable improvement from promotional execution.

**Here's the real concern:** If Q2 margins were boosted by unusual items, what happens in Q3 when they normalize? **The bull case requires consistent 11%+ net margins. That's not happening.**

### C. Promotional Dependency Signals Weakness in Brand Pricing Power

When a company needs to run "record BOGO" promotions to drive mid-single-digit revenue growth in a favorable consumer environment, it sends a clear signal:

**Organic pricing power is broken.**

- If CMG had pricing power, it would raise prices and accept traffic declines
- Instead, it's discounting aggressively and still only achieving +9.5% growth
- This suggests competitors (CAVA, Panera, others) are stealing share or consumers are trading down

The bulls want to believe this is a tactical choice. It's not—it's an admission that the brand has lost pricing authority.
