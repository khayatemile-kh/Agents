## **III. THE "TARGET BEAUTY STUDIOS" THREAT IS OVERSTATED—IT'S ACTUALLY A TAILWIND**

Bears love this argument: "Target is entering beauty retail! ULTA is doomed!"

Let me unpack why this is wrong:

### **Target Beauty Studios ≠ Ulta Distribution**

- **What ended:** Target's *exclusive* partnership with Ulta (which provided Ulta with dedicated beauty departments)
- **What's launching:** Target's *standalone* beauty concept (Target Beauty Studios) operating independently
- **The math:** One integrated distribution partner (Ulta) → becomes fragmented single-brand/limited-SKU competitor

**This is actually *better* for Ulta**, not worse. Here's why:

### **Fragmentation Favors Consolidation**

When Target runs its own beauty business:
1. **SKU depth is limited** — Target Beauty Studios will stock a fraction of Ulta's 140,000+ SKUs
2. **Customer experience is diluted** — No dedicated beauty associates/stylist services; just beauty products in a Target store
3. **Prestige brand access is restricted** — Brands will prioritize Ulta's dedicated stores over Target's part-time model
4. **Ulta gains customer freedom** — Customers who want true specialty retail *must* go to Ulta stores or online

**The strategic result:** Ulta becomes *more valuable* to prestige brands and consumers seeking curated expertise. Target Beauty Studios will capture casual/mass-market beauty buyers, *leaving prestige and loyalty-driven customers for Ulta.*

This is why comp sales grew **3.8%** in Q2 despite the announced Target transition—the market is already rotating to Ulta.
