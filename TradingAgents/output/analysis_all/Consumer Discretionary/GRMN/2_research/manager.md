**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: Garmin is a high-quality, high-margin, cash-generative business (60% gross margin, 30% operating margin, 24.5% net margin, $1.04B FCF, minimal debt) trading at 28.4x P/E after a 12.6% pullback from $313 to ~$275. The debate hinges on whether that multiple is justified by forward growth, and here the bear lands the more precise blows.

The bull's strongest points—balance sheet strength, margin quality, technical picture showing no capitulation (RSI reset from 80 to ~40, price still above 50/200 SMA, low volume on the decline)—are real and argue against panic-selling. But the bull's growth justification for the multiple is weak: they lean on the 35.3% YoY net income growth figure without acknowledging that this is a single-quarter comparison likely benefiting from margin expansion/cost leverage rather than durable revenue acceleration. The bear's rebuttal is convincing: revenue growth is only mid-single-to-low-double digits, Q1's growth rate was materially lower (~10-12%) than Q2's 35.3%, and margin expansion has a natural ceiling — you can't keep expanding op margin from an already-elite 30% level indefinitely. That makes the 2.93 PEG look more like an aggressive extrapolation than a comfortable safety margin, as the bear notes it embeds ~9.7% forward earnings growth with 'zero margin for error' against a revenue base growing more slowly.

On marine electronics, the bull calls it a 'goldmine' with pricing power; the bear correctly points out Garmin faces entrenched competitors (Furuno, Navico/Simrad/B&G, Raymarine) and that marine is a discretionary, financed purchase category that is first to get cut if rates stay elevated — a much more grounded characterization than the bull's blue-sky framing.

On macro, the bull argues an 8% recession probability is low and rates being held is stable/benign; the bear correctly reframes that an elevated held-rate environment is itself a headwind for financed discretionary purchases (boats, autos) and that even a modest rise in recession odds (from 8% to 15-20%) could compress the multiple toward 22-24x, implying $220-240 — a 15-20% downside case that is more quantitatively grounded than the bull's hand-wave that 'stable rates support stable multiples.'

Net take: the underlying business quality is not in question, but the entry point is. The stock priced in near-flawless execution (elevated multiple, PEG built on an unsustainable one-quarter earnings spike) right as it's rolling over from an overbought technical extreme, into a macro backdrop where the specific product categories most exposed to discretionary/financed spending (marine, and to a lesser extent auto) are the most rate-sensitive. That's a classic 'great company, priced for perfection, entering a softer setup' situation — not a broken business, but not a place to add risk. The technical damage is real even if not catastrophic (RSI collapse, momentum weakening), and there's no bull argument that decisively refutes the bear's point that the 35% growth print is not representative of trend. This tips the balance toward trimming/de-risking rather than holding steady or buying the dip.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing GRMN exposure by roughly 20-30% into any bounce toward the $280-290 area (below the recent $313 high), rather than adding on the current pullback — the bull's 'buying opportunity' framing is not supported by the growth math.
2) Do not initiate new/incremental positions at the current ~28.4x P/E / 2.93 PEG; require either (a) a price reset closer to 22-24x P/E (roughly $220-240) or (b) confirmed evidence that Q2's 35.3% net income growth is a trend rather than a one-quarter margin-driven spike (i.e., Q3 revenue growth reaccelerating into double digits) before re-adding.
3) Keep a residual core position sized to the company's genuine quality (elite margins, net-cash balance sheet, $1.04B FCF, dividend) — this is not a 'sell everything' call — but size it as a lower-conviction holding rather than a growth position.
4) Set a hard risk trigger: if price breaks decisively below the 50-day SMA (~$269) on rising volume, treat that as confirmation of trend change and reduce further; if price instead reclaims and holds above $300 on strong volume with a revenue re-acceleration print, revisit the rating upward.
5) Watch marine segment order trends and auto-OEM commentary specifically as the tell for discretionary/financed-purchase weakness the bear flagged; deteriorating marine/auto commentary alongside continued held-rate policy would be the signal to cut further.
6) Avoid leverage or options structures that assume multiple expansion; if hedging the residual position, consider protective puts or covered calls to monetize the elevated implied volatility around the current consolidation.
