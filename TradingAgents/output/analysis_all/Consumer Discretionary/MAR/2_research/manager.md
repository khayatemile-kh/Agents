That's a more coherent read of the technical picture than 'oversold = buy.'

On Luxury Expeditions: the bear's quantification (0.02-0.25% of revenue over 2026-2027E) convincingly shows this is not a near-term earnings driver, regardless of long-term optionality. The bull's Disney-experiences analogy is a multi-year growth story at best, not a catalyst that offsets current-cycle risk.

Macro: an 8% recession probability is genuinely low, so this isn't a full bear thesis with imminent collapse — that's why I'm not going to Sell. But the bear's observation that consumer discretionary is underperforming the S&P by ~15pts even with low recession odds is a real yellow flag for demand durability that the bull doesn't really answer.

Net: the bear wins on balance sheet resilience, cash flow cushion, technical trend, and near-term catalyst quantification. The bull's structural growth story is real but too small and too far out to offset the near-term risk/reward, and the valuation (34.45x TTM PE) isn't justified if earnings momentum is decelerating into a leveraged, negative-equity capital structure. This warrants trimming exposure rather than adding, but outright Sell is too aggressive given resilient EBITDA coverage (~5.8x) and low absolute recession risk — hence Underweight.
