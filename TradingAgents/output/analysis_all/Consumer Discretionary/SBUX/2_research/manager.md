# RESEARCH MANAGER'S FINAL ASSESSMENT: SBUX

## VERDICT: **HOLD**

---

## RATIONALE: Both Sides Score Real Points, But Neither Wins Decisively

### Where the Bull Wins

1. **Leverage metrics are genuinely improving.** The 19.6% YoY reduction in total debt ($27.89B → $22.43B) and 25% reduction in net debt are hard, verifiable numbers — not narrative. A company actively de-levering is not a company in crisis mode.

2. **The lease-accounting framing has merit, even if incomplete.** ASC 842 does mechanically depress equity for lease-heavy operators versus owner-operators like MCD. The bear is right that the *obligations are real regardless of where they sit*, but the bull is also right that equity sign alone is a poor solvency signal in this business model. Neither side should treat this metric alone as dispositive.

3. **The bull's peer framing (QSR/CMG/YUM operating with thin or negative equity) is a legitimate industry-context point** the bear does not fully rebut.

### Where the Bear Wins — and Wins More Decisively

1. **The EPS/net income beat is largely a mirage.** The bear's math is the most important exchange in this entire debate: stripping the $536M gain on sale reduces Q2 net income from $1.05B to ~$514M — roughly **flat to down YoY**, not +88%. The bull *concedes this explicitly* ("Strip out the gain: Net income ≈ $514M... down ~-8% YoY") and then pivots to forward guidance without new evidence to support it. This is the single most damaging admission in the debate, and it undercuts the centerpiece of the bull's "operational inflection" narrative.

2. **Revenue decline is unaddressed as a growth problem.** -1.5% YoY revenue with margin gains coming from cost-cutting and a 224-person layoff (a one-time structural adjustment, not a repeatable lever) is a classic "shrink to profitability" pattern, not a demand-driven turnaround. The bull's "pricing power" argument is asserted, not proven with data on traffic/comp trends in this prompt.

3. **Valuation is stretched against unproven forward numbers.** A 33.6x forward P/E requires believing $3.16 forward EPS is achievable *organically*, but Q1 2026 normalized EPS (~$0.45) and Q2 normalized EPS (~$0.44) show no sequential acceleration. The bull's growth assumption ("+83% EPS growth") rests on an unsubstantiated bridge — this is the debate's biggest evidentiary gap on the bull side.

4. **Capital returns funded against negative and worsening retained earnings** (-$8.54B) is a legitimate capital-allocation concern the bull never rebuts — it only reframes negative equity as an "industry norm," not addressing whether the dividend is sustainably funded by *organic* cash generation versus asset sales and debt issuance.

---

## WHY NOT BUY / OVERWEIGHT

The bull's core operational-inflection thesis is not supported once the one-time gain is stripped out. Forward multiple (33.6x) is pricing in growth that has not yet shown up in normalized quarterly results. Recommending increased exposure now would mean betting on unconfirmed guidance against a backdrop of declining revenue.

## WHY NOT UNDERWEIGHT / SELL

The balance sheet is de-levering in absolute dollar terms (a real, verifiable positive), and the negative-equity/lease-accounting issue — while real — is not new information nor a sign of imminent distress; it's a structural feature of the business model that bears have raised without demonstrating an actual liquidity or covenant risk. There is no evidence in this prompt of covenant breach, credit downgrade, or liquidity shortfall. Selling into a name with improving leverage trends and a demonstrated ability to monetize non-core assets is premature.

---

## ACTIONABLE PLAN

**Rating: Hold**

- **Maintain current position size.** Do not add on the "turnaround" narrative until at least one additional quarter confirms normalized (ex-gains) EPS growth and revenue stabilization/inflection.
- **Do not trim aggressively** — the de-leveraging trend and lease-accounting context provide a floor against a "balance sheet is collapsing" bear case.
- **Key catalysts to watch before revisiting stance:**
  1. Q3/Q4 2026 normalized EPS (excluding any further asset-sale gains) — needs to show organic growth, not just tough comps against one-time items.
  2. Revenue trend — does the -1.5% YoY decline stabilize or worsen into negative comps?
  3. Confirmation of whether margin gains persist beyond the 224-person layoff base effect (i.e., is there a second leg of cost efficiency, or was that the extent of it?).
  4. Any change in negative retained earnings trajectory — is the deficit narrowing or widening?

**Bottom line:** This is a name where the bull thesis requires you to look past clear evidence of earnings-quality dilution, and the bear thesis requires you to treat a structural (and arguably overstated) balance-sheet quirk as more alarming than it likely is. Wait for cleaner confirmation of organic earnings power before taking a directional stance.
