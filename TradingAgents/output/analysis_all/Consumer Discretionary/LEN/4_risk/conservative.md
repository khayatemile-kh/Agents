## The Aggressive Analyst's Technical Argument Ignores Mean Reversion Mechanics

Here's where I think the technical analysis becomes genuinely misleading. They're pointing to the Bollinger Band extremity and saying "this is exhaustion without reversal signals" because RSI is at 42 instead of below 30. But that's cherry-picking which technical indicators matter.

Let me reframe what's actually happening: LEN is trading below the lower Bollinger Band at $83.34, which means it's outside two standard deviations of the 20-day moving average. Historically, this condition resolves in one of two ways:

1. **The moving average itself turns down** (confirming further deterioration), or
2. **Price mean-reverts back toward the middle band** (which is normal market behavior)

The aggressive analyst is betting on scenario 1 and dismissing scenario 2 because "RSI isn't oversold enough." But that's not how mean reversion works. Mean reversion doesn't care about RSI threshold levels—it cares about statistical improbability. Stocks don't stay outside two standard deviations indefinitely. They either adjust through price action or through the moving average adjusting downward. One or the other will resolve the extremity.

Given that LEN has elevated volume on the down day (confirming selling conviction), and given that we're already 8.3% down from June highs, the probability that we get a mean-reversion bounce here is actually quite high. The aggressive analyst is timing the market at a point of maximum pessimism and calling it certainty.
