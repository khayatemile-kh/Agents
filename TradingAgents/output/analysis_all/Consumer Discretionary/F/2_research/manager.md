**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The debate lays out a fundamentally sound but currently deteriorating business. The bull's strongest points—cheap forward P/E (7.4x), 4.34% dividend yield, and the argument that Ford Blue/Pro cash generation funds the EV transition—are real, but they lean heavily on 'normalized' and 'forward' figures that assume a smooth recovery no one has proven yet. The bear's rebuttal is more forensic and harder to dismiss: the swing from +$12.5B FY2025 FCF to -$7.9B TTM FCF is a real, recent, and material deterioration, not a one-off; the 89% gross-margin collapse cannot be explained away by EV mix alone since EVs are only ~7-10% of unit volume, meaning the margin compression is bleeding into the core ICE/legacy business too (pricing power loss, structural wage and input cost increases from UAW contracts, elevated battery/lithium costs vs. Tesla). The bear also correctly notes that Ford Blue+Pro profits are being outweighed by Model e losses (roughly $5-7B profit vs $8-10B EV loss), and that this negative operating leverage is intensifying rather than easing given rising Chinese EV competition and Tesla's scale advantage. The cheap valuation multiples the bull cites are precisely the kind of 'value trap' signal — a low P/E and high dividend yield are already discounting distress, and dividend sustainability itself is questionable if FCF is negative on a trailing basis. The bull's tariff argument is speculative and contingent on policy that hasn't been enacted, so it carries little weight against the concrete, reported cash-flow and margin deterioration the bear cites. On balance, the bear's data-driven dismantling of the 'temporary EV ramp' story and the working-capital-inflated OCF argument is more convincing and more grounded in the actual reported numbers than the bull's forward-looking optimism. This tips the recommendation toward caution rather than a balanced Hold — the negative TTM FCF, margin collapse across the whole business (not just EVs), and structural cost pressures argue for reducing exposure until there's clear evidence of margin stabilization and FCF turning positive again. It's not a full sell only because the dividend yield and depressed valuation multiple provide some support and this is a widely-held, systemically important industrial name that isn't going to zero — but the risk/reward skews negative near-term.

**Strategic Actions**: Trim existing F positions by roughly 25-40% into any near-term strength (e.g., rallies toward $14.50-15.50), reallocating proceeds to names with confirmed positive and stable free cash flow. Retain a reduced core position to preserve dividend income optionality, but do not add on weakness until (a) TTM FCF turns positive again, (b) gross margin shows sequential improvement outside of one-off/restructuring adjustments, and (c) Ford Model e losses narrow on a per-unit basis for at least two consecutive quarters. Set a hard stop/re-evaluation trigger if the quarterly dividend is cut or FCF payout ratio breaches unsustainable levels, as that would confirm the bear's structural cash-burn thesis and warrant moving to full Sell. Avoid adding to the position on the 'cheap forward P/E' argument alone, since that multiple already reflects the market's skepticism about the EV transition and is not yet corroborated by improving cash conversion. Use options (covered calls against the remaining position) to harvest additional yield while waiting for clearer margin/cash-flow signals.
