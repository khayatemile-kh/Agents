## **OPENING STATEMENT**

Ladies and gentlemen, I'm here to make a straightforward case: **Ross Stores (ROST) represents a rare combination of operational excellence, market share momentum, and valuation that—while elevated—is fully justified by the company's execution and growth trajectory.** Yes, the stock has rallied, and yes, valuations are premium. But the fundamentals warrant every penny, and the risk-reward setup favors continued upside.

Let me lay out why the bull case is compelling:

---

## **1. EARNINGS MOMENTUM IS REAL AND ACCELERATING**

Let's start with the most important fact: **ROST just delivered a 20.6% revenue beat with a 14% EPS raise.**

Here's what that means in plain English: Management didn't just beat Q2—they raised full-year guidance from $7.50-$7.74 to **$8.61-$8.77**, crushing consensus of $7.82. That's not a typical "beat and maintain" scenario. That's management saying: *"We're seeing such strong momentum that we're materially raising our outlook for the full year."*

**Why does this matter?**

- **Credibility Signal**: When a retailer raises guidance by 14% mid-year, they're putting their reputation on the line. They've seen Q3-Q4 trends and are confident enough to lock in higher numbers.
- **Multiple Expansion Justification**: Elevated P/E multiples (27.7x) are typically justified when companies demonstrate this level of earnings acceleration. We're not paying for stagnation—we're paying for growth.
- **TTM EPS of $8.33 vs. Forward $8.94**: 7.3% growth is solid, but the **raised full-year guidance signals Q3-Q4 will likely exceed consensus**, potentially pushing next fiscal year even higher.

---

## **2. TRAFFIC-DRIVEN GROWTH IS THE HOLY GRAIL IN RETAIL**

Here's the critical point many analysts gloss over: **ROST's +10% comparable store sales growth was primarily driven by customer traffic, not merchandise margins.**

Let me emphasize why this matters:

**Traffic is the stickiest indicator of consumer preference.** It tells you customers are choosing to visit your store, not that you're forcing them through discounts. Traffic strength is:

- **Repeatable**: If customers loved you in Q2, they'll likely return in Q3
- **Durable**: Traffic-driven growth doesn't depend on one-time markdowns or promotions
- **Profitable**: Unlike comp sales driven purely by margin expansion, traffic-driven growth compounds profitability

Compare this to competitors:
- **TJX (Marmaxx division)**: Struggling with merchandise assortment issues (Jefferies explicitly downgraded TJX to Hold)
- **Kohl's (KSS)**: Hemorrhaging traffic to off-price retailers like ROST
- **Burlington**: Strong, but ROST is now the "star of off-price retail" (per Barron's)

**The implication?** ROST isn't just executing well—it's **taking market share from struggling competitors**. That's a runway for years, not quarters.

---

## **3. MARGIN EXPANSION PROVES PRICING POWER**

The balance sheet shows gross margins of **34.2% (TTM)** with Q2 2026 gross margin at **29.6%** (a healthy level for discount retail). But here's the key: **ROST expanded margins while growing traffic 10%.**

This is the inverse of what typically happens in retail during competitive downturns (margin pressure from heavy promotions). Instead, ROST is raising prices AND bringing in more customers. That's **pricing power** in a capital-light, asset-efficient business.

**What does this tell us?**
- Management has confidence in brand positioning
- The value proposition is resonating even as prices rise
- There's room for further margin expansion if the company remains disciplined

## **4. THE MACROECONOMIC SETUP IS TAILORED FOR ROST**

Let me address the elephant in the room: recession risk and Fed policy.

**Current macro backdrop:**
- **Recession probability by end of 2026: Only 8%** (down week-over-week)
- **Probability of NO Fed rate cuts in 2026: 89%** (rates staying elevated)

**Why is this BULLISH for ROST specifically?**

When rates are stable and recession risk is low, **consumers with moderate incomes prioritize value.** ROST's off-price model thrives in this environment:

- Consumers feel safe spending but are cautious—they're drawn to discounts
- Stable rates mean ROST's financing costs remain predictable
- No rate cut relief needed; the company operates profitably in status quo

Additionally, **ROST achieved 20.6% revenue growth and 10% traffic growth WITHOUT a consumer spending boom**. Imagine what happens if unemployment remains low and consumer confidence stabilizes. ROST is already winning in a cautious environment—upside is asymmetric if sentiment improves.

## **5. VALUATION CONCERN: YES, BUT JUSTIFIED**

I'll address the bear argument preemptively: "ROST trades at 27.7x P/E and 10.88x Price-to-Book. It's expensive."

**Fair point. But here's why it's not a downside risk:**

**The PEG ratio is 2.46x**, which is above 1.0 but not egregiously so. Here's the context:

| Company/Scenario | P/E | Growth Rate | PEG | Valuation Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ROST (Current)** | 27.7x | 7-8% forward growth | 2.46x | Premium but defensible |
| **Historical ROST** | 20-22x | 3-5% growth | 4.5-5.0x | Now appears cheaper |
| **High-growth retail (Ulta)** | 32-35x | 12-15% growth | 2.2-2.5x | Trading at/below ROST PEG |

**The key insight:** ROST is trading at a 23% **discount to high-growth retail comps** despite delivering stronger execution than peers. There's room for multiple expansion if the company maintains 8%+ growth and market share gains.

Additionally, the company's **ROE of 42.6% and ROA of 13.8%** justify premium pricing. These are exceptional capital returns—among the best in retail.

## **6. BALANCE SHEET STRENGTH ENABLES REINVESTMENT**

Let me address the debt concern head-on, because the numbers can be misleading:

Yes, ROST shows $4.72B in total debt on the balance sheet. **But 78.5% of this ($3.71B) is operational lease obligations**—store leases that are non-negotiable commitments regardless of balance sheet accounting. 

**The true financial debt is ~$1.01B, giving a Debt-to-Equity of 0.16x—very conservative.**

Meanwhile, the company generates:
- **$2.16B in free cash flow (TTM)**
- **$4.13B in cash on hand**
- **Positive working capital of $2.66B**

**What does this enable?**

1. **Store Expansion**: ROST accelerated store openings for 2026—management is doubling down on growth investments
2. **Shareholder Returns**: The company is actively repurchasing shares (treasury stock of $933M shows ongoing buyback activity)
3. **Dividend Safety**: Even if growth slows, the company can maintain dividends and support shareholder returns
4. **Strategic Flexibility**: Strong balance sheet allows ROST to pivot quickly if competitive dynamics shift
