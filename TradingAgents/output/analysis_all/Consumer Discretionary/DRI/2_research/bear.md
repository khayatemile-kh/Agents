## **I. THE "EARNINGS GROWTH" NARRATIVE IS BUILT ON SAND**

### **A. Q4 EPS Was Seasonally Inflated—Not Proof of Acceleration**

The bull cites Q4 EPS of **$3.51, up 32% from Q3's $2.66** as evidence of "accelerating profitability." This is misleading. Let me show you why:

**Quarterly EPS Pattern:**
| Quarter | EPS | YoY Change | Sequential | Seasonality Factor |
|---------|-----|-----------|-----------|-------------------|
| Q1 2026 | $2.24 | — | — | **Weak (post-holiday)** |
| Q2 2026 | $2.06 | — | -8.0% | **Weak (summer budget)** |
| Q3 2026 | $2.66 | — | +29.1% | **Moderate (back-to-school)** |
| Q4 2026 | $3.51 | — | +32.0% | **STRONG (fiscal year-end, summer/fall dining peak)** |

**Here's what the bull isn't telling you:** Q4 is *always* the strongest restaurant quarter. It captures summer dining, back-to-school traffic, and year-end celebrations. **This is not operational acceleration—this is the seasonal calendar.**

To validate real momentum, I need to see **Q1 2027 vs. Q1 2026 YoY comparison.** The bull doesn't provide this. Why? Because Q1 is historically weak for restaurants, and if Q1 2027 shows weakness relative to Q1 2026, the narrative collapses.

### **B. Forward EPS of $12.39 (+18.9%) Is Not Credible**

The bull touts forward EPS growth of +18.9% as evidence of durability. Let's stress-test this claim:

**TTM EPS: $10.43**
**Forward EPS: $12.39**

Here's the problem: **This forward estimate was likely issued 60–90 days ago.** Since then:

The forward estimate embedded **optimistic assumptions made before August's competitive deterioration.** When consensus gets revised (and it will), the stock will re-rate lower.

### **C. Margin Expansion Math Doesn't Survive Scrutiny**

The bull celebrates +290 bps gross margin improvement (Q1: 20.4% → Q4: 23.3%) as proof of "pricing power and operational efficiency." 

**Let me challenge this directly:**

**Gross Margin Components in Restaurants:**
- Food costs (variable)
- Direct labor (sticky, wage-inflationary)
- Occupancy (fixed, contractual leases)
- Marketing (discretionary but necessary)

The +290 bps margin expansion *within a single fiscal year* suggests one of two things:

1. **Aggressive pricing** (which erodes traffic/traffic, which contradicts the competitors' "traffic growth" narrative)
2. **One-time cost reductions** (such as commodity tailwinds or supply-chain normalization)

**Here's the red flag:** If pricing drove margins, competitors would report traffic pressure. Instead, they report *traffic gains*. This suggests:
- DRI raised prices AND absorbed some traffic loss (margin up, volume down)
- Competitors are gaining share by holding prices/investing in traffic
- **DRI is winning on margins at the expense of market share**

This is a **pyrrhic victory.** Margins are a temporary tailwind; market share is structural.
