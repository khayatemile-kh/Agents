**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points that roughly offset each other, so this is one of those genuinely balanced cases rather than a one-sided call.

The bull's strongest points: DECK trades at 11.3x TTM P/E versus 25-35x for LULU/NKE/ONON, a stark discount given FY2026 revenue still grew ~9.8% with a fortress balance sheet ($1.6B cash, 2.75x current ratio) and no distress indicators. The buyback reducing share count 4.6% is real, even if its timing is debatable.

The bear's strongest points are more data-driven and harder to dismiss: the sequential trend within FY2026 is genuinely deteriorating, not just noise — gross margin compressed from 59.8% (Q4'25) to 57.6% (Q1'26) to 56.4% (Q2'26), a persistent 340bp slide; net income fell sharply from $481M to $130-136M per quarter; and inventory jumped 66% sequentially ($487M to $808M) against flat-to-declining revenue. That inventory/margin combination is the same setup that caused the Dick's-driven selloff, and the bear correctly notes DECK stock fell in sympathy with VF Corp, Levi's, Under Armour, and Crocs — meaning the market may be pricing in real read-through risk, not just irrational contagion. The bear's point that a "cheap" P/E can simply reflect the market correctly pricing peak/declining forward earnings is a valid rebuttal to the pure multiple-comparison bull argument, and the buyback-timing critique (buying back stock now that's down 20-25% from where prior buybacks were executed) is a fair capital-allocation criticism rather than a pure conviction signal.

Neither side has decisively refuted the other: the bull hasn't addressed the inventory buildup or intra-year margin compression trend with hard evidence, and the bear hasn't disproven that a mean-reversion bounce could occur off oversold technicals, nor conclusively shown FY2027 guidance is deteriorating (only sequential compression within FY2026, which could partly reflect normal seasonality in a footwear business with Ugg's boot cycle). Given the balance-sheet strength and buyback support on one hand, and clear margin/inventory deterioration plus sector-wide sympathy selling on the other, the fair call is to stay neutral: keep existing exposure but don't add or trim aggressively until the inventory/margin trend clarifies in the next print.
