## 3. THE FORTRESS BALANCE SHEET IS A MIRAGE: LIQUIDITY DOESN'T HELP IF DEMAND EVAPORATES

### The Bull Argument: "$33.7B Liquidity + 29% Debt-to-Equity = Safety"

I need to challenge this directly: **A strong balance sheet is a defensive asset in a cyclical downturn, not an offensive weapon.** It keeps you alive—it doesn't keep earnings growing.

**Here's the actual risk:**

**DHI's $26.7B Inventory Problem:**
- 73% of total assets are inventory (homes in progress, land, development)
- In a declining housing market, this inventory becomes a **liability, not an asset**

**The Liquidity Paradox:**
- Yes, DHI has $33.7B in available liquidity

  - **Scenario 2 (I expect):** Demand remains weak; DHI burns cash to:
    - Carry unsold inventory
    - Mark down prices to move homes
    - Fund legal settlements (mold litigation)

**The Current Debt Picture Is Already Rising:**
- Current debt/LOC rose to $1.32B (Q2 2026) from lower levels
- This suggests DHI is already accessing credit lines to fund operations in a "flat" revenue environment
- If revenue actually declines, short-term borrowing will accelerate

**The fortress looks impressive until you ask:** What happens if this downturn lasts 2+ years instead of 6 months? Fortress balance sheets eventually deteriorate if cash burn persists.
