**Rationale**: Weighing both sides, the bear case lands harder on the specific quantitative issues that matter most for near-term price action. The B2B 'record demand' point cuts both ways, but bear's framing that record backlogs often mark cycle peaks (per Herman Miller/Steelcase precedent) is a reasonable historical caution rather than an outright refutation. On valuation, 22.7x trailing / 21x forward P/E with a 2.58x PEG is undeniably rich for a specialty home retailer facing consumer discretionary cyclicality, K-shaped consumption pressure on Pottery Barn/West Elm's mass-premium base, and margin compression — the bull's defense that 'the market has already priced in a discount' is a rationalization, not hard evidence of undervaluation. On technicals, oversold RSI (35) and price below the lower Bollinger Band do suggest a near-term bounce is plausible, but a technical mean-reversion bounce is not sufficient justification to add to a position when the fundamental margin trend is deteriorating and the multiple is elevated relative to that trend. Net: the bear's argument that current pricing is a rerating beginning, not a buying opportunity, is more grounded in the company's own reported numbers (margin compression despite tariff tailwinds, cost inflation in freight/labor/inventory carrying costs, unproven AI economics) than the bull's narrative-driven case. This doesn't rise to a full Sell — the company still posts genuinely elite profitability (17%+ operating margin, ~55% ROE, positive comps across all three brands) — but conviction should tilt cautious. I'm recommending Underweight: trim/lighten exposure into strength or technical bounces, avoid initiating/adding at current elevated multiples until margin trajectory stabilizes and AI/B2B claims show durable, non-tariff-inflated evidence over the next 1-2 quarters.
