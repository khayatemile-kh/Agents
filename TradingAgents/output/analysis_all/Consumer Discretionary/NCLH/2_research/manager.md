**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the facts—Q2 2026 showed a real sequential improvement (revenue +4.9% YoY, EBITDA margin 26.2%, EPS beat), but management's own Q3 guidance calls for an 8.9% YoY net yield decline. That guidance is the single most important data point in this debate because it's forward-looking, company-issued, and directly contradicts the bull's 'margin inflection is beginning' narrative. The bull's argument that slower 2.5% capacity CAGR signals pricing power is unconvincing next to the bear's more logical read: constrained capacity growth alongside a management-guided yield decline signals demand caution, not pricing discipline. The bear's quantification—an ~$220M/quarter revenue headwind from the 8.9% yield decline versus only ~$125M/quarter of cost savings—makes a credible case that further EBITDA deterioration, not expansion, is the more likely near-term path, even though the cost program is real and will help at the margin.

On the balance sheet, the bull's explanation of negative working capital as structurally normal for cruise operators (prepaid deposits, $3.65B deferred revenue) is fair and reduces solvency-panic concerns, but it doesn't change the fact that net debt of $14.82B and the sharp FCF deceleration (Q2 2026 FCF of $144.9M vs $381.2M in Q2 2025, a 62% decline) leave much less cushion than the bull's 'debt service is well covered' argument implies. The bull's forward-looking $2.4B annualized OCF estimate is built by simply annualizing one summer-peak quarter, which the bear correctly flags as a seasonality-driven distortion; Q3 2025's FCF was actually negative ($726.5M), which undercuts the idea that cash generation is durable across the year. That seasonality point is a legitimate and largely unaddressed critique of the bull's cash-flow coverage math.

The oil/fuel argument is a wash — both sides acknowledge it's a real but modest (50-60bps) headwind that isn't decisive either way.

Net: the bull thesis leans heavily on interpreting management's own negative forward guidance as noise to be dismissed, and on annualizing peak-season cash flow to justify leverage comfort — both are weak links. The bear's case, that guided yield deterioration outpaces disclosed cost savings and that FCF is decelerating meaningfully year-over-year even during a seasonally strong quarter, is better grounded in the actual disclosed figures. This doesn't rise to full 'Sell' conviction, however — the bull raises legitimate points about deposit-funded working capital being structurally normal for this industry and about real cost-cutting programs in motion, and valuation at 9.4x P/E already reflects a lot of pessimism. Given the credible near-term downside risk (yield guidance, decelerating FCF, high leverage) outweighing but not completely negating the recovery/valuation support, the appropriate stance is cautious: trim exposure rather than aggressively add or hold pat.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing NCLH exposure by roughly 25-35%, using any near-term bounce toward $16-17 to reduce the position rather than adding on weakness — do not treat the post-earnings selloff as a buying opportunity given management's own guidance for an 8.9% Q3 yield decline.
2) Do not initiate new or incremental long positions until there is explicit management commentary showing yield stabilization (not just cost-cut execution) for Q4 2026 or Q1 2027 bookings; the current lack of forward yield guidance beyond the negative Q3 print is itself a warning sign.
3) Monitor quarterly FCF trend versus the prior-year comp specifically (not just sequential quarter-over-quarter, which is distorted by seasonality) — a re-acceleration back toward the ~$380M Q2 2025 FCF level would be the key signal to revisit the bearish stance.
4) Watch net debt/leverage trajectory closely; with $14.82B net debt and FCF decelerating 62% YoY in the seasonally strongest quarter, any incremental deterioration in operating cash flow materially pressures debt service capacity — set a risk trigger to cut further if OCF guidance is revised down.
5) Keep a residual position (do not go to zero) to retain optionality on the deep-value/cost-cutting thesis and potential activist involvement (Klarman/Singer stakes), but size it as a small, clearly risk-managed allocation reflecting cautious rather than constructive conviction.
6) Reassess at next earnings print specifically on: (a) whether yield decline moderates from -8.9%, (b) whether the $500M cost savings program shows up in actual EBITDA margin holding flat or better despite yield pressure, and (c) capex/FCF trend versus prior year.
