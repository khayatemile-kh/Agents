On margins, the Conservative Analyst's rebuttal (that opex deleverage rather than promotions may explain part of the compression) is a fair caveat but doesn't overturn the core bear point: gross margin still compressed 350bps YoY to 55.7%, and the mechanism the bulls need—pricing power—is precisely what's being tested by the need to discount. No analyst produced hard evidence of a non-promotional driver strong enough to fully explain the compression.

The tariff risk remains genuinely unquantified by all three analysts, and the Neutral Analyst's take is the most credible resolution: the absence of pre-earnings clarity during an active Section 338 negotiation is a yellow flag given LULU's Canadian sourcing concentration, not a reason to assume the bull's blanket 2-3% gross-profit hit is the ceiling.

On technicals, the Neutral Analyst correctly punctured the Aggressive Analyst's "airtight" cascade narrative — price bounced from $115 to $120.26 despite the MACD death cross, which is inconsistent with an imminent breakdown — but this cuts toward avoiding a full Sell, not toward a bullish stance. Balance sheet strength ($2.2B working capital, still-positive FCF) is the decisive reason this is a trim, not a liquidation — there is no solvency risk, and a stabilization surprise could produce a sharp reflexive bounce given how depressed sentiment already is. That keeps the position short of a Sell/full-exit call, consistent with the Research Manager's plan and the Trader's proposed sizing, while acknowledging the Trader's raw "Sell" action label is directionally aligned with reducing exposure but is better implemented as a disciplined partial trim (Underweight) rather than a full exit, given the offsetting balance-sheet quality and the genuinely two-sided binary catalyst the Neutral Analyst identified.
