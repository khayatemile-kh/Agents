- **GM's 5-year historical EBITDA margin:** 8-12% (we're at the low end of a compressed range)
- **Toyota's EBITDA margin:** 12-15%
- **Ford's EBITDA margin:** 8-10%
- **Industry average (tier-1 OEM):** 9-12%

**Here's the critical insight:** GM's margins aren't at the *floor*—they're deteriorating *toward* the floor. The bull wants to tell you this is a "transition issue," but look at the quarterly data:

The forward P/E of 5.72x is priced assuming earnings stabilize around current levels. **But if margins continue compressing toward 7-8% EBITDA (more realistic given competitive pressure), earnings could fall 20-30% from here.** That means the "5.72x cheap stock" is actually trading at **7.5-8.2x normalized earnings**—not cheap at all.

## 2. TOYOTA'S HYBRID PUSH IS NOT A "MIX SHIFT"—IT'S MARKET SHARE COLLAPSE IN REAL TIME

The bull's rebuttal on Toyota is where the argument falls apart. Let me expose the weakness:

**The Bull's Argument:** *"Higher margin trucks offset lower volume. Market share in units doesn't matter—profitability does."*

**The Reality:** This is backwards. Here's why:

### A. Market Share Loss Accelerates, Not Stabilizes

The data shows:
- **Historical:** GM maintained 2:1 sales lead over Toyota for decades
- **Current (July 2026):** GM lead compressed to **100,000 vehicles**—a decline of 50%+ in the margin

**This isn't stabilization—it's acceleration.** When Toyota's hybrid strategy resonates this strongly with US consumers, it signals a **fundamental shift in demand preferences** that won't reverse. Consumers are choosing hybrids for *fuel economy*, not as a temporary alternative.

GM's response? Invest in the same hybrid technology Toyota perfected 20 years ago. **GM is now playing catch-up in a category where Toyota has the brand equity and manufacturing advantage.** That's not a sustainable competitive position.

### B. The Bull's Margin Assumption is Unrealistic

The bull claims:
> *"If Toyota gains 50,000 hybrid units at $3,000 net margin vs. GM losing volume but at $10,000 net margin, GM's profit scales differently."*

**This is fantasy.** Here's the actual math:

**What Actually Happens:**
- **Toyota gains 100,000 units** (not 50,000) at hybrid volumes accelerating globally
- **Toyota's profit per unit:** $4,000-$5,000 (hybrids have higher labor/parts costs, but volume offsets)
- **GM loses 100,000 truck units** at current trend
- **GM's profit per unit on trucks:** $8,000-$10,000 (higher, but volumes shrinking)

**The Math:**
- **Scenario 1 (Current):** GM: 100K trucks × $9K = $900M. Toyota: 200K hybrids × $4.5K = $900M. **Tie.**
- **Scenario 2 (Trend Continues):** GM: 50K trucks × $9K = $450M. Toyota: 300K hybrids × $4.5K = $1.35B. **Toyota wins 3:1.**

The bull's thesis assumes **market volumes stay constant and only mix shifts.** That's not how markets work. As consumers choose hybrids, **total addressable volume for expensive trucks shrinks**. GM can't maintain $9K profit per unit when the market is demanding cheaper, more efficient vehicles.

**Evidence:** Look at GM's Q4 2025 EBITDA collapse. When does a company see 93% EBITDA margin compression? **When they cut prices to compete and volumes still fall.** That's exactly what's happening.

### C. EV Margin Claims Are Optimistic Fantasy

The bull also claims:
> *"By 2027-2028, as EV volumes scale and costs normalize, GM's 3x revenue scale advantage over Tesla becomes a moat."*

**This is where the bull's argument breaks completely.** Here's why this EV thesis is wrong:

**EV Gross Margins Are Under Structural Pressure:**
- **Tesla's EV gross margins:** 25-30% (down from 45% just 2 years ago)
- **GM's EV gross margins (Bolt, Ultium):** 15-20% currently
- **Industry trajectory:** EV margins compress 3-5% annually as competition intensifies and battery costs fall slower than expected

**Why Scale Doesn't Help GM:**
1. **Battery costs fall for everyone, not just GM.** BYD, Tesla, and Chinese OEMs achieve similar or better battery economics. Scale helps, but doesn't create a durable moat.
2. **EV platforms commoditize.** The Ultium platform is good, but not uniquely defensible. By 2027, there will be 20+ EV platforms in the market. Differentiation erodes.
3. **Tesla's brand advantage persists.** Even as Tesla's margins fall, consumer demand for Tesla EVs (driven by Autopilot, Supercharger network, brand cachet) remains premium. GM's EVs are "me-too" products in a crowded market.
4. **Labor costs kill GM's advantage.** GM pays UAW workers $32+/hour fully-loaded. Tesla pays non-union workers $18-25/hour. **That's a $14-15/hour structural cost disadvantage that scale doesn't overcome.**

**The Result:** By 2028, GM's EV EBITDA margins will likely be **5-8%**, not the 12-15% the bull is implying. Combined with hybrids at 8-10%, **overall EBITDA margins compress to 6-7%—a death spiral for a highly leveraged company.**
