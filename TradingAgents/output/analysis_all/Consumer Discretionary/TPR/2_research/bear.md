## PART 1: THE COACH "GENERATIONAL WEALTH CREATOR" IS A FANTASY

### **The Bull's Argument: Coach at $10B Revenue = 125% of Current Profitability**

This is speculative nonsense dressed up as analysis. Let me explain why:

**1. The Math Is Built On Heroic Assumptions**

The bull claims:
- Coach reaches $10B revenue (it's currently at ~$7B)
- At 20% operating margin
- Therefore $2B in operating income

**Here's what they're ignoring:**

First, **Coach is NOT growing at 20%+ annually.** The luxury market reports show Coach growth is in the mid-to-high single digits to low teens range. Going from $7B to $10B requires:
- **42.8% total revenue growth** over the next 3-4 years
- That's **~10% annualized growth sustained**

Can Coach achieve that? *Maybe*. But it's not a certainty—it's an aspirational target buried in management guidance that has already disappointed the market multiple times (evidenced by the 25.5% crash).

Second, **the 20% operating margin assumption is aggressive.** Current TPR TTM operating margin is 19.82%. For Coach *specifically* (which has higher margins than Kate Spade), let's grant 22-23% at scale. But here's the problem:

**Cost inflation is real.** The bull acknowledges tariff risk in passing and then dismisses it by saying "luxury brands have pricing power." This is **dangerously incomplete analysis:**

- Tariff pressure isn't just about product costs; it hits the entire supply chain
- Labor costs are rising across Asia (manufacturing hub for luxury goods)
- Freight and logistics costs remain elevated relative to pre-COVID baselines
- Luxury consumers *do* have price sensitivity at margin—Coach's Gen Z audience is particularly price-conscious at the lower end of luxury

**Historical precedent doesn't support the bull's tariff argument.** In 2018-2019, when tariffs hit luxury brands:
- Margins compressed, not held steady
- LVMH's profitability declined year-over-year in 2019
- Tapestry itself saw margin pressure (this isn't hypothetical; it happened)

The bull is using selective cherry-picking: "pricing power works" (true in *some* scenarios) while ignoring that **volume can suffer when prices rise**, particularly for accessible luxury brands like Coach.

### **2. Coach Is Facing a Direct Existential Threat That The Bull Completely Downplayed**

The bull glossed over the **Gap/Reed Krakoff threat** in one sentence. That's a massive error in analysis.

**Here's what's actually happening:**
- Reed Krakoff is the *architect* of Coach's accessible luxury positioning
- Gap is launching a **$29.95-$498 handbag line** directly targeting Coach's sweet spot
- Gap has massive distribution advantages: physical retail, online, brand recognition

**Why this matters:**
Coach's entire value proposition is built on *brand recognition + accessible prices + quality.* Gap is now competing on all three vectors. This isn't like some random fast-fashion competitor; this is the *creator of Coach's playbook* building a competitive product for a company that desperately needs margin and will likely undercut Coach's pricing to gain market share.

The bull's response would probably be: "Coach is strong; it'll survive." But that misses the point entirely. The question isn't whether Coach *survives*—it's whether Coach hits its $10B revenue growth target *while competing against Reed Krakoff's Gap line.* 

**The odds of that happening just dropped materially.** And the market priced that in during the August 13 crash. The bull is arguing the market was wrong; I'm arguing the market was *prescient*.

### **3. Kate Spade Isn't a Rounding Error—It's Structural Underperformance**

The bull claims: "Kate Spade losses are already factored in; if they improve, that's upside."

This is technically true but deeply misleading. Here's why:

**Kate Spade's Situation Is Getting Worse, Not Better:**

Kate Spade is losing money not because of a temporary headwind—it's losing money because the brand has lost relevance with its target consumer. The bull offers no evidence that this is reversing. In fact:

- Gen Z consumers who are driving Coach's success don't view Kate Spade as desirable
- Kate Spade is stuck between Coach (cooler, more aspirational) and mass market (cheaper)
- The brand has no clear positioning strategy in a competitive luxury market

**The burden of proof is on the bull to show Kate Spade will stabilize.** They provide none. They just assume it remains a "small drag" indefinitely. But what if Kate Spade losses *accelerate*? What if management decides to divest or shut down the brand? That would trigger massive write-downs (recall the $854.8M impairment charge in Q2 2025—that could happen again).

**This isn't upside; it's downside risk disguised as a known headwind.**

## PART 2: THE "CASH FLOW MACHINE" ARGUMENT OBSCURES CRITICAL LEVERAGE PROBLEMS

### **The Bull's Argument: $1.4B FCF is Abundant; Negative Tangible Book Value Doesn't Matter**

Let me pull back the curtain on this misleading framing.

**1. The Bull Is Hiding Behind FCF While Ignoring Debt Reality**

Yes, TPR generates $1.4B in free cash flow. But here's the critical context:

| Metric | Value | Reality Check |
|--------|-------|---|
| **Total Debt** | $3.92 billion | Direct financial obligation |
| **Operating Leases** | $1.55 billion | Fixed obligations (retail stores) |
| **Total Debt + Leases** | $5.47 billion | Real economic burden |
| **FCF** | $1.40 billion | Annual cash after capex |
| **Net Leverage Ratio** | 3.9x to 4.0x | **Extremely high for luxury goods** |
| **Debt/EBITDA** | 1.93x | Manageable, BUT... |
| **Debt+Leases/EBITDA** | 2.73x | **Getting into risky territory** |

Here's the problem: **At current profitability levels, TPR needs to dedicate nearly 100% of its FCF just to service debt and maintain dividends/buybacks.** The bull celebrates the $1.4B FCF while ignoring that:

- $80-83M per quarter ($330M annualized) goes to dividends
- Buybacks are running at $400M+ per quarter ($1.6B annualized)
- That's $1.93B per year going to shareholders
- That leaves **only $0.45B** for net debt reduction

**This is capital allocation madness in an environment of rising rates and execution risk.**
