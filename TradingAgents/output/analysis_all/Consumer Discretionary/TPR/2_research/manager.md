**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points, but weighing them carefully, the bear case exposes real structural risks that the bull case underweights, while the bull's valuation and technical arguments, though attractive on the surface, rest on assumptions that haven't been stress-tested against the bear's counterpoints.

On the bull side: TPR does look statistically cheap (13.7x forward P/E, 0.62 PEG, 15-20% discount to luxury peers), generates real FCF ($1.4B TTM), and is technically oversold (RSI ~29.5) after a sharp 25.5% drawdown. The Coach brand's momentum with Gen Z and men's category expansion is real. If you believe this is a sentiment-driven capitulation rather than a fundamental re-rating, there's a mean-reversion trade here.

But the bear case lands harder on several fronts that matter more for a multi-quarter hold:
1. The Coach $10B target requires ~10% sustained annualized growth against a brand (Gap/Reed Krakoff) that is explicitly targeting Coach's accessible-luxury price point with the same architect who built Coach's playbook. That's a credible, structural competitive threat, not noise.
2. Kate Spade isn't a stable, priced-in drag — it's shown accelerating deterioration and already triggered an $854.8M impairment. The bull assumes stabilization with zero evidence; the bear rightly flips the burden of proof.
3. The leverage picture is more concerning than the bull's FCF framing suggests. Once you net out dividends (~$330M) and buybacks (~$1.6B annualized) against $1.4B FCF, there's minimal capacity left for debt paydown, and Debt+Leases/EBITDA at ~2.73x is elevated for a business facing brand and tariff risk simultaneously.
4. The tariff/pricing power argument cuts both ways — the bear correctly notes 2018-2019 precedent actually showed margin compression at LVMH and Tapestry itself, undermining the bull's cited "historical precedent."
5. Negative tangible book value of ~$995M is not just an accounting quirk — it's a magnitude problem: if the Gap/Krakoff competitive threat or Kate Spade weakness triggers further brand impairment, equity absorbs the hit directly, and we've already seen this play out with the $854.8M writedown.

The technical oversold setup (RSI, MACD, Bollinger support) is a trading signal, not a fundamental one — useful for tactical positioning but not sufficient to override the structural risk build-up the bear identifies. A V-bottom is possible, but it's a bet on sentiment reversal, and the stock cratered 25.5% on what appears to be a real guidance/competitive disappointment, not a liquidity-driven panic.

Net: the bear's structural arguments (Kate Spade acceleration, Coach competitive threat from Gap/Krakoff, leverage math after capital returns, impairment risk tied to negative tangible book value) are more concrete and harder to dismiss than the bull's largely valuation/technical-based optimism. This doesn't mean the stock is a table-pounding short — the cheap valuation and FCF generation provide some downside cushion — but the risk/reward skews cautious rather than aggressively bullish. I'm landing on Underweight: trim exposure and wait for clearer evidence that Kate Spade has stabilized and that Coach's growth/margin trajectory is holding up against the Gap/Krakoff threat before adding back.

**Strategic Actions**: 1. Trim existing TPR exposure by roughly 25-35% into any technical bounce (e.g., toward the $130-135 zone) rather than adding on weakness — do not chase the oversold RSI signal as a standalone buy trigger.
2. Do not add new capital until there is concrete evidence of: (a) Kate Spade revenue/margin stabilization for at least one full quarter, and (b) no incremental market-share loss from the Gap/Reed Krakoff accessible-luxury handbag line in Coach's core price tiers.
3. Monitor leverage metrics closely — specifically Debt+Leases/EBITDA (currently ~2.73x) and quarterly capital returns (dividends + buybacks ~$1.93B annualized) relative to FCF generation ($1.4B TTM); a widening gap here should trigger further de-risking.
4. Set a hard risk flag on any further goodwill/intangible impairment disclosures tied to Coach or Kate Spade — given negative tangible book value of ~$995M, another impairment event (similar to the $854.8M Q2 2025 charge) would be a strong incremental sell signal.
5. If retained, size the remaining position conservatively (underweight relative to benchmark/portfolio luxury allocation) and use tight stop-loss/trim discipline below the cited Bollinger support level (~$108) in case the "V-bottom" thesis fails and the decline resumes.
6. Reassess toward Hold/Overweight only if FY2027 guidance is reaffirmed alongside clear same-store/Coach brand comp acceleration and Kate Spade loss narrowing in the next 1-2 reporting periods.
