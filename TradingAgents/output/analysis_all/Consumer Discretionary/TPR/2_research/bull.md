### **3. Margin Expansion Is Real, Not Aspirational**

The bear case on margins typically hinges on tariff risk and Kate Spade drag. Let me address both:

**Tariff Risk: Manageable and Priced In**
- Yes, tariffs are a headwind. But luxury goods companies have pricing power that manufacturing-heavy companies don't
- Coach can implement price increases to offset tariff costs (luxury consumers are less price-sensitive than mass market)
- Historical precedent: When tariffs hit in 2018-2019, luxury brands maintained margins through pricing power
- **Management is confident enough in margin expansion to issue FY2027 guidance—that's $8.87 forward EPS**, which implies confident margin assumption

**Kate Spade: The Drag That's Already Factored In**
- Yes, Kate Spade is underperforming and generating losses. But here's the critical insight: **those losses are already impacting current earnings**
- Kate Spade revenue is approximately $1.5-2B (rough estimate from portfolio mix), which is small relative to Coach
- Management's guidance *assumes* Kate Spade losses continue—the $8.87 forward EPS already bakes in the headwind
- **If Kate Spade stabilizes or improves, that's upside to guidance, not a risk to it**

**The Math on Margin Expansion:**
- Current TTM operating margin: **19.82%**
- Management guidance implies achieving 20%+ operating margin
- At $8-8.5B revenue (conservative), even 100 basis points of margin expansion = $80-85M additional operating income
- That's roughly $0.20-0.25 of EPS, which is roughly 2.5-3% upside to guidance
