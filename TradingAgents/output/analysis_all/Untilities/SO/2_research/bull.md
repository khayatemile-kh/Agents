## ARGUMENT #2: THE DEBT STORY IS MISUNDERSTOOD — HIGH LEVERAGE IS ACTUALLY A FEATURE, NOT A BUG

### Let's Address the Elephant: 182% Debt-to-Equity Ratio

Yes, SO's debt-to-equity is 182%. In traditional corporate finance, that would be alarming.

**But we're not analyzing a traditional corporation — we're analyzing a regulated utility.**

Here's why the high leverage is actually *structural and manageable*:

#### **1. Regulated Rate-of-Return Framework**

SO operates under a regulated monopoly model where:
- Regulators allow utilities to recover **cost of capital** (including debt service)
- Rates are set to ensure a **fair return on invested capital**
- Revenue is highly predictable (not exposed to market competition)

This is fundamentally different from a cyclical business. Compare:
- **Cyclical Company**: High debt = risk of distress during downturns
- **Regulated Utility**: High debt = efficient capital structure because cash flows are guaranteed

**Result:** SO's debt isn't risky debt — it's **productive debt financing stable, regulated assets.**

#### **2. Cost of Debt Remains Manageable**

Let me show you the numbers:
- SO's current weighted average cost of debt: ~4.2% (historical context, recent issuances likely 4.5-5.0%)
- Regulated return on equity: typically 9.0-10.0%
- Therefore: Regulatory framework supports this capital structure

**Key Point:** Regulators *expect* utilities to operate at high leverage. If SO operated at 50% debt-to-equity, it would be:
- **Inefficient** (leaving money on the table)
- **Unprofitable for shareholders** (unnecessarily expensive equity)
- **Not aligned with sector norms**

Current leverage is **optimal within the regulatory framework**.

#### **3. Debt is Declining Relative to the Asset Base**

Look at the trend:
- Q2 2026 Debt-to-Equity: 182% (improved from 206% at YE 2025)
- Construction in Progress: $11.87 billion (up 12.8% YoY)
- Total Assets: $162.03 billion (up 3.98% YoY)

**What's happening?** SO is issuing equity to fund growth capex (OpenAI project, grid modernization, renewables). This is **exactly the right capital management move**, and it's already deleveraging the balance sheet on an absolute basis.

Over the next 3-5 years, as the OpenAI project generates cash flow and SO issues additional equity to fund capex, **the debt-to-equity ratio will normalize to 150-160% — well within sector norms**.

#### **4. Liquidity is Not a Concern**

The bear analyst will likely cite the 0.787 current ratio as evidence of liquidity stress.

**Let me explain why that's misleading:**

Utilities have **negative working capital by design**:
- Customers pay in advance or on net-30 terms
- The utility has 60-90 days to pay suppliers
- This creates a **float of advance payments** = negative working capital

Additionally:
- SO has $2.98 billion in cash (up from $1.64 billion YE 2025)
- Access to commercial paper markets ($billions available)
- Access to debt capital markets (investment grade, actively used)
- **Operating cash flow of $3.05 billion in Q2 2026** demonstrates ability to fund operations

**The current ratio is not a liquidity problem — it's an accounting artifact of how utilities operate.**
