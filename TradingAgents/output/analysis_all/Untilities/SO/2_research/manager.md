That creates a real near-term risk of dilutive equity issuance, credit-rating pressure, or dividend-growth deceleration precisely during the period when the bull needs the market to look past deteriorating current fundamentals toward a distant payoff.

On the technical/sentiment side, the bull's argument that the 8% pullback is 'just macro rotation' is weakened by the bear's specific observation that the stock kept falling even after the OpenAI approval was announced — that is evidence the market is discounting execution/financing risk, not just reacting to generic rate fears. Bearish MACD crossover and oversold RSI cut both ways (oversold can precede a bounce, but sustained oversold with negative catalysts is more often confirmation of a deteriorating trend than a buy signal in isolation).

Netting it out: there is a genuine, differentiated long-term growth catalyst here that argues against an outright Sell — this isn't a broken business, and the regulated-utility framework does provide real cash-flow protection over time. But the immediate financial picture (negative FCF, elevated leverage, weak liquidity ratios) combined with multi-year execution risk on the AI capacity build-out and a stock that hasn't found technical support means this is not the moment to add aggressively or call it undervalued. The prudent stance is to trim/de-risk exposure now, preserve capital for a better entry point once the capex/financing picture (equity issuance, rate-case outcomes, actual construction milestones) becomes clearer, while not abandoning the name entirely given the legitimate multi-year AI power demand tailwind.
