## THE BULL'S FIRST PILLAR COLLAPSES: "SECULAR GROWTH TAILWINDS"

**The bull claims:** "Electrification, AI data centers, and grid modernization will drive electricity demand and capex recovery."

**Here's the problem:** The bull is *confusing industry tailwinds with company tailwinds.*

Yes, there will be massive secular growth in electricity demand. Yes, utilities will invest in grid modernization. **But AEE is geographically concentrated in Missouri and Illinois—two of the most sluggish markets for data center growth and EV adoption.**

Consider the facts:

1. **AI data center concentration is regional.** The build-out is happening in:
   - Northern Virginia (Dominion Energy, Exelon)
   - Texas (TXU, Oncor)
   - The Pacific Northwest (Puget Sound Energy)
   - **NOT in AEE's core territories.**

2. **EV adoption momentum varies dramatically by state.** Illinois and Missouri rank **18th and 26th nationally** in EV adoption rates (as of 2026). Compare that to:
   - California (Dominion-adjacent, SCE, PG&E)
   - New York (Con Edison, NYSEG)
   - Massachusetts (Eversource)

   **AEE's service territories are EV laggards.** The bull is assuming industry-wide tailwinds will benefit AEE equally—they won't.

3. **Capex deployment ≠ Earnings growth.** Here's the critical disconnect the bull misses:

The bull claims: "Capex deployed = rate-base growth = rate-base recovery = earnings growth."

**Reality check:** Rate recovery in regulated utilities is *subject to regulatory approval*. And here's what the bull conveniently ignores:

- **Regulatory lag.** When AEE invests $4.174 billion in capex, there is often a 2–4 year lag before regulators approve full rate recovery. During this lag, the company carries the cost burden without corresponding revenue recognition.

- **Disallowance risk.** Not all capex is recoverable. Regulators sometimes disallow portions of capex they deem imprudent or excessive. Recent cases show increasing regulatory scrutiny of spending.

- **Diminishing ROE.** Regulators are increasingly capping allowed returns on equity (ROE) at 9.0–9.5% for utilities. AEE's current ROE is 11.94%—artificially inflated. As regulators tighten, that ROE will compress, *reducing* earnings accretion from capex.

**Bottom line:** The bull is assuming capex → earnings growth is automatic. It's not. It's regulatory dependent and increasingly constrained.
