**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make fair points, but the bear's argument is more grounded in verifiable, structural balance-sheet data while the bull leans heavily on speculative macro probabilities (Fed cuts, recession odds) and optimistic extrapolations of capex-to-EPS conversion. Key facts: CNP's debt-to-equity (2.10x) and debt/cap (71.3%) are materially higher than peers (DUK, ED, NEE, EXC), and net debt sits at $24.6B. Free cash flow has been deeply negative for three straight years (~-$2.1B average annually), meaning the company is structurally dependent on continuous debt/equity issuance to fund its capex program - the $700M debt raise is better read as a necessity than a vote of confidence. The bull's rate-base growth math ($0.27-0.92 EPS accretion) is a rough back-of-envelope estimate that ignores the drag from rising interest expense (estimated ~$1B+/year) and the multi-year lag between capex spend and regulatory rate recovery, during which leverage keeps climbing. The bull's macro tailwind argument (89% no-cut probability, 8% recession odds) is a real but time-limited support (through 2026) and doesn't offset the structural leverage and cash-burn concerns that play out over a multi-year horizon relevant to a capital-intensive utility. On valuation, CNP's forward PE is admittedly a touch below some peers, but that gap is arguably justified by its higher leverage and cash-flow risk, not a mispricing. Technicals (RSI 39, MACD turning) are short-term noise and don't outweigh the balance-sheet fragility. Given the deteriorating leverage trend, sustained negative FCF, and the multi-year execution/regulatory risk embedded in the expanded capex plan, I side more with the bear thesis, but the recession-resistant regulated revenue base and stable near-term dividend do provide a floor - so this isn't a full sell, but a clear caution signal warranting reduced conviction and trimming rather than adding.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing CNP exposure by 20-30% into any near-term bounce (e.g., if RSI recovers toward 50-55 or price approaches the recent pre-earnings range), rather than adding on weakness as the bull suggests. 2) Do not initiate new or incremental long positions until there is concrete evidence that (a) FCF burn is narrowing (operating cash flow catching up to capex) and (b) leverage ratios (debt/equity, debt/cap) show stabilization or improvement rather than continued expansion. 3) Monitor upcoming rate case outcomes and regulatory decisions on the expanded capex plan closely - approval with full cost recovery would be the key catalyst to revisit the bearish stance; rejection or trimming of the plan would confirm further downside risk. 4) Keep the dividend as a partial offset for holding remaining shares (yield ~2.4%), but do not treat it as a valuation floor given the leverage overhang - set a mental stop if debt/equity climbs materially above 2.10x or if interest expense growth outpaces earnings growth in the next 1-2 quarters. 5) Reallocate freed-up capital toward lower-leverage regulated utility peers (e.g., ED, NEE) that offer similar defensive characteristics with materially healthier balance sheets, until CNP demonstrates deleveraging progress.
