## **PART 1: THE LEVERAGE TRAP — WHY CNP'S BALANCE SHEET IS DETERIORATING, NOT STABLE**

The bull claims: *"CNP maintains a leverage profile typical of regulated utilities, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 2.10x. This level is sustainable given stable cash flows."*

Why does this matter? Because **leverage amplifies both upside AND downside risk.** The bull is betting that rate base growth will justify this leverage, but:

1. **If capex doesn't translate into rate base growth as expected, CNP has no margin for error.** A 10% disappointment in regulated ROE means leverage becomes *unsustainable*.

3. **Leverage limits financial flexibility.** If CNP faces an operational crisis, unexpected capex overruns, or regulatory setbacks, the company has *less cushion* than better-capitalized peers.

### **3. Interest Expense Is Growing Faster Than Earnings—A Silent Killer**

Let me show you the real problem hiding in CNP's income statement:

Looking at the fundamental data, CNP's operating income is $534 million (Q2 2026), but this is *before* interest expense and taxes. Here's what happens downstream:

- **Operating Income (Q2):** $534M
- **Implied Interest Expense (estimated ~$500M annually, or $125M quarterly):** This is a *major* cash drain
- **After-tax impact:** Interest expense reduces net income dramatically

**The math on the balance sheet tells the story:**
- **Total Debt:** $24.6 billion
- **Implied Average Interest Rate:** ~4-5% (post-recent 0% rate-cut environment)
- **Annual Interest Expense:** ~$985-1,230 million

That's **$1 billion+ per year in interest payments**, funded primarily by operating cash flow. As debt grows to fund capex, interest expense grows proportionally.

**Here's the vicious cycle:**
1. CNP needs capex to grow rate base
2. Capex must be debt-financed (FCF is negative)
3. Debt grows → interest expense grows
4. Interest expense must be covered by regulated revenues
5. To cover higher interest, rates must increase
6. Higher rates face political/regulatory resistance
7. If rates don't increase enough, the spread between debt costs and regulated ROE narrows
8. **Result: margin compression and stock underperformance**

The bull assumes regulators will happily approve rate increases to cover CNP's interest bill. **But regulators answer to voters, not shareholders.** Rate increases face political headwinds, and if CNP's cost structure becomes too high, regulators will push back.
