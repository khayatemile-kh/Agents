**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: AES trades at a steep discount (5.5-6x forward P/E) to utility peers, carries elevated leverage (Net Debt/EBITDA ~7.9x, D/E ~257%), generates negative TTM FCF (-$3.09B) against positive but modest OCF ($2.58B), and just cleared a major catalyst (CFIUS approval for the Horizon merger). The disagreement is about how to interpret these facts.

The bull's strongest points: interest coverage is still solid (~5.5x), the dividend payout ratio (51%) is conservative versus peers, OCF nominally covers the dividend several times over, and the CFIUS clearance removes a real overhang that the market hasn't fully priced. The Q1→Q2 2026 EBITDA growth and the Q4 2025 charge being isolated are reasonable data points suggesting some operational stabilization.

The bear's strongest points are more structurally damaging, though. A Net Debt/EBITDA of ~7.88x is roughly double-to-triple the peer group (3-4x), not just "elevated" — that's a categorical difference, not a matter of degree, and it directly explains most of the multiple discount without needing to invoke market irrationality. The bear also correctly flags that TTM FCF is deeply negative (-$3.09B) while capex is treated by the bull as pure "rate-base growth" — but rate-base capex needs to be debt-financed at a moment when refinancing costs are elevated, which is precisely the risk the market is pricing. The sequential Q1→Q2 net income decline (-12.5%) despite EBITDA growth is a legitimate red flag the bull didn't rebut — it implies rising interest/depreciation expense is eating into the operating improvement, which is exactly the deleveraging-headwind story the bear is telling. Declining forward EPS guidance ($2.68 TTM to $2.38 forward) is also unaddressed by the bull and is a concrete, forward-looking negative that outweighs backward-looking EBITDA trend argument.

On balance, the valuation gap is not simply a mispricing to be arbitraged — it's commensurate with a genuinely constrained balance sheet, and the bear's structural leverage argument, the negative free cash flow, and the declining forward EPS guidance collectively make a more evidence-grounded case than the bull's reliance on "utilities are different" and cherry-picked sequential quarters. That said, this isn't a pure sell/distress case either: coverage ratios are still fine, the dividend hasn't been cut, and CFIUS clearance is a genuine positive catalyst that reduces deal-execution risk. This nuance argues against an aggressive Sell and toward a cautious, trim-oriented stance.

Given the balance-sheet fragility, negative FCF, and declining forward earnings guidance outweighing the optical cheapness and the single positive catalyst, I'm leaning bearish but not to full capitulation — Underweight captures the recommendation to reduce exposure while acknowledging the dividend is intact for now and a re-rating catalyst (deal close, deleveraging progress) could change the picture.

**Strategic Actions**: 1. Trim existing AES position by roughly 25-35%, using the CFIUS-approval-driven strength (or any near-term bounce toward $15-16) as the exit window rather than selling into weakness.
2. Do not add to the position on "cheap valuation" alone — the 5.5-6x forward P/E is largely explained by the ~7.9x Net Debt/EBITDA and structurally negative FCF, not simply market irrationality; avoid the value-trap temptation the bull case leans on.
3. Set a hard trigger for further de-risking: another quarter of sequential net income decline despite EBITDA growth, any downward revision to forward EPS guidance below the current $2.38, or signs the $3.46B near-term debt maturities are being refinanced at materially higher spreads.
4. Keep a small residual position (rather than a full exit) to retain optionality on two remaining positives: (a) the Horizon merger closing cleanly post-CFIUS with synergy delivery, and (b) OCF continuing to outpace the dividend obligation (~5x coverage currently), which for now protects the ~4.7% yield.
5. Reassess toward Hold/Overweight only if the company shows two consecutive quarters of FCF improvement toward breakeven and Net Debt/EBITDA trending down toward 6x or below, confirming the bull's deleveraging-path thesis rather than the bear's structural-constraint thesis.
6. Size any residual holding so that AES represents a below-benchmark weight in the utilities sleeve, reflecting the asymmetry between capped near-term upside (deep discount already partly justified) and real downside tail risk (dividend cut/refinancing stress) if leverage doesn't improve.
