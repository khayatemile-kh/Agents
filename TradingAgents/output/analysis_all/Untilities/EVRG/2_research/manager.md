**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull's thesis rests on the standard "regulated utility debt is a feature not a bug" framework, arguing that negative FCF is just deferred revenue, the dividend is protected by regulatory precedent, and rate cuts will provide multiple expansion and refinancing tailwinds. The bear directly and effectively attacks each pillar: (1) the D/E trend is not stable at "normal utility levels" but has accelerated from ~138% to ~160% in under 18 months, which is a credit deterioration trend, not a steady-state capital structure; (2) rate case approval is not guaranteed—KCC/OCC are showing more skepticism, disallowance rates run 8-12% historically, and settlements often land at 60-75% of requested increases with 12-24 month lag, meaning the "100% recovery over 30 years" argument bulls lean on is optimistic rather than mechanical; (3) the negative FCF gap ($1.2-1.6B/yr) is being plugged by continuous new debt issuance, compounding leverage in a way that could trigger a ratings downgrade within 18-24 months, which would raise borrowing costs 75-100bps and potentially trigger covenants; (4) the dividend safety argument citing "no major utility has ever cut" is contradicted by real precedent (NiSource, and other cases the bear was building toward) — dividend cuts do happen when leverage/FCF deteriorate sufficiently; (5) the bull's macro rate-cut catalyst is speculative — it depends on a repricing of Fed policy that is currently priced at 89% probability of no cuts, i.e., the bull is fighting the market's own probability distribution, an admitted low-conviction, low-visibility catalyst.

Weighing this: the bull's structural argument about regulated utility mechanics is correct in a static sense, but the bear's key point — that the leverage trajectory is deteriorating quarter over quarter, not just elevated — is the more decision-relevant fact. A utility with high but *stable* leverage supported by predictable rate recovery is a reasonable Hold/Buy. A utility with high AND *rising* leverage, compounding negative FCF that's persistently outpacing OCF, real disallowance risk, and rating agency scrutiny is a different, more precarious situation. The bull didn't actually rebut the acceleration in D/E or the disallowance history — those are hard, specific data points versus the bull's more hand-wavy assertions about regulatory protection and future rate cuts that haven't happened yet. The dividend-cut counterexamples further undermine the bull's "regulators never allow cuts" framing.

Given that the bear supplied concrete, verifiable deterioration trends (D/E acceleration, disallowance rates, negative FCF trajectory) while the bull's rebuttal leaned heavily on macro speculation (rate cuts that are currently unlikely per market pricing) and regulatory assumptions that the bear specifically showed are not guaranteed, I'm siding with the bear's caution. This doesn't rise to a full Sell — EVRG still benefits from a regulated revenue base, has 3x dividend coverage from operating cash flow, and Q3 seasonality/rate case catalysts could still deliver upside — but the balance of near-term risk (leverage trajectory, downgrade risk, regulatory lag/disallowance) outweighs the bull's largely speculative macro catalysts. That argues for a cautious, Underweight stance: trim exposure and wait for evidence that the D/E trend stabilizes or reverses and that rate cases are being approved near requested levels before adding back.
