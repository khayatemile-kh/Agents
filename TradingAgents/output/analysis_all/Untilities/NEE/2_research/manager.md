**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The debate surfaces a real tension: NEE has a genuine secular growth catalyst (AI/data-center power demand, 35.1-GW backlog, FPL's regulated rate base compounding at ~11% ROE) but the bear makes a sharper, more evidence-backed case on the numbers that actually matter for near-term risk. Specifically: (1) EPS has been flat-to-down ($3.37 -> $3.30 -> ~$3.35 LTM) despite record capex, which directly undercuts the bull's claim of imminent margin-driven earnings acceleration from the backlog; (2) the bull's 30-35% contract margin figure is unsubstantiated and contradicted by peer data (Vistra 9-12%) and NEE's own reported renewable margins (14-16%), making the '$0.50-0.75 EPS accretion' projection speculative; (3) the balance sheet trend is deteriorating, not merely 'at industry norm' — D/E has climbed from 154% (2023) to 175% (2025) to 193% (Q2 2026), with debt growing 50% vs equity growing 20% over the same window; (4) interest coverage nearly halved (3.18x to 1.76x) as average cost of debt roughly doubled (2.3% to ~4.8%), meaning the leverage the bull calls 'safe because regulated' is being actively squeezed by a rising-rate refinancing cycle right as capex is peaking; (5) the Dominion merger carries real regulatory overhang (five-state opposition, 60-day delay) that the bull only partially addresses with a favorable historical analogy, not hard evidence of approval odds for this specific deal. The bull's rebuttal that 'benefits take 2-3 years to show up' is a valid point but it also concedes the near-term earnings-to-leverage mismatch the bear identifies — you're paying for growth that's 2-3 years out while debt costs and interest expense are rising today. Given that the financial evidence in this record shows deteriorating leverage, compressing interest coverage, and stagnant EPS coexisting with rising capex, the weight of hard evidence favors caution over the bull's largely narrative-driven margin assumptions. That said, this isn't a full sell — NEE retains a real regulated utility core (FPL) with predictable, protected cash flows, a still-reasonable payout ratio (~37%), and a genuine (if overstated) growth optionality from data-center demand. The prudent stance is to trim/de-risk exposure rather than add aggressively at what appears to be a peak-leverage, flat-EPS inflection point, while not abandoning the position entirely given the underlying regulated-utility cash flow support and dividend visibility.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing NEE exposure modestly (e.g., reduce position size by 15-25%) rather than adding into the current setup — do not chase the AI/data-center growth narrative until margin claims are substantiated with disclosed contract economics.
2) Do not initiate new/incremental buys at current levels; wait for concrete evidence that EPS is inflecting upward from the backlog deployment (i.e., at least one to two quarters of EPS growth outpacing capex growth) before adding back.
3) Closely monitor three hard metrics as gating criteria for re-upgrading: (a) interest coverage stabilizing/improving above ~2.5x, (b) D/E ratio flattening or declining from the current ~193% trajectory, and (c) actual disclosed margins on data-center/renewable contracts confirming figures materially above the 14-16% NEE has reported historically.
4) Treat the Dominion merger as a binary catalyst with asymmetric downside — keep position sized so that regulatory rejection (five-state opposition already on record) would not be a disproportionate hit to the book; consider hedging or using options collars around key regulatory decision dates if the position is retained.
5) Retain a residual core position sized around dividend/income allocation (given ~37% payout ratio and 2.7x coverage still function adequately for now), but treat NEE as a hold-for-income, not a growth-conviction name, until leverage and margin uncertainty resolve.
6) Reassess thesis in 2-3 quarters when capex cycle is expected to moderate (per company commentary) and when clearer evidence of margin realization or deterioration will be visible in reported financials.
