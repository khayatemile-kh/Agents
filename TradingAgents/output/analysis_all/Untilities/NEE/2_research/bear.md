## I. THE FUNDAMENTAL FLAW: THE BACKLOG THESIS ASSUMES EXECUTION PERFECTION IN A DETERIORATING ENVIRONMENT

### The 35.1-GW "Game-Changer" Is Not What the Bull Claims

The bull wants you to believe that NEE's backlog is a **$500B+ earnings goldmine** ready to be deployed. Here's what the data actually shows:

**First, let's contextualize what 35.1 GW actually means:**

- Yes, it's "10 months of US solar installation capacity." But here's the critical omission: **NEE's backlog is spread across multiple years, and deployment timelines have historically slipped.**
- The backlog includes projects in various stages of development—some are pre-permit, some are in permitting hell, and some won't materialize at all.
- **Historical reality check:** Large utilities have chronically underdelivered on projected capex deployment. When PG&E promised aggressive renewable buildout post-2011 wildfire settlements, did they deliver on time? When Duke Energy pledged aggressive clean energy, did margins hold? **No.**

### The "30-35% Margins" Claim Is Fantasy

This is where the bull's argument becomes mathematically indefensible. Let me expose this:

**The actual economics of renewable energy contracts:**

| Factor | Bull's Claim | Reality | Gap |

| **Contract Margin** | 30-35% net | 12-18% typical utility renewable margins | -50% to -65% |

**This is not stable—it's accelerating. In just three years (2023-2026), NEE's debt has grown from $73.2B to $110.2B (+50%), while equity grew only from $47.5B to $57.1B (+20%).** 

The bull claims "fixed-rate, regulated cash flows support leverage." **But here's the kicker: those fixed rates don't protect against refinancing risk when rates rise.**

### Rising Rates Are Crushing the Interest Coverage Math

Look at what happened to NEE's interest expense:

| Year | Interest Expense | Operating Income | Interest Coverage |
|------|-----------------|------------------|-------------------|
| 2024 | $2.24B | $7.13B | 3.18x |
| 2025 | $4.57B | $8.02B | 1.76x |
| **Q2 2026 (annualized)** | **~$4.9B** | **~$9.1B** | **1.86x** |

**Interest expense DOUBLED in one year.** And the bull is claiming this is sustainable? Interest coverage fell from 3.18x to 1.76x. While still above 1.0x, this is **deteriorating rapidly**.

**Why? Because NEE's debt is rolling over at higher rates:**
- Average cost of debt in 2024: ~2.3%
- Average cost of debt in 2025: ~4.8%
- Implied average cost in 2026: ~4.5-5.0%

When you have $110B in debt rolling at 4.5-5.0% rates (up from 2-3% a few years ago), **interest expense becomes a drag on earnings, not a minor footnote.**
