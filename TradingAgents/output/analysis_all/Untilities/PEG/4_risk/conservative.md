## THE MACRO ENVIRONMENT IS NOT THE TAILWIND THE AGGRESSIVE ANALYST CLAIMS

The aggressive analyst presents the macro backdrop as ideal for PEG: "89% probability of no Fed cuts, 8% recession risk, 7% inflation above 5%." They frame this as supportive. I frame it as exactly the wrong environment for a levered utility struggling with FCF.

Here's why: **in a stable-to-rising rate environment, the cost of capital for refinancing increases, but regulatory allowances for rate recovery lag.** This is not theoretical. This is historical utility dynamics. When the Fed keeps rates steady at elevated levels, utilities with large debt loads face pressure on two fronts:

First, **refinancing costs rise in real terms.** PEG issued $500M in long-term debt in Q2 2026. What yield did they have to offer? What will they have to offer in Q4 when another tranche matures? If refinancing costs tick up even 25-50 basis points—which is entirely plausible in a stable rate environment—that flows directly through to net income. For a company already showing weak FCF, incremental interest expense is *not* a tailwind.

Second, and more critically, **regulators in stable-rate environments have less urgency to grant aggressive rate base expansion.** The aggressive analyst keeps asserting that "regulated utilities get their returns regulated up to target ROE." That's technically true. But the timeline matters enormously. When you're waiting 18-24 months for a rate case decision, and you need to refinance debt *today*, you're exposed to a timing mismatch that no regulatory framework eliminates.

The macro environment the aggressive analyst describes is actually the *most dangerous* for a company like PEG: rates high enough that refinancing is expensive, but not so high that regulators are in a rush to grant relief. It's the Goldilocks environment—but for pain, not opportunity.
