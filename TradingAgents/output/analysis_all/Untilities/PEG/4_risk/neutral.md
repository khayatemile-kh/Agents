Regulators approve rate bases and ROE allowances on *schedules*, not in real-time. There's a lag. And during that lag, if leverage is already at 142%, the margin for error becomes gossamer-thin.

You're also doing something clever with the free cash flow numbers that I want to call out directly. That's not terrible. But it's not "this is a fortress balance sheet and everyone's panicking" either. It's more like "this company is in a perpetual state of needing credit market access to function." Which brings me to the real vulnerability you're missing.
