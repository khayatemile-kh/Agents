**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: EIX stock has collapsed ~27% on the failure of California's wildfire liability shield legislation, leaving the stock at deeply discounted multiples (6x trailing, 8.5x forward P/E) with a near-6% dividend yield, but with real structural uncertainty around wildfire liability exposure. The bull's strongest points are that EIX remains critical regulated infrastructure serving 15 million people, that regulators have historically allowed cost recovery through rate base mechanisms, that the technical picture (RSI ~33, price near lower Bollinger Band, capitulation volume) suggests an oversold bounce is plausible, and that the valuation discount versus utility peers is unusually wide. However, the bear's rebuttal is more rigorous and forward-looking: the PG&E precedent the bull cites as reassuring actually shows shareholders (not the utility itself) bear the brunt of wildfire liability -- equityholders were wiped out even though the utility survived as a going concern. That is the relevant risk for an EIX shareholder, not whether SCE keeps operating. Second, the failed liability bill was killed by consumer/environmental advocates for political reasons that are structural, not just a negotiating gap -- meaning any future compromise likely shifts more cost to utilities/shareholders, not less. Third, the bull's rate-recovery and insurance-market arguments assume close to 100% pass-through of costs to ratepayers, but the bear correctly notes this pass-through is capped by political and regulatory tolerance, and insurance markets are tightening capacity and raising premiums post-PG&E rather than expanding coverage cheaply. Fourth, and most concretely, the bear shows the earnings trajectory is deteriorating, not stabilizing -- forward EPS of $6.51 is already down ~28% from TTM, and if that trend continues into 2027-28 as litigation matures and capex rises, the payout ratio on the dividend moves well above 50%, undermining the "safe income" bull thesis. The interest coverage ratio the bull cites (~1.0x EBIT/interest) is itself alarming for what's typically a stable-cash-flow business -- that's a solvency-adjacent metric, not a footnote. Given the combination of: (1) unresolved, potentially expanding tail liability that specifically threatens equity value (not enterprise survival), (2) a hostile and shifting regulatory/political backdrop in California that make full cost socialization to ratepayers unlikely, (3) a deteriorating forward earnings trend that pressures dividend sustainability, and (4) leverage metrics that are tight even before an adverse liability outcome, I find the bear case more credible on the fundamental risk while acknowledging the bull's technical/valuation arguments have some near-term merit for a bounce. The wide valuation discount is not obviously a "mispricing" -- it looks like the market correctly discounting tail risk and earnings degradation that the bull hasn't adequately quantified. This isn't a clean Sell because the technical oversold condition and extreme dividend yield could produce a near-term relief rally, and the business isn't going away -- but the risk/reward skews cautious until there is more clarity on the liability framework and earnings stabilize. That argues for trimming/underweighting rather than aggressively buying the dip or holding full-size exposure.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing EIX exposure by roughly 25-40% into any near-term technical bounce (RSI oversold, potential mean-reversion toward the 20-day VWMA), rather than adding to the position on the dip as the bull suggests. 2) Do not treat the depressed P/E (6-8.5x) or the ~6% dividend yield as a screening buy signal -- validate against the deteriorating forward EPS trend before increasing size; set a re-entry trigger only after (a) a revised CA wildfire liability framework is enacted with clearer cost-recovery mechanics, and (b) forward EPS estimates stabilize or turn upward for two consecutive quarters. 3) Avoid adding at current sizing given interest coverage near ~1.0x EBIT/interest -- this leaves little cushion if litigation costs or capex accelerate; treat the balance sheet as a constraint on conviction, not a footnote. 4) Hedge remaining exposure with sector-relative pairs (e.g., long higher-quality regulated utility peers without wildfire liability exposure) or protective options if maintaining a core position for the dividend, to isolate EIX-specific tail risk from broader utility/rate-sensitivity exposure. 5) Set a hard risk checkpoint: if payout ratio on forward EPS exceeds ~55-60% or the dividend is cut/suspended, exit remaining position outright. 6) Revisit thesis in 2-3 quarters once there is more clarity on (i) the shape of any revised CA liability legislation, (ii) CPUC rulings on cost pass-through percentages, and (iii) trend in 2027-28 EPS estimates -- upgrade back to Hold/Overweight only if those resolve favorably.
