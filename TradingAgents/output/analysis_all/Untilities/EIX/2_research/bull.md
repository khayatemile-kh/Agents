### **PILLAR 1: THE REGULATORY ENVIRONMENT IS NOT BROKEN—IT'S RECALIBRATING**

**The Bear's Narrative:** *"California rejected the liability shield. Utilities face catastrophic lawsuit exposure. EIX is doomed."*

**The Reality:** This is catastrophizing based on a single legislative setback. Here's what's actually happening:

**A. California Utilities Are Too Important to Fail**

- EIX operates Southern California Edison, serving **15 million people** across 50,000 square miles of grid infrastructure
- This is **critical national infrastructure**—literally the electricity supply for Los Angeles, Orange County, and surrounding regions
- **Regulatory precedent matters:** California regulators *cannot* allow a major utility to become insolvent. It would trigger grid collapse, economic chaos, and a humanitarian crisis
- The failed liability bill is a *negotiating position*, not a policy endpoint

**B. Alternative Liability Protection Mechanisms Are Likely**

The market is pricing as if utilities face *unlimited* wildfire liability. This is naive. Here's what will actually happen:

1. **Rate Base Recovery**: California regulators can and will authorize utilities to recover reasonable wildfire-related costs through rate increases. This is standard regulatory practice—look at how utilities recovered storm restoration costs post-Hurricane Sandy.

2. **Insurance Market Solutions**: The market is developing wildfire insurance products specifically for utilities. AIG, Lloyd's of London, and specialized carriers are actively underwriting this risk. EIX's insurance coverage will evolve and improve.

3. **Litigation Reality Check**: Not all fires result in utility liability. Causation matters. Climate factors, vegetation management, and homeowner actions all play roles. Courts will recognize these nuances, limiting the "catastrophic" scenario some are pricing in.

4. **Legislative Compromise**: California's legislature *will* eventually address this. The original Newsom proposal fell apart over *negotiating details*, not core principle. A revised liability framework is virtually certain within 12-24 months.

**Evidence:** After PG&E's 2020 bankruptcy, did California utilities collapse? No—PG&E emerged stronger, rates were adjusted, and the company has since recovered. The market is ignoring this precedent.
