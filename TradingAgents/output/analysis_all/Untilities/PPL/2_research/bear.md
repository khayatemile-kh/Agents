- **NextEra Energy: 35.1 GW of COMMITTED data center backlog** (contractually locked in)

Notice the language difference? NextEra has **committed demand**. PPL has **potential demand in development**.

**Here's what this means:** The bull is projecting 6-8 GW of data center load capture without citing a single executed contract. The bull is relying on **forward guidance and aspirational planning**, not actual customer commitments.

**Real-world context:**
- Data center power projects have **12-24 month lead times** from commitment to revenue generation
- Regulatory approval timelines for grid upgrades often exceed 18 months
- Hyperscaler demand, while real, is **still being rationed across multiple utilities** competing for the same customers

The bull's own data undermines his position: PPL's 3-year TSR is **+53%**, which means **the market has already priced in these data center expectations**. If the bull is correct that PPL is about to capture 6-8 GW of new incremental load, **the stock should be trading up, not down -3.4% in the past 30 days**.

Let me attack the bull's arithmetic directly:

**The bull claims:**

**Here's what the bull isn't telling you:**

First, **those CapEx and OCF figures are highly speculative**. Actual data center load varies by geography, technology type, and grid conditions. The range of "$2-3B per GW" is so wide as to be meaningless.

Second, **even if we accept those numbers, the timing is wrong:**
- PPL is currently running a **-$1.94B TTM free cash flow**
- The bull projects this will flip positive in "2027-2028"
- But PPL's debt has risen from $19.4B (12/31/2025) to $20.2B (03/31/2026) in just 3 months

**The bull's own argument proves the unsustainability:** PPL is financing negative FCF with debt issuance **right now**, not 2027-2028. This is not a "transition period"—**this is a structural imbalance that's getting worse, not better**.

### **The Real Problem: PPL Is Trapped in a Debt Spiral**

Here's the mechanism the bull ignores:

1. **Today:** PPL has negative FCF of -$1.94B and must issue debt to fund operations and dividends
2. **Debt service costs rise:** With $20.2B in debt, even a modest 50 bps increase in average cost adds $100M+ in annual interest expense
3. **This interest expense crushes operating cash flow:** Higher debt service means less cash available to fund CapEx or service the dividend
4. **PPL must issue even more debt to fill the gap:** A vicious cycle ensues
5. **By 2027-2028, when the bull expects OCF to "normalize," PPL will have $22-23B in debt**, with annual interest costs approaching $1.2B

**The Federal Reserve prediction markets show an 89% probability of NO rate cuts through 2026.** This means PPL's refinancing window remains constrained, and the cost of capital stays elevated. The bull's entire thesis depends on a **benign refinancing environment that simply isn't materializing**.

- Recent precedent: **Utility commissions in multiple states have rejected or reduced utility CapEx plans** citing concerns about ratepayer impacts

**Second, the 9-10% allowed ROE is under pressure:**

- PPL's actual ROE of **8.63% (from fundamentals report) is already BELOW the 9-10% the bull assumes**

This is critical: **PPL is not even earning its allowed return today**. The bull's entire projection of rate base expansion driving EPS growth assumes PPL successfully captures 9-10% returns, but the company is currently delivering 8.63%.

The bull assumes **perfect execution**. History suggests otherwise.
