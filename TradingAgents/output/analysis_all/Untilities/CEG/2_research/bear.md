### **Why Won't Margins Expand? The Structural Problem**

The bull's argument rests on this logic: *"Once data center contracts become primary revenue drivers, reported margins will improve."*

**This is backwards.** Here's why:

1. **Contract Revenue ≠ Automatic Margin Expansion**
   - When CEG signs a 20-year PPA with a data center operator, that contract is **priced competitively** at the time of signing.
   - The bull assumes CEG can charge "premium prices" for reliability, but **data center operators are sophisticated buyers** who pit vendors against each other.
   - CEG isn't signing PPAs at 50% margins; it's signing them at **market rates**, which reflect high competition and margin pressure.

2. **The Real Problem: CEG's Cost Structure**
   - CEG operates nuclear plants that are **capital-intensive and aging** in many cases.
   - Nuclear plants carry **high decommissioning liabilities** and regulatory overhead.
   - Natural gas plants in CEG's fleet face **fuel cost volatility** that cannot always be passed through to customers.
   - The margin gap vs. peers suggests **CEG's cost of operations is inherently higher**, not that accounting mix will fix things.

3. **Peer Comparison Shows the Problem Persists**
   - If contract revenue naturally expanded margins, **why haven't Vistra or NextEra—also heavy PPA operators—seen similar margin compression?**
   - Vistra maintains 35% EBITDA margins *while also signing long-term PPAs*. This proves the bull's claim that "contract revenue improves margins" is **not supported by evidence**.
   - The margin gap is **structural**, not temporary.

**The hard truth:** CEG is a lower-margin operator than peers, and that won't change just because the revenue mix shifts. In fact, if the company is cutting PPA prices to win data center deals (which is what competitors are doing), margins may actually *compress* further in the near term.
