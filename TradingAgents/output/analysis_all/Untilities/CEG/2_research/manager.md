**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree the AI-driven data center power demand story is real and that CEG is a credible participant via its nuclear fleet and long-term PPAs. Where they diverge is on quality of earnings and balance sheet health. The bull's case leans heavily on narrative (guidance raise, DCF upside claims, low-volume 'coiled spring' technicals) but its own numbers admit Q2 net margin was only 6.8%, and the jump to $13.34 forward EPS was partly explained by the bull itself as coming from a security-sale gain and an anomalous Q1 gross margin quarter - that's a real red flag, not dismissed by them. The bear's rebuttal is more evidence-based: EBITDA margin gap of 700+ bps vs Vistra/NextEra, operating margin materially lower, and most importantly the quarter-over-quarter debt data showing total debt +9.8%, net debt +10.6%, equity down 4.5%, and Debt/EBITDA rising from 2.7x to 3.0x in a single quarter — that is a fast-moving deterioration trend, not a static leverage level. The bull never directly rebuts the leverage trajectory or the one-time-gain composition of the EPS guidance raise; it only asserts margins will improve 'naturally,' which the bear correctly identifies as unsupported by the peer comparison (Vistra/NextEra run heavy PPA books without seeing this margin compression). The valuation multiples bull cites (21x forward P/E, 3.1x P/B, 15.9x EV/EBITDA) are not obviously cheap for a utility with rising leverage and margin below peers — if anything they look full given the balance sheet trend. Technicals are genuinely mixed/neutral (RSI 57, price consolidating below 200-day SMA, low volume) and don't provide a tie-breaker either way given both sides can spin them.

Because the bear directly quantifies a negative, accelerating trend (leverage +0.3x in one quarter, equity erosion, non-recurring components inflating guidance) that the bull does not adequately answer, and because the bull's margin-expansion thesis is contradicted by the peer evidence (Vistra/NextEra maintain higher margins while also running PPA books), I side with the bear on balance and lean cautious rather than fully bearish — the long-term nuclear/data-center demand tailwind is real and provides some support against an outright Sell, but near-term deterioration in credit metrics and question marks around guidance quality argue against adding exposure and argue for trimming until leverage stabilizes and guidance is validated with recurring, contract-driven earnings rather than one-time gains.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing CEG exposure by roughly 20-30% into any bounce toward the $286-$296 resistance zone (upper Bollinger/200-day SMA), rather than chasing strength on low volume.
2) Do not add to the position on the guidance raise alone — wait for at least one full quarter of clean, recurring earnings (excluding security-sale gains and anomalous gross-margin swings) that validates the $13.34 forward EPS run rate.
3) Set a hard leverage watch: if Debt/EBITDA prints above 3.2-3.5x next quarter or net debt continues rising at the ~10% sequential pace seen in Q2, reduce further toward Sell-sized exposure.
4) Use options or partial hedges (e.g., covered calls against retained shares) to monetize the current consolidation range while limiting downside if the $264 (50-day SMA) support breaks.
5) Re-evaluate toward Hold/Overweight only if (a) EBITDA margin gap versus Vistra/NextEra narrows meaningfully as new PPAs come online at disclosed pricing, and (b) equity stabilizes/leverage trend reverses for two consecutive quarters — until then, treat CEG as a name to underweight relative to cleaner-balance-sheet IPP/utility peers.
