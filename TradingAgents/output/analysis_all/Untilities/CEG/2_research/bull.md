Before we debate CEG's operational details, let's acknowledge the **secular tailwind** driving the entire independent power producer space. The data center infrastructure required to support AI training, inference, and cloud computing is consuming **unprecedented amounts of electricity.** 

**Key facts:**
- AI data centers operate 24/7 with **extremely high power density requirements** (megawatts per facility, not kilowatts).
- Tech giants (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta) are signing **20–30 year power purchase agreements (PPAs)** because:
  1. They need **guaranteed, reliable baseload power** to justify multi-billion-dollar facility investments.
  2. They're willing to pay **premium prices** for power that supports their mission-critical infrastructure.
  3. **Intermittent renewables alone cannot meet this need**—data centers require *always-on* baseload.

This is where **nuclear becomes the strategic asset.** And CEG, as a major nuclear operator with 24 GW of generation capacity (including 4 plants providing ~14 GW of nuclear), is perfectly positioned to capture this demand.

**The evidence:**

**Bottom line:** CEG's "lower margins" are a **temporary accounting artifact**, not a structural weakness. As contract revenue dominates, margins will expand.
