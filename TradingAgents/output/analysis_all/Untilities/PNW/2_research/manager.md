**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: PNW is a regulated Arizona utility riding genuine secular demand tailwinds (semiconductor/data-center load growth, population growth), but Q2 showed revenue +7.1% while net income fell ~8.6% and EPS missed consensus by ~4%, with operating and net margins compressing ~200bps. The bear's margin-compression argument is the more decisive point in this round: it's not a valuation story, it's an earnings-quality story. Rising interest expense and depreciation from an accelerating capex cycle are outrunning revenue growth, and management reaffirmed rather than raised guidance after a beat-friendly seasonal quarter, which is a real (if circumstantial) tell. The bull's balance-sheet defense ('leverage is by design') is a reasonable structural point for utilities generally, but the bear correctly notes the regulatory ROE is capped while debt costs are rising, so the spread that leverage is supposed to capture is compressing in this specific rate regime — this is the crux and it undercuts the bull's 'optimized capital structure' framing. On liquidity, the bull's explanation (utilities rely on capital markets, not working capital) is standard and fair, so I don't weight the -0.59 current ratio heavily either way. On valuation, the bull's peer-multiple comparison (17.5x forward P/E vs 18-20x sector, 3.73% yield vs 3.2-3.5% peer) is real support for downside cushion, and that's why I'm not going full Sell — this isn't a broken business, it's a quality utility with a bad quarter and a genuine near-term margin/leverage headwind. Technicals (RSI 36, negative MACD, price near lower Bollinger band, -9% drawdown) confirm the market is already discounting some of this, but not all — an unraised guidance range after a "confident" tone, plus rising interest/depreciation drag, suggests more downside catalysts before the story clears. Net: the bear's earnings-quality and rate-spread argument is more specific and forward-looking than the bull's demand narrative, which is real but already well known and arguably priced. That tips me to a cautious stance rather than balanced Hold or aggressive Buy — trim exposure and wait for confirmation (a guidance raise, margin stabilization, or further oversold technical washout) before adding.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing PNW exposure by roughly 15-25% into any near-term bounce toward the $100-103 area, rather than averaging down at current oversold levels. 2) Do not add fresh capital until Q3 results confirm margin stabilization (operating margin back toward 22%+ and net income growth reconnecting with revenue growth) — the Q2 print showing +7.1% revenue vs -8.6% net income is the key disqualifying signal for a Buy/Overweight stance right now. 3) Watch the Q3 earnings call closely for an explicit guidance raise (not just reaffirmation) as the confirming bull signal; absent that, treat reaffirmed guidance as evidence management is managing down expectations. 4) Monitor interest expense trend and effective cost of incremental debt each quarter — if the debt/ROE spread continues compressing (debt costs >5% vs regulator-capped ROE ~9%), reduce further. 5) Use the dividend yield (~3.7%) as partial income offset for remaining position, but size the residual holding as a smaller, income-oriented allocation rather than a growth-oriented one until capex-driven depreciation growth decelerates relative to revenue. 6) Set a re-entry/upgrade trigger: sustained improvement in interest coverage above ~3.5x on a normalized (non-seasonally-inflated) EBITDA basis, or a stock pullback into the low $90s that fully prices in the margin risk, whichever comes first.
