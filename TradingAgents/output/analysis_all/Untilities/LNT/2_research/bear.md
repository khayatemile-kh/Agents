- **Total Debt: $12.12B**

- **At current maturity profile: $1.2B+ refinancing needed every year**

**What does this mean?**

If LNT needs to roll $1.2B annually in debt:
- At current 5%+ borrowing rates (up from ~3% in 2021)

- Over 5 years, a 2% average rate increase = **$120M+ in additional annual interest**

**But here's the killer:** LNT's interest coverage is already marginal at 2.36x. If interest expense rises another $100M-150M annually, coverage drops to 1.8-2.0x—dangerously low for a utility with this much debt.

The bulls think regulation will save them. But regulators move slowly. LNT's competitive position tightens while waiting for rate case approvals.

## 4. THE DEBT-TO-EBITDA AT 6.1x IS NOT "MANAGEABLE"—IT'S DANGEROUS

The bull claims: **"Debt-to-EBITDA at 6.1x is typical for utilities; range is 5-7x."**

I say: **That's exactly the problem. LNT is at the HIGH END of that range and accelerating upward.**

**Here's the trajectory:**

| Year | Total Debt | EBITDA | Debt/EBITDA |
|------|-----------|--------|-------------|
| 2022 | $9.97B | $1,495M | 6.66x |
| 2023 | $10.70B | $1,560M | 6.86x |
| 2024 | $10.90B | $1,776M | 6.13x |
| 2025 | $12.12B | $1,993M | 6.08x |

Notice the pattern? **EBITDA improved in 2024-2025, but debt kept accelerating.** The only reason Debt/EBITDA didn't worsen is because EBITDA grew. Strip away the earnings growth and this ratio is deteriorating.

**Why this matters:**

For a regulated utility with predictable cash flows, anything above 6.0x is approaching covenant territory. Credit rating agencies (Moody's, S&P) use this metric heavily:
- **6.0-6.5x:** Tight; watch list potential
- **6.5x+:** Likely downgrade territory
- **LNT at 6.1x, trending upward:** One bad year away from downgrade

**What happens if LNT gets downgraded?**
- Borrowing costs increase 50-100bps (another $50-120M annually)
- Dividend becomes vulnerable (historically, downgrades precede dividend cuts)
- Stock gets hammered further
