## THE REGULATED UTILITY "PASS-THROUGH" ARGUMENT OVERSIMPLIFIES REGULATORY RISK

This is where I need to be most direct, because the Aggressive Analyst's entire counter-leverage argument hinges on a misunderstanding of regulated utility mechanics.

Yes, LNT operates under regulatory oversight in Wisconsin and Iowa. Yes, utilities have pass-through mechanisms for cost recovery. **But there's a critical lag and approval risk that the Aggressive Analyst is hand-waving away.** Here's the actual sequence:

1. LNT incurs higher debt service costs (if Fed raises 25bps in September)
2. LNT files a rate case to pass costs through to ratepayers
3. Regulatory process takes 6-18 months for approval (depending on jurisdiction complexity)
4. **During that entire lag, LNT's interest coverage deteriorates, balance sheet leverage worsens, and refinancing costs spike immediately**
5. Only *after* regulatory approval does cost recovery flow through

The Aggressive Analyst implies this is seamless. It's not. **There's a 12-18 month window where LNT bears the cost shock before customers pay for it.** During that window, if credit markets seize or refinancing windows close, LNT faces real financial stress. This isn't theoretical—we've seen it in regulated utilities during tightening cycles.

Moreover, the Aggressive Analyst assumes regulators will *grant* full cost recovery. Regulators don't always do that. They balance customer impact against utility needs. If rate cases hit during an economic slowdown or political sensitivity around "utility rate hikes," approvals can be partial or delayed. LNT is not immune to this risk.

The trader's Hold thesis doesn't require a rate hike scenario to detonate—it just requires refinancing cost pressures to exceed what regulators will pre-emptively approve. Given interest coverage at 2.36x is *already* marginal, even modest refinancing headwinds tighten it further.
