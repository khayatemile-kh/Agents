## WHERE THE AGGRESSIVE ANALYST WINS—AND OVERSTATES IT

The Aggressive Analyst correctly identifies that RSI at 36.27 combined with price near the lower Bollinger Band represents a textbook oversold setup. That's technically accurate. The historical mean-reversion behavior in utilities is real. The MACD histogram tightening from -0.14 to -0.02 does signal momentum weakness, not momentum reversal. I won't dispute any of that.

But here's where the argument breaks down: the Aggressive Analyst conflates "statistically likely technical bounce" with "safe capital deployment at current position sizing." Those are not the same thing. Yes, utilities historically show mean-reversion behavior when RSI drops into the 30-40 zone. But utilities *with deteriorating coverage ratios* show mean-reversion behavior that **fails to hold** if fundamental stress emerges during the bounce.

The Aggressive Analyst's real error is this: they're treating the regulatory pass-through mechanism as a certainty when it's actually a *lagged probability*. They say, "LNT files rate cases, regulators approve, costs get passed through." That's accurate in a stable macro environment. But we're not in a stable environment—we're in a 50/50 Fed decision moment. If the Fed raises rates in September and the yield curve inverts further, regulators face political pressure to *limit* rate increases, not approve them unchecked. The Aggressive Analyst is pricing in a best-case regulatory scenario and calling it the base case.

More critically—and this is the real flaw—the Aggressive Analyst is ignoring the **timing mismatch between refinancing pressure and regulatory relief**. Interest coverage sits at 2.36x today. If LNT refinances $1.16B of current debt into a higher-rate environment (even just 25bps), interest expense rises immediately. Coverage drops further, maybe to 2.1x or 2.0x. That deterioration happens *before* any rate case approval. The Conservative Analyst nailed this: there's a 12-18 month lag before regulatory relief flows through. During that lag, LNT's credit profile weakens, refinancing spreads widen, and the company faces a real financing squeeze.

**So here's what the Aggressive Analyst got right:** Yes, a technical bounce to $71-73 is statistically probable. **Here's what they got wrong:** That bounce is unlikely to be durable if leverage stress materializes, and leverage stress is increasingly probable, not unlikely.
