## **PART II: THE MACRO TAILWINDS ARE OVERBLOWN (AND POTENTIALLY REVERSING)**

### **1. Natural Gas Demand: The Bull's Narrative vs. The Regulatory Reality**

The bull made a compelling case about natural gas demand from data centers and industrial re-shoring. Let me puncture this narrative with some regulatory facts the bull conveniently omitted:

**The Energy Transition Is Already Pricing Into Utility Regulation:**

**Real-world example:** When California's Public Utilities Commission approved utility rate cases recently, they explicitly *reduced* allowed returns for gas infrastructure projects and *increased* scrutiny of new gas connections. This is the future facing ATO across its service territory.

**The Data Center Argument Is Thin:**
- Yes, data centers need power. But increasingly, they're demanding *renewable* power (solar, wind)
- Microsoft, Google, and Meta are all **mandating** renewable-powered data centers
- The "natural gas will power data centers" narrative is 5-10 years behind current corporate ESG commitments

So the bull's bullish case on gas demand is built on dynamics that are either overstated (data centers) or already shifting against the company (regulatory environment).

### **2. The Interest Rate Tailwind Myth: Why 89% No Cuts Is Actually Bearish for ATO**

Here's where the bull made a critical error in their macro analysis.

The bull said:
> "Regulated utilities pass through financing costs to customers. If ATO's cost of capital rises 1%, the company petitions its state regulator for a rate increase of 1-2%, and customers pay for it."

**This is theoretically sound but practically naive.** Here's why:

**Rate Recovery Has Lags and Regulatory Friction:**

1. **Rate case approval takes 12-24 months** — Even if ATO can recover costs eventually, there's a multi-quarter lag where the company eats the increased financing costs in earnings
2. **Regulators scrutinize rates more carefully in high-rate environments** — When customers are already angry about inflation and energy bills, regulators face political pressure to *deny* or *limit* rate increases, even for "necessary" costs
3. **The 89% probability of NO Fed cuts through 2026 means financing costs will remain elevated** — ATO's refinancing schedule will face persistently high rates, pressuring margins during the lag period

**Real impact:** In Q2 2026, ATO was already showing:
- Rising interest expense as debt grew 24.5% YoY
- The company's debt is increasingly expensive to refinance
- Yet earnings have not grown proportionally

This is the squeeze happening in real-time.
