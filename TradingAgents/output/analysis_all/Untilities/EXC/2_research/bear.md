The company is spending $8.89B on capex and only generating $6.72B in operating cash flow. **They must fund this gap with debt issuance—which brings us to the core problem.**

### **The Rate Base Expansion Argument Is Mythical**

The bull claimed: "When EXC invests $8.9 billion in regulated assets, it expands the rate base, and regulators approve higher revenues."

**This is partially true, but the math doesn't work the way they're implying.**

**Regulatory Return Dynamics:**

**EXC is earning only $2.78B in net income on $25.33B in revenue.** If the rate base were earning 8-10% returns, net income should be $7-9 billion, not $2.78 billion.

- **Interest expense on debt** eats a significant portion

The bull's logic assumes that rate base growth automatically translates to earnings growth. **It doesn't—not in a regulated environment where depreciation offsets capex, and debt service consumes the spread.**
