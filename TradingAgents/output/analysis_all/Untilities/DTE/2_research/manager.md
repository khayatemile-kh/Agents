The bull's rebuttal to leverage (debt-to-total-capital ~68.7% is 'normal') is fair context but doesn't address the trend — it's the trajectory, not the snapshot, that matters here, and the trajectory is worsening. The bull's macro argument that flat/high rates are unambiguously good for DTE is also weakened by the bear's point that higher-for-longer rates increase refinancing costs on maturing debt and compress the effective regulatory spread on new capex — this is a legitimate two-sided effect the bull glossed over. Net: the bear's case is more grounded in DTE's actual recent financial trend data (OCF stagnation, margin compression, growing debt), while the bull leans more on generic sector heuristics and a technical-bounce narrative that wasn't fully substantiated. Dividend yield of 3.42% against decelerating cash generation and rising leverage is a caution flag rather than a clear value signal. Given deteriorating fundamentals actively undermining the bull's self-funding growth story, I'm leaning bearish, but not to a full Sell — DTE is still a regulated utility with real hard assets, an investment-grade rating (for now), and no sign of an outright dividend cut or imminent covenant breach flagged in the debate. The right call is Underweight: trim exposure and de-risk ahead of potential downgrade catalysts, without treating this as a company facing existential risk.
