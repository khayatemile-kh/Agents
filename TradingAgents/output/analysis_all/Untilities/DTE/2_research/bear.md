## REBUTTAL #1: THE MACRO CASE ISN'T AS CONSTRUCTIVE AS THE BULL CLAIMS

### The Bull Oversimplifies Rate Environment Benefits

My colleague argues: "89% probability of NO Fed rate cuts is exactly what utilities want because it stabilizes capex returns."

**Let me challenge this directly:**

**The reality: Higher rates DON'T uniformly benefit utilities—they create a bifurcated outcome:**

1. **Yes, stable rates are better than falling rates** — I'll concede that point. But the bull misses a critical nuance: **DTE's leverage creates a serious problem in higher-rate environments.**

   - When rates stay high, **DTE's cost of debt financing increases for refinancings**
   - DTE carries $26.97B in total debt—a massive base that requires continuous rolling
   - Each time DTE refinances maturing debt in a higher-rate environment, its interest expense increases
   - **This is a margin headwind, not a margin tailwind**

2. **Higher rates also reduce the present value of future capex returns:**
   - The bull claims: "capex projects maintain steady returns in higher-rate environments"
   - **This is backwards.** When discount rates are higher, the NPV of long-lived infrastructure projects *declines*
   - Yes, the PUC allows a fixed return (9-10%), but that fixed return is applied to a *lower* present value of future cash flows
   - The math: If risk-free rates are 5% vs. 3%, and your regulated return is 10%, the **regulatory spread compresses** (from 7% spread to 5% spread)
   - **Lower spreads mean lower real economic returns on capex**

3. **The real macro risk the bull ignores: A credit cycle downturn**

   The bull points to 8% recession probability and calls it "low." But look at what's actually happening:
   
   - **Midwest Grid Emergency Probability: 22%** (declining slightly, but non-trivial)
   - **Soft landing probability is falling:** -3.5pp week-over-week (from 61.5% to 58%)
   - **This suggests the market is *repricing recession risk higher*, not lower**
   
   If recession risk ticks up from 8% to 15-20% over the next 6 months (entirely plausible), **electricity demand will face headwinds:**
   - Commercial/industrial loads (automotive, pharma in Michigan) are cyclical
   - Consumer deferrals reduce discretionary electricity usage
   - Job losses reduce demand
   - **DTE's "stable" revenue base becomes less stable in a downturn**
