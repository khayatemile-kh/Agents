- **Q1 2026 FCF:** -$323M

That's already underwater. But here's where it gets worse—the **OCF has stagnated while capex remains elevated.** Over the past four quarters:
- Average OCF: ~$821M
- Average Capex: ~$1,196M
- **Structural FCF deficit: ~-$375M per quarter**

Compare this to Q1 2025, when OCF was $1,020M and FCF actually turned **positive at +$147M**. The deterioration is not cyclical—it's structural. The company is investing more for less operational return. That's the sign of a utility losing competitive positioning or facing regulatory headwinds that aren't pricing in yet.

Conservatives will argue "this is just how utilities work." Wrong. A utility generating stable OCF while maintaining capex is sustainable. A utility where OCF is *declining* while capex remains sticky is a dividend trap in formation.
