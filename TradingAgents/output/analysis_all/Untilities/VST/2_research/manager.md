**Recommendation**: Overweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree VST sits at the intersection of a real secular growth story (AI/data-center power demand, 20-year contracted nuclear deals) and a genuinely leveraged balance sheet (373% D/E, negative tangible book value, sub-1.0 current ratio). The bull's strongest points are the operational metrics that are hard to dispute in the data given: 30%+ EBITDA growth YTD, 42.96% ROE, EBITDA margin near 35%, insider buying by the CEO after a 29% drawdown, and a PEG ratio of 0.34 that implies the market is pricing far less growth than consensus forward EPS suggests. The bear's strongest points are also grounded in the same dataset: extreme quarter-to-quarter net income volatility ($1.03B to $305M), a cash balance that fell 45% in two quarters, a current ratio below 1.0, negative tangible book value, and the fact that analysts are cutting rather than raising estimates despite the contract backlog — which undercuts the reliability of the 75% forward EPS growth figure the bull leans on most heavily.

Weighing these: the bear's critique of earnings volatility and liquidity/leverage risk is a legitimate near-term concern, but it does not overturn the core structural bull case — VST has locked in multi-decade contracted cash flows with investment-grade counterparties, is executing at industry-leading margins and ROE, and management is putting personal capital behind the stock at depressed prices. The bear's argument that AI demand is 'speculative' is weaker than the bull's counter that VST's revenue is contractually protected rather than spot-price dependent; the bear never produces evidence that the actual signed contracts are being renegotiated or unwound, only hypothetical risk. At the same time, the bear is right that the balance sheet is stretched and near-term earnings visibility is noisy, which argues against treating this as a screaming, all-in Buy.

Net verdict: the secular growth and contracted-revenue thesis outweighs the leverage/volatility concerns, but the balance sheet fragility (negative tangible equity, softening liquidity, refinancing risk in a higher-for-longer rate environment) is real enough that this isn't a full-conviction Buy either — it merits a constructive, Overweight stance: add to the position opportunistically given the valuation dislocation and insider buying, but size it with discipline and monitor leverage/liquidity metrics closely rather than treating the stock as a low-risk value trap.
