   - Power prices are softer than expected

Any of these would destroy that 75% growth thesis.

If EBITDA declines even 10-15% while interest expense rises due to refinancing, coverage deteriorates sharply.

The bull argues that 3.0x Net Debt/EBITDA is acceptable for utilities. But this assumes:
- EBITDA remains stable or grows

- The company doesn't face refinancing stress

**None of these assumptions are safe right now:**

This is where I need to be particularly direct, because the bull has built his entire thesis on the premise that long-term contracts = revenue certainty. But contracts are only valuable if they're economically viable and if the counterparties remain solvent.

1. **Profitability of AI at Scale Is Unproven**: These tech giants are *spending* hundreds of billions on AI infrastructure, but **the ROI at scale remains speculative**. If AI revenue growth slows or profitability disappoints, these companies will cut capex.

2. **Power Economics Are Tightening**: Recent reports indicate that AI data center power costs are rising faster than anticipated. If power becomes prohibitively expensive, tech companies will optimize efficiency rather than build more capacity. This reduces demand for new baseload power.

3. **Alternative Energy Sources Are Emerging**: Tech companies are investing in alternatives—small modular reactors, on-site solar, even traditional renewables.
