## THE BALANCE SHEET RISK IS NOT A "RED HERRING"—IT'S THE CENTRAL ISSUE

The Aggressive Analyst makes a sweeping claim: "A 373% D/E ratio is only dangerous if cash flow deteriorates." This is dangerously naive. Let me explain why.

First, **yes, VST generates strong operating cash flow of $4.12 billion TTM.** But operating cash flow is not the same as free cash flow. The company is spending $1.57 billion on capital expenditure in H1 2026 alone. That leaves roughly $650 million in free cash flow for the first half—or about $1.3 billion annualized assuming consistency (which is optimistic given seasonality).

Now, here's the problem: **VST has $19.90 billion in total debt.** Even with $1.3 billion in annual free cash flow, it would take the company over 15 years to pay down this debt—*if* interest rates don't rise and *if* the company doesn't need cash for anything else. That's not a healthy capital structure for a utility; that's a leveraged bet on perpetually low rates and strong cash generation.

The current ratio of 0.973 is not "normal for a cash-generative utility" as the Aggressive Analyst claims. **A current ratio below 1.0 means the company has more liabilities due within 12 months than it has liquid assets.** Yes, utilities typically have strong cash generation to cover this. But in a rising rate environment—and remember, the prediction markets say there's an 89% chance of zero Fed rate cuts in 2026—refinancing costs will rise. The company will be forced to either cut the dividend, reduce capex, or raise dilutive equity.

And here's the kicker: the negative tangible book value of -$2.09 billion means **the company's equity is primarily intangible assets and goodwill.** If power prices soften and EBITDA disappoints, goodwill impairment is on the table. In a scenario where the company needs to raise equity capital, shareholders would be diluted significantly.

The Aggressive Analyst treats the high leverage as a feature—"look, it shows the market believes in the cash flows!" No. High leverage on a utility is a *bug*, not a feature. It means limited financial flexibility and increased refinancing risk.
