## SECTION 1: THE VALUATION "DISCOUNT" IS JUSTIFIED—HERE'S WHY

### The 13% Undervaluation Claim Ignores Execution Risk

The bull cites Simply Wall St.'s analysis of a 13% undervaluation. But **valuation models are only as good as their assumptions about future cash flows and risk premiums.** Let me expose what's missing from that analysis:

**The Critical Flaw:** The bull's valuation case assumes that DUK will successfully resolve Florida regulatory issues and capture data-center upside at similar economics to Xcel and NextEra. But here's the problem:

**Evidence That Assumptions Are Too Optimistic:**

1. **Florida's Public Counsel Already Rejected DUK's Proposal**
   - Walt Trierweiler (Florida PSC counsel): DUK's proposal "doesn't attempt to comply with the most basic provisions" of SB 484
   - This wasn't a close call or a negotiable difference. It was a **direct rebuke** of DUK's fundamental approach
   - Translation: DUK's first attempt to monetize the data-center opportunity was *rejected for non-compliance*

2. **Compare to Peer Execution:**
   - Xcel already has a **$70B+ capex plan (2026-2030) with explicit data-center demand integration** — they didn't need regulatory approval battles
   - PPL is already guiding to **6-8% annual EPS growth through 2029** — they're capturing upside *now*, not fighting for the right to capture it
   - NextEra (NEE) is already +12.6% YTD on the back of concrete AI/clean-energy execution
   
   **DUK is still in the starting blocks while competitors are already at the finish line.**

3. **The "Q4 2026 Catalyst" Is Speculative**
   - The bull assumes Florida PSC will resolve the rate issue by Q4 2026 or Q1 2027
   - But regulatory processes don't follow market timelines. The Florida PSC could extend proceedings, demand additional compliance filings, or impose unfavorable terms
   - If the outcome is even modestly unfavorable (e.g., tariffs allowing 8% ROE instead of 10%), the upside thesis collapses

**Valuation Reality Check:** If you model DUK assuming the Florida regulatory battle takes 18-24 months *and* results in only partial upside (lower margin data-center rates), the "13% discount" evaporates. In fact, the stock could be **fairly valued or even overvalued** at current levels.

## SECTION 2: THE DATA-CENTER UPSIDE IS SPECULATIVE, NOT STRUCTURAL

### The Bull Confuses Market Sentiment with Market Reality

The bull makes a sophisticated argument: *"DUK is absent from headlines celebrating AI data-center gains, but that means it's under-priced."* This is backwards reasoning.

**Here's what's actually happening:**

1. **Utilities in the AI/Data-Center Narrative Have PROVEN Capacity & Contracts**
   - **NextEra:** Operating existing power plants and signing long-term PPAs with hyperscalers
   - **Constellation Energy:** Already running nuclear fleet at near full capacity with data-center commitments
   - **Xcel:** Explicitly guiding to 6-8% EPS growth with data-center demand quantified
   
   These aren't headlines—they're **concrete, contracted revenue streams.**

2. **DUK's Data-Center Story Remains Theoretical**
   - The bull argues DUK "has the operational scale and regulatory relationships to capture market share"
   - But DUK hasn't announced *any* new large-load customer contracts
   - DUK hasn't articulated a capex plan comparable to Xcel's $70B initiative
   - DUK is currently **fighting with regulators** over the *right to serve* data centers at favorable economics

   **This is not a "misunderstood opportunity." This is a company that hasn't executed yet.**

### Florida's Regulatory Rejection Is a Red Flag, Not a Speed Bump

The bull frames the Florida PSC criticism as "friction" that will be "resolved by Q4 2026 or Q1 2027." I see it differently: **It's evidence that DUK's business model may not align with the regulatory environment required to capture data-center demand.**

**Here's the concerning pattern:**

- **DUK's Proposal:** Sought tariff structure for large-load customers
- **Florida PSC Response:** "Doesn't comply with basic provisions" of SB 484
- **The Implication:** DUK's approach to monetizing data-center loads is *misaligned with state policy objectives*

This suggests one of three outcomes:

1. **DUK caves to PSC demands and accepts lower margins** → Upside thesis collapses
2. **DUK fights the PSC and drags out the process** → Timeline slips, uncertainty persists
3. **DUK succeeds but only after material delay** → Competitors capture market share first

**None of these scenarios supports the bull's optimistic re-rating thesis.**

### The Data-Center Opportunity May Be Smaller Than Bull Assumes

The bull suggests that if DUK captures data-center demand "comparable to Xcel," the stock re-rates to 17.5-18x forward P/E. But here's a critical question: **How much data-center demand can DUK actually capture?**

**Constraint Analysis:**

1. **Transmission & Grid Capacity:** Data centers require massive, reliable power. DUK's grid infrastructure in NC/SC, Florida, and Ohio must be *upgraded* to handle hyperscaler loads. This is multi-year, multi-billion capex—before earning *any* incremental revenue.

2. **Competitive Market:** Hyperscalers shop across multiple utilities. If Xcel, PPL, and AEP all have capacity *and* regulatory clarity, they'll capture the demand first. DUK will get whatever's left *after* regulatory clarity is achieved.

3. **Margin Reality:** Even if DUK lands data-center contracts, the margins may be thinner than the bull assumes. Hyperscalers negotiate hard for bulk power rates. The margin uplift might be 50-100 basis points, not the 200-300 basis points the bull's model implies.
