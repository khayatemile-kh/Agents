## **SECTION 1: THE SECULAR GROWTH THESIS IS BORROWED TIME, NOT DURABLE VALUE**

### **1.1 The Data Center Dream Assumes Perfect Regulatory Execution in a Hostile Environment**

The bull's argument rests on a critical assumption: **FE will capture 10-15 GW of the 35+ GW hyperscaler demand wave and regulators will cheerfully approve rate increases to fund it.**

Here's the problem: **That's not how 2026 regulatory politics work anymore.**

**The Evidence:**

1. **Regulatory Backlash Is Already Visible**
   - State legislators and public utility commissions across the U.S. are increasingly skeptical of aggressive utility rate increases, especially tied to speculative capex programs
   - Ohio regulators have already scrutinized FE's rate recovery requests multiple times in recent years
   - The political headwind is real: politicians face constituent pressure when electricity bills spike 10-15% annually

2. **NextEra's 35.1 GW Backlog ≠ FE's Opportunity**
   - NextEra operates in Florida and the Southeast, where it has established regulatory relationships and lower political resistance to rate increases
   - FE operates in the Rust Belt: Ohio, Pennsylvania, and surrounding regions where **public sentiment about utility rate hikes is far more hostile**
   - The bull assumes FE will capture "10-15 GW" of the hyperscaler wave, but provides **zero evidence** that:
     a) Hyperscalers have actually signed deals in FE's territory
     b) FE has secured regulatory pre-approval for the capex
     c) Regulators will approve the necessary rate recovery

3. **Rate Recovery Risk Is Embedded in FE's Recent Earnings Miss**
   - Q2 2026 operating income fell 45.4% QoQ and 17.4% YoY despite revenue growth of +8.8%
   - Why did margins collapse if the growth thesis is so compelling? **Because cost pressures and regulatory constraints are outpacing the company's ability to recover capex in rate base.**
   - This isn't a seasonal blip—this is the beginning of the regulatory squeeze

### **1.2 The $36B Energize365 Program Is Not "Legislated Growth"—It's a Bet on Regulatory Approval**

The bull claims: "Each dollar of capex translates to immediate rate-base growth because regulators approve it and allow recovery."

**This is misleading.** Regulatory approval is *not* automatic. It's a contested, adversarial process. Let me show you why this matters:

**The Regulatory Reality:**

- **Rate cases are NOT guaranteed wins.** Utilities file; regulators litigate; interveners (consumer advocates, environmental groups, industrial customers) fight against rate increases
- **Earned returns ≠ authorized returns.** Utilities are *authorized* 9-10% ROE, but they often earn *less* when regulators disallow portions of capex
- **Political pressure mounts when bills spike.** Voters hate utility rate hikes. As inflation fears resurface and bill impacts become visible, regulators face pressure to deny or defer capex recovery

**FE-Specific Evidence:**

From the data: FE's operating margin compressed from 19.7% (Q1 2026) to 12.3% (Q2 2026)—a **740 basis point decline in one quarter.** 

Why? Because:
1. Revenue grew 8.8% YoY, but operating income *declined* 17.4%
2. This suggests capex was not recovered in full during the period
3. The company is investing heavily but failing to secure proportional rate recovery

**The Bull's Contradiction:**
The bull says FE will deploy $7.2B annually and earn $684M in incremental earnings, translating to $0.30/share in new EPS. But **if regulators are already denying or delaying rate recovery (as evidenced by the Q2 margin collapse), why should we believe future capex cycles will be any different—or better?**
