**Rationale**: The bull's thesis rests on the standard "regulated utility" playbook: capex is rate-base accretive, negative FCF is normal for utilities in a build cycle, and the 1.91x OCF/dividend coverage ratio proves the payout is safe. The bear's rebuttal is more rigorous and data-driven, and it exposes real cracks in that story. First, the capex/OCF ratio of ~170% is not "elevated but fine" — it's roughly double the 80-90% norm the bear cites for regulated gas peers, and it has been consistently in the 137-182% range for four straight quarters, which is a trend, not a one-off. Second, the bull's "1.91x coverage" stat is technically true but misleading: OCF covers the dividend in isolation, but the same OCF is simultaneously insufficient to cover capex, meaning the actual funding of the dividend plus capex program depends on continuous debt issuance (~$1.24B in 6 months, annualizing to $2.5B+). That's a materially different risk picture than "fortress coverage" — it means equity holders are relying on uninterrupted capital markets access, not organic cash generation. Third, leverage metrics the bear raises (debt/EBITDA ~5.74x, debt/equity ~146.6%) are genuinely stretched versus the 2.5-3.5x utility norm, and interest coverage near 2.1x leaves little margin if rates or credit spreads move against NI — this directly undercuts the bull's "rates are stable so refinancing is fine" argument, because stable rates don't protect against idiosyncratic credit-rating downgrade risk if leverage keeps climbing. Fourth, the bull's regulatory argument glosses over recovery lag and the secular electrification/decarbonization headwind for gas utilities that the bear flags — even if not immediately fatal, it caps long-term rate base growth optionality that the bull assumes is open-ended. On valuation, the bull's forward P/E (18.26x) and EV/EBITDA (6.4x) look reasonable in isolation, but they don't fully price in the credit deterioration risk the bear lays out — a name trending toward possible downgrade shouldn't trade at "fair value to slightly discounted" multiples; it should carry a discount for that tail risk. The bull's technical "capitulation is complete" argument is speculative and not supported by any confirmed reversal evidence (RSI merely oversold, no volume-confirmed bottom signal in the provided data). On the other hand, the bear's case is not airtight either — there's no evidence yet of an actual downgrade, dividend cut, or covenant breach; this is a forward-looking leverage/coverage risk, not a crisis unfolding today. So a full "Sell / exit position" call would be excessive given the business is still cash-generative at the OCF line and dividend is currently being paid without stress signals like covenant violations or suspended buybacks. But the weight of evidence — persistent negative FCF, capex materially outrunning OCF for four consecutive quarters, rising debt-to-EBITDA approaching downgrade thresholds, and a secular demand headwind for gas distribution — tips this toward caution rather than accumulation. The bull's income/defensive framing is only sound if leverage stabilizes, which the data doesn't yet show. Given that the stronger, more granular argument here is the bear's, I'm landing on Underweight: trim exposure and treat this as a name to reduce into strength rather than add to on weakness, while acknowledging the dividend is not immediately at risk today.
