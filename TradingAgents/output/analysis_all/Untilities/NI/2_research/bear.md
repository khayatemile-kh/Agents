- Operating Cash Flow: $1.12 Billion
- Capital Expenditures: $1.90 Billion
- **Free Cash Flow: -$779.9 Million**
- **Capex as % of OCF: 169.6%** ← This is unsustainable

**Historical Context (Last 4 Quarters):**

| Quarter | OCF | Capex | FCF | Capex/OCF |

| Q2 2026 | $680.4M | $1,100M | -$419.6M | 161.6% |

**The bull claims this is "industry-normal." It absolutely is not.** 

Industry benchmarks for regulated gas utilities show capex ranging from 80-90% of OCF for *stable operations*. NI is running at **160-180% consistently**. That's not "modernization investment"—that's a company that cannot fund its capital program from operations and is increasingly reliant on debt markets.

## **3. THE REGULATORY RECOVERY CLAIM IS OUTDATED**

### **"Regulators Will Approve Rate Increases" ≠ Financial Safety**

The bull repeatedly invokes regulatory approval as a financial backstop. **This is dangerously naive about the political economy of utilities.**

**Here's what's actually happening:**

**A) Rate Increase Lag Risk**

Regulators *do* approve capex, but there's a lag between investment and rate recovery:
1. Utility invests capital (Year 1)
2. Utility files rate case (Year 2-3)
3. Regulatory approval (Year 3-4)
4. Rates increase (Year 4+)

**During this lag, NI must finance capex through debt.** If capex growth accelerates (as it has), debt balloons *before* rate recovery materializes.

**Current Rate Case Backdrop (as of Sept 2026):**
- Multiple state utility commissions are under political pressure to *limit* rate increases due to consumer/political opposition
- Climate change and decarbonization initiatives are creating *headwinds* for natural gas utilities
- Several states (California, New York, others) are promoting electrification and *reducing* reliance on natural gas

**The regulatory environment for gas utilities is tightening, not loosening.** Rate increase approvals are becoming slower and smaller, not faster and larger.

**B) The Structural Headwind: Natural Gas Decline**

This is the bull's *biggest* blind spot:

- U.S. natural gas demand is flat to slightly declining in the long term
- Residential and commercial electrification is accelerating
- State-level net-zero mandates are pushing away from gas
- NI's customer base is slowly shrinking, not growing

**If customer base is shrinking and regulatory approvals are slowing, where does NI get revenue growth to service $17.4B+ in debt?**

**Answer: It doesn't.** The dividend grows at inflation only, and debt servicing becomes an increasingly large burden relative to revenue.
