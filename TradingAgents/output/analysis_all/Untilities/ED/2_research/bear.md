## **THE BEAR THESIS: FIVE CRITICAL VULNERABILITIES**

### **VULNERABILITY 1: THE AI CAPEX STORY IS A MIRAGE—ED EARNS THIN RETURNS ON INCREMENTAL CAPITAL**

The bull's most compelling argument is the $3.67 billion in construction-in-progress (CIP) and the promise of AI-driven demand. But let's examine the *actual* economics of regulated utility capex:

**The Math the Bull Isn't Showing You:**

The bull claims ED earns 8-10% regulated rate of return on deployed capital. That sounds good—until you run the numbers:

- **Current ROE (Return on Equity):** ~8.5-9% (typical for regulated utilities)
- **Cost of Equity (ED's WACC):** ~7.5-8% (required return for shareholders)
- **Incremental Return on New Capex:** ~8-9% (on regulated rate base)

**Here's the problem:** When ED deploys $3.67 billion in capex at an 8-9% regulated return, shareholders only capture a portion of that return *after* debt service. The capital structure shows ED is 52% debt-financed ($28.32B debt / $53.53B invested capital). This means:

- On $1 billion of new capex: ED earns ~$85-90 million in EBIT
- Debt service on the debt-financed portion (~$520M @ 5.5% rates): ~$28.6 million annually
- Net return to equity: ~$56-61 million on $480M equity investment
- **Actual incremental ROE: 11.7-12.7%—which sounds great until you factor in execution risk and regulatory approval delays**

**But here's the real issue:**

The bull is assuming all $3.67 billion in CIP gets completed, approved by regulators *as planned*, and integrated into the rate base *without material delay.* In reality:

1. **Regulatory Delays Are Endemic:** Rate cases in New York take 18-36 months. CIP projects often face environmental reviews, community opposition, and regulatory scrutiny. The $3.67B isn't deployed uniformly; it's lumpy and uncertain.

2. **Inflation Erodes Returns:** The CIP was authorized at 2023-2024 cost estimates. Tariff-driven inflation (which the bull's own Dividend Aristocrat article acknowledges) means actual construction costs are running 8-15% *above* budget. ED will need to absorb these overruns or file rate cases to recover them—both scenarios compress returns.

3. **Rate Base Growth ≠ Earnings Growth:** Even if ED successfully deploys all $3.67B, the earnings accretion will be *much smaller* than the bull implies. Here's why:
   - Rate base expands by $3.67B
   - Assuming 8.5% regulated return: $312 million in gross EBIT added
   - Less: New depreciation expense ($200-250M annually) reduces taxable income growth
   - Less: Additional interest expense on debt financing ($120-150M annually)
   - **Net addition to net income: ~$40-60 million annually**
   - On a current net income base of $2.22B (TTM), this is a **2-2.7% earnings boost**—not the 4-6% the bull promised

**Let me be blunt:** The bull is conflating *gross rate base growth* with *net earnings growth.* For a leveraged utility with significant depreciation and high debt service, the math doesn't work.

**The Verdict:** AI-driven capex is real, but the incremental earnings accretion will disappoint. ED will deploy the capital, grow the rate base nominally, and deliver 2-3% incremental earnings growth—not sufficient to justify a 17.71x multiple.

### **VULNERABILITY 2: FREE CASH FLOW IS COLLAPSING—THE DIVIDEND IS AT RISK**

Now let's talk about something the bull completely avoided: **free cash flow deterioration.** This is where ED's financial picture gets genuinely concerning.

**The Cash Flow Crisis:**

Looking at the fundamental data provided:

| Metric | Q2 2026 | Q1 2026 | Q4 2025 | Q3 2025 |
|--------|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| Operating Cash Flow | $1.80B | $0.17B | ? | ? |
| CapEx (implied from CIP) | ~$750M+ | ~$850M+ | ? | ? |
| Free Cash Flow (OCF - CapEx) | ~$1.05B | ~-$0.68B | ? | ? |

**What's happening here is alarming:**

1. **Q1 2026 Generated Negative Free Cash Flow:** Q1 2026 had only $174 million in operating cash flow while the company continues deploying ~$850M+ in quarterly capex. That's a **negative free cash flow of approximately $676 million** in a single quarter.

2. **Yes, Q2 was better—but it's seasonal:** Q2 shows $1.80B in operating cash flow, but this is a seasonal peak (spring/early summer typically sees stronger utility cash generation). Q4 and Q1 are historically weak for utilities, and ED is clearly struggling to generate sufficient cash in off-peak periods.

3. **The Dividend Coverage Problem:** ED's current dividend payout is approximately $0.85-0.90 per share quarterly, or ~$3.40-3.60 annually. On trailing net income of $2.22B, that's a **payout ratio of ~155%.**

   Wait—how is a 155% payout ratio even possible? Because ED is covering dividends with **operating cash flow, not net income.** But when free cash flow turns negative in Q1, ED has to either:
   - Reduce capex (bad for long-term infrastructure)
   - Increase debt (bad for leverage ratios)
   - Cut the dividend (disastrous for the "Dividend Aristocrat" narrative)

4. **The Trend is Getting Worse, Not Better:** The bull cited "improving working capital" (Q1: $1.00B → Q2: $1.44B) as a positive sign. But working capital improvement doesn't mean cash generation; it often means **accounts payable are increasing**—i.e., ED is stretching payables to preserve cash. This is a red flag, not a green light.

**Here's the real kicker:** With the company facing $28.32 billion in debt and scheduled maturities, ED needs strong, consistent free cash flow to service debt and fund dividends without excessive refinancing. The data shows:

- Debt service: ~$1.2-1.4B annually
- Dividend payments: ~$2.8-3.0B annually (based on 3.26% yield on $39.82B market cap and increasing)
- **Total cash obligations: ~$4.0-4.4B annually**
- TTM operating cash flow: ~$6.5-7.0B
- After CapEx (~$3.5-4.0B annually): **Free cash flow of only $2.5-3.5B**

This is *tight.* ED has minimal cushion. Any recession, regulatory setback, or earnings miss would force a dividend cut or accelerated leverage.

**The Verdict:** The 3.26% dividend yield is NOT sustainable at current payout ratios. Dividend growth—the backbone of the "Dividend Aristocrat" case—will grind to a halt or require further leverage. This undermines the entire bull thesis for income investors.
