### **FAILURE #2: NEGATIVE FREE CASH FLOW IS NOT A "REGULATORY FEATURE"—IT'S A FINANCIAL DETERIORATION SIGNAL**

The bull made a critical error here, and I need to correct it directly.

**The bull claimed:** "Negative FCF is *intended design* in regulated utilities. ETR finances capex through debt, earns regulatory returns, and pays dividends. This is normal."

**The reality:** This is *partially* true, but the bull omitted the crucial distinction between **sustainable negative FCF** and **deteriorating negative FCF**. And ETR's trend is clearly deteriorating.

Let me show you the data:

**Free Cash Flow Trend (Quarterly):**
| Period | OCF | CapEx | FCF | Trend |
|--------|-----|-------|-----|-------|
| Q1 2025 | $536M | -$1,750M | -$1,214M | Negative |

**What the bull won't admit:** Q1 2026 FCF is the *worst* in the entire dataset at -$1.574 billion. CapEx has increased 37% YoY ($1.75B → $2.40B), and OCF is *declining* despite the company's supposed "earnings growth."

**This is the opposite of a healthy capital cycle.** Here's why:

**In a healthy utility capex cycle:**
1. OCF grows steadily or flat as the company increases rates to recover capex
2. CapEx remains relatively stable as a % of assets deployed
3. The negative FCF gap *shrinks* over time as capex peak is reached

**What's actually happening at ETR:**
1. OCF is *volatile and declining* (Q1 2026: $829M vs. Q3 2025: $2,135M = -61% collapse)
2. CapEx is *accelerating* (+37% YoY)
3. The FCF gap is *widening*, not narrowing
4. Debt is being issued at an accelerating rate ($3.7B long-term debt in Q1 2026 alone)

**This is a company struggling to fund its capex cycle.** It's not a feature; it's a crisis.
