But the bear case lands some real punches that the bull doesn't fully rebut. First, the failed wildfire liability bill isn't just 'status quo'—it leaves SRE with genuinely unquantified tail risk, and the bear is right that regulatory contagion doesn't respect geographic nuance once liability frameworks or insurance markets reprice California utility risk as a sector. Fourth, the bull's technical read (capitulation = buying opportunity) is a reasonable hypothesis but is speculative; the actual technical picture (below 50/200 SMA, negative MACD, weak RSI) is bearish on its face and the bull is essentially betting on a reversal without confirming evidence.

Net-net: the bull's structural/valuation arguments are sound as a baseline utility framework, but the bear correctly identifies that the current environment has real, unresolved tail risks (liability, refinancing cost step-up, regulatory hostility) that could impair the very capex-recovery and earnings-growth assumptions the valuation case depends on. Neither side has decisively won — the bull's case is a reasonable 'normal utility economics' argument, the bear's case is a reasonable 'this cycle is different because of politics and rates' argument, and we don't have the tools here to adjudicate which one dominates over the next few quarters. Given genuinely balanced conviction and elevated event risk (regulatory outcomes, refinancing execution, analyst repositioning) that could break either direction, Hold is the appropriate stance rather than forcing a Buy or Sell call on incomplete information.
