## **CLAIM #1: "WEC IS TRADING 12% BELOW FAIR VALUE" — THIS IS CIRCULAR REASONING**

Here's what the bull is doing: citing a Simply Wall St. model that says WEC is worth 12% more, then using that as justification to buy. But **this ignores a critical question: what if the Wall Street consensus itself is too optimistic?**

Let me connect the dots on why I believe it is:

### **The Analyst Consensus is Built on Flawed Assumptions About Capex Returns**

The bull's entire thesis rests on this promise: **$37.5B in capex from 2026–2030 will mechanically generate 10-12% annual rate-base growth and "mathematically certain" earnings accretion.**

**Here's the problem:** This assumes perfect regulatory cooperation and zero execution friction.

Let me reference the actual data:

**WEC's debt has grown 33% in three years—from $17.29B (2022) to $22.98B (Q2 2026)—while net income growth has *decelerated* to just 0.4% year-over-year (2025 vs. 2024).**

Think about this carefully:
- **Debt up 33% in 3 years**
- **Net income up 0.4% in most recent year**
- **Interest burden growing faster than earnings**

This is *not* a company in control of its capital deployment. This is a company that's taking on massive debt to fund capex, betting that regulators will allow full cost recovery and that the market will reward the earnings accretion with higher multiples.

**But here's the regulatory risk the bull glosses over:**

Regulated utilities face increasing scrutiny on cost-of-service and rate recovery. Recent trends show:
1. **Lengthening regulatory approval timelines** (utilities waiting 12-18+ months for rate cases)
2. **Stricter scrutiny of capex justification** (regulators asking: is this necessary, or are you gold-plating?)
3. **Political pressure on utility rates** (consumers and politicians increasingly push back on rate increases)

If WEC deploys $37.5B and regulators disallow even 10-15% of that cost recovery, the earnings accretion evaporates. And the debt? That remains on the balance sheet.

**The bull assumes regulatory certainty. The market, correctly, is pricing in regulatory risk.**

Compare this to the full-year 2025 growth of just 0.4%. **That's the real trend—stalled earnings growth.**

The bull is cherry-picking a quarterly beat and extrapolating it as a multi-year theme. But the company's actual full-year results show **earnings growth has decelerated dramatically**, from 14.6% (2023–2024) to essentially flat (2024–2025).

**This is a yellow flag, not a green light.**

## **CLAIM #3: THE $37.5B CAPEX PLAN IS A GROWTH ENGINE — IT'S ACTUALLY A RED FLAG FOR DEBT SUSTAINABILITY**

Let me expose the math behind the capex thesis:

The bull claims:
- $37.5B capex over 5 years (~$7.5B annually)
- This will translate to 10-12% rate-base growth annually
- Which will drive 8-10% EPS growth

**But here's what the balance sheet tells us:**

WEC already spent **$4.40B in capex in 2025 alone**—a 58% increase from 2024. At this run rate, the company is on pace to deploy $8-9B annually going forward.

**Now, let's examine the debt service burden:**

- **Total debt: $22.98B**
- **Annual interest expense (implied at ~4.5% average rate): ~$1.04B**
- **Annual cash flow from operations: ~$3.2B (estimated from TTM)**
- **Annual capex: $4.4B (and climbing)**

**The math doesn't work without increasing leverage or cutting the dividend.**

After interest (~$1.04B) and capex ($4.4B), WEC has only **~$1.76B remaining from operations** to service debt principal, fund the dividend, and invest in growth. The company has a **dividend payout ratio of 58-62%**, which means it's committing ~$1.0B annually to dividends.

**That leaves only $760M for debt reduction and contingencies.**

This is unsustainable at current capex levels without either:
1. **Equity raises** (which dilute shareholders)
2. **Dividend cuts** (unlikely, but possible if debt metrics deteriorate)
3. **Regulatory rate increases** (not guaranteed; increasingly opposed)

**The bull is assuming regulators will allow WEC to pass through all capex costs to customers. But rising debt levels and flattening earnings suggest the market is already pricing in that this assumption may not hold.**

## **CLAIM #4: "THE DATA CENTER BOOM IS A TAILWIND" — IT'S PRICED IN AND OVERSTATED**

The bull cites "20-25% CAGR data center power demand growth" and suggests WEC will capture this.

**Let me puncture this narrative:**

### **Data center demand is being captured by many utilities, not just WEC.**

Duke Energy, American Electric Power (AEP), Southern Company, and others are *also* positioned in high-growth data center markets. In fact:

- **Duke Energy operates in North Carolina**, which has become a data center hub (with significant AWS and Google presence)
- **AEP operates in Ohio and Oklahoma**, both of which are attractive for data center development
- **Southern Company operates in Georgia**, which is aggressively courting data center investment

**WEC doesn't have a monopoly on this secular trend.** Yet if you listen to the bull, you'd think WEC is the only utility positioned to capture data center growth. It's not.

### **Data center power demand may already be reflected in guidance.**

More importantly, CEO Lauber mentioned data center opportunities in guidance, but **the full-year 2026 guidance of $5.51–$5.61 is what the market has to work with.** If data centers were a massive incremental opportunity *not yet priced in*, we'd expect to see guidance raised, capex plans expanded, or dividend growth accelerated.

**None of that happened.** Guidance was reaffirmed, not raised.

**This suggests data center growth is already baked into current expectations, not an upside surprise.**
