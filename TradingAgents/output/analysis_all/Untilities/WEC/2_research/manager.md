**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides made reasonable points, but weighing them carefully, the bear's case is more grounded in hard financial data while the bull leans heavily on narrative and technical noise. The bull's core argument—12% discount to fair value, $37.5B capex driving rate-base growth, data center tailwinds, and stabilizing technicals—sounds compelling on the surface, but the bear exposed real cracks: (1) full-year net income growth has decelerated from 14.6% to just 0.4% YoY, meaning the Q2 19.5% EPS beat is an easy comp anomaly, not a trend; (2) debt has grown 33% in three years to $22.98B while earnings have stalled, and the cash-flow math the bear laid out (op cash flow ~$3.2B vs. capex $4.4B+interest ~$1.04B+dividends ~$1.0B) leaves a thin ~$760M cushion, meaning the $37.5B capex plan is more likely to require dilutive equity raises or elevated leverage than deliver 'mathematically certain' accretion; (3) the data center tailwind is real but not WEC-specific—Duke, AEP, Southern are equally positioned—and guidance was reaffirmed, not raised, suggesting this upside is already priced in rather than a hidden catalyst; (4) the bull's rate-environment logic is shaky since higher-for-longer rates raise WEC's cost of capital on that large debt load even if allowed ROE nominally rises, and regulatory lag/pushback risk on rate cases is a real friction the bull waves away. The technical argument (MACD compression, RSI neutral) is a weak tie-breaker at best—it shows deceleration of selling, not a confirmed reversal, and doesn't offset the balance-sheet deterioration story. On balance, the bear's data-driven dismantling of the debt/earnings trajectory and capex funding math outweighs the bull's valuation-gap and secular-tailwind narrative, which relies on assumptions (regulatory cooperation, capex translating cleanly to earnings, WEC capturing disproportionate data-center share) that aren't yet confirmed by the numbers. This isn't a strong enough case for outright Sell since it's still a regulated utility with a stable dividend and no guidance cuts, but the risk/reward skews cautious rather than balanced enough for a plain Hold.

**Strategic Actions**: Trim existing WEC exposure modestly rather than adding on the recent pullback. Specifically: (1) Do not treat the $104-106 zone as a high-conviction 'buy the dip' entry—wait for confirmed evidence that full-year (not just one-quarter) earnings growth is reaccelerating above the 0.4% 2025 pace before adding back. (2) Monitor debt metrics closely each quarter—if total debt continues rising faster than net income (currently 33% debt growth vs ~0.4% earnings growth), reduce position further. (3) Watch upcoming rate-case outcomes and regulatory commentary in Wisconsin/Michigan for signs of disallowed capex recovery; any negative regulatory ruling should trigger further de-risking. (4) Retain a small residual position for the dividend yield (~3.6%) and defensive utility characteristics, but size it as underweight relative to sector benchmark given the weaker earnings trajectory and stretched cash-flow coverage of capex+interest+dividend. (5) Reassess if guidance is explicitly raised (not just reaffirmed) on data-center demand, or if debt-to-EBITDA metrics stabilize/improve — either would be a trigger to move back to Hold/Overweight.
