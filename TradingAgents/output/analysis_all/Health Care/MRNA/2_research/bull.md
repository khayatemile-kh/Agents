**Fair points. Now let me show you why they're missing context.**

### **A. Cash Runway Analysis: The Company Has Time**

Yes, the company is cash flow negative. But let's do the math:

- **Liquid Assets:** $5.138 billion (cash + short-term investments)
- **Quarterly Burn Rate (Q1-Q2 2026 average):** ~$609 million
- **Annualized Burn:** ~$2.4 billion
- **Cash Runway:** ~2+ years

But here's what changes that calculation:

1. **Seasonal Revenue Patterns:** Q3 and Q4 typically see **stronger vaccine sales** due to seasonal flu / respiratory season. Q2 was historically weak. The bear is cherry-picking the worst quarter.

2. **Partnership Revenue from Merck:** The Merck melanoma vaccine deal includes **upfront payments, milestone payments, and future royalties**. These aren't fully reflected in the Q2 numbers. As the program advances, Moderna **receives significant non-dilutive capital**.

3. **Cost Structure Flexibility:** The company has demonstrated it can **modulate spending** based on clinical timelines. If cash becomes constrained, Moderna can slow lower-priority programs or defer capex—but they won't.

4. **Potential Capital Raises:** With the stock at $154 and proven Phase 3 data, Moderna could raise $2-3 billion in equity at favorable terms if needed. But they likely won't need to given the runway above.

**Bottom Line:** The company has a **2+ year runway to profitability**—and that's only counting existing cash, not partnership revenue, potential milestone payments, or equity raises. This is not a near-term liquidity crisis.
