## **THE MERCK PARTNERSHIP: REAL OPTIONALITY OR MIRAGE?**

The aggressive analyst frames the Merck partnership as **de-risking** the development and **guaranteeing** commercialization expertise. That's half-true and dangerously misleading.

Here's what we actually know about the Merck deal:
- Moderna and Merck announced positive Phase 3 data for melanoma on August 19, 2026
- Merck contributed IP and manufacturing expertise
- The parties are co-developing the program

Here's what we *don't* know and what presents existential risk:
- **What are the terms of milestone payments?** We don't have visibility into the deal structure. Milestone payments in biotech partnerships are typically tied to clinical events and regulatory approvals—not guaranteed cash disbursements. If the program hits a regulatory snag or requires additional studies, those milestones *disappear*.
- **What is Merck's actual financial exposure?** Merck is a commercial partner, not a financing partner. They're incentivized to co-develop because they believe in the commercial opportunity. But they're not obligated to fund Moderna's operations. If Moderna hits a cash crisis before they can fund their own operations, Merck can—and will—renegotiate terms or walk away.
- **Has Merck actually committed cash to offset Moderna's burn rate?** There's no indication they have. The partnership is a development collaboration, not a financing arrangement. 

**So here's the risk the aggressive analyst has buried:** The Merck partnership provides *optionality* on the upside (shared commercialization, de-risked development). But it provides **zero protection** on the downside if Moderna runs out of cash before the program generates revenue.
