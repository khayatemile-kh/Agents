Layering a $2.25B largely debt-funded acquisition onto this structure pushes Debt/EBITDA from a currently tolerable ~1.65x toward ~2.0x, right at the level where rating agencies start to get twitchy, especially given the equity base is already negative. The bear is also right to flag that Precision Medicine's actual margin and revenue contribution is unverified — the bull is extrapolating industry benchmarks onto a specific acquisition target without confirmed financials, and CRO-style cross-sell synergy claims are historically prone to under-delivery (client stickiness, differing sales motions, integration/talent-retention risk).

Net-net: the growth optionality from the acquisition and the secular tailwind are credible and could support upside if execution goes well and normalized FCF proves durable, but the leverage/negative-equity/working-capital fragility is a legitimate structural risk that caps how much conviction I can put behind an Overweight or Buy right now. Nothing in the debate rises to a Sell-level thesis either — revenue scale, EBITDA generation, and reasonable relative valuation argue against exiting. This is a genuine two-sided case with unresolved key unknowns (actual Precision Medicine financials, whether FCF volatility normalizes or persists, and post-acquisition leverage trajectory), so Hold is the appropriate, honest call rather than forcing a lean in either direction.
