**Why does this matter?** Because if we're trading at a forward P/E of 24.2x while margins normalize, the risk/reward is asymmetric. At normalized 4%+ operating margins (which is achievable for HUM based on historical performance), we're looking at sustainable EPS well north of current pricing.

### **2. VALUE-BASED CARE IS A MARGIN EXPANSION MOAT—AND IT'S ACCELERATING**

This is where HUM has a structural competitive advantage that the market is underpricing.

**The Strategic Reality:**

HUM's **DaVita partnership expansion** (10,000+ Medicare Advantage members with CKD) is not a PR win—it's a **margin expansion engine**. Here's the economics:

- **Traditional MA model:** 1-2% net margins, exposed to claims volatility
- **Value-based care model:** 3-5% net margins, outcomes-based incentives aligned with lower utilization

DaVita has **proven economics** in managing chronic kidney disease. By shifting 10,000+ members into this outcomes-based model, HUM is:
1. **De-risking** its medical loss ratio
2. **Capturing** shared savings from lower hospitalizations and procedures
3. **Building** long-term contractual relationships that competitors can't replicate

And here's the kicker—**this is just the beginning.** The CenterWell expansion (HUM's owned primary care and adjacent care delivery network) will compound this advantage. Unlike United or CVS, HUM is building integrated value-based care on its own platform, not acquiring it piecemeal.

**Time Horizon:** This won't show up in full force until 2027-2028, but the foundation is being laid *now*. Early investors get the upside before the market prices in consistent margin expansion.

Here's the uncomfortable truth: the Villages Health overbilling scandal happened *before* HUM fully integrated the acquisition. Now that the settlement is behind them, here's what's actually occurring:

1. **The financial impact is quantified:** $542M is material but not existential for a $48B market-cap company. It's already priced in—HUM stock is up 46% YTD *despite* this headline.

**Compare this to the bear case:** If HUM's governance was truly broken, we'd expect recurring settlements. Instead, we're seeing isolated incidents being resolved. That's the *opposite* of systemic failure.

### **"What about Medicare Advantage plan non-renewals? Isn't that revenue loss?"**

**My response:** This is strategic repositioning, not capitulation.

The bears will frame plan non-renewals as "HUM is shrinking" or "CMS is squeezing margins." But that's a surface-level read. Here's what's actually happening:

**The Strategic Context:**

1. **Selective portfolio optimization:** HUM is *choosing* to exit low-margin, low-value plans. This is healthy portfolio management. **Higher-margin plans are being expanded.**

2. **Market signal from CMS:** The non-renewal notices (coming early October per guidance) are based on CMS Star Ratings and profitability targets. HUM's strategy is to focus on plans it can profitably deliver—that's *smart*, not weak.

3. **Member continuity:** These aren't *catastrophic* exits. Members are being transitioned to other HUM plans or competitive offerings. Revenue is declining, yes, but **profitability per member is improving.**

**The Math:**
- If HUM exits $2-3B in low-margin revenue but improves margins from 2.8% to 3.5%, the EPS impact is **neutral to positive.**
- The forward guidance of 54% EPS growth assumes this non-renewal dynamic is already factored in.

This is not bearish—it's portfolio discipline. And the market rewards that over time.
