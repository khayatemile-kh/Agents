## **PART I: THOSE "ELITE" MARGINS ARE DETERIORATING, NOT STABLE**

The bull analyst opens with a flourish: "27.7% net margins! 40.7% operating margins! Elite profitability!"

This is where the bear case starts, because **the bull is confusing profitability with *trajectory*.** Let's look at what's actually happening to these margins.

### **Margin Compression: The Evidence the Bulls Ignore**

Here's the inconvenient quarterly data:

| Quarter | Revenue | Gross Margin | Operating Margin | Net Margin |
|---------|---------|--------------|------------------|-----------|
| **Q2 2026** | $2,468M | 72.7% | 40.4% | 28.0% |
| **Q1 2026** | $2,262M | 71.7% | 36.3% | 26.6% |
| **Q4 2025** | $2,387M | 70.2% | 34.2% | 25.3% |
| **Q3 2025** | $2,400M | 71.5% | 39.4% | 30.0% |
| **Q2 2025** | $2,474M | 73.1% | 39.0% | 29.3% |

**What this *actually* shows:**

1. **Gross margins are under pressure:** Comparing Q2 2026 (72.7%) to Q2 2025 (73.1%) shows a **40 basis point erosion year-over-year.** The bull says margins are "sticky"—but they're moving in the *wrong direction*.

2. **Operating margin volatility is extreme:** Q3 2025 at 39.4% vs. Q1 2026 at 36.3%—that's **310 basis points of swings**, suggesting operational control is slipping.

3. **Net margin under fire:** Q4 2025 (25.3%) to Q2 2026 (28.0%) looks positive on the surface, but Q1 2026 (26.6%) reveals the weakness. We're not seeing *consistent* improvement; we're seeing **noise and compression**.

### **Why Margins Are Collapsing: The Real Drivers**

The bulls attribute the U.S. companion-animal decline (-11% YoY) to "cyclical consumer softness." That's wishful thinking. Here's what's *actually* happening:

**1. Pricing Power is Evaporating**

In Q2 2026, the company reported:
- **Total revenue: flat YoY (-0.24%)**
- **U.S. companion-animal products: -11% YoY**

If U.S. companion-animal is down 11%, the ONLY way total revenue is flat is if other segments grew 5-8%+. But here's the problem: **you can't grow your way out of a collapsing segment with higher margins elsewhere.** This signals the company is being *forced to accept lower-margin business* to keep revenues from total collapse.

The bull ignores this. They say "cyclical, temporary." But declining pricing power is the death knell for pharma. Once you lose it, you don't get it back.

**2. Operating Leverage is Negative**

Look at the math:
- Q2 2025: $2,474M revenue, $965M EBIT
- Q2 2026: $2,468M revenue, $927M EBIT

**Revenue down 0.24%, but EBIT down 4.0%.** This is *negative* operating leverage. It means fixed costs are eating into profits as volume contracts. This is the opposite of what a "fortress" business should show.

**3. Mix Deterioration**

The U.S. companion-animal segment is typically *higher margin* than livestock and medicated feed (which Zoetis divested). If the high-margin business is collapsing while lower-margin segments stay flat or shrink, **net margins compress inexorably**.

The Phibro divestiture? The bulls frame it as "capital allocation discipline." I frame it as: **Zoetis had to sell assets because they couldn't generate returns on them anymore.**

3. **The TTM is an artifact.** It still includes Q2 2025 ($605M FCF) and Q3 2025 ($755M). As those roll off and get replaced by Q2 2026 ($538M) and Q1 2026 ($291M), watch what happens to the TTM figure. **It's going to drop 25-35% within two quarters.**

**Because the company is going to need to invest MORE in operations, not less.** If margins are compressing, the company will need to:

- Invest in facilities to support international expansion (the one growth lever they have left)

This means **capex as a percentage of revenue is likely to *increase*, not stay flat.** That's another FCF headwind the bulls aren't accounting for.
