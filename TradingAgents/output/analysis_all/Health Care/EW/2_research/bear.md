**Answer: Because the market has already capitalized decades of expected growth into the current valuation.**

### **The Growth Rate is Decelerating, Not Accelerating**

The bull claimed TAVR is growing at "15-18% CAGR," but let me present the actual Q2 2026 data:

- **EW Q2 2026 Total Revenue Growth:** Only **5.6% sequential** (Q2 vs Q1)

### **Downtrading is Not Market Expansion—It's Margin Compression**

The bull argued that TAVR "downtrading" (moving to lower-risk patients) *expands* the addressable market. I respectfully call this a misreading of what downtrading actually means for profitability:

1. **Lower-risk patients have cheaper procedures** - simpler cases, shorter hospital stays, fewer complications mean lower reimbursement per case
2. **Competitive intensity increases** - When the market expands to lower-risk patients, *that's when Boston Scientific, Medtronic, and Abbott aggressively compete on price*
3. **Volume growth ≠ profit growth** - EW may do more TAVR cases, but at lower margins

The fundamental economics have shifted: **EW is trading volume growth (quantity) for price cuts (margin erosion).** This is classic business cycle dynamics—market maturation.

**EW's leverage metrics:**
- **Debt-to-Equity: 6.57x** (this means $6.57 in debt for every $1 in equity)

1. **Real tangible equity is only ~$7.28B** (total equity minus intangibles)
2. **Debt-to-tangible equity is much higher than 6.57x** - closer to **9.7x**
3. **The company is financing buybacks with leverage**, which is fine in a growth environment but dangerous when growth slows
