## Now Let's Examine This "Massive Gap" in Sequential Growth They're Hammering

The aggressive analyst says 5.6% sequential revenue growth in Q2 versus a 15-18% TAVR CAGR claim represents a "gap" that indicates deceleration and valuation compression ahead. But this is where they're committing a serious analytical sin: **they're conflating sequential quarterly growth with full-year CAGR projections**.

Think about this logically. Companies don't grow at CAGR rates uniformly every single quarter—that's not how business works. There are seasonal factors, product cycle timing, adoption curves that accelerate, and mix dynamics. A 5.6% sequential quarter doesn't disprove a 15-18% multi-year CAGR. In fact, if the company is growing at 15-18% on an annualized basis, you'd *expect* to see quarterly variations around that trend line.

Furthermore, Q2 represents a recovery quarter coming off the July crash. The company bounced 13.3% in seven weeks. The sequential growth number reflects that stabilization. What matters is whether the underlying TAVR adoption trajectory is intact—and the data suggests it is. The company wouldn't maintain 77.8% gross margins and 29.8% operating margins if demand had truly collapsed. These are not the numbers of a company facing demand destruction.
