## **REFUTING THE BULL'S CORE CLAIMS**

### **BEAR COUNTER #1: "Forward P/E of 18.15x Is Reasonable for 50% EPS Growth"**

**The Fatal Flaw in This Argument:**

The bull is quoting forward EPS growth of **50% without scrutinizing where it comes from.** Let me show you the math:

**Current Financials (TTM):**
- TTM Net Income: $8.74B
- TTM Revenue: $38.1B
- Forward EPS estimate: $24.40
- Current EPS: $16.25

**This implies 50% EPS growth, but here's the problem:**

The bull is comparing a TTM EPS that *already includes Q2 2026's $4.37 EPS* to a forward estimate of $24.40. **That forward estimate assumes extraordinary earnings acceleration that requires one or more of these conditions:**

1. **Revenue growth of 30-40% annually** — There's no evidence AMGN can achieve this. TTM revenue growth is in the **low-to-mid single digits** at best. Repatha and Tezspire are incremental revenue drivers, not game-changers.

2. **Aggressive cost-cutting/margin expansion** — If achieved through R&D cuts, this destroys the "pipeline strength" the bull touts. If achieved through COGS reduction, it's already baked into current 72% gross margins.

3. **Massive accretive M&A** — AMGN has a history of transformational acquisitions (Enbrel, Neulasta, Prolia via buyouts). But with $57.3B in debt and only $13.99B in cash, the company has **limited financial flexibility** to pursue large deals without further balance sheet stress.

4. **Share buybacks masking flat earnings** — Here's the most likely scenario: AMGN's actual *operational* earnings are growing 8-12% organically, but the company is aggressively repurchasing shares to juice per-share metrics. This is exactly what negative retained earnings (-$22.28B) signals: **decades of excess capital returns disguised as growth.**

**The math doesn't work.** A forward P/E of 18.15x is not a discount—it's the market **correctly pricing in that 50% EPS growth is unsustainable fantasy.**
