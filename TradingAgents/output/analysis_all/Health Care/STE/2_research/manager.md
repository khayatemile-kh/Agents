Netting it out: the fundamental crux of this debate is whether the 27% EPS growth is repeatable. The bear's argument that it is not — that margins are approaching a plateau and organic growth is closer to high single digits — is the more disciplined, numbers-grounded case, and it directly undermines the valuation the bull is leaning on. A stock trading at 18.81x forward earnings and 28.9x trailing earnings, with a PEG that implies expensive-for-growth positioning once the math is properly reverse-engineered, is not a screaming bargain — it's arguably priced for continued strength that the underlying growth algorithm doesn't clearly support. That said, the balance sheet is genuinely solid in absolute terms (low net leverage, strong operating cash flow growth of ~17% YoY), and the business itself (sterilization/infection prevention) has defensive, recurring-revenue characteristics that argue against an outright Sell — this isn't a broken business, just a richly-priced one with earnings-quality risk. Given the bear's stronger case on earnings quality and valuation, but acknowledging the balance sheet strength and defensive end-market that make this a lower-conviction bear case rather than an outright collapse thesis, the appropriate stance is a cautious trim rather than a full exit.
