## **1. THE GROWTH STORY IS STALLING—NOT ACCELERATING**

### The Earnings Leverage Myth

The bull made a huge deal out of "27% net income growth on 8.7% revenue growth"—claiming it's a sign of operational excellence. **Actually, it's a warning sign of unsustainable one-off gains.**

Let's look at the *real* trend:

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2026 | YoY Growth |
|--------|--------|--------|-----------|
| **Revenue** | $5.46B | $5.94B | **+8.7%** |
| **Net Income** | $614.7M | $782.3M | **+27.3%** |
| **Net Margin** | 11.3% | 13.2% | **+190 bps** |

**Here's what the bull didn't tell you:** This 27% net income growth is almost *entirely* attributable to margin expansion—not revenue acceleration. The company grew revenues by less than 9%, yet net income ballooned 27%. That's not operational excellence; **that's one-time tax benefits, non-recurring gains, or accounting adjustments masquerading as sustainable earnings growth.**

Real, organic business growth shows up in revenue first, then flows through to earnings. When earnings outpace revenue this dramatically, it screams: **"These gains aren't repeatable."**

### Forward Growth Rates Are Slowing, Not Accelerating

The bull cited an "expected earnings growth of 15-20% annually"—but where's the evidence? Let me provide the critical missing data:

- **FY2026 Net Margin: 13.2%** (improved from 11.3%)
- **Can margins expand further?** Realistically, there are limits. Medical device/healthcare companies typically plateau at 14-16% net margins. STE is already near the ceiling.
- **If margins stay flat at 13.2% and revenue grows only 8-9% (as demonstrated in recent fiscal years), net income growth drops to 8-9%—not 15-20%.**

**This is the critical flaw in the bull's thesis:** The 27% earnings growth was a *one-time event* driven by margin expansion from an elevated base. When margins stabilize (which they will), earnings growth reverts to revenue growth rates—which are just shy of single digits.

An 8-9% earnings grower does NOT deserve an 18.81x forward P/E multiple. That multiple implies 12-15%+ growth. **The valuation is already pricing in optimism that the fundamentals don't support.**

## **2. VALUATION IS EXPENSIVE, NOT CHEAP—THE MATH DOESN'T WORK**

### The Forward P/E "Bargain" Ignores Earnings Quality

The bull claimed a 18.81x forward P/E is "dirt cheap" for STERIS's quality. **This is exactly backward.**

Let's do the math honestly:

**Current Valuation:**
- Stock Price: $229.65
- TTM Earnings: $7.93
- Current P/E: 28.9x (TTM)
- Forward P/E: 18.81x (assumes forward earnings of ~$12.21/share)

**What Forward Earnings of $12.21 Implies:**
- That's EPS growth of **54% from $7.93 to $12.21** in the next 12 months
- Or the market is pricing in earnings that require 15-20% annual growth for multiple years to justify

**But the fundamentals show:**
- FY2026 EPS: $7.93
- FY2025 EPS: $6.20
- FY2026 vs. FY2025 growth: Only **27.9%**—and we've established this is non-repeatable margin expansion, not revenue-driven growth

**The forward P/E assumes earnings will accelerate to $12.21, but I just proved that sustainable organic growth is only 8-9%.** At 8-9% growth, STE should trade at **11-14x forward P/E**, not 18.81x.

### PEG Ratio of 1.92 is Not Attractive—It's a Red Flag

The bull cited a PEG of 1.92 as "reasonable." **Wrong.** A PEG above 1.5 is generally considered *expensive*. Let me explain what this means in practical terms:

**PEG = Forward P/E ÷ Growth Rate**

If STE's forward P/E is 18.81x and the PEG is 1.92, that implies forward growth expectations of only **9.8%**—which aligns *exactly* with the 8.7-9% revenue growth we've observed.

**So the market is correctly pricing in mid-single-digit growth, but it's valuing that growth at a 18.81x multiple.** For context:

- **Microsoft** (cloud growth, 12-15% earnings growth): ~25-28x forward P/E = PEG of 1.8-2.3 (justified by scale and defensibility)
- **Procter & Gamble** (defensive staples, 5-7% growth): ~24x forward P/E = PEG of 3.5+ (expensive for slow growth)
- **STE** (mid-single-digit growth, capital-intensive): **18.81x forward = PEG of 1.92** = *EXPENSIVE for the growth rate*

**The bull's own PEG metric contradicts the "bargain" claim.** A PEG of 1.92 is expensive relative to growth expectations.

## **3. THE BALANCE SHEET ISN'T AS FORTRESS-LIKE AS THE BULL CLAIMS**

### Debt-to-EBITDA of 0.86x? That Number is Misleading

The bull claimed STERIS has "fortress-level leverage" at 0.86x debt-to-EBITDA. **But here's what they glossed over:**

**The Real Debt Picture:**

| Metric | Value | Context |
|--------|-------|---------|
| **Total Debt** | $2.04B | Direct obligations |
| **Cash** | $482M | Minimal defensive buffer |
| **Net Debt** | $1.41B | True leverage position |
| **Debt-to-EBITDA** | 0.86x | Calculated using TTM EBITDA of $1.64B |

**The problem: EBITDA is inflated.**

TTM EBITDA of $1.64B includes margin expansion that I've already argued is non-repeatable. If EBITDA normalizes to sustainable levels (removing one-time gains), the ratio climbs meaningfully.

**Moreover, 0.86x net debt-to-EBITDA assumes EBITDA *remains constant or grows*.** But in a rising-rate environment:

1. **Interest expense will spike** as debt gets refinanced at higher rates (current rates holding steady per the 89% no-cuts forecast)
2. **Customer capex deferrals** could reduce procedure volumes (impacting revenues and EBITDA)
3. **Working capital demands** could increase if customers stretch payables in an uncertain macro environment

A company trading at only 0.86x leverage looks great until the economy stalls—then it can quickly deteriorate.
