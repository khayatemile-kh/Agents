**First, let's contextualize what 6 basis points actually means for a mature healthcare distributor.** The company operates at ~3% net margins in a highly competitive, low-friction distribution business where 100 basis points of margin expansion is a generational event, not an annual expectation. A 6 bps improvement in net margins while simultaneously:
- Growing revenue 6.8% year-over-year
- Expanding gross margins from 30.7% to 31.8%
- Maintaining operating margins at 5.78% (Q2 2026)

...is actually *healthy operational execution*, not financial engineering smoke and mirrors.

**Second, the aggressive analyst is deliberately misconstruing what share buybacks represent.** Yes, the company funded buybacks with debt. Do you know what that means in the current macro environment? It means management correctly assessed that:
- Cost of debt (~3-4% after-tax in a 4-5% rate environment) is *lower* than the cost of equity
- Returning capital via buybacks at reasonable valuations is value-accretive to remaining shareholders
- The company has sufficient cash flow and liquidity to service that debt while maintaining operations

This is *basic finance*, not a Ponzi scheme. Every well-managed company does this. The alternative—hoarding cash and avoiding buybacks at reasonable valuations—would be *destroying* shareholder value.

But here's the critical point the aggressive analyst glosses over: **the company reported 17% EPS growth despite margin expansion of only 6 basis points because it grew earnings per share *denominator*, not because it inflated the numerator artificially.** That's the inverse of financial engineering—it's earnings quality improvement through scale and operating efficiency.
