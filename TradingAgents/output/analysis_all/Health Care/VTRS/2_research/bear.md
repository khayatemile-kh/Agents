## THE BEAR CASE: THREE CRITICAL FLAWS

### **FLAW #1: THE IMPAIRMENT ISN'T "BEHIND US"—IT'S A SYMPTOM OF DEEPER DYSFUNCTION**

The bull wants us to view the $2.94B goodwill writedown as "cleaning house" and "disciplined management." I see it differently: **it's evidence of catastrophic capital allocation and serial strategic failure.**

Let me walk through the reality:

#### **The Real Story Behind That Impairment:**

- Viatris took a $2.94B **non-cash writedown**. This doesn't mean the problem is solved; it means the problem is being **admitted after years of denial**.
- This impairment originated from Viatris's 2020 merger of Mylan and Upjohn. The combined entity was supposed to create synergies and drive growth.
- **Six years later, that merger has destroyed nearly $3B in value.** If that's not a red flag about management execution, I don't know what is.

#### **Why The Bull's EBITDA Defense Fails:**

The bull points to "$2.52B in normalized EBITDA" as proof the business is healthy. But here's what "normalized EBITDA" really means: **we're removing all the things that actually hurt profitability.**

Let me show you the math:

| Metric | 2025 | Implication |
|--------|------|------------|
| Operating Income (GAAP) | $253.5M | Real, reported operating profit |
| Normalized EBITDA | $2,520M | Operating profit PLUS depreciation/amortization |
| **Gap** | **$2,267M** | Nearly **9x the reported operating profit!** |

**This massive gap tells us something critical:** Viatris is carrying **enormous D&A charges**—depreciation and amortization from past acquisitions and capital investments. That's not just accounting; it reflects real cash invested in assets that are not generating proportional returns.

**Here's the bear's point:** When management says "normalized EBITDA," they're essentially saying: *"If you ignore all the costs we're actually paying, the business looks great."* That's not investing; that's fantasy.

The bull wants credit for disciplined cost management because EBITDA held up despite margin compression. But **EBITDA is not cash flow**—it's earnings before accounting adjustments. Looking at actual cash flow metrics (which we haven't fully unpacked yet) would reveal whether this company is actually generating cash or just burning it at a slightly slower rate.

#### **The Deeper Issue: Why This Impairment Matters for Future Earnings**

When a company takes a $2.94B goodwill write-down, it signals:
1. **Past management made terrible acquisition decisions**
2. **The market overpaid for those acquisitions initially**
3. **Those assets are now worth far less than paid**

**The forward implication:** If management was so wrong about the value of past acquisitions, why should we trust their projection for future growth? This isn't "cleaning house"—it's evidence of poor capital allocation decision-making that **will likely persist**.

### **FLAW #2: THE "INDUSTRY TAILWINDS" NARRATIVE IGNORES STRUCTURAL MARKET REALITY**

The bull claims biosimilars and specialty generics will save Viatris. Let me explain why this is wishful thinking:

#### **The Biosimilar Fantasy:**

The bull says: *"Biosimilars command 30-50% better margins than commodity generics, and Viatris has an active pipeline."*

**The reality is far grimmer:**

1. **Biosimilar Pricing Pressure is REAL**
   - Yes, biosimilars start at premium pricing vs. commodity generics
   - But **the biosimilar market is crowded and consolidating**
   - When multiple biosimilars enter the market (which happens within 3-5 years of launch), pricing collapses toward generic commodity levels
   - Example: Humira biosimilars launched in 2023-2024 with supposed 30% premiums; current pricing shows 40-60% discounts vs. original branded Humira
   - **The bull's margin expansion thesis is already being disproven in real time**

2. **Viatris Has No Competitive Moat in Biosimilars**
   - The biosimilar market is dominated by companies like Sandoz (which the bull acknowledges), Amphastar, and large generics players
   - Viatris isn't mentioned as a leader in biosimilars—just "positioned to benefit"
   - **Translation:** Viatris is a follower in the fastest-growing segment, not a leader. Followers capture margin-compressed deals, not premium pricing

3. **New Launch Track Record: Mediocre**
   - The research mentions "new product launches showing traction"—but no specifics, no data
   - Looking at Viatris's recent pipeline, most launches are incremental specialty generics in crowded therapeutic areas
   - **Launch success rate in generics is abysmal**—most new entrants fail to reach meaningful commercial scale

#### **The Specialty Generics Mirage:**

The bull claims: *"New product launches in specialty therapeutics command 3-5x price points of commodity generics."*

**This is misleading on multiple fronts:**

1. **Higher Price ≠ Higher Margin**
   - Yes, specialty generics cost more in absolute terms
   - **But they also require more R&D, regulatory approval, manufacturing complexity, and marketing spend**
   - Viatris's gross margin compression from 41.8% → 35.1% **proves that specialty generics aren't delivering the margin expansion the bull promises**

2. **The Shield Therapeutics Deal Actually Proves the Opposite**
   - The bull frames transferring US commercial control as "reallocating resources to better-margin opportunities"
   - **I see it differently: Viatris gave away a promising specialty product to focus on what it does best—commodity generics**
   - If Viatris believed it could capture superior margins on specialty products, why hand off the Shield product to another partner?
   - **This deal signals management's lack of confidence in its specialty generics execution**

3. **GLP-1 Downstream Opportunity: Vastly Overblown**
   - The bull mentions "generic diabetes and insulin products—a segment where VTRS has strength"
   - Insulin is a **mature commodity product** with razor-thin margins and brutal price competition
   - The bull seems to confuse investor enthusiasm about GLP-1 brand drugs with opportunity for generic manufacturers
   - **In reality, increased GLP-1 usage reduces insulin demand**, which is where Viatris has existing exposure

#### **The Real Headwind: Structural Generic Pricing Collapse**

Here's what the data actually shows:

| Year | Gross Margin | Implication |
|------|--------------|------------|
| 2023 | 41.8% | Strong baseline |
| 2024 | 38.2% | -360 bps decline |
| 2025 | 35.1% | -310 bps further decline |
| Trend | -680 bps in 2 years | **Accelerating compression** |

**The bull's explanation:** "Mix shift toward lower-margin commodity generics, but biosimilars/specialty will fix this."

**The bear's reality check:** Viatris has been **actively shifting toward specialty generics** for two years now, and margins have *accelerated downward*. If the strategy were working, we'd see margin stabilization. Instead, we're seeing **continued deterioration despite management executing the bull's exact playbook**.

**This proves the thesis wrong.**
