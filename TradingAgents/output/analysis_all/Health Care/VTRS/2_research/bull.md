## THE BULL CASE IN THREE PILLARS

### **PILLAR 1: IMPAIRMENT ≠ OPERATIONAL FAILURE**

The bear thesis will start here: *"Look at that -$3.51B net loss in 2025! The company is broken!"*

**Wrong.** Let me be clear about what happened:

The 2025 loss was driven by a **$2.94B goodwill impairment charge**—a non-cash, accounting writedown of past acquisition valuations. This is **painful, yes, but it's also cleaning house**. Here's why I see this as **constructive, not destructive**:

1. **It's Non-Recurring:** Non-cash impairments don't represent ongoing operational bleeding. Once you write down goodwill, you've reset the balance sheet. Future earnings won't be dragged down by amortization of inflated intangibles.

2. **Management is Being Realistic:** Rather than pretend past acquisitions were worth their original values, Viatris took the hit upfront. This signals **discipline and honesty**—exactly what you want to see after an M&A stumble.

3. **Normalized EBITDA Still Positive:** Even *after* the impairment, normalized EBITDA stood at **$2.52B in 2025**. That's real operational cash generation, and it validates that the underlying business is producing cash—not just accounting profits.

4. **The TTM Picture Improves:** Looking at TTM normalized EBITDA (stripping out the one-time hit), we're looking at a business generating **$2.5B+ in stable cash flow annually**. That's not a broken company; that's a company recovering from a bad acquisition decision.

**The Bull's Takeaway:** The impairment was the price of admission. Now that it's paid, we're investing in a clean balance sheet with real operational capability.
