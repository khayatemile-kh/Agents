Now let me dismantle the technical argument, because this is where the aggressive case does real damage to risk management.

The aggressive analyst sees the MACD histogram turning positive and calls it a "reversal." But a positive histogram doesn't mean the bull market is beginning. It means bearish momentum is *slowing*, not that bullish momentum is *accelerating*. There's a critical difference.

For a true bullish crossover, the MACD line needs to cross *above* the signal line. We're not there. A positive histogram just means they're getting closer. It's like saying "the recession is ending" because unemployment stopped rising. It's premature by at least two data points.

And here's the really important part: the volume analysis reveals what's actually happening. The aggressive analyst claims declining volume on recovery signals quiet institutional accumulation. This is backwards. Declining volume on recovery is a classic bear trap setup. It means:

1. The selling exhaustion at $15.92 was retail capitulation, not institutional validation

A real institutional accumulation phase shows up as volume expanding on recovery, not contracting. When smart money is buying, they're willing to lift the offer.
