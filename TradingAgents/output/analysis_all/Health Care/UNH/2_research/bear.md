- PA removal affects **30% of high-frequency services** (cardiology, labs, therapy, musculoskeletal)
- Historical data shows PA denials prevent approximately **5-10% of requested procedures** that are deemed unnecessary or duplicative

**Simple math:** 10M members × 30% of services × even conservative 3% utilization increase = **900,000 additional procedures annually**

The bull says these are "routine, high-volume services" where removal "improves efficiency." **Wrong.** These are the *exact* services where PA denial rates are highest because they're most prone to overutilization:

- **Cardiology:** Complex, expensive procedures; prone to unnecessary testing
- **Lab testing:** Chronic overordering is a well-documented clinical problem
- **Therapy:** One of the most abused categories; patients request it, insurers manage it
- **Musculoskeletal:** Physical therapy and imaging are among the most frequently denied categories

When you remove the gatekeeper for these services, you don't get "better outcomes"—you get *higher utilization* with *equivalent or worse clinical outcomes*. This is empirically documented in healthcare literature.
