**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Weighing both sides: the bull correctly highlights genuinely strong fundamentals — a Q3 beat on revenue and EPS, 320bps operating margin expansion, accelerating pharma (12%) and advanced therapeutics (30%) growth, a raised FY26 guide, and a healthy balance sheet (net debt/EBITDA 0.74x, ROE ~21%, improving liquidity). Those are real, verifiable positives that argue against a bearish fundamental view.

The bear's strongest points are on two fronts: (1) valuation math — the PEG of 1.24x only looks reasonable if you assume ~24% sustained growth, but company-wide growth is running 8-10%, with the 12% and 30% figures being narrow segment outliers rather than the consolidated growth rate; extrapolating segment-level hyper-growth onto the whole multiple is a real analytical error the bull is making. The P/S comparison (5.8x vs. peers' 3.2-4.8x) also suggests the stock is pricier than "in-line" framing implies. (2) Technicals — price broken below the 10 EMA, MACD bearish crossover, RSI collapsing from 80 to 53 in five days, and a distribution-pattern volume spike on a down day. Fast RSI unwinds after overbought extremes plus a MACD cross are legitimate short-term momentum warnings, especially for a stock that already ran 48% in twelve months on an easy comp base (Q2'25 EPS artificially depressed).

Neither side fully rebuts the other's core point: the bull doesn't adequately defend the PEG assumption or the P/S premium, and the bear doesn't dispute that the underlying business (margins, cash flow, deleveraging) is fundamentally healthy — the bear's case is about price/entry timing and multiple sustainability, not business quality deterioration.

Net: this isn't a "the story is broken" bear case — it's a "great company, but the stock has run too far, too fast, on a multiple that already prices in best-case segment extrapolation, with technicals confirming near-term exhaustion" case. That combination — quality business + stretched valuation + rolling-over momentum — argues against adding here and argues for trimming/right-sizing rather than either aggressive buying or full exit. The fundamentals aren't bad enough to sell outright, but the valuation/technical setup is unfavorable enough that this isn't a hold-and-do-nothing situation either.

**Strategic Actions**: Trim existing exposure into strength rather than adding at current levels. Specifically: (1) Reduce position size by roughly 20-30% to lock in gains from the recent run, using the break below the 10 EMA and bearish MACD crossover as the technical trigger. (2) Do not add on the pharma/advanced-therapeutics growth narrative until segment strength shows up in consolidated revenue growth above the current 8-10% run rate — treat 12%/30% segment figures as encouraging but not yet multiple-justifying. (3) Set a re-entry/add zone if the stock pulls back toward the 200-day trend support and RSI/MACD reset to neutral-to-oversold with volume confirming accumulation rather than distribution. (4) Retain a core position to stay exposed to the genuine balance-sheet and margin-expansion story (net debt/EBITDA 0.74x, ROE ~21%) in case guidance raise proves durable, but size it as a reduced, non-conviction holding rather than a full-weight position. (5) Watch next earnings print and forward guidance closely — a deceleration in pharma/advanced therapeutics growth or margin give-back would justify further trimming, while broad-based (not segment-narrow) acceleration toward double-digit consolidated growth would be the signal needed to upgrade back to Hold/Overweight.
