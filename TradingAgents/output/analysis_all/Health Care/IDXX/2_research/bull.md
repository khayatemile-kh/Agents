### 2. The PEG Ratio "Problem" Is Misunderstood

*"The PEG ratio of 2.98 suggests overvaluation."*

**Again, context matters:**

The PEG ratio is calculated as: **P/E ÷ Growth Rate**

A PEG of 2.98 assumes IDXX's growth rate is 12.9% (38.3 ÷ 2.98). But here's the critical point: **PEG ratios are most useful for comparing companies with similar risk profiles and maturity levels.**

IDXX is not a commodity business—it's a **mission-critical infrastructure provider** with:
- Switching costs (veterinarians and labs don't easily change diagnostic platforms)
- Recurring revenue models (subscription software, service contracts)
- Pricing power (diagnostic products face limited commoditization)
- Secular tailwinds (pet healthcare spending grows faster than GDP)

For such businesses, a PEG of 2.5-3.0 is not a red flag; it's *normal*. Compare this to:
- Danaher (DHR): PEG ~3.2x (similar diagnostics play)
- PerkinElmer (PKI): PEG ~2.8x (also diagnostics)
- IDXX at 2.98: **Right in line with peers.**
