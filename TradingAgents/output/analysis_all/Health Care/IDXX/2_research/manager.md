That undercuts the multiple the market is currently paying.

On top of the fundamental repricing risk, the technical picture reinforces caution rather than offering a clean entry point: price broke below the 50-day SMA, MACD is deeply negative and the histogram is expanding (accelerating downside momentum), and the low-volume grind lower is arguably more concerning than a panic flush because it suggests steady institutional distribution rather than a washout that's likely to snap back. The bull's counter that buybacks signal undervaluation is weaker than it looks — repurchases running near 100%+ of operating cash flow limit the balance sheet's flexibility and could themselves be inflating EPS growth in a way that isn't fully organic.

Where the bull is right: this is a high-quality, cash-generative business with genuine competitive moats (switching costs, recurring revenue, asset-light model) and pet healthcare remains a decent secular category. But the bear correctly notes the growth narrative here has matured from an accelerating secular story to more of a plateau (3-4% category growth now vs. pandemic-era double digits), and that quality alone doesn't justify paying 32x forward earnings if growth normalizes to high-single/low-double digits.

Net: the debate isn't balanced enough to warrant a plain Hold. The bear's growth-adjusted valuation argument, the discount-rate critique, and the technical deterioration collectively outweigh the bull's case, which relies on generous multiple justifications and an inflated FCF-yield assumption. This points to trimming exposure rather than adding or holding steady — not an outright Sell, because the underlying business quality, cash generation, and defensive characteristics of pet diagnostics are real and prevent this from being a high-conviction bear case for full exit.
