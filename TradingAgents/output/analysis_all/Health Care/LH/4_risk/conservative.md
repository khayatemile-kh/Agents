## **THE RSI COLLAPSE NARRATIVE IS TECHNICALLY MISLEADING**

The Aggressive Analyst makes a lot of hay out of RSI contracting from 77.17 to 59.62 in seven days, framing this as "capitulation of momentum" and a non-confirmation signal. Here's the problem: they're cherry-picking a technical indicator at a moment that supports their conclusion while ignoring the broader context that contradicts it.

First, let's acknowledge what actually happened. RSI at 77 on August 24 was overbought—that's textbook. The contraction to 59.62 is therefore *exactly what should happen after a stock rallies hard and consolidates*. That's not capitulation; that's normalization. The fact that price has held at $327.61—just 3.3% below the peak—while RSI has cooled to neutral territory is actually a *healthy consolidation pattern*, not a warning sign.

More importantly, the technical report itself notes that RSI at 59.62 is in "neutral territory (neither overbought at >70 nor oversold at <30)." The Aggressive Analyst is interpreting the *direction* of RSI (downward) as bearish, but they're ignoring that the *absolute level* of RSI (59) is neither extreme. This is a classic trap: a directional indicator moving toward the center is not the same as an indicator signaling a reversal. If anything, RSI settling into neutral territory after an overbought state suggests the market has **absorbed the correction and found equilibrium**—not that a larger drawdown is imminent.

The historical record supports this. When RSI normalizes from overbought to neutral during an uptrend, the stock typically *consolidates sideways or bounces higher within the band*, rather than rolling over. The Aggressive Analyst is betting on a scenario where the normalization *accelerates into oversold territory*, but that's a hypothesis, not a fact.

## **THE MACD HISTOGRAM SIGNAL IS BEING OVERSTATED**

Now let's address the MACD histogram argument, because this is where the Aggressive Analyst's reasoning really breaks down.

They claim the negative histogram at -1.18 is "the first warning bell in weeks" and evidence of "momentum collapse disguised as stability." But here's what they're conveniently overlooking: **a negative MACD histogram doesn't predict a reversal; it measures the current rate of momentum deceleration relative to trend.** The histogram turned negative because the MACD line (9.68) dipped slightly below the signal line (10.85)—a difference of 1.17 points. That's *noise*, not signal.

Look at the magnitude. The MACD line itself is still at 9.68, which is solidly positive and elevated relative to historical levels. A true momentum breakdown would show the MACD line itself crossing below zero or the signal line declining sharply. Instead, what we have is a minor technical crossover that occurs *constantly* in consolidation phases. The fact that the main MACD line remains positive and above zero tells you the underlying momentum is still positive; we're just in a brief pullback.

Moreover—and this is critical—the research report explicitly notes that the MACD histogram peaked at 11.77 on August 25 and has since declined to -1.18. But the report also states that "the MACD line (9.68) is still above its signal line (10.85) *but just barely*." Translation: we're on the edge of a crossover, not in the middle of one. In my experience, when technicians obsess over "imminent crossovers," they're usually wrong because the market often bounces *before* completing the reversal signal.

The Aggressive Analyst is treating a minor technical squiggle as if it's a crystal ball. It isn't. The histogram swinging negative briefly during consolidation is completely consistent with an uptrend taking a healthy pause, not a prelude to a 10-15% drawdown.
