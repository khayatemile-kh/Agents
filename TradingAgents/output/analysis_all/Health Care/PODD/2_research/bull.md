## Part 2: The Growth Deceleration is Cyclical, Not Terminal

### **Type 2 Retention Concerns Are Overblown (And Misunderstood)**

The guidance reset flagged "U.S. Type 2 retention concerns." The bear will leap on this: "The company can't retain customers—the moat is breaking!"

**But here's the context the market is ignoring:**

1. **GLP-1 adoption is a *temporary* headwind, not a permanent market loss.** Semaglutide and tirzepatide are managing *weight loss and glycemic control*, but they're *not* eliminating the need for insulin. In fact, many GLP-1 patients will *eventually* require insulin therapy as the disease progresses. PODD's job is to capture those patients when they graduate to insulin—with a *better* pump than competitors.

2. **"Retention" doesn't mean the market is saturating.** Type 2 diabetes is a growing population. The question isn't whether Type 2 patients need insulin—it's *when* they do and *which pump they choose*. A temporary slowdown in upgrade cycles is not the same as market death.

3. **PODD's OmniPod is still the *only* truly tubeless patch pump with seamless integration.** Medtronic has tubed pumps. Tandem has tubed pumps. The differentiation is real. The GLP-1 headwind is cyclical; PODD's innovation advantage is structural.

**Bull point #3: Type 2 retention pressures are a 2-3 quarter pause, not a secular decline.** As GLP-1 hype moderates and the "patient journey" clarifies, PODD's differentiated technology will reassert itself.
