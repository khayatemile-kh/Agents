## THE CORE DISAGREEMENT: WHAT DOES "PRICED IN" ACTUALLY MEAN?

Both analysts are arguing about whether bad news is "priced in," but they're using the term differently in ways that matter.

The aggressive analyst says bad news is priced in because the forward P/E is compressed. But forward P/E compression isn't the same as bad news being priced in. It's the market making a *bet* that earnings will grow despite the headwinds. If that bet is wrong—if the patent cliff accelerates, if zola-cel stays paused for 24 months, if competitive pressure intensifies—then the forward P/E actually *underestimates* the downside. The market hasn't priced in failure; it's priced in recovery.

The conservative analyst correctly notes this, but then overcorrects by treating any execution risk as a reason to stay on the sidelines. That's not calibrated risk management; that's risk aversion masquerading as prudence.
