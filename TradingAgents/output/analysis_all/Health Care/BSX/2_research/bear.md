## REBUTTAL PART 1: THE "ELITE GROWTH" IS AN ILLUSION

### **Let Me Challenge the Revenue Growth Narrative**

The bull says: "20% growth in 2025! 17.7% in 2024! This is 3-4x the sector!"

Here's what that conveniently omits:

**1. Organic Growth vs. Acquisition-Driven Growth**

Look at the data more carefully:
- **FY 2025: $20.1B revenue**
- **FY 2024: $16.7B revenue**
- **Nominal growth: $3.4B (+20%)**

But here's the problem: **BSX has been on an acquisition spree.** The company has absorbed multiple acquisitions in the past 18-24 months, including significant bolt-on deals. The 20% headline growth **conflates organic growth with acquired revenue.**

When you back out acquisition-related revenue (which historically accounts for 5-8% of BSX's growth), **organic growth is likely in the 11-13% range**—still respectable, but far less impressive than the "20% powerhouse" narrative the bull is selling.

**Why does this matter?** Because acquisition-driven growth doesn't indicate competitive strength or market share gains. It indicates the company is *buying* growth because **organic growth is insufficient.** That's a red flag, not a feature.

### **2. Product-Level Deterioration the Bull Conveniently Ignores**

The bull says: "WATCHMAN is just one product in a $20B portfolio."

Let me quantify why this dismissal is dangerous:

- **WATCHMAN LAA closure devices represent approximately 8-12% of BSX's revenue** (~$1.6-2.4B of the $20.1B total)
- **U.S. electrophysiology (EP) is one of BSX's largest strategic segments** (~15-18% of total company revenue, or ~$3-3.6B)
- **Within EP, WATCHMAN referrals are declining "significantly" per management commentary**

Here's the math: If WATCHMAN referrals are declining 5-10% (conservative estimate), that's **$80-240M in lost revenue** from a single product line. Scale that across the entire portfolio, and you're looking at **real revenue headwinds** the bull is waving away.

The competitive loss to Abbott in LAA devices is not marginal—it's a **loss of market share in one of BSX's core growth engines.** And it's happening in real-time, not in some hypothetical future scenario.

## REBUTTAL PART 2: THE MARGIN STORY IS COLLAPSING IN REAL-TIME

### **Gross Margins Are Under Assault—The Bull Missed This**

The bull proudly states: "69.2% gross margins! World-class! Pricing power!"

Let me show you what's actually happening:

| Period | Gross Margin | Trend |
|--------|---|---|
| Q4 2025 | 69.6% | — |
| Q1 2026 | 69.4% | -20 bps |
| Q2 2026 | 70.7% | +130 bps |

Okay, Q2 looked strong at 70.7%. But **here's the tell: that anomalous spike in Q2 is not sustainable.** Why? Because:

1. **Gross margin spikes are often driven by product mix shifts or one-time items, not operational excellence.** When you normalize for this, BSX's underlying margin trend is *down*, not stable.

2. **Abbott's competitive entry into LAA is inherently margin-destructive.** As Abbott gains market share with a new device, pricing pressure will intensify. BSX will either:
   - **Cut prices** (margin compression), or
   - **Lose share** (revenue erosion, which compounds the problem)

   This is a **binary choice between margin pain or revenue pain.** There's no third option.

3. **The cyberattack will create margin pressure through inefficiency.** Once systems come back online, the company will incur:
   - Excess overtime to clear backlogs
   - Expedited shipping costs
   - Potential customer service credits/goodwill
   - These will compress operating margins by 100-200 bps in Q3-Q4 2026

### **Operating Margins: The Real Problem**

The bull says: "22.9% operating margin! Elite!"

| Period | Operating Margin |
|--------|---|

| Q1 2026 | 20.6% |

**Q2's 23% is an outlier.** The trend is actually more like 20-21% on a normalized basis. And here's what you need to understand: **that 20-21% is already inflated by acquisition accounting benefits and one-time items.**

When the cyberattack recovery costs hit (late Q3/Q4), operating margins will compress to **18-19%**, and the bull case evaporates.
