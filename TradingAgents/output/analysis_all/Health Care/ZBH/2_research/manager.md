**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull case rests almost entirely on a forward P/E of ~11x driven by a projected EPS of $9.07 versus TTM EPS of $4.12 - a 120% growth assumption. The bear correctly flags that this is not supported by the actual quarterly trend: net income fell from $238M in Q1 2026 to $198M in Q2 2026, and EBITDA margins swung wildly from 30.6% to 20.2% in adjacent quarters, driven by recurring 'one-time' restructuring charges ($22M, $48M, $128M in consecutive quarters totaling ~$198M over three quarters). A forward multiple that assumes near-flawless execution and disappearance of restructuring drag is a forecast, not a fact, and the PEG/forward-P/E framing is only as good as that EPS number - which looks fragile given the quarter-over-quarter volatility.

On leverage, the bull's reframing to 2.94x net debt/EBITDA is reasonable in isolation, but the bear's stress-test is the more prudent read: if EBITDA reverts toward the weaker end of its recent range (Q4-style 20% margins), leverage jumps to 3.75-4.2x, which is a meaningfully different risk profile. Tangible book value is deeply negative (-$1.757B), meaning the balance sheet is entirely a function of goodwill/intangibles holding value - that's a real vulnerability if any impairment event occurs, even if it's not an immediate solvency threat given the 1.69x current ratio.

The cash flow picture, when netted for capex, debt service, and restructuring cash outflows, leaves a much thinner cushion (~$1.0-1.1B) than the headline $1.74B OCF suggests - and buybacks/dividends of $400-500M/year are being funded out of that thinner margin, which is a "maintain the stock price" profile more than a "growth machine" profile.

On macro, 8% recession probability is not negligible, and more importantly the bear rightly separates recession risk from procedure-volume/reimbursement-pressure risk - GPO/hospital cost containment and bundled reimbursement are structural headwinds regardless of the macro backdrop, and are not addressed by the bull's macro-stability argument.

The technical setup (bullish MA alignment, price above 200 SMA) is a real but shorter-term positive, and doesn't outweigh the fundamental earnings-quality concerns. The "zero news flow = quiet institutional accumulation" argument is speculative and unfalsifiable - it could just as easily mean the stock is off the radar because there's no compelling growth catalyst, which cuts both ways.

Net read: the bull thesis is a valuation call almost entirely dependent on hitting a punchy forward EPS number that has no support in the trailing quarterly cadence, while carrying real leverage sensitivity and thinning free cash flow after mandatory outflows. The bear's stress-tested numbers are more conservative and defensible, and the recurring "special charges" pattern over three consecutive quarters is a legitimate structural concern rather than noise. This tips the balance toward caution rather than a genuine 50/50 hold - I'm not going to full Sell because there are real cash-generation and balance-sheet-liquidity positives (1.69x current ratio, investment-grade-adjacent leverage today, real OCF), but the risk/reward is not attractive enough to add or hold at current weighting given the fragility of the growth assumption underpinning the bull's upside case.
