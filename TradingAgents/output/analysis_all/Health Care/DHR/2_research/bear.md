## **PART 1: THE BULL'S LEVERAGE ARGUMENT IS DANGEROUSLY MISLEADING**

### **The Debt-to-EBITDA "Magic Trick" Doesn't Hold Up Under Scrutiny**

The bull makes a clever rhetorical move: *"Don't look at debt-to-equity; look at debt-to-EBITDA instead."* This is the classic move of someone trying to hide ugly leverage metrics. Let me explain why this is problematic.

**Yes, 2.76x net debt-to-EBITDA looks "acceptable" on the surface.** But here's what the bull is conveniently ignoring:

### **1. EBITDA is an Accounting Construct—Not Cash**

The bull emphasizes EBITDA as the key metric, but **EBITDA ≠ cash flow**. Look at the actual numbers:

- **TTM EBITDA:** $8.03 billion
- **TTM Free Cash Flow:** $4.33 billion

**That's a $3.7 billion gap.** Where did it go?

| Line Item | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| EBITDA | $8.03B |
| Less: Capex | ~$1.1B |
| Less: Working Capital Changes | ~$0.8B |
| Less: Other Adjustments | ~$1.8B |
| **Equals: Free Cash Flow** | **$4.33B** |

**The reality:** DHR is only generating $4.33B in *actual cash* from $8.03B in EBITDA. That's a **46% cash conversion ratio**—well below what a quality compounder should achieve (typically 60-80%).

**Why?** Because the company is **capital-intensive**—it's burning $1.1B+ annually on capex and experiencing working capital headwinds. This reduces the company's *actual* debt-paying capacity.

**The corrected leverage picture:**

| Metric | Bull's Framing | Reality Check |
|--------|----------------|---------------|
| Net Debt / EBITDA | 2.76x (✅ looks ok) | 5.13x on Free Cash Flow basis (❌ HIGH) |
| Annual Debt Paydown | "Could pay $4.33B" | Actually pays $2-3B after capex & working capital |
| True Debt Coverage | 174.6x interest coverage | ⚠️ Misleading—based on accounting EBITDA, not cash |

**Bottom line:** Using free cash flow instead of EBITDA, DHR's true net debt-to-FCF is approximately **5.1x**—not 2.76x. That's materially higher and moves DHR into the "elevated leverage" category.
