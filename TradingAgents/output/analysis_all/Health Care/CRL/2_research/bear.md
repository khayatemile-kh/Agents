The bull argues: **"Recession risk is 8%, Fed won't cut rates, so pharma R&D budgets are secure."**

**1. Higher Interest Rates = Pharma Companies Invest Less, Not More**

Biotech and pharma companies are increasingly venture-backed or rely on capital markets for funding. **When rates stay higher-for-longer (89% probability per the macro analysis), capital becomes more expensive.** This constrains R&D budgets, not supports them.

The bull is confusing "no recession" with "bullish environment for pharma R&D." These are not the same thing. You can have a slow-growth economy with rising rates that starves biotech companies of capital without triggering a recession.

**2. Biotech IPO Markets Are Already Rolling Over**

- 2024 biotech IPOs were down ~40% from 2023
- 2025 biotech capital raises are declining

**This is CRL's customer base drying up in real-time.** Less biotech funding → fewer drug candidates → less need for safety testing.

I read it as **structural weakness in certain business lines.** When manufacturing support and other segments remain depressed while DSA is merely "improving," that's not a sign of broadening recovery—it's a sign the company is losing market share in those areas.
