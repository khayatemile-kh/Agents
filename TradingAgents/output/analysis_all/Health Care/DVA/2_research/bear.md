## CHALLENGE #2: THE PARTNERSHIP WITH HUMANA IS BEING OVERSOLD

The bull emphasizes the DVA-Humana partnership expansion as a "game-changer" that "the market hasn't priced in yet." Let me provide a reality check.

### What We Actually Know About the Partnership

- **10,000+ Medicare Advantage members** with chronic kidney disease stages 3B-5
- **Launched July 1, 2026**
- **Value-based care model** (risk-sharing vs. fee-for-service)

### Why This Is Likely Already Priced In (Or Will Disappoint)

**First, the size is modest.** DVA operates approximately 2,600 dialysis centers serving 200,000+ patients. Even if this Humana program grows to 50,000 members (a 5x expansion), it's still only 25% of DVA's base. Not transformational.

**Second, value-based care programs are operationally difficult.** The bull claims 100-300 basis points of margin improvement. But value-based care requires:
- Better care coordination (higher operational cost)
- Better clinical outcomes (financial penalties for failures)
- Patient compliance management (unpredictable)

Early-stage CKD patients (3B-5) are *less compliant* than dialysis patients—they often don't even know they have kidney disease. DVA is taking on clinical and financial risk that may not generate the promised margin lift.

**Third, Humana has incentive misalignment with DVA.** Humana profits when it *prevents* progression to dialysis. DVA profits when it *treats* dialysis patients. As value-based care scales, DVA could actually cannibalize its own high-margin dialysis business. The bull frames this as upside; I frame it as hidden risk.

**Fourth, the partnership is now 2 months old (as of Sept 1).** There's zero actual performance data. When Q3 2026 earnings come out, if enrollment is slow or margins don't materialize, this stock will gap down *again*.
