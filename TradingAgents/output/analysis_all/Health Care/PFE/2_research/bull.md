Let's start with what just happened. Pfizer resolved **over 6,000 product liability cases** related to Depo-Provera (intracranial meningioma litigation) with a confidential settlement and **no admission of liability**. This is not a trivial event.

**Why this matters:**

- **Removes tail risk:** For years, investors carried a discount on PFE shares for unresolved litigation risk. That overhang is now gone. Settlement finality removes uncertainty that depressed valuation multiples.
  
- **Precedent from peers:** When J&J, Merck, and other large pharma companies resolve major litigation, we typically see 5-10% valuation re-rating within 6 months as risk premiums compress. PFE's stock is already up ~13.8% in the past month—this is the market pricing that de-risking.

- **Balance sheet clarity:** With the settlement behind them, management can now provide guidance without litigation overhang clouding earnings predictability. This directly supports multiple expansion.

**Counter to the bear narrative you're about to hear:** "But the balance sheet is already weak!" 

**Not so.** Let me address this head-on with the data:

- **Net debt of $62.2B** is substantial, BUT the company generates **$25.4B in EBITDA** (39.9% margin), translating to a **net debt-to-EBITDA ratio of only 2.45x**—well within investment-grade territory and comparable to JNJ (2.2x) and Merck (2.8x).

- More importantly, **Pfizer is actively deleveraging.** The recent Q2 2026 impairments ($4.3B) are *non-cash charges*—they don't impact cash flow. They simply write down assets on the balance sheet to fair value, improving transparency and reducing future impairment risk.
