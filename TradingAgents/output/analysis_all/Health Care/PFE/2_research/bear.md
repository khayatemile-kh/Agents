## REBUTTAL 1: THE SETTLEMENT IS NOT A CATALYST—IT'S A RED HERRING

### The Depo-Provera "De-Risking" is Already Baked In

My colleague presents the Depo-Provera settlement as a major catalyst that removes tail risk and justifies multiple expansion. **This argument fundamentally misunderstands how pharma litigation is priced.**

**Here's what actually happened:**

- **The settlement terms are "confidential"** — This is critical. We don't know if Pfizer paid $500M or $5B. The lack of transparency suggests the settlement cost was material enough to warrant confidentiality agreements.

- **6,000+ cases resolved with "no admission of liability"** — This is standard legal language, but it obscures a reality: Pfizer settled *because continued litigation carried unacceptable risk*. The settlement itself is *evidence* of liability exposure, not exoneration.

- **Pfizer already took impairments** — The Q2 2026 data shows $4.3B in special charges and $8.8B in write-downs during 2025. These non-recurring charges already signal management's assessment of pipeline/asset quality problems. The settlement is just one of many simultaneous de-risking actions, not a standalone catalyst.

### Why the Bull's Peer Precedent Fails

The bull cites J&J and Merck litigation settlements as precedent for 5-10% re-ratings. **But this comparison is false:**

- **J&J's talc litigation (2021-2023):** J&J settled ~$9B in talc claims *after years of appeals*, but the stock was already down 20%+ from peaks. The re-rating that followed was partial recovery, not multiple expansion into new highs.

- **Merck's Vioxx settlement (2007):** Merck paid $4.85B for Vioxx. The stock did not experience a significant re-rating upward; instead, the market repriced expectations *downward* once the full financial impact became clear.

**The pattern:** When pharma companies settle large litigation, the market doesn't celebrate—it reassesses the company's risk management and pipeline quality. That's *already happening* with Pfizer, as evidenced by the 12-month underperformance vs. S&P 500 that both reports explicitly acknowledge.

**The market's verdict is clear: This settlement changes nothing about the fundamental deterioration at Pfizer.**

The CFO change is part of the problem, not the solution. New financial leadership doesn't fix clinical trial failures or competitive erosion—it just rebalances the deck chairs.

In pharma-speak, "louder recovery" typically means: *"We're talking up the stock because near-term catalysts are weak."*

Compare Pfizer's messaging to actual pharma turnarounds:

- **Indication:** Avelumab enfortumab vedotin for advanced urothelial carcinoma (bladder cancer)
