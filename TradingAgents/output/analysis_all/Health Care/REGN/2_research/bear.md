## I. THE VALUATION CASE: BURIED IN ASSUMPTIONS

### **The 44.8% Earnings Growth Projection is NOT a Given**

The bull hangs their entire thesis on REGN trading at 14.06x *forward* earnings with 44.8% projected growth. **This is where the analysis falls apart.**

Let me be direct: **forward earnings projections are consensus guesses, not guarantees**. And consensus is frequently wrong—especially in biotech, where pipeline execution risk is substantial. Let me show you why:

#### **The Earnings Growth Math Doesn't Work Without Heroic Assumptions**

- **Current TTM EPS**: $41.83
- **Projected Forward EPS**: $60.61
- **Implied growth**: 44.8% year-over-year

**For this to happen, REGN needs:**
1. Dupixent to maintain or grow revenue despite intensifying competition from newer IL-4 antagonists
2. Eylea to stabilize despite competitive pressure and the wet AMD market maturing
3. All new approvals (Pasatru, pipeline candidates) to immediately contribute materially to earnings
4. Zero pipeline setbacks, regulatory rejections, or pricing pressure
5. Operating margins to remain at 33%+ despite increasing competition and R&D spending

**This is not realistic.** Here's why:

#### **Dupixent Isn't the Growth Engine the Bull Claims**

The bull touts Dupixent as an "$8+ billion franchise that's still growing." Let's examine this claim:

**Reality Check on Dupixent Competition:**
- Multiple IL-4 antagonists are now approved or in late-stage trials (AbbVie's rinvoq has IL-4 antagonism components, new players entering)
- Dupixent faces market saturation in developed markets; atopic dermatitis penetration in the US is already substantial
- New indications (like EoE with ~500K patients) are *incremental* to the base, not transformative
- Pricing pressure is real: managed care is increasingly aggressive on biologics; net prices are flattening

**The inconvenient truth:** Dupixent growth is decelerating. The bull assumes it will accelerate or maintain, but the market data suggests otherwise. If Dupixent revenue growth drops from double-digits to mid-single digits (as is typical for maturing franchises), the entire earnings growth thesis collapses.

#### **Eylea is in Structural Decline**

The bull tries to spin Eylea as "evolving" with Eylea HD and extended-dosing regimens. **This is narrative theatre, not a growth strategy.** Here's the reality:

| Factor | Bull Narrative | Bear Reality |
|--------|------------------|--------------|
| **Market dynamics** | "Aging demographics = expanding market" | Wet AMD market is SHRINKING due to anti-VEGF competition; new therapies like Beovu, Vabysmo are taking share |
| **Pricing** | "Premium positioning maintained" | Net prices are declining 3-5% annually in ophthalmology; managed care won't tolerate price increases |
| **Eylea HD** | "Opens new patient segments" | Marginal improvement in efficacy doesn't justify premium pricing; still requires monthly injections |
| **Revenue trajectory** | Stabilizing or growing | Peak revenue likely passed (2021-2023); expect 2-4% annual declines |

**The research supports this**: ophthalmology biologics are facing unprecedented pricing pressure. Eylea isn't special—it's just older and losing ground to newer alternatives.

**Result: Eylea likely contributes LESS to earnings growth, not more.** The bull's forward earnings estimate relies on Eylea holding steady; I'd bet on 5-10% revenue decline over the next 12-18 months.

### **The PEG Ratio Argument is Cherry-Picked**

The bull emphasizes the PEG ratio of 1.17, claiming it signals undervaluation. **This is misleading.**

**The PEG ratio only matters if the growth assumptions are correct.** But as I've just outlined, the 44.8% earnings growth projection is unreliable. Let me recalculate:

**If we use MORE REALISTIC growth assumptions:**
- **Dupixent**: Mid-single digit growth (4-6% CAGR) instead of double-digit
- **Eylea**: Flat to declining (-2% to 0% annually)
- **Portfolio growth**: 8-12% overall instead of 44.8%

**Then the PEG ratio becomes:**
- PEG = 14.06x forward P/E ÷ 10% realistic growth = **1.41x**

**Suddenly, the stock looks fairly valued at best, overvalued at worst.** And this assumes no pipeline setbacks—which brings me to the next point.

## II. PIPELINE RISK: THE BULL GLOSSES OVER EXECUTION FAILURES

### **Mixed Melanoma Results = Canary in the Coal Mine**

The bull mentions "mixed melanoma trial results" in a single sentence, then moves on. **I won't.** This is critical.

**Here's what it means:**
- REGN attempted to compete in melanoma therapeutics—a competitive, well-established market
- The trial results were "mixed"—code for "didn't meet expectations"
- This signals **execution risk across the pipeline**

**Why melanoma matters:**
1. **It's not a niche market**: Melanoma therapeutics are worth billions; this should have been a material revenue driver
2. **Competition is intense**: Merck, Bristol Myers Squibb, and others dominate; REGN couldn't crack it
3. **It reveals R&D productivity issues**: If REGN can't win in a market with clear unmet needs, how confident should we be in pipeline candidates?

**The bull wants you to focus on Pasatru's rare-disease win; I want you to ask: "Why did REGN fail in melanoma, and how many other pipeline programs are facing similar execution challenges?"**

### **Pasatru Revenue is Negligible**

Let's be honest about FOP:
- **Ultra-rare disease**: ~1 in 25,000 births = ~130,000 global prevalence
- **Adult population with FOP**: Maybe 30,000-50,000 globally (many don't reach adulthood; many are diagnosed late)
- **Peak sales potential**: $200-300 million globally, optimistically
- **Timeline to peak**: 3-5 years

**In context of REGN's $15.53B revenue base, Pasatru is rounding error.** It's not moving the needle on earnings—period.

The bull admits this ("revenue impact is limited") but then minimizes it by calling Pasatru "validation of execution." **But validation of what?** Regeneron's ability to run a Phase 3 trial in a 50,000-patient market? That's not impressive; that's table stakes.

**Real question: How many Pasatrus does REGN need to hit the 44.8% earnings growth target? Answer: Dozens. And they don't have them.**

### **Regulatory and Pipeline Headwinds Are Accelerating**

Beyond melanoma, REGN faces broader pipeline risks:

1. **FDA scrutiny on rare disease indications**: Regulators are increasingly demanding real-world evidence for ultra-rare approvals; Pasatru is an outlier in an era of stricter standards
2. **Competitive pipeline density**: Biologics are becoming commoditized; each new entrant faces price compression
3. **R&D productivity decline**: Historically, pharma companies see declining R&D productivity over time; REGN is no exception
4. **Patent expirations**: While not imminent, some key patents are approaching expiration; the company must replace this revenue through pipeline success

**The bull assumes flawless execution. History suggests otherwise.**
