## 1. THE ACQUISITION ISN'T A LIABILITY; IT'S A GROWTH ACCELERATOR

**The Bear Will Say:** "Look at that debt-to-equity ratio of 36x! The company is drowning in leverage from an acquisition. That's reckless."

- WAT just executed a **major strategic acquisition** (evidenced by the $24.5B in total assets as of Q1 2026, up from $5.08B at year-end 2025—that's an **$19.4 billion increase in a single quarter**)
- This acquisition was likely financed with $3.53 billion in new debt issuance (Q1 2026), which is **standard practice** for transformative M&A
- The debt-to-equity ratio will **normalize significantly** as:
  1. The acquired company's EBITDA is consolidated
  2. Interest coverage improves from combined cash flows
  3. The company deleverages over the next 12-24 months

**Let me put this in perspective:** Major pharmaceutical and diagnostics companies routinely operate at 2-4x net debt-to-EBITDA following acquisitions. If we calculate WAT's pro-forma leverage:
- **Net Debt:** $4.75 billion
- **TTM EBITDA:** $1.45 billion (but this is pre-acquisition; the combined entity will be significantly higher)
- **Current Net Debt/EBITDA:** ~3.3x (this will improve as combined EBITDA is realized)

This is **not reckless**; it's a disciplined use of leverage to acquire accretive, cash-generative assets in a high-margin industry.

**Normalized operating performance is solid.** Look at the gross margin of 56.4%—that's a **clear signal that the underlying business is profitable and pricing power remains intact.**
