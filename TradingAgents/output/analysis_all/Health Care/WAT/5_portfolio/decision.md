Applying the lesson from the NVDA precedent (avoid escalating prospective risks to full downgrade before one confirming earnings cycle), this situation differs materially: unlike NVDA's speculative competitive-substitution risk, WAT's red flags (negative operating income, cash flow collapse, receivables blowout) are already reported, hard financial facts from the most recent quarter, not hypothetical future risks — so demanding confirmation before adding is prudent, but the fragility is severe enough to warrant trimming now rather than simply holding at full size.
