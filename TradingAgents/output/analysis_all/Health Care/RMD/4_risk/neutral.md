**If RMD is generating $939 million in annual free cash flow on a $33.3 billion market cap (2.8% FCF yield), and if the company genuinely believes its growth story is intact and its cloud transition is structural, why isn't management deploying that capital more aggressively?**

The fundamentals report doesn't detail share buybacks, dividend policy, or M&A activity. But I'm going to make an educated inference: if management were highly confident in forward growth prospects, we'd expect to see either aggressive capital returns to shareholders (buybacks signaling capital discipline and conviction) or strategic M&A to accelerate the cloud transition narrative.

The fact that the aggressive case doesn't address capital allocation, and the conservative case only hints at it as a "flag," suggests this is a blind spot for both. And blind spots in equity analysis are where real losses hide.

If management is hoarding cash, that could mean one of three things:
1. They're genuinely uncertain about forward prospects (contradicting the aggressive case)
2. They're preparing for a major acquisition (which could be transformational or value-destructive)
3. They're simply running the company conservatively and letting cash accumulate (which reflects weak conviction at the top)

None of those scenarios suggest immediate upside from current valuation levels.
