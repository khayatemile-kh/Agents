## THE VALUATION ARGUMENT ASSUMES THE DEAL CLOSES—A DANGEROUS ASSUMPTION

Now let's dissect the valuation claim. The aggressive analyst argues FOX trades at 1.48x sales and 10-11x earnings, therefore it's "absurdly cheap" and warrants M&A upside of 25-40%. This is where the analytical sleight-of-hand really concerns me.

**First, those multiples exist for a reason.** Media companies trade at lower multiples than tech or healthcare because they face structural headwinds. FOX isn't trading at 10x earnings because the market has stupidly undervalued it. It's trading at 10x earnings because:

1. **Cord-cutting is real and persistent.** Linear TV is in secular decline. Every year, fewer households subscribe to cable. That's not a temporary cycle; it's a structural shift. The market is discounting future cash flow erosion. Paying 10-11x earnings for a media company reflects the risk that earnings are heading lower, not higher.

2. **Streaming competition is intensifying, not easing.** Disney, Netflix, Amazon, Apple—they're all fighting for eyeballs. FOX's content has to compete with unlimited options. That margin compression isn't going away. The aggressive analyst assumes cost synergies of 200-300 basis points from a re-merger. Sure, you can cut redundant costs. But you *can't* stop cord-cutting or reverse consumer preferences. You're trimming costs on a shrinking revenue base.

3. **Unusual items and cash flow volatility are not one-time events.** The fundamentals report flags FY2026 had -$912M in unusual items. But this isn't the first year. Look at FY2024: -$90M in unusual items. FY2025: +$99M. This is recurring noise in the earnings stream. That's a sign of a company managing portfolio stress, asset write-downs, and restructuring. **This is the operational reality of legacy media.** It's not going to be solved by eliminating back-office redundancy with News Corp.

The valuation isn't cheap; it's appropriately cautious. The market has priced in the risks. And the aggressive analyst is asking us to *ignore* those risks in exchange for a rumored deal that has no timeline, no regulatory clarity, and no confirmation from management.
