## I. THE CORE BULL THESIS: THREE PILLARS

### **PILLAR 1: UNDERVALUED FUNDAMENTALS WITH HIDDEN EARNING POWER**

Let's start with the basics. FOX trades at **$59.03** with a market cap of **$25.3 billion** and trailing-twelve-month (TTM) revenue of **$17.13 billion**. That gives us a Price-to-Sales ratio of just **1.48x**—remarkably cheap for a profitable, dividend-paying communications company.

Here's what's critical: **reported earnings are artificially depressed by one-time charges.** 

FY2026 reported net income was $1.685 billion, down 25.5% from FY2025's $2.263 billion. On the surface, that looks ugly. But dig deeper, and you see **-$912 million in unusual items**, primarily a -$761M gain on sale of securities. **Strip those out, and normalized operating earnings power is approximately $2.4 billion—easily on par with historical levels.**

Translation: FOX is trading at a **10.5x normalized earnings multiple**—this is *value territory* for a business generating consistent mid-$2B+ annual net income with a strong balance sheet.

For comparison:
- The S&P 500 trades at ~22x earnings
- Entertainment/streaming peers like Disney trade at 30x+
- FOX's multiple is nearly 50% cheaper than the market while maintaining superior cash generation

**The bull case:** The market is pricing FOX as a distressed media company, not recognizing that normalized earnings power and dividend support a significantly higher valuation.

### **PILLAR 2: STRATEGIC M&A OPTIONALITY—A REAL CATALYST**

Now, here's where it gets interesting. **Reuters reported in late August 2026 that Fox and News Corp. (NWSA) could revive their re-merger.** This isn't idle speculation; it's material strategic optionality that the market has barely priced in.

Why does this matter?

**The Numbers on Consolidation:**
- Combined FOX + NWSA would create a ~$50B+ media powerhouse with massive cost synergies
- Duplicate corporate functions (IT, legal, finance, back-office) could yield **$500M-$1B in annual cost savings**
- Combined scale would improve negotiating power with advertisers and content distributors
- A consolidated entity would have stronger leverage in the increasingly competitive streaming landscape

The 2019 separation that created FOX occurred when Rupert Murdoch and his family decided to split News Corp's publishing/Fox assets into two entities. That decision was driven by tax and strategic considerations at the time. **But market conditions have changed dramatically.** Streaming competition, cord-cutting acceleration, and M&A activity across the sector have made scale the competitive imperative.

**If a re-merger is consummated:**
- Current FOX shareholders would likely receive a premium to current trading levels (historically 15-25% for media consolidation deals)
- Alternatively, the stock could see significant uplift on deal announcement *before* any premium is paid
- Conservative scenarios suggest re-merger discussions could add **$10-$15 per share (17-25%)** in value over 12-18 months

**The bull case:** This isn't priced in yet. Reuters-sourced deal speculation typically reflects informed institutional positioning. The market is underweighting this catalyst.

### **PILLAR 3: CASH GENERATION, DIVIDENDS, AND CAPITAL ALLOCATION EXCELLENCE**

Let's talk cash, because that's what matters to shareholders.

**FOX's Cash Generation Profile (TTM):**
- **Operating Cash Flow:** $3.2 billion
- **Free Cash Flow:** ~$2.8 billion (after capex)
- **EBITDA:** $3.88 billion (22.7% EBITDA margin)

This is a **cash cow.** And management is deploying that cash brilliantly for shareholders:

1. **Dividend:** FOX pays a dividend (~2.2% yield at current price), currently around $1.30/share annually. This is **sustainable and likely to grow** given normalized earnings power.

2. **Share Buybacks:** Over the past three years, the company has reduced share count from ~490M to ~420M shares—a **14% reduction.** This buyback program is accretive to EPS and demonstrates management's confidence in valuation.

3. **Debt Management:** Total debt has been stable at $7.4-7.6B, with a net debt-to-EBITDA ratio of 0.78x—**very manageable.** Interest coverage of 6.65x is healthy. The company has refinanced its debt appropriately and isn't overleveraged.

**Capital Allocation Math:**
- $2.8B annual free cash flow supports:
  - $0.5B in dividends
  - $0.8-1.0B in buybacks
  - $0.5B in debt reduction
  - *Still leaves room for strategic M&A or investment*

**The bull case:** FOX is returning capital to shareholders aggressively while maintaining financial flexibility. This is a value investor's dream—you get current yield, EPS accretion from buybacks, and optionality.
