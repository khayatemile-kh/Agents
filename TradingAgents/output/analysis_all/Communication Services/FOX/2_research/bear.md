## I. THE BULL'S "NORMALIZED EARNINGS" TRICK—WHY IT DOESN'T WORK

Let me start by addressing the elephant in the room: **the bull's entire valuation thesis rests on stripping out $912 million in "unusual items" to arrive at $2.4 billion in normalized earnings.**

This is where the argument falls apart.

### **Those "Unusual Items" Aren't Actually Unusual**

The bull frames these as one-time charges that should be ignored. But here's the uncomfortable truth: **for FOX, these "unusual" charges have become routine.**

Let's look at the actual historical pattern:
- **FY2026:** -$912M in unusual items
- **FY2025:** +$99M 
- **FY2024:** -$90M
- **Pattern:** Significant one-time charges appearing **every single year** across a 3-year window

When you have write-downs, impairments, and asset sales occurring *consistently* rather than as true one-time events, that's not accounting noise—**that's a sign of a deteriorating business shedding value.** The $761M gain on sale of securities in FY2026 is particularly telling: FOX had to *sell off assets* just to generate a gain. That's not a sign of a thriving business; it's a sign of financial engineering.

### **The "Normalized Earnings" Number Is Inflated**

The bull claims normalized net income is $2.4B, but let's pressure-test this:

**Actual TTM Net Income (as reported):** $1.685B  
**Claimed Normalized Earnings:** $2.4B  
**The Math:** The bull is adding back $912M in unusual items, but **only $761M of that was a gain on asset sales—a non-recurring item that genuinely should be excluded.** The remaining $151M in special charges and impairments are actually *operational failures* that signal underlying business deterioration.

**More importantly:** If we're normalizing earnings, we should also normalize for the fact that FOX is in structural decline. The company is hemorrhaging linear TV viewers, facing accelerating cord-cutting, and its streaming assets are generating losses or minimal returns. **The "normalized" number assumes these trends are cyclical, not structural.** They're not.

### **Here's the Real Problem with the 10.5x Multiple Argument**

The bull says 10.5x earnings is "cheap" compared to the S&P 500 at 22x and Disney at 30x. 

**But that comparison is fundamentally dishonest.** Here's why:

1. **Disney trades at 30x because it owns dominant streaming platforms (Disney+, ESPN+) with 150M+ subscribers, growing content ecosystems, and theme park cash flows.** Despite streaming challenges, Disney has *optionality*—its streaming can eventually reach profitability.

2. **FOX trades at 10.5x because the market is correctly pricing in that the company is in a structurally declining business with limited growth catalysts.** The discount isn't because the market is irrational; it's because FOX is a value *trap*.

3. **The valuation multiple compresses when earnings are expected to decline.** And FOX's earnings trajectory is clearly downward:
   - **FY2025 to FY2026 net income:** Down 25.5% ($2.263B to $1.685B)
   - **TTM net income:** Only $1.685B, well below historical normalized levels
   - **The trend is deteriorating, not normalizing**

When a company is trading at 10.5x earnings *and* earnings are *falling*, you're not looking at a discount—**you're looking at a value trap waiting to spring.**

## II. THE M&A FANTASY—PRICED IN MORE THAN THE BULL ADMITS

Now let's talk about the crown jewel of the bull's thesis: the re-merger with News Corp.

I want to be very clear: **I'm not saying a re-merger can't happen. I'm saying the bull is wildly overestimating its probability and its valuation impact.**

### **The Reuters Report Was Speculative, Not Confirmatory**

The bull cites a Reuters report saying re-merger discussions "could still be revived." Let me parse that language carefully:

- "Could be revived" = speculative, not confirmed
- No official company statement
- No indication of deal probability or timeline
- **Reuters is reporting on rumors, not facts**

Compare this to actual M&A processes: When deals are being actively discussed, you get:
- Formal statements from both parties
- SEC filings (if material)
- Institutional investor calls
- Timeline indications

**We have none of that.** What we have is a single rumor headline. And yet the bull is building a multi-billion-dollar valuation case on speculation.

### **The Re-Merger Makes Strategic Sense for News Corp.—Not Necessarily for FOX Shareholders**

Here's the critical insight the bull misses: **even if a re-merger happens, it may not be accretive to FOX shareholders.**

Why? Because News Corp. (NWSA) has fundamentally different assets:
- **NWSA** = Publishing (Wall Street Journal, Financial Times, Australian newspapers), real estate, book publishing
- **FOX** = Linear TV, cable networks, streaming losses

**Publishing assets have actually held up better than linear TV during media disruption.** If a re-merger happens at a negotiated price, NWSA shareholders could easily demand terms that are *dilutive* to FOX shareholders. The bull assumes FOX gets a "15-25% premium," but that's pure speculation. In reality:

1. **The 2019 separation was *chosen* by the Murdoch family because they wanted to separate a declining business (FOX linear TV) from a better-positioned one (News Corp publishing).** Why would they reverse that now unless FOX offers them a sweetheart deal?

2. **If anything, FOX should be paying a *premium* to News Corp. to convince them to reunify.** That means FOX shareholders get *diluted*, not enriched.

3. **The real scenario:** A re-merger might get announced at an *equal or slightly negative* price for FOX shareholders, touted as "strategic synergies." The stock might rally 10-15% on the announcement (deal premium), but then fade as the market prices in integration risk and dilution.

### **The Market is Already Pricing in Re-Merger Optionality**

Here's what the bull refuses to acknowledge: **The August rally from $52.62 to $62.69 wasn't justified by earnings fundamentals—it was driven by re-merger speculation.** That rally already priced in the re-merger possibility. The market went from ignoring FOX to front-running deal rumors.

Now we're at $59.03, having pulled back 6% from the speculative high. **The re-merger catalyst is already mostly priced in.** If deal probability declines or talks stall, the stock has significantly downside—and there's *no fundamental support* at lower levels.
