## **The Hedge Cost Argument Misses the Real Insurance Purpose**

The aggressive case dismisses protective puts and collars as "paying for insurance on a house that isn't on fire." That's a fundamental misunderstanding of what hedges are for in a risk-managed portfolio.

Hedges are not purchased because we expect the house to burn tomorrow. They're purchased because we acknowledge that *tail risk exists* and that the cost of the hedge is materially lower than the cost of unexpected losses. When a stock has deteriorating momentum, declining sentiment flow, and a major binary event 8 weeks away, the probability of a -15% move in the downside scenario is meaningfully elevated. 

A protective put at the $170 level costs maybe 2-3% of position value in option premium. If LYV declines to $150 on disappointing guidance, that hedge is worth 11-12% upside (the difference between unhedged loss of -15% and hedged loss of -3%). That's a *favorable risk-reward trade*, not a waste of capital.

What the aggressive case frames as "theta decay capping upside" is actually "paying a small fee to eliminate tail risk." If LYV rallies to $195, yes, the hedge costs us 2-3% of upside. But we don't buy hedges on the assumption we'll be right about the upside. We buy them because we acknowledge we might be *wrong*.
