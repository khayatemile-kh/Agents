## **PILLAR 1: THE DEAL IS NOT "SUBSTANTIALLY DE-RISKED"—IT'S FUNDAMENTALLY FRAGILE**

The bull frames 68 international approvals and CMA clearance as "structural validation." This is a dangerous misreading of how antitrust actually works.

### **The International Approvals are Red Herrings**

Yes, the UK CMA cleared the deal. But here's what the bull is ignoring: **the CMA operates in a fundamentally different legal framework than U.S. state attorneys general.**

**The Critical Distinction:**
- The CMA reviews deals under a *national competitive standard*. It asks: "Does this harm UK consumers and competition in the UK market?"
- U.S. state AGs operate under a *federalism framework* where each state has independent enforcement authority. California, New York, Illinois, and Texas are not bound by federal approval or international consensus. They can block the deal unilaterally through injunctive relief in their courts.

**This is not theoretical—it's the operating reality of U.S. antitrust law.** When the bull says "68 countries approved," he's citing regulatory frameworks that have zero binding effect on Rob Bonta's California AG office. That's a category error.

Here's why that number is potentially inflated and increasingly unreliable:

**The 85% probability conflates "deal closes in some form" with "deal closes on terms beneficial to shareholders." These are very different propositions.**
