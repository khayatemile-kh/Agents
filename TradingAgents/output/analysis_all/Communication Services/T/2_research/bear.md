## **1. THE MARGIN EXPANSION STORY IS ALREADY PRICING IN PEAK GROWTH**

The bull is celebrating a 17.4% net margin as if this is the beginning of an uptrend. **I'd argue it's the *peak* of a cycle that's already reflected in the stock price.**

Here's the critical problem: **margin expansion from 5G and fiber is fundamentally a *one-time* rerating event, not a repeatable driver.**

Consider the math:
- **5G deployment is largely complete** (as of 2026). The capex-heavy, margin-dilutive build phase was 2020-2024. We're now in the harvest phase—meaning incremental margin contribution is *slowing*, not accelerating.
- **Fiber buildout has geographic limits.** AT&T can't achieve fiber saturation indefinitely. The marginal cost of reaching the last 20% of unserved areas is exponentially higher than the first 80%. This is a classic S-curve adoption dynamic.
- **Bundling efficiency gains are already realized.** If service bundling was going to materially improve margins, it would have started showing up in 2024-2025 data. The fact that we're celebrating 17.4% margins *now* suggests this is where it peaks, not where it accelerates.

**The bull is buying a stock on the back of a margin improvement that has likely *already topped out*.** That's textbook "buy high."

What happens when margins plateau or compress? The entire narrative inverts. And the market will eventually realize this.
