The bull wants to sell me on "pristine moving average alignment" and "expanding MACD." I see something different: **a late-stage rally running on fumes, approaching inevitable reversal signals**.

### **RSI at 62 is Deceptively High for an Uptrend That's Only 15 Trading Days Old**

Let me challenge the bull's framing here. Yes, RSI 62 is technically in the "50-70 strong uptrend zone." But here's what the bull is conveniently omitting:

- **RSI at 62 after only 15 days of rallying = aggressive move**
- RSI typically takes weeks or months to climb this high in healthy uptrends
- **The speed of the climb (from 42.64 on 08/10 to 62.32 today) is a red flag**, not validation
- This velocity suggests **momentum-chasing retail buying**, not institutional accumulation

**The bull says:** *"RSI didn't exceed 70, so there's room to run."*

**My counter:** RSI at 62 after such a sharp recovery *is* excessive. In healthy, multi-month uptrends, RSI climbs gradually and oscillates 40-60. When it shoots to 62 in 3 weeks, it's often followed by:
1. Mean reversion pullbacks (RSI drops 10-15 points)
2. Consolidation periods where price stalls
3. **Potential reversal if momentum breaks**

The bull is treating "not yet at 70" as bullish. I see it as "already stretched, approaching mechanical resistance."

The bull wants to tell me this is "exactly how institutional accumulation works." I call it **distribution disguised as accumulation**. Institutions dump on momentum, then retail chases in on FOMO as volume fades.

The bull treats the alignment of price > 10 EMA > 50 SMA > 200 SMA as proof of institutional buying. This is misleading.

**Moving average alignment just means:** The stock trended up. It's a *lagging indicator*, not proof of ongoing support.

Here's what actually matters:

- **The 50 SMA at $38.64** — yes, it "held" during the August pullback. But it was tested *once*, when MTCH briefly hit $36.73. That's not a battle-tested support level; that's a single touch before buyers returned. A true support level is tested multiple times and holds repeatedly.
  
- **Distance from 200 SMA (+22.4%)** — the bull calls this "strong, not extreme." I call it a **setup for mean reversion**. When stocks are 22% above their 200-day average after a 3-week rally, they're *vulnerable*. The historical data shows stocks in this position often pull back 10-15% within the next 2-4 weeks.

**The real test?** If the 50 SMA holds at $38.64 on the *next* correction, then I'll concede the institutional support thesis. Until then, it's just a number on a chart.
