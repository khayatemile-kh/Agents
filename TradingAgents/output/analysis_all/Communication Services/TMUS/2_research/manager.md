**Recommendation**: Overweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make reasonable points, but when I weigh the substance, the bull case rests on firmer, more verifiable ground while the bear case leans heavily on speculative extrapolation and unverified assumptions. Let's break down what actually holds up.

On valuation: the bull's forward PE of 12.97x against a PEG of 0.64 is a real, current market data point. The bear's rebuttal doesn't dispute the current multiple—it argues the forward EPS growth estimate won't materialize because revenue growth is 'decelerating.' But the bear's own math is shaky: they annualize a single Q2 quarter and compare it to full-year 2025 revenue to claim deceleration from 8.4% to 3.2%, which is an apples-to-oranges comparison (quarterly seasonality vs. full-year comps) and is exactly the kind of cherry-picking they accuse the bull of. That undermines the credibility of their central valuation attack.

On profitability: the bull points to a clear multi-year trend of operating margin expansion (10.2% to 21-25%) with actual cash flow to back it up. The bear says this is a "mirage" and one-time cost cutting, but offers no hard evidence that these costs are non-recurring — this is asserted, not demonstrated. The bear does raise a fair, structural point worth carrying forward: cost-cutting-driven margin gains are inherently less durable than revenue-driven gains, and competitive ARPU pressure from AT&T/Verizon is a real risk to monitor. That's a legitimate flag on quality of earnings, but it's not sufficient to override the actual balance sheet and cash flow strength being demonstrated today.

On cash flow and debt: the bull's numbers ($16.2B TTM FCF, ~8% FCF yield, debt paydown alongside shareholder returns) are concrete and, taken at face value, indicate a company with real financial flexibility, not distress. The bear's rebuttal on this point was cut off before delivering a substantive counter-argument, so there's no strong bear evidence to weigh against it.

On the satellite/Starlink narrative, the bull's argument that this is a complement rather than existential threat, backed by BofA's infrastructure math, is reasonably persuasive and wasn't directly challenged by the bear.

Net-net: the bull thesis is grounded in verifiable, multi-year trends in margins, cash generation, and shareholder returns, plus a reasonable valuation discount (PEG 0.64) that leaves room for error even if some of the forward growth is optimistic. The bear raises legitimate quality-of-earnings and competitive-intensity concerns that argue against going all-in aggressively, but doesn't present hard evidence disproving the bull's core financial data. Given the imbalance in evidentiary weight — real trailing financials favoring bulls vs. speculative deceleration/one-time-item claims from bears — I'm leaning bullish, but with some tempering given the bear's fair points on sustainability of margin gains and competitive ARPU pressure. That argues for Overweight rather than a full-conviction Buy: constructive, add-on-strength stance, while keeping position sizing disciplined pending confirmation of margin durability in Q3/Q4 2026 results.
