**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make credible points, but the bear's argument is more grounded in the hard sequential data and is harder to dismiss. The bull leans heavily on TTM/YoY figures (28% growth, 81% gross margin, fortress balance sheet) which are real and impressive in absolute terms, but the bear's quarter-over-quarter breakdown exposes real deterioration: Q1 2026 revenue fell 6% sequentially, Q2's 28% YoY comp is flattered by an unusually weak year-ago quarter, and operating margin cratered ~970bps sequentially (40.6%→30.9%) as R&D and SG&A spiked (SG&A +64% QoQ is a genuine red flag suggesting cost discipline is slipping, not just 'investment'). That kind of margin volatility in a single quarter is not typical of a business executing cleanly, and it undercuts the bull's 'elite, stable margin' narrative.

On capex, the bull's 'moat' framing is undermined by the bear's comparative capex-intensity table showing META spending far more of its market cap on PPE than Google, Microsoft, or Apple — and Llama being open-source cuts against the moat argument rather than for it, since it's freely replicable. The $80B in construction-in-progress is capital tied up generating no current return, and rising accumulated depreciation will be a growing earnings headwind once these assets are placed in service. The bull didn't really rebut this point substantively.

On technicals, the bull's 'capitulation and recovery' story is plausible but speculative pattern-reading (MACD histogram turning positive, RSI recovering from oversold) — it's a weak counterweight to concrete fundamental deceleration and margin compression. The bear's technical rebuttal (further declines could just be a dead-cat bounce, not confirmed reversal) is also speculative, but the more concerning issue for a manager weighing this is that price already dropped 15% off highs coincident with the quarter that showed margin compression — that's a fundamentally justified repricing, not just technical noise.

Where I side with the bull: valuation is not crazy (~22x P/E for a business with 30% margins and still double-digit-plus growth), the balance sheet is genuinely strong (low net debt/EBITDA, huge cash pile), and macro conditions (low recession odds, steady rates) aren't hostile. This argues against an outright Sell.

Netting it out: the bear's quarter-over-quarter evidence of decelerating/lumpy growth and a sharp, unexplained margin contraction alongside surging SG&A is the more decision-relevant signal for the next 1-2 quarters, and it directly challenges the bull's core 'unstoppable growth machine' framing without being fully rebutted. This isn't a broken business — balance sheet strength and profitability are still very real — but the risk/reward here is not compelling enough for a Buy or Overweight until margin trends stabilize and the market gets more clarity on whether Q1's sequential revenue decline and Q2's SG&A spike were one-off items or the start of a trend. I'm calling this a cautious/underweight-leaning Hold: don't chase the recovery rally aggressively, but the balance sheet and valuation don't yet justify an outright Sell either. Given the strength of the bear's margin/growth deceleration case relative to the more speculative bull technical-recovery narrative, I'm leaning Underweight rather than a pure Hold.
