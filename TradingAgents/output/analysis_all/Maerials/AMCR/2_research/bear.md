My colleague claims:

- Interest coverage of 4.5x is "comfortable"
- FCF after dividend of $850M annually can "de-risk" the balance sheet

Let me demolish each of these claims with actual data:

**First, the leverage ratio lie:**

Yes, 2.2x Net Debt/EBITDA sounds manageable. But here's what the bull isn't telling you:

1. **That 2.2x calculation assumes TTM EBITDA of ~$6.5B**, which is based on Q1 2026's elevated numbers

3. **The company's leverage has been *rising*, not falling:**
   - Sept 2025: Net Debt = $13.99B
   - March 2026: Net Debt = $14.27B (up $280M in 6 months)

**This is not a company on a deleveraging path. It's a company where debt is growing faster than it's being paid down.**

### The Cash Flow Trap

Here's the real problem with the bull's "$850M annually for debt paydown" claim:

**It assumes TTM FCF of $1.5B is sustainable and repeatable. It is not.**

The company's quarterly FCF is:
- Q1 2026: -$42M
- Q4 2025: $282M
- Q3 2025: -$371M
- Q2 2025: $894M (outlier)

**The average of the last four quarters, excluding the outlier Q2, is approximately $-44M.** Not $1.5B annualized. Not even close.

And here's the kicker: **even if we take the bull's optimistic $1.5B FCF figure and his claim of $850M available for debt paydown after dividends**, at that pace, the company reduces net debt by only $850M per year. Starting from $14.27B:
- Year 1: $13.42B

**It takes 4+ years to get to 1.8x leverage, assuming FCF never deteriorates.** And we know FCF is deteriorating, not improving.

### Interest Coverage: The Hidden Vulnerability

The bull claims interest coverage of 4.5x. Let me contextualize this:

**Investment-grade companies typically target 5.0x+ interest coverage for stability.** AMCR is *below* that threshold, meaning:
- Any earnings disappointment threatens debt covenant compliance
- Refinancing becomes riskier if credit conditions tighten
- Rating downgrades from BBB- to BB (high-yield) become a real risk within 12-18 months if FCF deteriorates

And given that FCF has already turned negative in Q1 2026, that risk isn't theoretical — it's imminent.

### The Refinancing Risk Nobody's Talking About

Here's what keeps me up at night about AMCR: **with $16.7B in total debt and $653M due within 12 months, the company faces continuous refinancing needs.** 

In a scenario where:
- Credit spreads widen (any recession signal, Fed cut panic, etc.)
- FCF deteriorates further (which Q1 2026 suggests)
- Rating gets downgraded to BB (highly possible)

**The company faces a refinancing crisis.** Suddenly, the cheap debt issued in 2020-2022 (likely in the 2-3% range) needs to be rolled over at 5-6%+ rates in a higher-rate environment.

The bull assumes rates stay elevated ("89% probability of zero Fed cuts"). But what if that changes? What if *one* recession signal emerges, and suddenly markets flip to expecting 3-4 cuts? **AMCR's refinancing costs spike, FCF gets consumed by higher interest expense, and the leverage math breaks entirely.**
