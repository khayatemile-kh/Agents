## PART I: THE MARGIN RECOVERY THESIS IS A MIRAGE

### The Bull's Core Error: Confusing Temporary Pricing with Durable Profitability

The bull says: *"Containerboard prices are rising, and SW has pricing power. Margins will recover to 7-8%."*

Here's what the actual data shows:

**Q2 2026 Reveals a Deeply Concerning Trend:**

|--------|---------|---------|---------|---------|-----------|
| **Gross Margin** | 17.4% | 16.5% | 18.2% | 19.6% | ⬇️ -220 bps |
| **Operating Margin** | 5.3% | 4.0% | 6.1% | 7.6% | ⬇️ -230 bps |
| **Net Margin** | 1.1% | 0.8% | 1.3% | 3.1% | ⬇️ -200 bps |

**This is not an inflection point. This is a deterioration.**

The bull cites Q2 showing +130 bps sequential improvement (4.0% → 5.3%) in operating margin. But let's zoom out:

- **Q3 2025:** 7.6% operating margin
- **Q4 2025:** 6.1% operating margin (-150 bps)
- **Q1 2026:** 4.0% operating margin (-210 bps)
- **Q2 2026:** 5.3% operating margin (+130 bps bounce)

**This is noise and mean reversion, not inflection.** Q1 was a trough; Q2 is a partial bounce back to still-depressed levels. We're *still* 240 basis points below Q3 2025 normalized levels. The bull wants us to believe we'll reach 7-8% operating margins, but we're still far below even 2025 levels.

### The Brutal Truth About Rising Containerboard Prices

The bull says: *"Rising containerboard prices flow directly to the bottom line with a 1-2 quarter lag."*

This is where the bull fundamentally misunderstands the industry dynamics. Let me explain why rising prices are actually **bad news** for SW's margins:

**1. Customer Pass-Through Resistance is Real**

SW's largest customers—Amazon, Walmart, Target, major CPGs—have **massive negotiating leverage**. When SW tries to raise prices:
- These customers have alternative suppliers (DS Smith, International Paper, Huhtamaki, Mondi)
- They explicitly negotiate price increases down or delay implementation
- They threaten to source from competitors or shift to recycled alternatives
- **The result:** SW cannot fully pass through price increases to large customers

The market research report noted that "some corrugated experts still question whether this will move the needle"—**this is the real market consensus, not fringe skepticism.** Industry experts are skeptical that pricing power translates to bottom-line benefit.

**2. Input Costs Are Rising Faster Than Output Prices**

Here's what the bull ignores:
- **Recycled fiber prices:** Rising (upstream cost pressure)
- **Labor costs:** Inflationary pressure persists in industrial manufacturing
- **Energy costs:** Containerboard production is energy-intensive; energy prices remain elevated
- **Transportation costs:** Freight for raw materials and finished goods remains sticky

**The math:** If input costs are rising 60-80% as fast as output price increases, then margin expansion is *impossible*. Historical precedent from 2023-2025 shows exactly this dynamic—when containerboard prices rose in 2021-2022, they faced immediate input cost inflation that prevented lasting margin gains.

**3. The "Pricing Power" Cycle Is Temporary**

The bull points to 2021-2022 as precedent: *"300-400 bps margin expansion."* But what happened after?
- 2023: Prices normalized, demand softened
- 2024-2025: Margins collapsed as customers pushed back and input costs remained sticky
- Current environment: We're riding another temporary pricing cycle that will face the same normalization

**Cyclical businesses don't have "pricing power" in durable sense. They have temporary pricing windows that reverse.**
