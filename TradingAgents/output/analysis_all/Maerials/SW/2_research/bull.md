### Why SW's Integration Model Wins in a Rising-Price Environment

Here's where scale and integration become competitive moats:

**1. Vertical Integration Protects Against Input Cost Volatility**
- SW controls containerboard production (the upstream commodity)
- When recycled fiber prices spike, integrated players absorb and manage the shock
- Smaller competitors (pure box converters) get margin-squeezed immediately
- In a rising containerboard price cycle, integrated players like SW *gain share* from non-integrated competitors

**2. Global Scale = Pricing Power**
- $23.79B market cap; $31B+ in revenue
- This is the #1 or #2 global containerboard/corrugated player
- When rising prices are announced industry-wide, SW's customers (large retailers, CPGs, e-commerce fulfillment centers) negotiate with SW *and smaller competitors*—but they stick with SW for reliability and scale
- Larger customers actually *prefer* working with one global vendor during price transitions

**3. Capital Intensity as a Moat**
- Yes, $22.67B in net PPE is capital-heavy
- But this is also a barrier to entry; new competitors can't easily build capacity
- Existing capacity utilization is relatively stable; SW can increase throughput and margins without massive new capex
- This is why operating leverage in the current cycle is so attractive

**4. The Used/Recycled Box Opportunity**
- Rising virgin containerboard prices make recycled box economics compelling
- SW's integrated model means it captures value across virgin, recycled, and converted segments
- This diversification protects against pure-play virgin board compression
