## HERE'S WHERE BOTH SIDES MISSED THE ACTUAL RISK

Both camps have been arguing about whether to sell or hold, but they're actually arguing about the wrong question. The real question is: **What is the optimal position structure given the specific uncertainty we face?**

Let me reframe this entirely. NUE is currently trading at $251.92. The market has repriced it down 6.8% post-earnings, but it's still 23.9% above the 200-day moving average. This is not a stock in freefall. It's a stock that's taken a correction and is now consolidating. The Aggressive case wants to trim 25-35% now because they think there's a directional move coming down. The Conservative case wants to hold because they think the recovery is real and we're fighting the mean.

**Both of those framings accept the premise that we should have full conviction one way or the other. We shouldn't.**

Here's what the data actually tells us: We have a stock where the base case (76% odds) supports current or higher valuations, but the tail case (24% odds) creates 38-50% downside risk—not 50-60% as the Aggressive case overstated, but material nonetheless. We have technical evidence of consolidation, not capitulation or breakout confirmation. We have institutional capital rotating away (Berkshire reduction), but that's ambiguous—it could be rebalancing, it could be conviction, we don't know. We have retail sentiment bullish on the narrative, which is historically a contrarian warning sign.

**In that environment, the optimal trade is not binary. It's conditional.**

## THE ACTUAL PROBLEM WITH THE AGGRESSIVE PROPOSAL

Here's what bothers me about the aggressive recommendation when I dig into the mechanics:

They want to sell 25-35% at $251.92 with a stop at $265-267. Let's say we have a $1 million position, so we're selling $250,000-$350,000 of exposure. We're taking a $1.3-1.6 million loss if the stop is hit on the remaining position (65-75% of original, which is $650K-$750K, times $13-15 per share risk). 

But here's the kicker: **If tariffs persist and NUE rips to $300, we've sold the most liquid 25-35% at exactly the wrong time, capturing $251.92 but missing the move on that tranche while holding the rest.** The Aggressive case frames this as "capturing momentum without overcommitting," but that's marketing language. What it actually is: selling a predetermined quantity into a bounce where we don't have genuine conviction the bounce is ending. We're using a technical pattern (MACD divergence, neutral RSI) as a proxy for "the market is wrong," but we don't actually know that.

Conversely, if tariffs dissolve and we get a 38-45% earnings cliff (the more realistic downside from the Conservative analyst's math), we do capture some downside protection by having trimmed. But we're also underwater on the remaining 65-75% position.

**In both scenarios, we're suboptimizing.** We're not fully short the risk we're worried about, and we're not fully long the upside we're hoping for. We're just... sitting in the middle, which is the worst place to be in a binary outcome environment.

## WHERE THE CONSERVATIVE CASE BREAKS DOWN

The Conservative analyst's counter-argument is essentially: "The bounce is normal, MACD lagging is expected, the revenue growth is real, and the Berkshire move is routine rebalancing, so let's just hold and see."

That's intellectually reasonable but operationally incomplete. Here's why: **The tariff policy binary doesn't resolve in our favor by waiting.** The 48% probability that tariffs get resolved by December 31, 2026 (per prediction markets) is actually quite material. We have approximately four months before there's a meaningful policy catalyst. During that time, if tariff resolution becomes increasingly likely (through negotiations or political developments), NUE won't wait for December 31 to reprice. The market reprices *forward*.

So the Conservative case is implicitly asking us to hold NUE into the policy uncertainty without any structure to manage that risk. That's not risk management—that's hope. And yes, the revenue growth is real, but the Conservative analyst's own math says that tariff resolution creates a 38-45% earnings cliff from $5.04 to $3.08-$3.31 EPS. If the market reprices forward and expects, say, 30% tariff resolution probability in September (climbing over time), we could see re-rating to the $200-220 range before the tariff drama fully plays out.

The Conservative case gives us no defense against that scenario except "wait and see if volume confirms the recovery."

- Buying a $240 put (December 2026 expiration) to protect against tail risk if tariff resolution accelerates and we get the 38-45% earnings compression cliff
