## **PART 2: TARIFF PROTECTION IS INHERENTLY UNSTABLE—THE "76% CONFIDENCE" IS FALSE COMFORT**

### **Prediction Markets Are Pricing Persistence, But History Says Otherwise**

The bull touts the prediction market odds as if they're gospel:
- Only 6% probability of tariff resolution by Sept 30
- 76% probability they persist through Q4

**This is exactly backwards reasoning.** Here's why:

#### **Tariff Policy Regimes Are Volatile and Reversible**

History demonstrates this repeatedly:
- 2018-2019: Steel tariffs imposed → industry boomed → tariffs gradually relaxed → margins compressed
- 1930s: Smoot-Hawley tariffs enacted → retaliatory tariffs followed → trade collapsed
- 2020-2024: Tariffs became a geopolitical cudgel → constantly adjusted

**The point:** Even a 76% probability of persistence is meaningless when the downside (tariff removal) would collapse earnings by 40-60%.

#### **Why Tariff Removal Is More Likely Than Prediction Markets Suggest**

1. **Trade Negotiations Always Conclude with Deals** — Tariffs are negotiating tools, not permanent policy. Eventually, both sides declare victory and normalize. The U.S.-Canada relationship always reverts to trade cooperation because both economies benefit.

2. **Domestic Steel Demand Doesn't Require Tariffs** — The bull claims tariffs reduce foreign competition and support domestic demand. But here's the reality: **U.S. construction, automotive, and manufacturing will continue regardless of tariffs.** The difference is *price*. Without tariffs, steel is cheaper, and industries use more of it. With tariffs, steel is expensive, and usage falls. Net demand effect? Ambiguous.

3. **Retaliation Risk Is Already Building** — Canada and other trading partners are imposing counter-tariffs. If those escalate, Nucor faces *higher input costs* (e.g., energy, logistics) while export markets shrink. The "home field advantage" becomes a trap.

4. **Political Pressure From End-Users** — Construction companies, automotive OEMs, and appliance manufacturers will lobby for tariff removal because higher steel prices hit their margins. This pressure isn't theoretical—it's already beginning.

#### **The Real Risk: Cliff Event**

Here's what keeps me up at night about NUE:

**Earnings could swing 50-60% in a single quarter if tariffs are removed.** Not because operations deteriorate, but because pricing power evaporates overnight.

If NUE is trading at $251.92 on forward EPS of $19.04 (13.2x P/E), and that guidance drops to $10-12 per share because tariffs are resolved, the stock faces a 40-50% downside *immediately*. And shareholders won't get a warning—they'll get an earnings miss.

**That's not cyclical risk. That's binary policy risk.**
