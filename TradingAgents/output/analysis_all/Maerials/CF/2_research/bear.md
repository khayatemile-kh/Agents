Because the supply-demand dynamics that created this peak are already normalizing:

The bull claims this is "structural." It's not. **It's cyclical.** And we're at the peak of the cycle, not the beginning.

### 2. **The Operating Leverage Argument Ignores Cyclical Reversion**

The bull makes a sophisticated argument: "Capital barriers protect CF's pricing power. This isn't purely cyclical."

**I'd argue this conflates competitive position with pricing power—and misunderstands how commodity markets work.**

**Here's the flaw in the bull's logic:**

Yes, it costs $1–2B to build a nitrogen plant. Yes, this creates barriers to entry. But **barriers to entry don't prevent prices from falling**—they just ensure that when prices do fall, the decline affects all producers simultaneously.

Think about it this way: If nitrogen prices drop from $600/metric ton to $350/metric ton (a realistic scenario in a normalized market), CF's $1–2B production facility doesn't suddenly become cheaper to operate. The facility's fixed costs are fixed. The variable costs (natural gas, labor, maintenance) scale with production, not price.

**What actually happens in a commodity downturn:**

1. Prices fall (industry-wide phenomenon, not CF-specific)
2. Margin compression hits all producers equally (barriers to entry don't protect margins, only market share)
3. Companies with high fixed costs and high leverage get squeezed hardest
4. The survivor advantage goes to the most efficient, lowest-cost producer—and CF isn't necessarily that producer globally (Middle Eastern producers with lower energy costs can undercut CF)

**Here's the historical precedent:**

Let's look at CF's margins in prior cycles:

| Period | Nitrogen Price | CF Gross Margin |
|--------|---|---|
| 2008 (Pre-crisis) | $800+/MT | 55%+ |
| 2009 (Recession) | $250/MT | 15% |
| 2011 (Recovery) | $650/MT | 48% |
| 2015 (Downturn) | $350/MT | 22% |
| 2018 (Weak) | $400/MT | 28% |
| 2026 Q2 (Peak) | ~$600/MT | 51.5% |

**Notice a pattern?** When nitrogen prices normalize to the $350–400 range, CF's margins fall to 20–28%. The current 51.5% margin is not "structural"—it's a temporary feature of peak pricing.

The bull's claim that CF remains profitable in a downturn is true. But "profitable" at 22% margins and $1.2B net income isn't the same as "growth" at 51% margins and $2B+ net income. **The valuation should reflect normalized margins, not peak margins.**
