**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull case leans heavily on the Q4'25→Q2'26 net income/margin swing, deleveraging, and a nascent technical breakout (MACD cross, RSI mid-range, price above 10/50 SMA). The bear case correctly identifies the central flaw: ALB is a commodity producer whose Q2'26 margin (33.9% gross, 26.4% operating) is a function of a lithium price spike, not structural efficiency gains — and spot lithium pricing is already reported to be rolling over (JPMorgan's Aug 25 cut, spot in the $10-12/kg range vs. the $13-15/kg implied in Q2). That directly undercuts the bull's assumption that mid-30s gross margins and an $11.30 forward EPS are the new run-rate; the bear's reconstruction (normalized EPS closer to $4-5, not $11.30) is a more defensible extrapolation given commodity-cycle history.

On the balance sheet, the bull's "structural deleveraging" story is weaker than it looks once you run the cash-flow arithmetic the bear laid out: EBITDA of ~$1.48B minus ~$400M interest, $600-800M capex, and $200-300M taxes leaves only $400-500M of organic FCF — nowhere near the $1.45B net-debt reduction actually posted. That gap implies asset sales/working-capital releases drove much of the improvement, which is not repeatable, and the 18.98x debt/equity, even after the improvement, remains far outside normal specialty-chemicals leverage (1.5-2.5x). That's a legitimate structural risk, not a "relic," especially with rates likely to stay higher-for-longer per the bear's cited probability.

The bull's technical arguments (MACD cross, RSI room, Bollinger compression) are short-term and momentum-based; they don't address the fundamental question of where normalized earnings power sits once lithium pricing reverts toward the bear's more conservative range. Price is still meaningfully below the 200 SMA ($135.54 vs. $155.70), and the "early recovery" framing requires the lithium tailwind to persist — which the bear's spot-price evidence contradicts.

Net: the bear's dismantling of the sustainability of Q2's margin/EPS run-rate and the cash-flow math on deleveraging is more rigorous and better grounded in the concrete numbers cited by both sides. The bull's technical setup is real but secondary to a commodity-cycle risk that could compress margins hard in H2 2026. I'm siding with the bear thesis, but not to the point of a full Sell — there's genuine balance-sheet progress and a real (if fragile) technical base, so outright capitulation isn't warranted. This is a Underweight: trim exposure, don't add into what looks like a peak-cycle earnings print, and wait for confirmation that lithium pricing has actually stabilized before considering re-adding.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing ALB exposure by roughly 25-35%, using the current bounce (price near $135.54, above the 10/50 SMA) as a favorable exit point for a portion of the position rather than chasing the rally.
2) Do not add on the MACD/RSI bullish setup — treat it as a possible short-term relief rally within a still-declining 200 SMA trend ($155.70 and falling), not a structural reversal, until lithium spot pricing confirms stabilization above the $13/kg level implied by Q2 margins.
3) Set a hard risk trigger: if Q3 2026 gross margin prints materially below Q2's 33.9% (e.g., sub-28%) or management guides down full-year lithium price assumptions, cut the remaining position further toward a token/watch-only size.
4) Keep a residual position (rather than a full Sell) to retain optionality on the balance-sheet repair story and in case lithium prices stabilize faster than the bear case assumes — size the remainder so it would not be painful to hold through a further 10-15% drawdown toward the $120-125 support zone (near/below the 50 SMA).
5) Re-evaluate toward Hold/Overweight only if two consecutive quarters show gross margin holding above ~30% AND net debt/EBITDA remains under 0.5x with FCF (not asset sales) driving the improvement.
6) Avoid using options/leverage to press the short thesis given the stock's demonstrated volatility (60% Bollinger compression, prior sharp swings); express the view primarily through underweighting sizing rather than aggressive shorting.
