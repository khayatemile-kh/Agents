## THE FUNDAMENTAL DISCONNECT: CONFUSING VALUATION WITH DETERIORATION

First, let me acknowledge what the Aggressive Analyst got right: yes, NEM trades at a forward P/E of 12.4x embedded in elevated gold prices. Yes, if gold normalizes to $2,200-$2,300, earnings compress by 30-35%. I'm not disputing the math—I'm disputing the *relevance* of that scenario given current macro conditions.

Here's the critical flaw in the aggressive case: it assumes gold *will* normalize to $2,200-$2,300 as though that's inevitable. But let me walk through what the actual macro data suggests. The Polymarket data shows 89% probability of NO Fed rate cuts through 2026. That's not a headwind—that's crucial context the aggressive case is misinterpreting.

When the Fed keeps rates elevated but inflation remains controlled (as predicted by the soft-landing scenario with 59% probability), that's *precisely* the environment where gold doesn't normalize downward. Gold thrives on two conditions: either uncertainty/crisis, or real rates being suppressed. In a controlled-inflation, stable-rates environment, gold doesn't crash to $2,200. It stays elevated because it's capturing the *opportunity cost* of holding cash at positive real rates. That's not a new story—that's the story that's held gold above $2,000 for the past five years.

The aggressive case wants to treat the 2011-2015 gold crash as a precedent for current conditions. But 2011-2015 had *entirely different* macro drivers: the Fed was tightening into a strong dollar, China's economy was showing cracks, and real rates were rising sharply. We're in none of those conditions today. The Fed is *on hold*, the dollar is stable, China's stimulating, and real rates are actually becoming *more negative* as inflation remains sticky. This isn't 2011. The burden of proof is on anyone claiming gold normalizes sharply lower.

It's how bull markets don't turn into crashes.

The RSI cooling from 77.97 to 56.81 looks dramatic on paper, but the aggressive case is misrepresenting what happened. NEM didn't collapse in price while RSI plunged. If NEM had fallen 20-25% and RSI had only dropped to 65, *then* you'd have a serious bearish divergence. What we have instead is proportional normalization after an overbought extreme.

Moreover—and this is critical—NEM is currently trading *above* both its 50 SMA at $104.70 and its 200 SMA at $107.47. The price is 17.1% above the medium-term support and 14.1% above the long-term support. That's not a breakdown. That's healthy consolidation within an uptrend. ## THE MACRO ARGUMENT: MISREADING THE 28% GOLD PROBABILITY

Here's where the aggressive analyst makes a critical error in interpretation. They're looking at the 28% probability that gold outperforms equities in 2026 and treating that as evidence gold is a bad allocation. That's backwards logic.

First: 28% isn't trivial. In a 60/40 or 70/30 portfolio, you *want* some allocation to assets that have a meaningful (not speculative) probability of outperformance during periods when equities struggle. Gold with 28% odds is actually appropriately weighted in a diversified portfolio, not overweighted.

Second—and more importantly—the 57% probability for equities doesn't mean equities *always* outperform, and it doesn't mean gold investors get wiped out. It means *in expectation*, equities are more likely to do better. But that's conditional on the economy remaining stable. The moment we see Fed pivot signals, geopolitical escalation, or inflation reacceleration, those probabilities flip violently. Gold positions act as portfolio insurance. You don't buy insurance because you think disaster is likely—you buy it because the cost is reasonable and the downside is catastrophic if uninsured.

And here's the thing: NEM isn't just a gold bet. The company has explicit copper exposure through its Copper Indio project and operational leverage to copper demand from data centers and EVs. The institutional news flow explicitly highlighted this dual-growth thesis as a competitive advantage over pure-play gold miners. That's a structural bull case the aggressive analyst dismissed as "already priced in" without justification. Has the market truly incorporated NEM's copper optionality into the 12.4x forward P/E? I'd argue not fully, given that copper is trading at elevated levels driven by real structural demand.
