## PILLAR 1: THE MARGIN EXPANSION MIRAGE—TEMPORARY, NOT STRUCTURAL

### Let's Deconstruct the Bull's Key Claim

My colleague highlighted **Q2 2026 gross margin of 20.6%**, up 150 basis points from Q1 2026's 19.1%, and declared this "the margin inflection moment."

I have three fundamental problems with this analysis:

### Problem #1: One Quarter Does Not Make a Trend—Look at the Actual History

Let me provide historical context the bull case omitted:

| Period | Gross Margin | Operating Margin | Comment |
|--------|--------------|------------------|---------|
| Q2 2026 | 20.6% | 11.7% | One strong quarter |
| Q1 2026 | 19.1% | 10.6% | Weak start to year |
| Q4 2025 | 18.9% | 7.1% | Distressed by write-downs |
| Q3 2025 | 21.8% | 14.0% | Peak cycle quarter |
| **Full Year 2025** | **19.8%** | **12.2%** | Normalized baseline |
| Full Year 2024 | 19.3% | 11.8% | Pre-cycle |
| Full Year 2023 | 20.3% | 12.0% | Near peak |
| Full Year 2022 | 22.3% | 15.0% | **Pre-inflation peak** |

**What this actually shows:** 
- PKG's historical gross margins peak at **22-23%**, not the 20.6% the bull claims as "inflected"
- The Q3 2025 peak of 21.8% was a cyclical high, not the start of a new regime
- The company has *never sustained* above 22% gross margins for a full year
- Q2 2026's 20.6% is actually **below historical norms**, not above them

The bull is claiming that Q2's one-quarter performance proves a structural shift. This is precisely the kind of cyclical thinking that traps value investors.

### Problem #2: Containerboard Pricing "Strength" is Predictable and Already Priced In

The bull makes a critical logical leap: rising containerboard prices = sustained margin expansion.

Let me challenge this directly:

**First, containerboard pricing is cyclical, not structural.** Here's the industry dynamic:

1. **Tight capacity → Rising prices** (what we're seeing now in 2026)
2. **High returns attract investment → New capacity added** (typically 18-24 month lag)
3. **Excess capacity floods market → Prices collapse** (happens every cycle)
4. **Industry capacity utilization falls to 85-90%** (vs. current 95%+)
5. **Return to margin compression** (4-6 years into the next cycle)

**We are approximately 2-3 years into a cyclical price trough recovery.** This is *predictable*, not surprising.

**Second, market participants have already priced in containerboard strength.** The Barchart analyst commentary specifically noted: *"Rising containerboard prices historically support packaging producers' pricing power and margins."*

Translation: This is old news. If pricing power was a surprise, we'd see analyst upgrades. Instead, the stock has *underperformed* despite this known tailwind. That's a red flag, not a buying signal.

**Third, containerboard pricing power is not symmetric for PKG's customers.** Here's the awkward truth:

- PKG sells containerboard at rising prices to competitors (other box makers)
- PKG uses containerboard to make boxes, but it's an *internal transfer*
- When PKG raises box prices to customers, they push back (especially large retailers with procurement power)
- PKG can't pass through all containerboard cost increases to customers

**Real gross margin expansion is much smaller than headline containerboard price moves suggest.**

The bull claimed: *"If PKG can sustain Q2's 20.6% gross margin into Q3 and Q4 2026, we're looking at annual EBITDA potentially reaching $2.1-2.2B."*

Historical seasonality shows:
- Q3 and Q4 typically run $2,300-2,400M (lower volume in slowdowns)
- Gross margins compress in weaker quarters
- The bull is cherry-picking the *peak* quarter and annualizing it

**More realistic scenario:**
- Q3 2026: $2,350M × 19.8% = $465M gross profit
- Q4 2026: $2,300M × 19.5% = $448M gross profit

That's **BELOW** the TTM $2,039M, not above.

The bull case requires:
- No seasonal margin compression

- None of which is realistic in a packaging market
