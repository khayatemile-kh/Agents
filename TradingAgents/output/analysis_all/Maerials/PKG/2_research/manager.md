**Rationale**: Both analysts agree on the core facts: PKG generates real operating cash flow (~$1.15B TTM) with reasonable interest coverage, but leverage has risen sharply (+57% in a year) to fund $1.8B of acquisitions, and the Q2 2026 gross margin uptick (20.6%) sits within—not above—the company's historical 18.9%-22.3% range, meaning the bull's 'margin inflection' framing is not well supported by the data itself. The bull's valuation defense relies on assuming forward margin expansion persists at Q2's peak level annualized, which the bear correctly flags as an unrealistic flat-quarter extrapolation; more conservative full-year math the bear presents actually shows gross profit dipping versus TTM, undermining the bull's EBITDA-growth-driven re-rating argument. At the same time, the bear has not fully dismantled the cash-generation quality (61% EBITDA-to-FCF conversion is genuinely healthy), and the balance sheet metrics (1.75x net debt/EBITDA, 15.2x interest coverage) are not distressed in absolute terms even though the trajectory of leverage growth is concerning and worth flagging as a risk to monitor, particularly given integration execution risk on recently acquired assets. Given that the stock trades at a full P/E (29.75x trailing) and elevated P/FCF (48-53x) even before assuming any margin normalization, and given that the debt run-up coincided with a cyclical peak in containerboard pricing (a classic late-cycle capital allocation red flag), the weight of evidence tilts toward caution rather than aggressive buying. This isn't a broken business, so outright Sell is too strong, but the multiple already prices in a benign margin/leverage outcome the data doesn't fully support, and the debt-funded M&A during a pricing upcycle raises real deleveraging risk if the cycle turns. The prudent stance is to trim/de-risk exposure rather than add, while watching Q3/Q4 margin and leverage trends for confirmation either way.
