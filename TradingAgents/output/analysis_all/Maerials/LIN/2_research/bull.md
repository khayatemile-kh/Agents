- Compare this to a consumer discretionary company with $20B debt and $3B OCF (6.7x)—that's concerning. LIN's leverage is *built for industrial reality*, not a capital structure mistake.

**Why This Matters:**
LIN generates enough free cash flow to service debt, fund growth capex, pay a growing dividend, AND buy back shares. In Q2 2026 alone:
- Operating cash flow: $2,271M
- This covers the entire quarterly dividend AND capex with cash to spare

The negative working capital of -$1.95B? That's a **feature, not a bug.** It means LIN collects from customers faster than it pays suppliers—a sign of pricing power and operational efficiency.

#### Debt Maturity Profile: Well-Laddered
With $20.7B long-term and only $7.3B current debt, plus $4.5B commercial paper, LIN has flexibility. The $4.9B cash position isn't idle—it's strategic liquidity for opportunistic M&A or weathering downturns.
