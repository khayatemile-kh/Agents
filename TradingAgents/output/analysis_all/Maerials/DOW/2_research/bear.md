## **1. THE VALUATION TRAP: "25% UPSIDE" IS BUILT ON BORROWED ASSUMPTIONS**

### **The Bull's Sleight of Hand: Forward P/E Masking Deterioration**

My colleague points to a "17.04x Forward P/E" and claims this is "cheap." Here's the problem: **forward earnings are a projection, not a reality**. Let's examine what's actually happening:

**The TTM Picture Tells the Real Story:**

| Metric | TTM Reality | Bull's Projection | Gap |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|-----|
| **Net Income (TTM)** | **($1,301M)** | Assumed $3-4B normalized | **Fictional** |
| **EPS (TTM)** | **($1.88)** | Assumed $1.77+ forward | **Backward-looking** |
| **Profit Margin (TTM)** | **-3.13%** | Assumed 8-10% | **No evidence** |
| **ROIC (implied)** | Negative | Assumed 8-12% | **Not materializing** |

**Here's what really happened:** DOW reported a strong Q2 2026, yes. But this single quarter cannot erase the fact that the company lost **$1.3 billion over the trailing twelve months**. The bull is asking us to extrapolate from *one good quarter* and assume that becomes the normalized run rate.

**This is dangerous thinking.** Why?

1. **Q2 2026 benefited from one-time tailwinds** (chemical supply tightness, working capital reductions, cost deferrals). As competition returns and supply normalizes, margins will compress.

2. **The "25% upside to fair value" assumes DCF models that require sustained 8%+ EBITDA margins.** The company is currently at 9.6% EBITDA margin (TTM), which is *barely above* recession-level profitability. One cyclical shock—oil spike, demand drop, or recession—obliterates that assumption.

3. **Forward earnings projections are notoriously optimistic for cyclical companies at inflection points.** Wall Street analysts regularly overestimate chemical company earnings recoveries because they assume *perfect* cycle timing. DOW's actual track record suggests margins normalize to 6-8%, not the 10%+ needed to justify $38-40 fair value.
