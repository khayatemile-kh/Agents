
Bear Analyst: # BEAR CASE FOR STEEL DYNAMICS (STLD): Why This Rally Is Built on Sand

---

## Opening Statement: The Bull Case Is Dangerously Oversimplified

Look, I respect the bull thesis—it *sounds* compelling. Tariffs, margin expansion, 62% earnings growth. On the surface, it checks boxes. But this is exactly the kind of narrative-driven story that gets retail investors crushed in mean-reverting cycles. **STLD isn't a value opportunity; it's a momentum trap masquerading as one.** Let me dismantle this systematically.

The fundamental problem: **The bull thesis depends almost entirely on a tariff protection that is explicitly temporary and increasingly uncertain.** Remove that tariff, and STLD's entire valuation edifice collapses. That's not investing—that's speculation. And the market data suggests the tariff risk is far greater than the bulls admit.

## **PART I: THE TARIFF TAILWIND IS A HOUSE OF CARDS**

### The Bull's Own Data Proves the Risk

Here's where I'm going to use the bull's own Polymarket data against them:

**Bull Claims:** "Market is pricing in 4-6 months of tariff protection, delivering 5-6 quarters of inflated earnings."

**Reality Check:** Look at what happened to those probabilities *week-over-week*:
- Sept 30 resolution: **DOWN 33.5 percentage points** in one week
- Dec 31 resolution: **DOWN 20.5 percentage points** in one week

**What does this actually mean?** The market is becoming *increasingly skeptical* that the tariff will hold. A 48% probability of resolution by December 31 means **there's already a coin-flip bet priced in that the tariff disappears before year-end.**

Compare this to the bull's framing: they're claiming "4-6 months of protection," but the market is literally betting there's a 50/50 chance it's over in 4 months.

### Here's the Problem With the Timing Arbitrage Argument

The bull says: *"Even if tariffs end, STLD shareholders capture 5-6 quarters of inflated earnings, which sustains valuation at higher levels."*

**This is where the logic breaks down completely.** Let me explain why:

**Scenario 1: Tariff Ends in Q4 2026 (48% probability, per Polymarket)**
- Q3 2026: STLD reports inflated earnings, guided by tariff support
- Q4 2026: Tariff is removed or negotiated down
- Q4 2026 Earnings Call: Management issues massive guidance cut. Margins normalize. Stock crashes 15-25% *overnight*.
- **Timeline to Peak**: Sept → Dec (3-4 months)
- **Then**: Mean reversion for 12+ months as market reprices

This isn't "sustained valuation at higher levels." This is a **classic pump-and-dump cycle**: tariff supports inflated earnings for 1-2 quarters, equity narrative explodes, then reality hits.

**Scenario 2: Tariff Persists (52% probability)**
- Market gradually stops believing the tariff is temporary
- Multiple compression as "temporary earnings" becomes "normalized earnings"
- Stock drifts sideways as tariff benefit is fully baked into valuation
- **Result**: 0% gain for next 6 months while opportunity cost accumulates

**Either way, risk/reward is skewed to the downside.**

### The Structural Cost Advantage Argument Is Oversold

The bull claims: *"STLD has $8.5B in modern EAF assets—a low-cost production infrastructure that retains structural advantage even after tariffs end."*

**Okay, but let's be honest about what this means in practice:**

1. **EAF mills are NOT a proprietary advantage.** Most large steelmakers have converted to EAF in the last decade. Cleveland-Cliffs, Nippon Steel, ArcelorMittal—they all have modern EAF capacity. STLD's asset base isn't special; it's table stakes.

2. **The real cost advantage of EAF comes from two things:**
   - Lower scrap input costs (commodity input, varies by market cycle)
   - Lower labor costs (but STLD pays prevailing wages; no permanent advantage vs. peers)

3. **After tariffs end, STLD doesn't magically keep its margin advantage.** Prices normalize to *cost + competitive return*. Without tariffs, Canadian and other competitors return to undercutting on price, compressing margins back to historical levels of 11-13%.

4. **Look at the historical data the bull ignored:**
   - Q3 2025: 15.7% gross margin
   - Q4 2025: 12.0% gross margin (a 570 bps collapse!)
   - Q1 2026: 14.6% gross margin
   - Q2 2026: 15.7% gross margin

**That Q4 2025 drop to 12% shows what "normalized" margins look like.** When demand softened and tariffs weren't a factor, margins fell 570 bps *in a single quarter*. The bull is betting that won't happen again, but why? What's changed structurally?

**Nothing.** STLD is just as exposed to commodity steel pricing cycles as it always has been.

## **PART II: THE EARNINGS GROWTH NUMBER IS AN OPTICAL ILLUSION**

### The 62% Forward Growth Claim Requires Huge Assumptions

Let's deconstruct this carefully.

**Bull Claims:** Forward EPS estimate is $18.95 vs. TTM of $11.66, implying 62% growth.

**Here's what this assumes:**
1. Q3 2026 and Q4 2026 each deliver EPS of ~$4.70+
2. This requires maintaining the Q2 2026 operating margins of 12.4%+
3. Revenue stays in the $6B+ quarterly range
4. No seasonal weakness, no demand deterioration, no tariff uncertainty slowdown

**But let's reality-check this:**

**Q2 2026 was a *peak* quarter:**
- Revenue: $6.1B (16.7% QoQ growth)
- Operating margin: 12.4% (peak level)
- EPS: $3.69 (33% QoQ growth)

**The historical pattern shows Q4 is ALWAYS weaker than Q2:**
- Q2 to Q4 typically sees 10-15% revenue decline (seasonal)
- Q4 margins compress as year-end inventory destocking occurs
- Q4 2025 was only $4.4B revenue with 12.0% margin, delivering $1.82 EPS

**So for the forward estimate of $18.95 to be real, we need:**
- Q3 2026: $4.5+ EPS (higher than the Q2 peak)
- Q4 2026: $4.5+ EPS (higher than the Q2 peak and defying seasonal weakness)

**This is not just optimistic. It's ignoring 5+ years of seasonal patterns.** The bull is cherry-picking the *best quarter ever* (Q2 2026) and extrapolating it as a new baseline.

### The Operating Leverage Argument Works Both Ways

The bull celebrates the 2x multiplier: revenue +16.7%, EPS +32.7%.

**Here's what they conveniently ignore: this multiplier works in *reverse* too.**

If revenue declines 10% (very realistic given seasonal/tariff uncertainty), and the company has the same fixed cost base, then EPS *declines 20%+*.

**This is why the tech boom created massive valuations—growth multiples justified the P/E premium. But STLD isn't growing structurally; it's benefiting from a temporary tariff.** Once that tariff expires, revenue could fall 10-15% overnight, taking EPS down 25-30%.

The bull's operating leverage argument actually **confirms the downside risk**, not the upside potential.
