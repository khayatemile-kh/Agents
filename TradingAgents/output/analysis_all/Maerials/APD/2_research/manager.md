That's a real red flag, not just a rhetorical one.

Paying a scarcity/quality premium makes sense only if the balance sheet has room to absorb shocks — and right now it doesn't, given negative FCF and deteriorating working capital/cash trends (cash down 58% YoY).

The technical picture is genuinely balanced/neutral (price above both 50/200 SMA but RSI cooling and MACD histogram turning negative) — this supports patience rather than urgency in either direction, but it does not offset the fundamental leverage and cash-flow deterioration concerns.

Net: the bear's rebuttal is better grounded in the actual reported numbers (negative FCF, deteriorating cash and working capital, thin current ratio, and stress-tested leverage sensitivity), while the bull's growth/quality thesis is more narrative and forward-looking without offsetting near-term balance-sheet risk. That tips this toward a cautious stance rather than a balanced Hold — the risk/reward skews to the downside if execution stumbles or the macro turns even mildly worse, and there's essentially no valuation cushion for that scenario. I'm not going full Sell because the underlying operating business (normalized EBITDA margins, contracted revenue streams, investment-grade profile) is still fundamentally sound and the earnings beat plus guidance raise show management isn't panicking — but the combination of stretched valuation, negative free cash flow, and leverage with little room for error warrants trimming exposure rather than adding or holding steady.
