### **COUNTER 3: END-MARKET TAILWINDS ARE OVERSTATED AND FACING HEADWINDS MOST BULLS IGNORE**

Now let's talk about the supposed secular tailwinds the bull cited.

**Semiconductor:** Yes, the sector is growing. But let's be precise about DD's exposure:
- DD primarily supplies specialty chemicals and materials to chip *fabs* (fabrication plants)
- Fab utilization has been sliding since late 2024. Current fab utilization is in the **60–70% range**, well below the 80%+ "healthy" threshold
- If utilization doesn't improve, chipmakers will delay or defer capex, which directly impacts DD's volumes
- Additionally, the semiconductor supply chain is diversifying away from concentration in Taiwan and the US—DD faces competition from Chinese and Japanese specialty chemical suppliers gaining share in second and third-tier fabs

**Aerospace:** Yes, Boeing has a backlog. But:
- Aerospace demand is cyclically sensitive and often peaks right before recession hits
- Spirit AeroSystems (major aerospace supplier) is showing margin compression from elevated raw material and labor costs
- DD's composite resins and coatings are *important*, but they represent a small fraction of total aircraft BOM (bill of materials); not margin-moving at scale
- Any production disruption (labor strikes, supply chain issues) directly impacts DD's volumes

**Water Treatment (MemCor):** This is *one* contract win. Congratulations. But:
- MemCor is a **legacy product line**, not a growth driver for DD overall
- Water treatment is growing, but it's also a *commoditizing* market with intense competition from Suez, Veolia, and others
- Margin compression in this segment is real; this is not a high-margin growth market

**Destocking Tailwind:** This is the bull's sleeper argument, and it's the most dangerous because it's *time-limited.*
- Yes, chemical inventories are normalizing
- But destocking normalization creates a **one-time demand surge**, not a structural growth driver
- Once inventory gets back to normal levels (6–9 months out), you're left with underlying consumption demand—which is *flat*
- The bull is essentially arguing the stock should be bid up for a temporary inventory cycle rebound. **That's not a durable bull case; that's a trading setup that will reverse.**
