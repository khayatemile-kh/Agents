**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make reasonable points, but when I weigh them the bear case is more grounded in verifiable arithmetic while the bull case leans heavily on macro narrative and technical pattern-reading that cuts both ways. On the fundamental side, the bull's headline stat—21.4% forward EPS growth—doesn't survive the bear's stress test: Q2 2026 net income of $204M vs Q2 2025's $189M is only ~8% YoY growth, not 21%, so the growth number being used to justify the '15.59x forward multiple is cheap' argument is likely an inflated analyst estimate rather than a demonstrated run-rate. That matters a lot for the valuation case, because if realized growth comes in closer to high single digits, the forward P/E discount the bull cites stops looking like a bargain and starts looking like a normal cyclical multiple with no margin of safety.

The leverage picture is the other swing factor. A 158.65x debt-to-equity ratio is extreme by any standard, and while the bull is right that ~$1B TTM FCF and 2.3x net debt/EBITDA mean near-term debt service isn't an emergency, the bear's point about refinancing risk in a 'no rate cuts' regime is well-taken — locked-in higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of rolling over any maturing debt, and a balance sheet with essentially no equity cushion has very little room for a growth disappointment or margin reversal. The bull's response to leverage was basically 'FCF covers it,' which answers debt service capacity but not the more important question of resilience if the cyclical upturn stalls.

On margins, the bear actually conceded that gross/operating/net margins are all trending up sequentially, which undercuts the 'collapsing foundation' framing — but the bear's caution that margin expansion is likely operating leverage from a cyclical upswing rather than a structural advantage is fair, and it's a real risk if volumes soften in H2.

On technicals, this is close to a wash — RSI neutral, price sitting on multiple support levels, Bollinger contraction; you could read this as basing-for-breakout or as a broken 10 EMA trend rolling over. I don't think technicals should be the deciding factor here given how mixed the signals are.

Net-net: the macro backdrop (low recession odds, resilient consumer) is a genuine modest positive, and the FCF generation and shareholder returns are real strengths that argue against an outright Sell. But the combination of (a) growth math that doesn't cleanly support the multiple, (b) extreme leverage leaving little error margin in a higher-for-longer rate environment, and (c) valuation that is already pricing in a lot of good news, tips this toward caution rather than aggressive buying. I don't see enough evidence of mispricing in AVY's favor to add exposure, but I also don't see the kind of demand collapse or margin implosion that would justify exiting the position outright. This is a 'trim and de-risk' situation, not a 'buy the dip' or 'sell everything' situation.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing AVY exposure modestly (roughly 15-25% of the position) rather than adding on this pullback — the growth assumptions embedded in the 15.59x forward P/E look aggressive once you normalize for the YoY (not QoQ) net income comparison (~8% actual YoY growth vs. the 21.4% forward estimate being used to justify the multiple).
2) Do not add to the position near current support ($170-172) purely on technical grounds; RSI neutrality and Bollinger contraction are not strong enough signals to override the leverage and growth-durability concerns.
3) Set a hard risk trigger: if Q3/Q4 2026 guidance shows revenue growth decelerating toward mid-single digits or gross/operating margins reversing even modestly, reduce the position further — the 158.65x debt-to-equity leaves little room for error if the cycle turns.
4) Retain a residual position (do not go to zero) to keep exposure to the ~2.3% dividend yield and continued buyback/dividend cash returns, which remain a genuine positive as long as FCF (~$1B TTM) holds up.
5) Revisit the thesis after the next two quarterly prints specifically to check whether net income growth is converging toward or diverging from the 21.4% forward EPS estimate — use that as the key confirming/disconfirming data point before making any further sizing changes.
6) Avoid initiating new positions or options-based leveraged bets on AVY until either (a) leverage metrics improve (debt-to-equity trending down, net debt/EBITDA holding at or below 2.3x) or (b) the stock re-rates to a more clearly discounted multiple reflecting realistic (not headline) growth.
