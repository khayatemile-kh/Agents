## **The Oil Price Question Is More Nuanced Than Either Side Admits**

Let me address this directly because it's the crux of the disagreement.

The Aggressive Analyst says oil is spiking (true), margins compress (true), and therefore the stock should be sold (logical, but not inevitable). The Conservative Analyst says oil spikes happen and management has tools to manage it (also true). Both are correct in isolation.

But here's what matters operationally: **DAL's forward guidance was likely built on oil assumptions in the $70-75 barrel range**. The current WTI spike to $80+ territory means those assumptions are now at risk. But—and this is critical—this is a *guidance risk*, not a covenant risk. It's a question of whether Q3 and Q4 earnings come in at expected levels.

The real question should be: at $76.38, are you being appropriately compensated for that guidance risk?

Here's my analysis: If WTI stays above $80, you might see Q3-Q4 earnings come in 10-15% lower than consensus. That would put forward EPS around $7.40 instead of $8.69. At $76.38 today, you're essentially saying the market is pricing in that lower outcome *already*. The P/E on that $7.40 forward would be about 10.3x, which is reasonable for an airline in a macro headwind environment.

So the stock *may* be fairly valued at current levels *if* you believe oil stays elevated and guidance disappoints modestly. But the stock also has 15-20% downside if earnings miss badly *and* oil stays elevated *and* the market re-rates the multiple lower. And it has 10-15% upside if oil comes down and guidance holds.

That's a 20% downside risk / 12% upside potential. That's asymmetric risk.
