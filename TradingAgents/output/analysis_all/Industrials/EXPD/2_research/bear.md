- **S&P 500 average PEG:** ~1.0–1.2x

## **PART 3: THE MACRO BACKDROP IS NOT AS SUPPORTIVE AS YOU CLAIM—AND YOU'RE IGNORING CYCLICAL HEADWINDS**

### **The Trade Deficit Argument Is Circular**

You point to "71% probability of $700–900B trade deficits" as evidence of robust cross-border commerce.

**I'll call this out directly: Higher trade deficits don't automatically translate to higher freight volumes for EXPD.** Here's why:

**1. The Composition of Trade Matters**

If the trade deficit is driven by:
- Cheap imports from China (which face tariff pressure)
- Used automotive imports (depressed market)
- Commodities with low transportation costs relative to value

...then EXPD doesn't benefit proportionally. Air freight forwarding is most profitable on **high-value, time-sensitive goods** (electronics, pharmaceuticals, fashion). If the trade deficit is commodity/bulk-heavy, EXPD's margin profile suffers.

**2. Tariff Uncertainty Is a Real Headwind**

The macro report acknowledges: *"Tariff/trade policy uncertainty remains a structural headwind."*

But you're glossing over it. Here's the reality:
- **Tariff escalation would crater import volumes** (companies stop buying, or they source domestically)
- **Tariff uncertainty causes companies to defer purchases**, which reduces freight volumes
- **EXPD derives significant revenue from China-US trade.** Any trade war escalation is an earnings headwind, not a tailwind.

The "0–4% probability of rate cuts" you cite doesn't insulate EXPD from tariff shocks. **A 20% tariff on Chinese imports would hit EXPD revenue harder than a 50 bp rate cut would.**

### **International Expansion Isn't As Profitable As You Suggest**

You make a big deal of UAL's 10 new international routes and UPS's $2B infrastructure investment.

**Fair points, but here's the counterargument:**

**1. Competition Intensifies in International Lanes**

When major carriers (UPS, FedEx) expand their own logistics infrastructure, they **reduce reliance on third-party forwarders like EXPD.** UAL expanding capacity doesn't automatically mean EXPD gets more volume—it means UAL can negotiate harder on forwarding fees or do more in-house logistics.

This is a **structural threat to EXPD's margins**, not a tailwind.

**2. International Logistics Is Capital-Intensive**

You tout EXPD's "asset-light model," but if EXPD is expanding into new international markets, it needs:
- Customs brokerage licenses (expensive, regulatory)
- Warehousing and distribution networks (capital-heavy)
- Local partnerships and staff

**This is a margin compression story, not a margin expansion story.** The balance sheet data already shows this: stockholders' equity fell from $2.28B (Q1) to $2.12B (Q2), while net income was $266.2M. That's earnings retention rate dropping—why? Because capex and restructuring costs are eating into profits.
