**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: EXPD just posted a blowout Q2 (32% revenue growth, 45% net income growth, 51% EPS growth) against an easy 2025 comp, trades at ~27.3x trailing / 23.5x forward PE, PEG ~1.66-2.97 depending on which growth rate you use, P/B 11.4x, P/S ~2.0x, ROE 42.7%, ROA 15.2%, and has run 70% off its 52-week low with recent consolidation near highs. The disagreement is about durability.

The bull's strongest points: this is a scaled, asset-light, high-ROE business with genuine operational leverage (margins holding at 13.5% gross despite huge volume growth), a benign macro backdrop (low recession probability, robust trade deficit), and sector-wide tailwinds (UAL capacity additions, UPS infrastructure spend) that could support continued share gains in higher-margin air freight/customs brokerage. Technical structure shows institutional-driven breakout, not retail speculation.

The bear's strongest points land harder for me: the growth rate is explicitly decelerating on a forward basis — using the company's own guidance ($7.93 forward EPS vs. an annualized ~$8.12 run-rate off Q2), growth is closer to flat/16% than the trailing 45-51% being cited to justify the multiple. That means the PEG the bull uses (2.97 off trailing growth) understates the multiple bears' recalculated PEG (~1.66x on forward growth) is still not cheap for a freight forwarder. Historically cyclical peers (ZIM, JBHT) trade at a fraction of EXPD's P/S, and even the bull didn't really rebut why EXPD deserves such a premium versus those comps other than "quality." The equity base actually shrank quarter over quarter ($2.28B to $2.12B) even as net income was strongly positive — that's a flag on capital efficiency/buybacks-vs-reinvestment dynamics the bull didn't address, and it undercuts the "ROE will stay elevated" argument since a shrinking equity base flatters ROE mechanically without necessarily reflecting durable business quality.

Also, the bear's point about competitive disintermediation risk (UPS/FedEx/airlines building out their own logistics infrastructure, potentially reducing reliance on third-party forwarders like EXPD) is a real structural risk that the bull's "rising tide lifts all boats" framing glosses over — expanding carrier capacity does not mechanically flow through to forwarder volumes or margins; it could just as easily compress the fees EXPD can charge.

Net: the bull thesis leans heavily on trailing growth metrics and a "quality justifies premium" narrative, while the bear correctly reframes the more relevant forward-looking growth math and shows the current multiple is already pricing in continued cyclical strength that the company's own guidance suggests is decelerating. Tariff/trade-policy uncertainty is a real, acknowledged headwind that isn't priced for downside. Given the stock is already up 70% off its low and consolidating near all-time highs, the risk/reward skews unfavorable at current levels — this isn't a "sell everything" collapse case, but it's not a "keep adding at 27x trailing PE into a decelerating growth story" moment either. I'm siding with the bear's valuation discipline over the bull's momentum narrative, while acknowledging the business itself remains fundamentally sound (high ROE, asset-light, real earnings) rather than broken. That combination — good business, bad entry point, decelerating growth priced as if it's accelerating — argues for trimming exposure rather than adding, but doesn't rise to a full sell/exit call given the balance sheet strength ($1.03B cash) and genuine profitability.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing EXPD exposure by roughly 20-30% into current strength (stock is consolidating near 52-week highs after a 70% run), rather than adding at 27.3x trailing / 23.5x forward PE.
2) Do not initiate new full-size positions here; if adding at all, wait for a pullback toward the $160-170 range (prior consolidation zone) where forward PE would compress closer to 19-20x, offering a better risk/reward entry against the company's own guided ~16% forward EPS growth.
3) Use any bounce toward the $189-192 recent highs as a level to take partial profits given the disconnect between trailing 45-51% growth being used to justify the multiple versus the company's own forward guidance implying near-flat sequential growth off the Q2 run-rate.
4) Set a re-evaluation trigger: if forward guidance is raised meaningfully above $7.93 EPS in the next print, or if margin expansion continues despite volume deceleration, revisit toward Hold/Overweight. Conversely, watch for confirmation of the bear case via (a) further sequential shrinkage in stockholders' equity, (b) any tariff escalation headlines on China-US trade lanes, or (c) commentary from UPS/FedEx/airlines indicating in-house logistics build-out is displacing third-party forwarder volume — any of these should prompt further trimming toward Sell.
5) Keep a residual core position (do not fully exit) given the balance sheet strength ($1.03B cash, high ROE/ROA) and lack of any balance-sheet distress signal — this is a valuation/timing call, not a solvency or business-quality call.
6) Avoid adding leverage or options exposure that would amplify downside if the stock reverts toward its multi-month consolidation range as growth normalizes.
