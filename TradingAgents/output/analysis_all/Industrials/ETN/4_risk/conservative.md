But here's what they're ignoring: **that July 29 capitulation occurred on 4.55 million shares of volume—panic selling that exhausted itself.** We're now at 2.02 million shares on current weakness. The aggressive analyst frames this as "exhaustion selling" with bullish implications. I frame it differently: **it's low participation, which means there's no conviction either direction.** When volume dies on a decline, the next move could be either way—lower with less resistance, or higher with continued apathy.

More critically, the aggressive analyst is cherry-picking the bounce narrative. Yes, July 29 to mid-August recovered 35%. But look at what happened next: the stock peaked at $459.96 on August 12 and has surrendered 15% of those gains in just 20 days. That's not a "reset zone"—that's a failed rally that's now retesting lows. The fact that the bands are *widening* (from $84 spread at peak normalization to current wider spreads) indicates volatility is *increasing*, not exhausting. Increasing volatility typically **precedes larger moves down, not up**, especially when momentum indicators (MACD) are still negative.

Let me phrase this clearly: if RSI is truly oversold and a reversal is imminent, we should see **MACD crossing above its signal line as confirmation.** We don't have that yet. The negative MACD at -4.01 above signal line at +1.73 creates a technical divergence—the very thing that warns "this oversold reading might deepen before it reverses."
