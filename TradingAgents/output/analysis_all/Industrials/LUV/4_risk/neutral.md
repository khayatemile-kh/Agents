The Aggressive Analyst says the MACD histogram expanding from -0.62 to -0.45 means "momentum is still deteriorating." That's backwards. A histogram moving from -0.62 to -0.45 is moving *toward zero*, which is literally the definition of momentum convergence. That's a material factual error in the Aggressive case, and it undermines the entire "bear trap for reversal traders" thesis.

More critically, the Aggressive Analyst's claim that Southwest is "losing pricing power" doesn't hold up. Revenue is growing 16.4% year-over-year. The company beat Q2 earnings expectations with $0.94 adjusted EPS. Record managed business revenue. These aren't signs of a company hemorrhaging customers. The debt-to-equity ratio of 97.28x is presented as apocalyptic. But the Aggressive Analyst strategically avoids mentioning that Southwest *just secured* a $2-3 billion credit facility in a rising rate environment. Lenders don't do that for companies heading toward distress. They do it for companies with operational cash flow. The company generating $760 million in quarterly EBITDA can service $2.156 billion in current debt. The ratio looks catastrophic because of low equity, but airlines are inherently high-leverage businesses. This isn't a technology company with a 97x ratio—that would be insane. For an airline with real assets and cash flow, it's elevated but survivable.

Where the Aggressive Analyst really goes off track is dismissing the 737 MAX 7 and operational modernization as irrelevant to "surviving 2026." This is precisely the mentality that causes traders to exit at generational lows. When a company is in a cyclical downturn and visibility exists on margin recovery within 12-18 months, that's not a reason to exit—that's a reason to trim tactically and position for recovery. The Aggressive Analyst is treating LUV like a company in structural decline when the data suggests it's a company in a cyclical squeeze.
