A Neutral analyst might say, "Well, the $2 billion capex and strategic pivot will drive this." But that's **assuming execution of a turnaround while the company is simultaneously bleeding cash and burning through dividend coverage.** That's not a bet I'm comfortable asking shareholders to make.

Because it means **the company has almost zero financial flexibility to weather a real shock.** If we get a recession (probability: 8%, but non-zero), or if parcel volumes contract more sharply than expected, or if the company's credit rating is downgraded, the refinancing costs on that $28.67B debt ball will spike. The company won't be able to roll the debt cheaply. The debt-service burden will compress margins even further. And suddenly you're looking at a real solvency crisis, not just a profitability issue.

From a risk-management perspective, this level of leverage in a cyclical, commoditized business is disqualifying. It's not just a reason to avoid new positions—it's a reason to exit existing ones.
