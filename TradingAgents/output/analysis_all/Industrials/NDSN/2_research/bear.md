The bull points to "broad-based segment growth"—but if margins are collapsing, that growth is *profitless growth*. It's worse than no growth; it's dilutive.

### 1.3 The Backlog Illusion: Orders Aren't Profit

Here's where the bull's argument breaks down: **A 35% YoY backlog increase doesn't matter if those orders are priced at razor-thin margins.**

Think about this logically:
- **Flat YoY revenue** but **+35% backlog growth** means either:
  a) The company is taking *longer* to fulfill orders (suggests inefficiency or capacity constraints)
  b) The new orders are priced *lower* than historical orders (margin compression)
  c) Both

Given that margins are *simultaneously* compressing, the answer is clearly (b) and (c). NDSN is likely taking market share through aggressive pricing—which looks good on the backlog line but is *terrible* for profitability.

**The bear's question to the bull:** If backlog orders were accretive to margins, why are net profit margins down 430 basis points? That's not coincidental.
