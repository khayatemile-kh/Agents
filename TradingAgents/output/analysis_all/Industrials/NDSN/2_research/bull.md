## SECTION 1: THE GROWTH CASE IS REAL

### 1.1 Backlog Growth: Forward Revenue Visibility That Matters

Look at this metric carefully: **+35% year-over-year backlog growth in Q2 2026.** This isn't a vanity metric—in industrial machinery, backlog is *hard cash waiting to be recognized*. It's orders already placed by customers who've committed capital. 

**What this means operationally:**
- A 35% backlog increase suggests approximately **6-12 months of forward revenue visibility** at current burn rates.
- For a company doing ~$3B in annual revenue with a normalized quarterly revenue run of ~$750M, a 35% backlog spike translates to roughly $1B+ in additional committed work.
- This directly *de-risks* revenue forecasts. Management doesn't need to hunt for new orders tomorrow; they're executing on booked work *today*.

**Why this matters to investors:** In cyclical industrial sectors, backlog is the most honest signal of business health. It's not guidance (which can be walked back), it's not analyst estimates (which are often wrong), it's *actual customer commitments*. NDSN's backlog surge suggests that:
1. Customers see strong demand in their own markets
2. They're willing to *prepay* or *commit* capex despite sticky interest rates
3. The company's competitive position is strong enough to win significant new orders

The bear might argue: *"But backlogs can be cancelled."* Technically true, but industrial equipment orders are rarely cancelled. Cancellation rates in specialty machinery are typically <2% in non-recession environments. With recession odds at just 8% per prediction markets, this is a low-risk assumption.
