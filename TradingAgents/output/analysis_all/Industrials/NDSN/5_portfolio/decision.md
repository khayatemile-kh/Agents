**Investment Thesis**: The bear/aggressive case rests on concrete, verifiable multi-quarter deterioration: sequential net income fell 22.6% (Q3'25 $151.6M → Q2'26 $117.3M), diluted EPS fell from $2.69 to $2.09, and margins compressed across the board (operating -190bps, net -430bps, EBITDA -570bps YoY). This is happening concurrently with a 35% YoY backlog surge and flat revenue, which the bear plausibly links to margin-dilutive order intake (pricing concessions to win business) — a logical explanation the bull never rebuts with hard data, only with forward promises about conversion. The balance sheet critique is also verifiable and material: net debt/EBITDA ~1.86x is not fortress-level, and goodwill/intangibles of $3.98B versus negative tangible book value of -$782M creates real impairment optionality if earnings compression persists or macro conditions weaken customer capex, an outcome the world-affairs context (0% priced Fed cuts, sticky rates) does not clearly rule out.

The conservative/neutral analysts raise legitimate counterpoints that prevent full capitulation: gross margin has stayed stable near 55%, suggesting the margin hit is concentrated in SG&A/operating deleverage rather than outright price-cutting on cost of goods, which softens (but doesn't eliminate) the bear's pricing-concession thesis. Management's 15% dividend hike with a 63-year streak and 2.3x FCF coverage is a real signal of confidence, and the technical pullback (RSI 75.62→50.16, -6.6% over 11 days) looks more like normalization from overbought than a confirmed structural breakdown, since price remains above both the 50 SMA ($303.06) and 200 SMA ($276.43). However, as the neutral analyst correctly notes, we have no forward visibility confirming margin normalization — this is a bet on unconfirmed future execution, not a fact in evidence, and 1.86x leverage offers less cushion than it appears if EBITDA contracts further from an already-compressed base.

Weighing this, the totality of evidence — deteriorating profitability that is current and measured versus growth/quality signals that are backward-looking (dividend) or unconfirmed (backlog conversion) — argues for de-risking now rather than either aggressive selling or complacent holding. The trader's 20-25% trim with a $320 stop is directionally right, but the stop is arguably too tight given technical support doesn't meaningfully break until $303 (50 SMA); the more durable risk line is a close below that level combined with confirmation of persistent margin compression in Q3/Q4 commentary on Advanced Technology/Medical segment pricing.
