## THE PENTAGON INTERCEPTOR CONTRACTS AREN'T THE "LOCKED-IN REVENUE" THE AGGRESSIVE ANALYST CLAIMS

Here's where the Aggressive Analyst really loses me: they argue that the Pentagon interceptor contracts are "already awarded" and therefore "locked-in revenue." This fundamentally misunderstands how government contracts work and, more importantly, what kind of execution risk is embedded in ramping production on a complicated, margin-sensitive program.

Yes, LMT won the award. But **winning an award doesn't mean you make money on it.** Defense contractors have a long history of taking massive write-downs on programs that didn't scale as expected, that had cost overruns, or that faced technical challenges. The F-35 program is LMT's crown jewel, but it's also been a nightmare of cost overruns and schedule delays.

The "multi-year, high-volume production commitments" the Aggressive Analyst references are only profitable *if* LMT can execute production at the assumed cost structure. If there are supply chain issues, engineering delays, or labor cost inflation, those margins evaporate. And here's what nobody is talking about: **the defense industry is in a tight labor market right now.** Skilled aerospace workers are scarce. Ramping production while managing wage inflation is a real execution challenge.

The Aggressive Analyst is pricing in 12% annualized earnings growth as a "sustainable baseline." But that's predicated on flawless execution of a production ramp on a complex program. **One execution miss, one supply chain disruption, one quality issue, and that earnings model gets invalidated.**
