**Recommendation**: Hold

**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points that roughly offset each other, which is why I'm not willing to commit strongly in either direction right now.

The bull's strongest argument is the real, government-confirmed interceptor contract award to a limited set of primes (LMT included), combined with a genuinely strong Q2 print (23.4% sequential net income growth, 10.9% revenue growth QoQ) and a favorable macro backdrop (low recession odds, no near-term Fed cuts, sustained defense spending). Valuation also isn't stretched on a forward P/E basis (16.25x) relative to the growth being guided. The debt picture, viewed through Debt/EBITDA (~1.4-1.5x) and FCF coverage, is manageable rather than alarming, and management is visibly de-levering (halted buybacks, paying down debt in Q1/Q2).

The bear's strongest counters land hard, though: (1) one quarter of strong growth is not proof of a sustainable multi-year margin expansion story — defense revenue is lumpy and contract-timing driven, so extrapolating Q2 into "23-40% EPS growth in 2027" is speculative; (2) the bull's claimed 12-15% interceptor segment margins are unsupported by any disclosed segment data and are inconsistent with the company's blended 11.96% operating margin — this is a real hole in the bull case; (3) the bear's recalculation shows leverage is at the high end of "reasonable" (1.5-1.6x normalized net debt/EBITDA) with zero room for execution missteps, not the "conservative" framing the bull uses; (4) the persistent underperformance of LMT versus smaller, nimbler defense vendors despite the same geopolitical tailwinds is a legitimate structural yellow flag about where technology/margin leadership is shifting in the supply chain, and the bull hasn't fully rebutted it; (5) technical weakness (10% pullback) even as the "iron-clad" macro case plays out is a signal the market isn't buying the bull's growth durability assumption without more confirming data (Q3/Q4 trends, backlog visibility, capex plans).

Neither side has resolved the core uncertainty: is the Q2 acceleration a durable production ramp or a lumpy one-off contract timing effect? Until we get Q3/Q4 confirmation and actual segment-level margin disclosure on interceptor programs, I can't underwrite the bull's aggressive EPS growth assumptions, but I also can't ignore that the fundamental setup (real contracts, reasonable valuation, de-leveraging trend) doesn't support an aggressive bearish stance either. This is a genuine case where the evidence is balanced enough to warrant patience rather than conviction in either direction.

**Strategic Actions**: Maintain current position size — neither add aggressively nor trim materially until more data resolves the debate. Specifically:

1. Hold existing exposure; do not initiate new buying at current levels given the unresolved margin/backlog visibility question, but do not sell into a name with a real contract catalyst and reasonable valuation either.
2. Set a watch list of confirming/disconfirming signals before adjusting the rating: (a) Q3/Q4 2026 revenue and margin trends to see if the Q2 acceleration sustains or reverts, (b) any management disclosure of segment-level margins for interceptor/missile production, (c) backlog and book-to-bill data confirming multi-year visibility versus one-off timing, (d) continued debt paydown trajectory and whether buybacks resume (a premature resumption would be a red flag on capital discipline).
3. Use the recent 10% pullback as a level to consider modest opportunistic adds only if accompanied by confirming fundamental data (e.g., a strong Q3 print with margin detail) — do not average down purely on the macro/geopolitical narrative alone.
4. Hedge or trim if leverage metrics deteriorate further (net debt/EBITDA pushing toward or above 1.6-1.7x) or if smaller-vendor outperformance persists/widens, as that would validate the structural competitiveness concern raised by the bear.
5. Reassess formally after next earnings release with updated backlog, margin, and capex disclosures — be ready to upgrade to Overweight/Buy if execution confirms the ramp, or downgrade to Underweight if leverage worsens without offsetting growth confirmation.
