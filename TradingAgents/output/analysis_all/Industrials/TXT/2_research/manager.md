**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make reasonable points, but when I weigh the evidence, the bear case is more grounded in hard data while the bull case leans heavily on narrative and extrapolation. Key considerations: (1) Valuation - the 10.91x forward P/E and 0.93 PEG look attractive on the surface, but that cheapness coincides with a stock that has fallen 17% in 35 days and broken below all major moving averages (10 EMA, 50 SMA, 200 SMA) with RSI at 30.67 and a deteriorating MACD (-2.32 and expanding negative histogram) - this is a persistent downtrend, not a one-day oversold blip, and low volume suggests capitulation without buyers stepping in, not a 'gift' entry point. (2) The Citation/CJ4 story is compelling on paper (500th delivery, Gen3 launch) but the bear's point that Q2 revenue ($3.83B) is actually down sequentially from Q4 2025 ($4.18B) undercuts the 'accelerating demand' narrative, and Gen3 revenue is speculative/future, not in current numbers. (3) Margin expansion is real but modest (77bps over 18 months) and gross margins have actually compressed (18.3%->17.6%), meaning the operating margin gains are from SG&A leverage, not unit economics - this has a natural ceiling. (4) Most concerning is the cash flow volatility: Q1 2026 operating cash flow was negative (-$117M) and FCF was -$250M, which is a real red flag for working capital deterioration (inventory build, receivables issues) that doesn't square with a smoothly accelerating growth story. Q2 recovered to $304M but the swing itself signals instability. (5) On macro, the bull's defense/rate argument cuts both ways - the bear correctly notes elevated rates are a headwind for discretionary corporate jet purchases, and defense budget growth is low-single-digit, not a re-rating catalyst. Given the combination of persistent bearish technical momentum, sequential revenue deceleration, cash flow deterioration, and a margin story that's more limited than advertised, I don't think this is simply a case of an oversold stock ripe for mean reversion - it looks more like a stock where the market's post-earnings response is correctly pricing in execution risk. I don't think it's a full-blown Sell (the low valuation and defense stability provide some downside cushion, and the business isn't collapsing), but the risk/reward skews negative enough that adding to or holding a full position isn't warranted. The technical breakdown plus the cash flow red flags tip the scale toward trimming exposure rather than defending it as unassailable value.

**Strategic Actions**: Trim existing TXT exposure by roughly 25-30% into any near-term bounce rather than adding on the 'oversold' dip - the trend is broken (below all major moving averages, negative/expanding MACD) and volume patterns show institutional distribution without retail/dip-buying support. Do not initiate new long positions at current levels; wait for confirmation of either (a) a technical base with volume-backed reversal above the 50 SMA, or (b) a Q3 print that shows sequential revenue re-acceleration in Citation deliveries and two consecutive quarters of positive operating cash flow before re-engaging. Set a hard risk level below the recent Bollinger low (~$79.25) - a decisive close below that on volume should trigger further de-risking toward a full Sell. For remaining exposure, consider hedging via defense-sector peers or protective puts given the binary risk around whether the $7.26 forward EPS guide is achievable, since a miss would likely compress the multiple further from already-depressed sentiment. Reassess at next earnings for confirmation of margin ceiling (watch gross margin trend, not just operating margin) and working capital normalization (inventory/receivables vs. revenue growth) before considering a return to Hold or Overweight.
