## I. THE BULL'S VALUATION THESIS IS BUILT ON SHIFTED GOALPOSTS

### **The "Cheap Forward P/E" Is a Mirage Masking Deteriorating Confidence**

The bull correctly notes that TXT trades at a forward P/E of 10.91x. But here's what's *actually happening*: that "attractive valuation" exists **because the market has lost confidence in the guidance itself.**

Let me be precise:

**The forward EPS guidance of $7.26 was *lowered or maintained conservatively*, not raised with enthusiasm.** When a company provides guidance and the stock *immediately declines 2.7%*, what the market is telling you is: *"We don't believe these numbers, or we're pricing in significant downside risk to achieving them."*

This is crucial: **A cheap valuation multiple on suspect guidance is not a discount—it's a warning label.**

#### **The Real Question: Can TXT Actually Deliver $7.26 EPS?**

Look at the actual trajectory:
- **TTM EPS**: $5.28
- **Forward Guidance**: $7.26
- **Implied Growth**: +37.5%

That's not incremental improvement—that's a *dramatic* earnings expansion in a single year. Now, where does this growth come from? The bull cites Citation momentum and margin expansion. But let's examine whether those drivers are sustainable:

### **Citation Momentum: A Dangerous Extrapolation**

The bull celebrated the 500th Citation CJ4 delivery as "proof of sustained demand." I'd argue it's actually a **warning sign of deceleration disguised as a milestone.**

**Here's why:** Milestone announcements (100th, 500th, 1000th delivery) are *typically* made when:
1. Production rates are plateauing (not accelerating)
2. Management needs a narrative boost for investor sentiment
3. Sequential growth is slowing and needs to be reframed as cumulative success

Consider: **If Citation demand was truly accelerating, why isn't the company guiding to substantially *higher* revenue growth rates for the remainder of 2026?**

The company achieved Q2 revenue of $3.83B, which is **actually 8.5% *below* Q4 2025 levels of $4.18B**. That's a sequential *decline*, not acceleration. The bull wants us to believe Citation is firing on all cylinders, but the revenue trajectory tells a different story.

**The CJ4 Gen3 launch is forward-looking PR, not current revenue.** Gen3 aircraft haven't shipped yet. We don't have firm orders. We're being asked to value a product that exists only in PowerPoint presentations.

---

### **The Margin Expansion Narrative Ignores the Cost Compression Limit**

The bull touts expanding operating margins (6.2% → 6.97%) as evidence of operational excellence. **But this represents just 77 basis points of improvement over 18 months—with acknowledged inflationary headwinds.**

Here's the uncomfortable truth: **Textron is likely near the margin expansion ceiling.**

Why? Because:

1. **Gross margins have actually *compressed* from 18.3% (2024) to 17.6% (TTM)** — that's a 70 bp decline in the raw profitability of the business. The operating margin expansion is coming almost entirely from operational leverage (SG&A absorption), not from improved unit economics.

2. **The company is absorbing cost inflation through pricing and productivity, but there's a natural limit.** Defense and aerospace customers aren't passive; they have budget constraints and competitive supplier options. At some point, pricing discipline hits a wall.

3. **The working capital deterioration signals margin pressure ahead.** Look at this data:
   - **Q1 2026 Operating Cash Flow: -$117M** (negative!)
   - **Q2 2026 Operating Cash Flow: $304M** (recovery)
   - **This volatility suggests working capital is *consuming* cash in certain periods**, likely due to inventory and receivables buildup ahead of sales. This is a red flag for collection delays or inventory obsolescence risk.

The bull is extrapolating 77 bp of margin improvement into perpetuity. The data suggests we're approaching the limit.
