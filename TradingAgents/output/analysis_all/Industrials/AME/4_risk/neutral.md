And here's the really problematic part: the Aggressive Analyst keeps saying "the market isn't worried about the balance sheet; it's rewarding cash generation." The market is essentially saying: "We like the cash, but we're nervous about the leverage." That nervousness is being expressed as a compression relative to peers with lower debt ratios.

So the question is: does that nervousness get validated or does it get dismissed? The Aggressive Analyst is betting it gets dismissed because FCF covers the debt maturity. But that's a *base-case assumption*, not a risk-adjusted view. And here's where the macro backdrop becomes critically important.

The market is saying: "We like AME's cash generation, but we're paying you a meaningful discount relative to Roper because we're not confident that cash converts to shareholder value given your leverage profile."

That discount already exists. It's already priced in. So the question becomes: does the market decide to bridge that gap, or does it decide to widen it further?
