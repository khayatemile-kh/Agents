**Recommendation**: Hold

**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points that roughly offset each other, which is why I'm not comfortable committing capital aggressively in either direction right now. The bull case is real: WAB has genuinely strong TTM fundamentals—17%+ revenue growth, margin expansion (operating margin up 150bps YoY, EBITDA margin at 23%), 86%+ FCF conversion, $1.1B TTM FCF, and a secular narrative around rail modernization (FLXdrive, Norfolk Southern retrofit work) that gives the stock a legitimate growth-driven bull thesis. A PEG of 1.37 isn't crazy for an industrial compounding earnings at double-digit rates, and the recent 8.4% pullback with RSI near 39 does look more like digestion of a July breakout than a broken thesis.

But the bear case lands some real punches that I can't wave away. The quarter-over-quarter growth data is the crux: Q1 2026 revenue grew only 2.2% YoY while Q2 2026 jumped 17.4% YoY — that's a huge swing that smells like lumpy order timing rather than a clean acceleration story, and it directly undermines the bull's use of the Q2 number as the new run-rate. If normalized growth is closer to 9-10% rather than high-teens, then the 22.5x forward P/E and the $12.44 forward EPS assumption embedded in the bull case are much harder to justify — that gap between "what's priced in" and "what's demonstrably sustainable" is the single biggest risk here. The bear is also right that the Norfolk Southern contract is a headline, not yet quantified revenue in the reported financials, so leaning on it as the anchor of the multi-year growth story is speculative until it shows up in the numbers. And the margin expansion, while real, has been modest sequentially (Q1 23.7% -> Q2 23.0% EBITDA margin bps math the bear cites) and is occurring against a backdrop of inflation/tariff headwinds the company is only "outperforming despite," not "growing because of" — that's a subtler, less durable tailwind than the bull frames it as.

Net-net: this isn't a stock I'd call cheap (37.6x trailing) nor one I think is broken. The fundamentals are good, the secular story has real substance, but current pricing already assumes sustained high-teens growth that the underlying quarterly data doesn't yet confirm, and the biggest single catalyst (Norfolk Southern-driven retrofit revenue) hasn't been validated in reported financials. Given the balanced, well-supported arguments on both sides — strong FCF/execution vs. growth-rate ambiguity and valuation-priced-for-perfection risk — a Hold is the honest call: maintain current exposure, don't add aggressively into strength at these multiples, but don't exit a business with this quality of cash generation and secular positioning on the back of one soft quarter.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Maintain current position size — do not add and do not trim materially until the Q3 2026 print clarifies whether the 17.4% YoY growth was durable or a one-off (watch specifically for sequential revenue growth and whether Q1's 2.2% YoY anomaly repeats).
2) Set a valuation discipline trigger: if forward P/E expands materially above ~23-24x without a corresponding upward revision to consensus forward EPS (i.e., the growth doesn't show up to justify the multiple), consider trimming toward Underweight.
3) Track Norfolk Southern and other retrofit contract revenue recognition explicitly in upcoming filings/earnings calls — an explicit dollar disclosure or contribution to backlog would upgrade the thesis to Overweight/Buy; continued silence or delay is a reason to downgrade.
4) Use any further technical pullback toward oversold levels (RSI <35, price toward $260-270 support) as an opportunity to average in modestly if fundamentals hold, rather than chasing strength near prior highs.
5) Monitor margin trajectory quarter-over-quarter (EBITDA margin, operating margin) for signs of the bear's "peak margin" thesis playing out — deceleration or reversal in margin expansion would be an incremental bearish signal.
6) Keep position sized at a neutral, benchmark-like weight (i.e., not underweight, not overweight) reflecting genuinely balanced risk/reward until one of the above triggers resolves the debate.
