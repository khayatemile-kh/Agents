Strip away the rhetoric from both analysts, and the real debate is about this question: **Will GEV's margins recover to the 23% TTM level, or will Q2's 6% margin become the new normalized baseline?**

The aggressive analyst assumes recovery. They point to backlog conversion and structural demand. The conservative analyst assumes deterioration. They point to accelerating working capital stress and margin compression.

First, the TTM 23% margin included some one-time gains. The fundamental analysis report mentions $4.41 billion in one-time items that inflated Q1 2026 results. Strip those out, and the normalized TTM margin is closer to 15-17%, not 23%. The aggressive analyst has anchored to an unsustainably high number.

Second, the Q2 6% margin reflects a specific backlog composition—likely early-stage, lower-margin contracts. As the company cycles through the backlog and moves to later-stage, higher-margin projects, margins should improve sequentially. But they won't return to the inflated TTM number because the backlog itself is likely lower-margin than peak pricing power would suggest. Why? Because GEV signed many of these contracts during the boom, before margin pressure emerged.

So the realistic base case is: margins improve from Q2's 6% to maybe 14-16% by 2027, settling into a sustainable 12-15% range long-term. That's still healthy for industrials, but it's materially lower than what the aggressive analyst is implying when they defend the 23% TTM figure.

## **THE MACRO CONTEXT THAT BREAKS THE TIE**

So the macro backdrop is selectively bullish for GEV: strong for power infrastructure, weak for discretionary industrial capex. That's a net positive if GEV's backlog is truly weighted toward power and grid modernization.

But—and this is critical—it doesn't justify the current valuation. It justifies maybe 22-24x forward multiples, not 36.77x. And that's assuming flawless execution.
