The core of the bull case leans on a single contract announcement and DCF/analyst commentary whose assumptions aren't visible or verifiable in the debate, while the bear's critique of unproven, unscaled BESS contribution and intensifying competition (Tesla Energy, Eos, Form Energy, in-house hyperscaler engineering) is more grounded in what's actually knowable right now. Given the stock is in a confirmed technical downtrend with legitimate execution-risk concerns underpinning the recent selloff, and the bull case requires taking a lot on faith (contract size, margin, ramp), I don't think this qualifies as a high-conviction Buy/Overweight, but it's also not a full conviction Sell — the core industrial business (engines, powertrain) remains a real cash-generative franchise, and there is genuine optionality in BESS/data-center power that shouldn't be dismissed outright. The prudent stance is to lean cautious given the unresolved catalyst risk and heavy technical damage, while not exiting a position that retains real underlying industrial value and optionality — this points to trimming exposure rather than adding into a falling knife or holding blindly.
