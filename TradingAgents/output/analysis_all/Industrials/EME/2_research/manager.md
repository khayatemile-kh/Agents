**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points that roughly offset. This isn't just noise - it directly undermines the bull's PEG-based valuation thesis. The bear's sensitivity table is compelling: if forward EPS growth comes in at 10% instead of the assumed 14.6%, the stock is already roughly fairly valued at current price, and if growth slows to 5% (plausible given margin instability and lower-margin data center mix), there's ~16% downside. That means the 'historic disconnect' the bull cites has much less margin of safety than advertised once you stress-test the growth assumption embedded in the PEG ratio. On technicals, the bear's evidence is broader and more current: price is below the 10 EMA, 50 SMA, and 200 SMA simultaneously, MACD histogram is sharply negative with a bearish crossover, and RSI at 39.55 - while not deeply oversold - confirms broad-based deterioration across multiple timeframes rather than an isolated short-term dip. The bull's reframe of oversold RSI as 'capitulation buying' is speculative and not supported by confirming reversal signals in the data provided. Given genuinely strong fundamentals (backlog, balance sheet, returns) counterbalanced by real execution/margin risk and a technical picture that is bearish across multiple timeframes with a valuation that has little cushion if growth normalizes down, this is a case where the evidence is balanced enough that conviction on either side isn't fully earned. I'm not comfortable calling this a Buy given the margin instability and technical deterioration, but I'm also not comfortable calling it a Sell given the balance sheet strength and real secular demand tailwind. This nets out to Hold - maintain existing exposure, don't add aggressively into technical weakness, but don't exit a fundamentally sound franchise either.
