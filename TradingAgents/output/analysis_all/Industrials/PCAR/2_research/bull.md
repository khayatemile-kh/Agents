Here's why this matters operationally:

1. **Pricing Power:** When foreign competitors face 20-30% tariffs, suddenly PCAR can raise prices 5-10% and still undercut imports. That directly flows to margins.
2. **Market Share Gains:** PCAR can take share from international OEMs without sacrificing profitability. The math is compelling.
3. **Revenue Growth:** If tariff policy persists (likely through 2028 at minimum), PCAR doesn't just maintain current volumes—it gains incremental share from lower-cost imports that are now uncompetitive.

**Quantifying the upside:** If tariff policy adds just 5-10% to PCAR's addressable market share, that's $1.5-2.0 billion in incremental annual revenue at current pricing. At PCAR's 12% operating margin, that's **$180-240 million in operating income—or $0.35-0.46 per share in earnings upside.** On a 17.15x forward multiple, that's $6-8 per share of additional value.

**Bear concern I anticipate:** "Tariff policy is uncertain; it could reverse."

**My response:** True, but that's already embedded in the discount! The market is pricing PCAR at 17x forward earnings *despite* tariff tailwinds. If policy reverses, we're back to fair value.
