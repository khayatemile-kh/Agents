**Rationale**: The debate hinges on whether the 61% forward EPS growth ($2.43 trailing to $3.91 forward) is credible, and whose accounting is done properly. Net income growth funded partly by rising debt (1.78x net debt/EBITDA, "stable for 3 quarters" per the bull's own framing, i.e., not de-levering) is consistent with the bear's story of debt-fueled growth rather than organic value creation.

Third, and most damaging to the bull case: the bear cites a documented 160bps YoY gross margin contraction (43.7% to 42.1%) alongside revenue growth. Margin compression during a period of reported revenue acceleration is a legitimate warning sign of pricing pressure, mix shift, or cost inflation that isn't being passed through — and it directly contradicts the bull's claim that "you don't achieve 12% net margins through poor capital allocation" being proof of quality. Margins moving the wrong direction while multiples still price in near-flawless 61% EPS growth is an asymmetric risk to the downside, not the upside.

The bull's strongest points — the macro backdrop being roughly neutral-to-supportive (89% odds of no cuts just means stability, not a headwind by itself; 8% recession probability is low) and the technical oversold bounce potential (RSI ~31) — are real, but they're secondary. They argue for "this isn't a crisis," not for "this is undervalued and safe to add to." The core valuation case (PEG 0.61, 19.1x forward P/E cheap) is entirely dependent on the $3.91 forward EPS number holding up, and the bear gave a specific, credible mechanism (unrealistic implied 2H26 EPS acceleration of ~$1.48, absent margin expansion, unexplained) for why that number could be revised down. Until there's a clean quarter that either confirms the normalized earnings trend or resolves the margin compression, the risk/reward skews toward the bear's structural concerns rather than the bull's re-rating story. This doesn't rise to a full Sell — there's real revenue growth, low recession risk, and no debt-market distress signal (per the bull) — but conviction in the bull thesis's foundation (earnings quality and margin trajectory) is too shaky to stay neutral or add exposure.
