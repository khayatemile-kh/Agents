## The ROE Critique Conflates Two Separate Problems

The aggressive analyst argues that ROE of 9.47% is "below the cost of equity" and therefore represents value destruction. This is where the analysis becomes dangerously imprecise.

The cost of equity for IR is probably in the 8-10% range given its beta, size, and dividend yield. That's not exciting, but it's also not a catastrophic indictment of management.

More importantly, **ROE is a lagging metric.** It's calculated on historical net income divided by historical equity. If the company is experiencing margin compression now but is expected to deliver earnings growth, then ROE should *improve* forward-looking. The aggressive analyst is using a backward-looking metric to justify an aggressive forward-looking sell recommendation, which is analytically inconsistent.

The real question isn't "is ROE adequate?" The real question is "are management's capital allocation decisions creating a pathway to *higher* returns going forward?"
