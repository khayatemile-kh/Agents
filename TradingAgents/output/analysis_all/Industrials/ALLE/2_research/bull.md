**ALLE's business is not a mortgage origination or commercial construction play.** 

The company sells **access control systems, locks, and security hardware.** These are:

1. **Maintenance & replacement-driven.** Even if new construction slows, existing buildings still need security upgrades, lock replacements, and digital system retrofits. This is *recurring revenue*, not lumpy capex-dependent.

2. **Essential spending.** Security is not discretionary. Banks, hospitals, government facilities, and corporations will maintain their access control systems regardless of the rate environment. The 2008-09 financial crisis proved this—security spending held up better than most industrial categories.

3. **Increasingly software/SaaS-driven.** Our sentiment report and news headlines explicitly note the tailwind from **IoT and cloud-based access control adoption.** These are high-margin, recurring-revenue streams that are *less sensitive to interest rates* than capital-intensive hardware projects. **The company's gross margin expansion to 45.2% reflects exactly this mix shift toward higher-margin software/services.**

**The Fed holding rates steady is actually neutral-to-positive for ALLE:**
- Neutral for maintenance/replacement demand (not rate-sensitive)
- *Positive* for operating margins (less price competition as weaker competitors struggle with higher financing costs)
- *Positive* for M&A opportunities (ALLE's strong FCF generation allows strategic acquisitions while competitors are capital-constrained)
