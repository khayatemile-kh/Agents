**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both analysts agree on the core operating story: ALLE has genuinely expanded margins (gross margin +480bps to 45.2%, operating margin +310bps to 21.1%) and grown earnings at a healthy clip (40.5% cumulative over three years) while generating substantial free cash flow ($685.7M in 2025, ~15.9% FCF margin). Where they diverge is on durability and balance-sheet trajectory. The bear's strongest point is the one the bull never actually rebuts: TTM free cash flow appears to be decelerating sharply (~$484M annualized run-rate vs. $685.7M in FY2025) even as total debt has stayed flat-to-up ($2,031M) and debt/EBITDA is drifting back up toward ~2.0x-2.1x. That combination — decelerating cash generation plus static leverage — is a real yellow flag heading into a year where the Fed is priced for zero cuts (89% probability), because it means the balance sheet cushion the bull leans on for 'M&A optionality' is thinner than advertised, and refinancing/renewal costs stay elevated longer.

The bull's rebuttal to macro risk (security spending is 'essential' and rate-insensitive) is only half right: replacement/maintenance demand is genuinely more defensive, but the bear correctly notes that a meaningful share of ALLE's revenue is tied to project-based commercial/CRE construction and retrofit activity, which is exactly the cyclical, rate-sensitive piece that softens when cap rates are elevated and vacancy is high. The bull never quantifies what fraction of revenue is truly maintenance-driven versus project-driven, so that argument remains more assertion than proof on both sides — but the burden of proof was on the bull to defend the more aggressive 31.8% implied forward EPS growth, and instead of defending it with segment-level evidence, the bull backed into a lower, more defensible 10-12% organic growth scenario, which effectively concedes that the market's forward multiple may be pricing in growth that's hard to underwrite from the fundamentals presented.

On valuation, the PEG of ~2.0x and forward P/E of 15.87x aren't outrageous for an industrials name with double-digit margin expansion, so I don't think this is a name to actively short or exit — the bear didn't establish that the business is broken, only that the more optimistic bull growth assumptions are shaky and that leverage/cash-flow deceleration warrants caution before adding. Given the stock has already corrected ~10% off its August high on unusually heavy volume (6.5x normal), some of this risk is being priced in by the market already, which argues against a Sell. But the unresolved leverage/FCF deceleration risk, the real exposure to CRE/capex cycle softness via project-based revenue, and the risk of Q3/Q4 guidance cuts into a seasonally weak reporting window argue against adding exposure here or treating the pullback as an unambiguous buying opportunity.

Net: this is a name with good underlying operating quality but a debate that surfaced real near-term balance-sheet and cyclical-revenue risk that the bull didn't adequately neutralize. That combination points to trimming/capping exposure rather than either aggressively buying the dip or moving to an outright sell — a genuinely mixed picture tilted slightly bearish on near-term catalysts (leverage trend, FCF deceleration, seasonal guidance-cut risk), even though the long-term fundamental story (margin expansion, cash generation, capital returns) remains intact.

5) Retain a core position rather than exiting entirely — the underlying margin expansion, gross margin trajectory, and capital return program ($255M returned to shareholders in 2025) are real and not currently broken, so this is a sizing/risk-management call, not a thesis-ending one.
