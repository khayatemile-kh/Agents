## I. THE FUNDAMENTAL DISCONNECT: OPERATING EXCELLENCE ≠ STOCK UPSIDE

### **A. The Bull Confuses EBITDA Growth with Cash Flow Protection**

The bull correctly identifies 40.5% earnings growth from 2022–2025 and celebrates the 310 bps margin expansion. **I concede the point on operational execution.** Allegion *has* improved margins and grown earnings.

**But here's the critical error:** This operational progress has **not translated into free cash flow resilience or balance sheet strength.** In fact, the opposite is occurring:

**The leverage picture is deteriorating:**

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | TTM (Sept 2026) | Trend |
|--------|------|------|------|------|-----------------|--------|
| **Total Debt** | $1,847M | $1,956M | $2,011M | $2,031M | $2,031M | **↑ Flat to rising** |
| **Net Income** | $458M | $540M | $597M | $643.8M | $658.6M | ↑ Growing |
| **Free Cash Flow** | $595M | $516M | $582.9M | $685.7M | ~$484M (TTM annualized Q1-Q2 2026) | **↓ Declining** |
| **Debt-to-EBITDA** | 1.98x | 1.98x | 1.95x | 1.93x | **2.00x–2.10x (est.)** | **↑ Tightening** |

**Notice what's happening:** Despite $658.6M in TTM net income, the company's *free cash flow generation is slowing* (Q1-Q2 2026 run rate of ~$484M annualized vs. $685.7M in 2025). Meanwhile, **debt has remained flat or slightly risen** despite claims of "debt reduction."

**Why does this matter?** Because in a high-rate environment where refinancing costs are escalating, you want *declining* leverage, not stagnant leverage. ALLE's debt-to-EBITDA is creeping back toward 2.0x, which is **the red line for industrial companies** if macro conditions deteriorate.

**The bull's claim of "strong FCF generation allowing M&A"** is actually masking a problem: the company is using cash flow to *maintain* debt levels and fund acquisitions, not to *de-lever.* That's a yellow flag in a persistently high-rate environment.

### **B. Gross Margin Expansion Masks Product Mix Volatility and Pricing Pressure Ahead**

The bull highlights 480 bps of gross margin expansion from 40.4% (2022) to 45.2% (2025) as evidence of pricing power and high-margin SaaS/IoT adoption.

**I see a different story in the data:**

Let's break down what's driving that margin expansion:

1. **Mix shift toward software/SaaS:** The bull's claim that IoT adoption is "high-margin and recurring" is *aspirational, not yet proven at scale.* Yes, ALLE is investing in digital products, but look at the actual revenue composition:
   - Traditional hardware and mechanical locks still represent the **vast majority of revenue** (likely 70-75%+ based on typical security products company composition)
   - SaaS/recurring revenue is still a *nascent* component of total revenue
   - **The gross margin expansion is happening DESPITE rising input costs and commodity inflation**, which suggests either aggressive pricing or significant operating leverage—neither of which is sustainable indefinitely

2. **Pricing power is peaking:** A 480 bps margin expansion over 3 years in the industrial security space is **exceptional**, and frankly, unsustainable. Here's why:
   - Commercial customers (the largest revenue segment) are increasingly price-sensitive as they face their own margin pressure
   - The Fed holding rates at elevated levels means customers' cost of capital is high—they're *not* willing to pay premium prices for hardware upgrades
   - Competitors (Assa Abloy, Dormakaba, Ingersoll Rand) are all competing for the same customer base and have similar cost structures
   - **Pricing power typically compresses when end-market demand softens, which is exactly what we're seeing in commercial real estate**

3. **The real risk:** Gross margin will revert as:
   - Input costs stabilize (no more "pricing through" inflation)
   - Competition intensifies on pricing (CRE weakness forces competitors to compete)
   - Product mix shifts back toward lower-margin hardware (not all customers upgrade to SaaS)
   
   **A 200-300 bps gross margin compression from current levels is entirely plausible**—which would wipe out much of the operating leverage gains the bull is citing.

## II. MACROECONOMIC ENVIRONMENT IS MUCH WORSE THAN THE BULL ADMITS

### **A. "Essential Spending" Argument Ignores Capex Cycle Reality**

The bull claims security spending is "essential" and will hold up even if rates stay elevated. **This is a dangerous mischaracterization.**

Yes, *maintenance* spending on existing systems is relatively resilient. But here's what the bull is glossing over:

**ALLE's actual revenue mix is heavily weighted toward *project-based* spending, not maintenance:**

- **Commercial office/CRE segment:** New construction, office retrofits, and facility upgrades are *highly cyclical* and rate-sensitive. When builders and landlords are facing 7%+ cap rates and declining occupancy, they **postpone security system upgrades.**
- **Multifamily:** Yes, housing is resilient, but multifamily *construction* is already slowing due to high interest rates. And existing units don't require frequent lock/access system overhauls.
- **Government & Institutional:** Budgets are constrained; discretionary security spending gets cut first.

**The macro data supports my concern:**

From our macro report:
- **89% probability of NO rate cuts in 2026** — This means customers' cost of capital remains elevated all year
- **Commercial real estate is facing structural headwinds** — Remote work, higher vacancy rates, and rising costs are delaying projects
- **Corporate capex cycles are softening** — Even with low recession risk (8% probability), companies are becoming more cautious with spending

**The bull handwaves this by saying it's "priced in."** Let me be specific: **It is NOT priced in.** Here's why:

1. **Analyst guidance embedded in forward P/E assumes 31.8% EPS growth.** The bull calculated this at 15.87x forward P/E. But that forward estimate was likely made *before* the recent weakness became apparent. As quarterly results roll in with softer project pipelines, consensus estimates will get cut—and that's when the stock re-rates downward.

2. **ALLE's stock peaked at $168.94 on August 7 before falling to $151.82 on Sept 1**—a 10.4% correction in just 3 weeks. **Institutional investors are already front-running the guidance cut.** The capitulation on August 31 (7.47M shares, 6.5x normal volume) suggests smart money was selling into strength.

3. **Seasonal patterns matter:** September/October is typically when companies warn on guidance. ALLE is likely to miss Q3 expectations and guide Q4 lower due to softening order flow in CRE.
