On technicals: RSI of 27 and price near the lower Bollinger Band do argue for a near-term bounce, and I don't dismiss that a mean-reversion trade toward the 50-SMA is plausible. But an oversold reading is a trading signal about the next few weeks, not evidence that the underlying earnings power or balance sheet risk has improved. The bull's technical argument does not offset the deteriorating fundamental trend.

The structural tailwinds (HVAC replacement cycles, data center cooling, building codes) are real and worth acknowledging as a long-term positive, but they don't resolve the near-term margin compression and leverage risk that's showing up in the actual reported financials right now. A long growth runway doesn't protect the stock if the company can't convert growth into margin expansion, and current data shows the opposite is happening.

Net: the bear's argument is grounded in harder, verifiable YoY financial deterioration (margins, leverage trajectory, tangible book value) while the bull's argument is built substantially on technical exhaustion signals and optimistic reinterpretation of the same data. That tips this toward a cautious stance rather than a genuine 'balanced' toss-up. I'm not going full Sell because the technical setup and long-term secular tailwinds provide some legitimate offsetting upside potential and the valuation discount, even if overstated, isn't zero — but the fundamental deterioration and balance sheet fragility warrant reducing exposure rather than holding steady or adding.
