**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the near-term operational strength: LEAP deliveries +41% YoY, revenue +20.6% YoY, deferred revenue of $19.2B providing multi-year visibility, and margin expansion (31.1% gross, 20.6% operating). Where they diverge is on whether the current 36-39x forward P/E is justified.

The bear's strongest points that I can't dismiss: (1) the valuation math is genuinely demanding — at ~36x forward P/E with PEG near 4x, the market is pricing in near-perpetual high-single-digit-plus growth, which leaves very little room for error if LEAP delivery growth decelerates as backlogs normalize (a well-documented pattern in aerospace cycles); (2) the extreme leverage metric (113x D/E) cited is a real balance-sheet fragility flag, even if driven by accounting/pension/spin-off mechanics rather than operating distress — it still means equity value is highly sensitive to any earnings disappointment; (3) technical picture (RSI 36.66, MACD negative, price below moving averages, -13.2% from August peak) confirms the market is actively repricing risk right now, not just noise; (4) GEK800/defense TAM is likely a rounding error near-term ($100-200M/yr) versus the bull's $2-3B framing, so it shouldn't be leaned on as a valuation support; (5) deferred revenue, while real, is subject to margin-risk-at-recognition and program timing slippage, a legitimate aerospace-specific risk.

The bull's strongest points I do credit: LEAP has genuine structural demand support (huge OEM backlogs, most-produced narrowbody family), margins are expanding not compressing today, cash generation is real and funding buybacks/dividends, and GE Vernova's repositioning toward AI-driven power demand is a legitimate optionality call. But these support a 'good business' thesis, not necessarily a 'good entry point right now' thesis — the bull never adequately rebuts the core valuation-compression risk or the technical deterioration, and leans on speculative multi-year defense TAM figures the bear credibly cut down.

Net: This is a quality business trading at a valuation that has very little margin of safety, currently in a confirmed technical downtrend on real risk repricing (not just macro noise). The right stance is not full Sell — the deferred revenue book and LEAP franchise are too strong to justify exiting a core aerospace holding entirely — but definitely not to add or hold at current levels either. The prudent path is to trim/de-risk exposure now, waiting for either a valuation reset closer to a 25-28x multiple or clear technical stabilization (reclaiming the 50/200-day) before considering re-adding. This is an Underweight call, not Hold, because the bear's valuation and technical arguments are quantitatively stronger and current price action confirms the market agrees.
