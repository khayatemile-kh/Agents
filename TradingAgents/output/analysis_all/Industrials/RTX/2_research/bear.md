## II. THE BULL'S BACKLOG NARRATIVE IS FICTION

### $289B Backlog Sounds Great Until You Examine the Details

The bull presents the $289B backlog as a "fortress" with "3.1 years of revenue visibility." This is misleading in several critical ways.

**First: Backlog Doesn't Equal Profit**

- RTX's gross margin is only 20.3%
- On $289B in backlog, that's ~$58.8B in gross profit
- Operating margin is 12.7%, so net operating profit = ~$36.7B
- Net margin is 8.3%, so actual profit = ~$24B

**But here's the kicker:** The bull assumes backlog converts at historical margins. **Inflation is eroding margins across the aerospace sector.**

The data shows:
- **GE Aerospace facing "inflationary cost pressures"** (per the research)
- **Boeing reporting margin compression** on defense contracts
- **TransDigm cost inflation visible** in Q3 results

**RTX is no exception.** The Tomahawk contract, while large, commits RTX to ramping production from 60 to 1,000 units annually. That's a massive manufacturing scale-up in an inflationary environment. The company is likely to *lose margin* on this contract as it absorbs hiring, facility expansion, and supply chain costs.

**Real backlog value ≠ $24B in future profit. More like $15-18B, depending on execution.**

---

**Second: Backlog Composition Risk—The Commercial Lie**

The bull sells the idea that 50%+ of the backlog is "commercial aerospace," which supposedly hedges geopolitical risk.

**This is misleading.** Here's why:

1. **Commercial Aerospace Is Cyclical and Vulnerable to Recession**
   - Prediction markets say 8% recession probability, but that's *optimistic* long-term forecasting
   - Aerospace orders collapse during recessions (see 2008-2009, 2020)
   - The backlog was built during the post-pandemic recovery; if travel demand softens even slightly, order cancellations could spike

2. **Airline Capex Cycles Are Predictable—And Turning**
   - Airlines typically order in *clusters* during peak profitability
   - Current high fuel prices and labor pressures are squeezing airline margins
   - The 5-10 year commercial runway the bull assumes could easily compress to 2-3 years if airlines defer orders

3. **Commercial Backlog ≠ Locked-In Revenue**
   - Commercial aerospace orders often have *cancellation clauses*
   - Unlike defense contracts (which are largely non-cancellable), airlines can walk away
   - During downturns (2020, 2008), we've seen commercial cancellations spike 30-40%

**The bull's claim that commercial diversifies risk is only true if the commercial backlog holds up. If it doesn't—and there are early signs of softening—RTX is exposed to a full defense-sector downturn.**
