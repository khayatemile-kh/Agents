## 1. THE "DRAYAGE ADVANTAGE" IS VASTLY OVERSTATED—AND TEMPORARY

### The Fundamental Problem the Bull Ignores

Yes, JBHT has in-house drayage operations. The bull celebrates this as a "self-reinforcing competitive moat." Let me explain why this is actually **a liability masquerading as an advantage.**

**Here's the reality:**

When you operate drayage in-house, you're **directly exposed to labor cost inflation.** You don't get to outsource the pain to third-party vendors—you absorb it entirely on your P&L.

**The data supports this:**

- **Drayage driver shortages are real, but they're industry-wide.** JBHT's answer—keeping operations in-house—doesn't solve the shortage; it just exposes JBHT more directly to wage escalation.
- **Wage inflation in trucking has been running 5-7% annually.** With JBHT's large internal driver fleet, this compounds directly to operating expense lines.
- **The bull claims JBHT captures "pricing power" from this arrangement.** But here's the problem: **pricing power in logistics is cyclical and weak when demand softens.** The current environment of 92% no-recession probability won't last forever.

### The Competitive "Moat" Will Evaporate

The bull argues that JBHT's in-house drayage creates a **"self-reinforcing"** advantage. This is wrong for three reasons:

**1. Competitors are adapting, not dying:**
- Werner, Schneider, Knight, and other major carriers are also investing in owned capacity or long-term vendor partnerships to address drayage constraints.
- The drayage shortage is a **structural industry problem, not an JBHT-exclusive opportunity.** Every carrier with scale is solving for it.
- JBHT's "advantage" isn't proprietary; it's just standard industry evolution.

**2. The drayage shortage is temporary:**
- Driver shortages ease when rates rise, attracting new entrants to the pool.
- Automation (autonomous drayage vehicles) and better scheduling software are emerging as solutions.
- By 2028-2030, this "shortage" may be completely solved, and JBHT's strategic bet on internal capacity becomes a cost albatross rather than an advantage.

**3. Outsourcing flexibility beats internal rigidity:**
- Carriers that outsource drayage maintain **operational flexibility**—they can scale up or down with demand.
- JBHT's internal model requires fixed labor costs and asset commitments. In a downturn, this becomes a liability.
- **This is exactly why the balance sheet is so leveraged**—JBHT has built a highly fixed-cost operating model.

**The bear case:** The drayage "moat" is a **temporary phenomenon that coincides with industry supply constraints.** Once those constraints ease (and they will), JBHT will be left with excess internal capacity and high fixed costs, while competitors with flexible models adapt and maintain profitability. The bull is mistaking a cyclical advantage for a structural one.

## 2. PROFITABILITY IS DETERIORATING, NOT NORMALIZING

### The Bull's "Normalized Earnings" Argument Doesn't Hold Water

The bull claims: *"2023 was an anomalous peak. 2025 earnings of $598.3M is normalized, and we're on track for $700M+ by end of 2026."*

**This is narrative manipulation.** Let me show you the actual trend:

| Year | Net Income | YoY Change | EBITDA | Operating Margin |
|------|------------|-----------|--------|-----------------|
| **2023** | $728.3M | — | $1,738.8M | 7.74% |
| **2024** | $570.9M | -21.6% | $1,599.7M | 6.88% |
| **2025** | $598.3M | +4.8% | $1,584.1M | 7.21% |
| **Q2 2026** | $181.0M | +27.8% (Q1→Q2) | $440.1M | 7.42% |

**Here's what this data actually shows:**

1. **A three-year profitability decline:** Net income fell 21.6% from 2023 to 2024, recovered only 4.8% in 2025, and remains **18% below 2023 levels.** That's not "normalization"—that's deterioration.

2. **EBITDA collapse:** EBITDA fell from $1,738.8M (2023) to $1,584.1M (2025)—a **$154.7M or 8.9% decline.** The bull wants us to ignore this because it was a "one-time" peak, but EBITDA declines are not one-time events; they reflect structural margin compression.

3. **The Q2 2026 "bounce" is a mirage:**
   - Q2 2026 net income of $181M is strong, **but Q1 2026 was only $141.6M.**
   - The bull annualizes Q2 at 4 quarters to get $724M, but this ignores **quarterly volatility and seasonality.**
   - **More importantly, sequential improvement from Q1 to Q2 is normal seasonality in trucking.** Q1 is typically weak (post-holiday, poor weather); Q2 is strong (spring pickup in demand). This is not evidence of a turnaround.

### Where the Margin Compression Is Really Coming From

The bull glosses over the fundamental issue: **unit economics are deteriorating.**

**Evidence from the fundamentals:**

- **Gross Margin:** 12.67% (Q2 2026) vs. 13.15% (Q2 2025) = **-48 bps YoY deterioration**
- **Operating Margin:** 7.42% (Q2 2026) is recovering, but still below the 7.74% (2023) long-term normal
- **Net Margin:** 5.18% (Q2 2026) vs. historical norms of 5.5-6% = compressed

**Why?** The bull's own data shows it:

1. **Freight rates are normalizing downward** after the COVID/supply-chain inflation spike of 2023.
2. **Operating costs aren't falling proportionally.** Driver wages, fuel, insurance, and maintenance remain elevated.
3. **JBHT is caught in a margin squeeze:** Revenue per mile is declining faster than costs are falling.

**The bear case:** The "normalized earnings" at $598M-$700M is actually a **lower structural level**, not a temporary dip. The company has permanently lost pricing power as freight demand cycles normalize and competition intensifies. Annualizing Q2 results ignores this reality.
