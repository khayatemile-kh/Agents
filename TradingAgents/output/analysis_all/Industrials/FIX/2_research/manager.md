**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the underlying facts, but disagree on interpretation. The bull's strongest points are real: FIX is genuinely benefiting from a structural data-center/AI infrastructure buildout, with 81.2% electrical and 40.2% mechanical segment growth, 95.8% YoY net income growth, expanding operating margins (17.1%) and EBITDA margins (18.7%), a low leverage balance sheet (0.10x normalized D/E, debt down to $328M), and consensus Buy ratings with upward earnings revisions. That's a legitimately strong operating business, not a fabrication.

However, the bear lands the more decisive blows on three fronts that matter most for a fresh capital-allocation decision today: (1) Valuation is stretched — a 38.4x trailing P/E and ~4.9x revenue multiple leaves very little margin of safety, and requires the current hyper-growth (81% segment growth) to persist for it to be justified, which is inherently unlikely given hyperscaler capex is famously lumpy and front-loaded. (2) The technical/sentiment setup has clearly deteriorated — a 17.4% drawdown from the highs, MACD histogram solidly negative, RSI approaching oversold but not confirmed reversal, and price hovering just above the 200-day SMA support. Whether this is a "coiled spring" or a broken trend is unproven; the bull's technical rebuttal is essentially hope dressed as analysis, since none of the momentum indicators have actually turned yet. (3) The regulatory overhang (72% probability of a state data-center moratorium) plus the CEO's $25.77M insider sale reflect real, current-dated risks that the bull dismisses too quickly rather than refutes with hard counter-evidence. The bear's point about working capital growing 42.3% YoY — faster than revenue — is a legitimate red flag on receivables quality/cash conversion that the bull never addressed.

Net: the fundamentals are strong, but the entry point is precarious. Paying 38x earnings for a cyclical engineering & construction contractor whose recent hypergrowth is disproportionately levered to a single secular theme (AI data-center capex) that faces documented regulatory pushback, right as insiders are taking chips off the table and the chart is breaking down, is not a low-risk place to add exposure. This doesn't invalidate the long-term structural story, but it argues against chasing the name here. The debate is not balanced 50/50 — the bear's valuation and technical-deterioration arguments are more evidence-based and actionable in the near term than the bull's "priced in" hand-waving on regulatory risk and insider selling. That tips this to a cautious/underweight stance rather than a Hold, but the strength of the underlying business (rock-solid balance sheet, real margin expansion, genuine secular tailwind) keeps this from being an outright Sell — this is a "trim into strength, don't chase, wait for a better entry" call, not a "the business is broken" call.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing FIX exposure modestly (e.g., reduce position by 20-30%) into any near-term bounce toward the $1,700-1,800 zone rather than at current oversold levels around $1,554 — don't sell into weakness, but use strength to de-risk.
2) Do NOT add new capital at current valuation (38.4x TTM P/E, ~4.9x revenue) until either (a) the stock consolidates meaningfully above the 200-day SMA ($1,477.76) with MACD/RSI confirming a genuine reversal, or (b) valuation compresses closer to 25-28x earnings, which would better reflect the cyclicality of construction/engineering multiples even with AI-driven segment growth.
3) Set a hard risk level just below the 200-day SMA (~$1,470-1,480); a decisive break below that on volume would confirm trend breakdown and warrant further de-risking toward Sell.
4) Actively monitor three catalysts before re-engaging on the buy side: (i) confirmation of organic vs. acquisition-driven electrical segment growth in next quarterly filing, (ii) any state-level data-center moratorium developments given the 72% probability flagged, and (iii) further insider transaction activity following the CEO's $25.77M sale — a second large insider sale would be a strong incremental red flag.
5) For investors who want to keep long-term secular exposure to the AI/data-center infrastructure theme, consider trimming FIX specifically (given its stretched multiple and single-name concentration risk) while keeping core thesis exposure via cash reserve for a better re-entry rather than rotating fully out of the theme.
6) Do not use leverage or add on margin here — the combination of stretched valuation, deteriorating technicals, rising working capital/receivables (42.3% YoY growth outpacing revenue), and regulatory overhang argues for patience over conviction-buying the dip.
