- This growth prompted management to **raise full-year 2026 guidance**
- The bookings environment is described as "strong," indicating forward revenue visibility

The commercial aftermarket is the most reliable, recurring revenue stream for aerospace suppliers. It's where the cash flows most predictably. A 17% YoY surge isn't a one-quarter anomaly—it reflects:

1. **Normalized Fleet Utilization:** Post-pandemic, commercial aircraft are flying closer to pre-COVID levels. More flight hours = more maintenance, repairs, and component replacements.
2. **Supply Chain Normalization:** By mid-2026, aerospace supply chains had largely stabilized, allowing airlines to normalize maintenance schedules that had been deferred during COVID.
3. **Pricing Power:** Strong aftermarket growth often includes price realization, not just volume. This is critical for margins.

**The Bear Argument I Anticipate:** *"But the commercial aerospace cycle is mature. We're near peak demand. How sustainable is 17% growth?"*

**My Rebuttal:** Let's look at peer validation. HEICO (HEI), the diversified aerospace/defense supplier, posted **record profits in Q3 2026** with strong organic growth. Astronics (ATRO) reported **record backlog**. This isn't TDG in isolation—this is an industry-wide tailwind. Hexcel (HXL) is securing long-term agreements with Boeing and Deutsche Aircraft.

If the cycle were topping, we'd see these signals reversed: margin compression, order cancellations, inventory buildups. We're not seeing that. We're seeing the opposite.

**Valuation Reality Check:** With commercial aftermarket growing at 17%, TDG's forward EPS is projected at $48.40, implying a **forward P/E of ~23.8x** at today's $1,155 price. For an aerospace company with 46% operating margins and strong FCF generation, that's not expensive—it's fairly valued to modestly attractive.

**The Context They're Missing:**

| Metric | TDG | Implication |
|--------|-----|------------|
| Debt/EBITDA (TTM) | ~6.6x | High, but manageable for aerospace |
| Interest Coverage (EBITDA/Interest) | ~9.9x | **Strong** — easily services debt |
| Free Cash Flow (TTM) | $1.46 billion | **Robust** — covers ~45% of annual debt burden |
| Operating Margin | 46% | **Exceptional** — among best-in-class |

**The Financial Engineering Narrative:**

Yes, TDG carries significant debt. But let's look at *why*:
1. **Strategic M&A:** The company has aggressively acquired smaller aerospace suppliers, integrating them into its platform. This is value-accretive if the integrations succeed (and historically, they have).
2. **Shareholder Returns:** TDG repurchased $1.09 billion in stock in Q2 alone. This signals management confidence in valuation and commitment to shareholder value.
3. **Goodwill Concentration:** 63% of assets are goodwill/intangibles—again, this reflects acquisition-driven growth, not financial distress.

**The Critical Data Point:** Despite $33.5B in debt and 46% of assets tied up in goodwill, TDG generated **$1.46 billion in free cash flow over the trailing twelve months**. That cash is being used to:
- Service debt (interest burden of ~$2.1B annually)

- Buy back stock
- Potentially fund future acquisitions

**Is the debt sustainable?** Let's run the math:
