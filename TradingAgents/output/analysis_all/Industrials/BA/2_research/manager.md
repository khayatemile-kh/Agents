**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull case rests on real but largely non-cash, long-duration catalysts: an $131B F-15 sustainment ceiling, an $85B defense backlog moving into LRIP, a 737 MAX production ramp, and a benign macro backdrop (8% recession odds). These are legitimate reasons the stock isn't a zero, and they support the idea that revenue visibility is unusually strong for an industrial name. However, the bear case lands harder on the metric that actually determines whether shareholders get paid: the ability to convert that backlog into cash and earnings without further diluting or leveraging the balance sheet. The numbers cited are stark and go largely unrebutted - debt-to-equity near 790x, negative tangible book value (-$13.2B), interest expense (~$2.6B annualized) that dwarfs the bull's own estimate of F-15 contract earnings contribution ($360M/yr), operating margins oscillating between roughly -20% and +2% over the last six quarters, and operating cash flow that has been negative in multiple recent quarters with 'positive FCF' quarters explainable mostly by one-off asset/investment sales rather than core operations. The bull's own math (F-15 contract annual earnings of ~$360M) is dwarfed by interest expense alone, which is a critical, quantifiable rebuttal the bull never addresses. Backlog and contract wins are real, but they are claims on future execution, and Boeing's trailing four-to-six quarters of execution have been inconsistent to poor, with margins near zero or negative even before absorbing the potential ~$500M+ annual cost of a labor settlement and an unresolved strike risk in the near term. The bull's argument that 'both sides have incentive to avoid a strike' is plausible but is a soft assumption, not evidence, and doesn't offset the harder balance-sheet and cash-flow facts on the table. Given that backlog conversion take years to show up in cash while the leverage, negative tangible equity, and cash-flow volatility are current and immediate risks, the bear's structural balance-sheet critique should carry more weight right now. That said, this isn't a full Sell - the defense wins and production ramp are real assets that argue against capitulating out of the position entirely, and an outright bankruptcy/collapse thesis is not well-supported either (recession odds are low, backlog is enormous, and this is a systemically important industrial/defense supplier). The balanced call is Underweight: acknowledge the long-term optionality from the backlog and defense contracts while respecting that the balance sheet fragility, negative-to-thin operating margins, unpredictable operating cash flow, and unresolved labor risk make this a name to reduce exposure to and wait for confirmation of a real de-levering, margin-stabilizing trend before adding back.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing BA exposure by roughly 25-35% of position size now, rather than exiting fully - the defense backlog and 737 MAX ramp keep tail-risk of a bankruptcy scenario low, so this is a de-risking move, not a full Sell. 2) Do not add to the position ahead of the September/October labor negotiation - treat the strike vote as a binary near-term catalyst; wait for resolution before considering any incremental buys. 3) Set a watchlist trigger to revisit the thesis toward Hold/Overweight if: (a) two consecutive quarters of positive operating cash flow excluding asset-sale proceeds, (b) operating margin sustainably above mid-single digits, and (c) labor contract resolved without a strike and without runaway cost inflation. 4) Avoid using options leverage or adding on dips purely on backlog headlines - require confirmation via cash-flow statements, not order announcements. 5) If already underweight/no position, remain in cash or use the proceeds to rotate into aerospace/defense peers with cleaner balance sheets (positive tangible book value, sub-3x leverage) until BA's deleveraging path becomes clearer. 6) Keep a small residual position (not zero) to retain optionality on defense contract execution and MAX ramp upside, sized so a positive surprise doesn't leave the portfolio fully sidelined, but sized small enough that a strike-driven margin shock or another goodwill/impairment event doesn't materially impair the book.
