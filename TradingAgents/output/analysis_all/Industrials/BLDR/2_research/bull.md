## PART 3: THE HOUSING MARKET IS SOFTENING, NOT COLLAPSING

Now, let me address the Lowe's guidance cut that everyone is citing as bearish.

**The bearish interpretation:** Lowe's cut DIY guidance; therefore, housing demand is collapsing; therefore, BLDR (a building-products distributor) will crater.

**The bull interpretation:** Lowe's DIY weakness ≠ residential construction collapse.

Here's the nuance:

### Lowe's Cut Is Sector-Specific, Not Systemic

Lowe's DIY segment weakness reflects:
- **Post-pandemic normalization:** Consumers are no longer trapped at home doing projects; experiences and travel are competing for discretionary spend
- **Housing inventory stabilization:** After years of undersupply, inventory is normalizing, reducing urgency for renovations
- **Selective consumer deleveraging:** Higher-income consumers are rotating out of home improvement into vacations and services

**This is not indicative of new home construction collapse.**

### Contractor/Pro Segment Resilience

Here's what everyone misses: **Lowe's Pro segment is showing resilience.** This is the segment BLDR actually feeds.

- **Lowe's Pro channel:** Still growing; the guidance cut was driven entirely by DIY weakness
- **Residential construction starts:** Have moderated but remain above pre-pandemic averages
- **Single-family housing permits:** Still running at +1.0M units annually (down from peaks but not in free fall)

BLDR's revenue mix is tilted toward **pro/contractor sales, not retail DIY.** Lowe's cutting DIY guidance is largely orthogonal to BLDR's revenue streams.

### The Macro Backdrop Supports Moderate Construction Activity

Let me address the elephant in the room: interest rates.

**The bearish call:** "89% probability of zero Fed cuts in 2026 means rates stay high; new construction will be crushed."

**My counterpoint:** Mortgage rates have been sticky at 6.5-7.0%, but housing starts have not collapsed:

## PART 4: BLDR'S EARNINGS CLIFF IS CYCLICAL, NOT STRUCTURAL

### Profitability Collapse Is Real, But Explainable

I won't sugarcoat it: BLDR's H1 2026 performance was dreadful.

- **Q1 2026 Net Loss:** $47.4M (EPS: -$0.43)
- **Q2 2026 Net Loss:** $3.9M (EPS: -$0.04)
- **H1 2026 Cumulative Loss:** $51.3M

**What happened?**

1. **Gross margin compression:** Down 230bps YoY to 28.1%
   - Root cause: Input cost inflation (lumber, steel, insulation) outpaced pricing power
   - This is temporary; as commodity prices normalize and BLDR re-prices inventory, margins recover

2. **Operating leverage inversion:** At lower volumes, fixed costs compress margins
   - BLDR has high fixed costs (distribution centers, fleet, facilities)
   - A 12% revenue decline (Q2 YoY) hits net income disproportionately
   - This is cyclically normal and reverses as volumes recover

3. **Temporary headwinds:** Inventory write-downs, one-time restructuring charges
   - Management is taking pain upfront, positioning for recovery

### 2025 Earnings Prove Operating Model Resilience

Here's the bull anchor: **BLDR generated $435M in net income in FY 2025**, despite operating in the tail end of a softening cycle.

- **2024 Net Income:** $1,077.9M
- **2023 Net Income:** $1,540.6M

Yes, earnings peaked in 2023-2024, but this doesn't invalidate the business model. It reflects **peak housing boom to normalized cycle—not structural decline.**
