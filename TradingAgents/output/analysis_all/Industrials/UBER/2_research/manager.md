**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides raise valid points, but the bear case lands more decisive blows on the specific issues that matter for near-term positioning. On profitability, the bear's dissection of the TTM net income figure is compelling and largely unrebutted: a $6.63B Q3 2025 quarter driven by a one-time security gain is inflating the headline $9.58B TTM figure, and normalized quarterly operating income run-rate (~$6B annualized) implies a real P/E closer to 26x rather than the bull's 16.5x. That's a material valuation gap the bull never addresses with hard numbers—the bull's rebuttal on margins/layoffs never circles back to defend the earnings quality point directly.

On the layoffs, the bull's math doesn't hold up under scrutiny: the bear correctly points out that even a 15% headcount cut only nets $450-600M against a claimed $1.1-1.65B margin improvement target—the numbers don't reconcile, and the bull never corrects this gap. The bear's read that layoffs preceding flat sequential revenue ($14.19B Q2 vs $14.37B Q4) is a demand-strain signal rather than pure discipline is more consistent with the data than the bull's "proactive strength" framing.

On macro, the bull's argument that higher-for-longer rates helps Uber because competitors burn cash is undercut by the bear's point that the most credible AV competitive threats (Waymo/Aurora) are backed by Alphabet/Amazon balance sheets, not rate-sensitive capital—so this isn't really a moat. The bear's capex reality check ($8-15B/year needed for AV/EV/data center investment against $55B revenue) is a legitimate structural concern the bull dismissed too quickly by calling capex "discretionary."

On balance sheet, the bull's net-debt/EBITDA (1.05x) and negative-working-capital-as-a-feature arguments are reasonable and do blunt some of the bear's alarm — I don't fully buy the goodwill/dead-money framing as decisively as the bear presents it, since impairment hasn't actually been flagged in the given data. So the balance sheet argument is roughly a wash, tilting slightly bullish on liquidity/leverage optics.

Netting it out: the earnings-quality problem (normalized EPS far below headline) and the flat-sequential-revenue-with-layoffs combination are the two most concrete, numbers-backed points in this debate, and they both favor the bear. The technical picture (death cross, MACD negative, disengaged retail sentiment) also leans bearish and wasn't meaningfully refuted beyond "it's just consolidation." Given this, I don't have enough to call outright Sell — the bull's leverage/liquidity defense and the "no recession" macro backdrop provide some cushion, and the stock is still nominally profitable and cash generative. But the balance of evidence — inflated headline earnings, decelerating real growth, uncertain layoff economics, and negative technical momentum — is enough to tilt toward trimming exposure rather than holding steady or adding.

5) Keep residual exposure (do not go to zero) given the genuine net debt/EBITDA (~1.05x) cushion and asset-light cash-generation characteristics, which argue against a full Sell, but size the position more conservatively than a Hold/Buy stance would warrant given the earnings-quality concerns.
