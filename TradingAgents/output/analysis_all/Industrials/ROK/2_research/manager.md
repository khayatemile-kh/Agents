**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make points worth weighing, but the bear case lands harder on the two things that matter most for a 39x P/E industrial name: (1) the sustainability of the earnings growth rate, and (2) the macro/capex backdrop. The bull's headline stat—38% net income growth vs 7.9% revenue growth—is a 4.8x multiplier that the bull never actually explains with hard evidence; the bear correctly flags this as a red flag that needs a real explanation (margin mix, buybacks, one-time items) rather than being waved away as "operating leverage" on a company only running 20.8% operating margins. Until that gap is explained with disclosed drivers, it's not something I'm willing to underwrite as durable, especially in a name priced at 39x trailing earnings.

The bull's product-launch thesis (TechConnectIQ, OT cybersecurity, Plex QMS/VisionAI) is directionally sensible—these are real secular trends—but the bull himself concedes "the analyst community hasn't fully modeled this yet," which is another way of saying there's no revenue disclosed yet. That's a call option on future monetization, not a reason to pay up today at a premium multiple. The bear's point that these are "unproven and unmonetized" is fair; we have no evidence in the debate of dollar contribution from these products.

On valuation, the bull's own sensitivity table is actually a tell: even in the bull's presented scenarios, applying a normalized multiple (20x) to the bull-case 2027 EPS ($20.05) yields a stock price of ~$401 — below today's $417. That means the bull's own math, once you apply a more normalized multiple rather than assuming 39x persists, implies limited to negative upside even if the aggressive growth materializes. That's a weak risk/reward, not an asymmetric one.

Macro backdrop reinforces the bear case: prediction markets pricing ~89% odds of no Fed cuts in 2026, wide/uncertain ISM distribution barely hovering near the 50 expansion threshold, and industrial sector underperforming the S&P by ~1,330bps — all point to a capex environment that's cautious-to-contracting, which directly pressures ROK's core hardware/software attach revenue for the next several quarters. The CFO share sale, while not dispositive on its own, at minimum removes it as a supporting bull data point and the bear's read (insider skepticism amid a growth narrative) is at least as plausible as the bull's "routine profit-taking" framing.

Technical picture (13% drawdown, oversold RSI, sector underperformance) could ordinarily be a contrarian buy signal, but oversold conditions in isolation aren't a catalyst — they need a supportive fundamental/macro backdrop to trigger a bounce, and here the macro and earnings-quality picture argues the selloff is repricing a name that got ahead of itself on 39x trailing earnings, not that it's a capitulation-driven overreaction.

Net: the bear case's critique of earnings quality (unexplained 4.8x growth multiplier), the unmonetized nature of the new AI/cyber products, the bull's own valuation math implying limited upside under normalized multiples, and a genuinely hostile capex/rate backdrop outweigh the bull's narrative-driven growth story. This doesn't rise to a full conviction Sell — the company is still a quality franchise with real recurring-revenue potential once these products scale, and multiple compression from 39x to the high-20s is already partly underway — but the risk/reward skews negative near-term, so I'm recommending trimming exposure rather than adding into this decline.
