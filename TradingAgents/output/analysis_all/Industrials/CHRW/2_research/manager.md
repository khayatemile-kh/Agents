On the technical side, both agree the stock is deeply oversold (RSI ~30) with a 30% correction, and the bull's argument that this is capitulation with a MACD reversal is a fair near-term trading signal, but it doesn't offset the fundamental margin-compression and equity-erosion concerns for a position-sizing decision at the portfolio level. Valuation (forward P/E ~19x, PEG 1.8) is reasonable but not so cheap that it compensates for deteriorating gross margin trend and balance sheet leverage. Net: the bear's evidence-based rebuttals (2022 baseline comparison, shrinking equity base inflating ROE, actual sequential gross margin decline validating the insurance cost risk) are more specific and harder to dismiss than the bull's more narrative-driven optimism about oversold technicals and capital return discipline. This warrants a cautious, underweight stance rather than outright sell (business is still profitable and cash-generative, dividend intact, valuation not absurd) or hold (evidence isn't balanced — bear has the stronger, more falsifiable case on the margin trajectory and capital structure).
