### The ROE Red Herring

The bull touts a 37% ROE as evidence of "elite company" status. I need to call out what's actually happening here.

**The ROE Calculation Problem:**

ROE = Net Income / Shareholders' Equity

CHRW's ROE is inflated because **stockholders' equity has been decimated through aggressive capital returns:**

| Period | Stockholders' Equity | YoY Change |
|--------|----------------------|-----------|
| 2024 Year-End | $1.72B | Baseline |
| 2025 Year-End | $1.85B | +7.6% |
| **Q2 2026** | **$1.70B** | **-7.5% YoY** |

**The equity base is actually *shrinking*, not growing.** The company is using aggressive buybacks and dividends to artificially inflate the ROE ratio. This is financial engineering, not operational excellence.

Let me put this in perspective: A company with a 37% ROE financed by destroying the equity base through buybacks is **returning capital faster than it's generating returns.** That's not strength—that's a cannibalization strategy that temporarily boosts per-share metrics while eroding long-term financial stability.

If CHRW were truly generating 37% returns on a stable equity base, stockholders' equity would be *expanding*, not contracting. The fact that it's shrinking while ROE looks "elite" tells you the metric is misleading.

### Margin Expansion: A Fragile House Built on Pricing, Not Operations

The bull celebrates 190 basis points of gross margin expansion from 2023 to 2025. I see this as a **sustainability risk**, not a strength signal.

2. **Modest pricing power during recovery:** As shippers faced tighter capacity, CHRW could raise rates slightly.
3. **Cost normalization:** Some temporary cost pressures from 2023 subsided naturally.

**Why this margin expansion is fragile and vulnerable:**

Enter the **real risk the bull is downplaying: the insurance cost headwind is exactly what's threatening to reverse these gains.**

Here's the institutional signal the bull dismissed too quickly: **FreightWaves reported that broker insurance costs are rising materially**, and an industry insurance executive painted a "challenging outlook for brokers."

This isn't speculation—this is *live* cost inflation hitting the broker segment *right now*. And here's what the bull missed: **Q2 2026 gross margins are *already showing compression*.**

Look at the quarterly data:
- **Q1 2026 Gross Profit:** $320.0M
- **Q2 2026 Gross Profit:** $307.8M
- **Quarter-over-Quarter Change:** -2.4%

**Gross profit is declining even as revenue rises 2.6%**. That's a margin compression signal, not a pricing power signal. The bull pointed to Q2 as evidence of "pricing power," but the data shows the opposite: revenues up, gross profits down.

This is the canary in the coal mine. If insurance costs continue to pressure margins and the company can't pass them fully to customers, we could see this 8.37% gross margin start deteriorating back toward 7-7.5% levels. That would represent a 50-90 basis point hit to profitability.

**The Working Capital Expansion Illusion:**

The bull notes that working capital increased from $0.64B (2024) to $1.11B (Q2 2026)—a 73% expansion—and frames this as "strong operational momentum."

Wrong. Let me show you what's actually happening:

Working capital expansion primarily reflects **higher accounts receivable, not better operations.** Here's why this matters:

| Component | Q2 2026 | 2025 Year-End | Change |
|-----------|---------|---------------|--------|
| **Accounts Receivable** | $2.54B | ~$2.35B | +$190M |
| **Current Liabilities** | $1.90B | ~$1.64B | +$260M |

The company is extending payment terms to customers to boost sales (AR up) while its suppliers are getting paid faster or the company is taking on more obligations. **This is working capital *extension*, not strength.** It ties up cash that could be deployed elsewhere.

A healthy, profitable company naturally generates working capital—it doesn't have to engineer it. The fact that CHRW needed to expand AR by $190M to drive 2.6% revenue growth suggests **weak organic demand that requires extended payment terms to incentivize customers.**
