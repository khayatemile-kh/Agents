## THE BEAR THESIS: FOUR CRITICAL FAILURES IN THE BULL CASE

### **FAILURE 1: GROWTH ASSUMPTIONS ARE BUILT ON A HOUSE OF CARDS**

The bull makes a compelling-sounding case about infrastructure and data centers, but let's examine the actual evidence:

#### **The Construction Segment Growth is a Mirage**

Yes, FAST reported 17% construction growth in Q2 2026. But here's what the bull conveniently omits:

**1. This is a Single Quarter—Not a Trend**
- We have *one data point* of 17% construction growth
- There's no quarterly progression data showing whether this accelerated from Q1 or decelerated
- The bull assumes this is "structural," but cyclical businesses *always* show temporary spikes

**2. The Infrastructure Cycle is Already Mature, Not Inflectionary**
- The Biden Infrastructure Bill was passed in November 2021—**nearly 5 years ago**
- By Q2 2026, we're not in the "early innings;" we're in the **late innings to maturity phase**
- The bull claims "50% of Biden spending still undeployed," but provides *no source* for this claim
- Infrastructure projects have front-loaded deployment timelines; we're likely past peak capex rates

**3. Comparable Companies Show Declining Growth Momentum**
- Other industrials and construction-exposed companies have already reported slowing growth in 2026
- The construction cycle typically runs 3-5 years from announcement to completion; the 2021 announcement means **peak spending was likely 2024-2025, not 2026**
- If FAST's construction segment is truly accelerating while peers decelerate, *that's suspicious*—it suggests either accounting nuances or temporary customer destocking

#### **Data Center Capex is Real, But Fastenal Gets a Sliver**

The bull points to AI infrastructure spending as a secular tailwind. Fine. But here's the critical gap:

**1. Data Centers Buy Directly from Specialized Suppliers**
- Large hyperscalers (AWS, Google, Microsoft, Meta) don't source fasteners and tools through traditional industrial distributors
- They use direct relationships with manufacturers or specialized supply partners
- Fastenal gets the *periphery business*—HVAC installation, electrical work, general construction supplies—not the core infrastructure buildout

**2. Data Center Construction is Lumpy and Project-Based**
- These aren't recurring revenue streams; they're discrete capex projects
- A single data center complex might represent $50-100M in fastener demand *over 18 months*, not recurring annual revenue
- Once complete, the revenue stream collapses until the next project
- This creates *volatility*, not sustainable growth

**3. The Bull Can't Point to Specific Data Center Wins**
- Management hasn't broken out data center as a separate segment
- There's no guidance on what portion of construction growth comes from data centers vs. traditional infrastructure
- This suggests it's not material enough to call out—yet the bull builds an entire case around it

#### **Normalization Will Be Severe**

The bull concedes that 17% will "normalize" to 10-12%, treating this as a minor hair-cut. But let's do the math:

- **Current Situation:** Revenue $8.75B growing at ~15% = $10.1B in a year
- **Normalized Scenario:** Same base growing at 5% (industrials average) = $9.2B
- **Difference:** $900M in annual revenue *not realized* vs. bullish expectations

At Fastenal's 15.5% net margin, that's **$139M in annual earnings foregone**. On a current stock price of $48.75 with 1.147B shares, that's a **$0.12 EPS miss**—which at a 41.7x multiple translates to **$5 of downside**.

**The bull is asking us to pay for 17% growth while hoping it sticks at 10-12%. History says that's wishful thinking.**

### **FAILURE 2: VALUATION IS INDEFENSIBLE, NOT JUSTIFIED**

Now let's address the bull's core valuation defense, which hinges on a manipulative reframing of the PEG ratio.

#### **The Bull's PEG Sleight of Hand**

The bull argues: *"A forward P/E of 34x on 20% growth converts to a normalized PEG of 1.7x—perfectly reasonable."*

**This is mathematical slight-of-hand. Let me expose it:**

**1. Forward Growth of 20% is Unsustainable**
- The bull assumes 20% earnings growth over the next 2 years
- But this is *predicated on 17% construction growth continuing*
- If construction normalizes to 5-8% (a reasonable assumption for a mature cycle), overall revenue growth drops to 8-10%
- At 8% revenue growth with flat margins, earnings growth is ~8%—not 20%

**2. The Real PEG Ratio at Current Valuation**

Let me calculate what we're *actually* paying:

| Scenario | Revenue Growth | Earnings Growth | Forward P/E | PEG Ratio | Assessment |
|----------|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull Case (17% Construction Maintained)** | 15% | 20% | 34.24x | 1.71x | Acceptable IF true |
| **Base Case (Construction → 8%)** | 8% | 8% | 34.24x | 4.28x | **EXPENSIVE** |
| **Bear Case (Construction → 5%)** | 6% | 6% | 34.24x | 5.71x | **SEVERELY EXPENSIVE** |

**The bull is betting the farm on the optimistic scenario while ignoring that the base case (construction moderating to single digits) is far more likely.**
