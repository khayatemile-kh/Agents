**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts—OTIS is a high-margin, service-heavy industrial with negative equity and rising absolute debt—but they disagree sharply on trajectory and what that means for valuation. The bull's strongest points are the structural quality of the business: 70%+ recurring service revenue, non-discretionary maintenance demand, a $3B deferred revenue backlog, healthy interest coverage (~13x), and a valuation multiple (10.2x EV/EBITDA) that's below typical diversified industrial peers (12-14x). The bear's strongest points are the trend data: quarter-over-quarter cash flow deceleration (Q1 $413M OCF vs Q2 $267M OCF, FCF conversion dropping to 52% in Q2), debt increasing YoY even as buybacks continue ($807M repurchases funded partly by new debt issuance), and a genuinely fragile balance sheet with -$5.45B equity that leaves zero room to absorb a downturn or raise equity capital without severe dilution.

Weighing these: the bear's use of intra-year trend deceleration (Q1 to Q2 cash flow decline) is a legitimate red flag that the bull doesn't adequately address—simply calling negative equity "a feature" ignores that the trend is moving in the wrong direction (rising debt, falling cash conversion) at the same time buybacks continue. That combination—weakening cash generation plus increasing leverage plus continued aggressive capital return—is a classic pattern that precedes credit-rating pressure or dividend/buyback cuts, even if coverage ratios look fine today. On the other hand, the bear's valuation argument is somewhat circular ("it trades at a discount because it deserves to"), and doesn't refute the structural moat of recurring elevator maintenance being genuinely hard to disrupt.

Given the balance-sheet fragility is real and quantifiable (negative equity, rising net debt, deteriorating quarter-over-quarter cash conversion) while the bull's growth catalysts (modernization, EM urbanization) are more speculative and longer-dated, I lean bearish-to-cautious rather than aggressively bullish. But this isn't a full "sell" case — the core business remains profitable, cash-generative in absolute terms, and the debt levels, while rising, are not yet at a level that threatens solvency (interest coverage still ~13x). The right call is a cautious/underweight stance: trim exposure and wait for clearer stabilization in cash-conversion trends and debt trajectory before adding, rather than chasing the bull's "cheap versus peers" argument that ignores deteriorating quarterly cash generation.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim OTIS position modestly (e.g., reduce by 15-25% of current weighting) rather than adding on the "cheap valuation" narrative, given the quarter-over-quarter cash-flow deceleration (Q1 OCF $413M vs Q2 OCF $267M, FCF conversion falling to ~52%) and rising net debt ($7.62B, up YoY) alongside continued aggressive buybacks ($807M in H1 2026).
2) Do not initiate new buying at $70.60 based on peer-multiple comparisons alone; wait for at least one to two quarters of stabilized or improving FCF conversion and evidence that debt growth has plateaued before considering re-entry or increasing exposure.
3) Monitor covenant/credit-rating commentary closely — with -$5.45B stockholders' equity and no balance-sheet flexibility, any negative EBITDA surprise or additional debt-funded buybacks should trigger a further de-risking of the position.
4) Retain a small core position (do not fully exit) to preserve exposure to the genuine structural moat (recurring, non-discretionary maintenance revenue, ~70% of earnings) and optionality from modernization/EM urbanization tailwinds, but size it so that a 10-15% revenue shock scenario (recession case) would not meaningfully impair the portfolio.
5) Set a re-rating trigger: upgrade back to Hold/Overweight only if OTIS shows (a) FCF conversion returning above ~70%, and (b) net debt/EBITDA trending down rather than up, confirming the bull's "intentional optimization" thesis rather than the bear's "financial engineering under strain" thesis.
