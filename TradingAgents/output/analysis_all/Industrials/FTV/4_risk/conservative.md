That's not fraud or misrepresentation; it's standard M&A accounting.

Here's why I'm skeptical of their skepticism:

But the company has been systematically *shifting* its portfolio toward higher-margin, less capital-intensive service and software businesses (think diagnostic platforms, cloud-based monitoring). A lower-CapEx model isn't inherently suspicious if it reflects a deliberate portfolio shift.

**Second, the company's manufacturing footprint has been optimized.** Many of Fortive's brands operate in shared facilities, and the company has invested heavily in automation and efficiency over the past 5+ years. It's entirely plausible that normalized maintenance CapEx has declined to near-zero in a given quarter if they're not deploying major new facilities or equipment.

**Third, and most importantly**, if the zero-CapEx reporting were a data error or fraud, it would have already triggered auditor questions, analyst inquiries, or SEC scrutiny. I see *no evidence* of any of those. The stock would likely be trading at a deeper discount if there were genuine accounting concerns.

The aggressive analyst says this is "impossible." I say it's *unusual and worth monitoring*, but not sufficient on its own to justify an emergency sell at today's depressed levels.
