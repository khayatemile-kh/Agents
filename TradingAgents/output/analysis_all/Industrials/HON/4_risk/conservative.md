- 2022: $20.5B
- 2023: $21.5B (+$1.0B, +4.9%)

## The Margin Compression Is NOT a One-Time Event—It's Structural

The aggressive analyst's second major claim was that the 160 basis point margin compression is temporary and "driven by non-recurring restructuring charges." They argued that if you strip out special charges and impairment items, margins are "likely" 19-20%.

I need to challenge this on multiple fronts:

### First: The Data Doesn't Support the "Temporary" Thesis

Let me examine gross margin compression more carefully:

- 2023: 37.5%
- 2024: 38.5% (improvement of 100 bps)
- 2025: 36.9% (decline of 160 bps)

So margins peaked in 2024 and have now fallen below 2023 levels. The aggressive analyst wants to attribute this to "one-time restructuring charges." But here's the problem: **Gross margin is the most structural measure of pricing power and input costs.** It's not significantly affected by restructuring charges or impairments—those hit operating margin and net income.

The fact that **gross margin has declined suggests underlying input cost inflation, pricing pressure, or supply chain inefficiencies that are not being recovered through price increases.** This is the exact margin compression problem the research identified: Honeywell is losing pricing power relative to its cost base.

### Second: The Operating Margin Compression Is Even More Concerning

Operating margin fell from 19.2% (2024) to 17.6% (2025)—a 160 bp decline. The aggressive analyst claimed this is "only" special charges. Let me disaggregate this:

- Gross margin compression: 160 bps (down from 38.5% to 36.9%)
- SG&A as % of revenue: stayed relatively flat
- Special charges increased: $1.5B (2025) vs $600M (2024), an $900M swing

If special charges added roughly 240 bps of margin pressure ($900M on ~$37.4B revenue), and gross margin fell 160 bps, then **even on a normalized basis (stripping special charges), operating margin would be approximately 18.0%, still down 120 bps from 19.2%.**

This suggests that **even after removing one-time items, underlying operational margin has deteriorated.** That is NOT a temporary phenomenon. That is structural.

### Third: The "Margin Recovery" Path Is Speculative

The aggressive analyst forecasts "clear path to margin recovery to 19%+ by 2027" based on leadership changes and completed restructuring. But this is precisely where I must inject skepticism:

**What is the actual catalyst for margin recovery?**

- Cost reductions? The company has already announced restructuring; further cuts would require additional headcount reductions or facility closures, which would generate more one-time charges in near term.
- Pricing power? The industry is competitive. The recent cybersecurity settlement and HONA guidance miss don't suggest Honeywell has improved its negotiating position.
- Operating leverage? This only materializes if revenue accelerates significantly. But the 2025 revenue declined ~7.7% versus 2024. There's no evidence of revenue stabilization yet.

The aggressive analyst is essentially betting that **management execution in 2027 will reverse a multi-year deterioration trend.** This is speculative. For a risk manager, this is a reason to wait for actual evidence, not to deploy capital in anticipation of it.
