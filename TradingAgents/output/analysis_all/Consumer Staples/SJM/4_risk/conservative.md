They don't sustain RSI 70+ for three consecutive sessions unless they're front-running a narrative they believe will self-propagate. But narratives break. And when they do, the same institutions that were "convicting buying" are the first to exit, because their risk management systems are built around volatility thresholds and profit-taking triggers.

## **The "Forward P/E of 12.08x is Cheap" Argument Ignores Normalized Risk**

This is where the aggressive analyst and I truly diverge on philosophy. He says "12x forward is reasonable for a defensive consumer staple with improving momentum." I say **that multiple assumes the guidance is achievable and the improvements stick.**

But let's test that assumption. The forward P/E of 12.08x is built on consensus expectations. Those expectations are based on:
- Coffee pricing power continuing
- Volume remaining flat-to-positive
- No further impairment charges
- Tariff benefits persisting or being offset by operational improvements

Now, how many of those assumptions are guaranteed? Let's score them:

**Coffee pricing power:** Dependent on coffee bean futures and consumer elasticity. If bean prices drop 20%, margin pressure is immediate. If consumer push-back emerges (value-seeking behavior accelerating), pricing power evaporates. This is *not* guaranteed.

**Volume staying positive:** One quarter of positive volume after years of declines is a data point, not a trend. The aggressive analyst wants me to believe this inflection is permanent. But packaged foods is a mature category. Demographic trends favor e-commerce and meal-kit services over traditional packaged goods. A pullback to negative volumes is entirely plausible.

**No further impairments:** Given that SJM has written off nearly $2B in assets over 18 months, does anyone really believe there are no more portfolio problems lurking? The Uncrustables acquisition and other recent M&A could face similar scrutiny.

**Tariff benefits persisting:** The company disclosed $0.84/share from tariff refunds in Q1. That's non-recurring, full stop. Management will guide based on that, but if tariffs return or the refund program changes, boom—guidance miss, stock falls 15%.

When you model a scenario where *just two* of these assumptions break—say, coffee pricing normalizes and volume turns negative—the forward 12x multiple compresses to 8-9x, and the stock reprices down to $95-105 range.

That's not edge case catastrophe thinking. That's normal mean reversion in a packaged foods company.
