### B. The PEG Ratio of 0.56 Assumes Growth That Doesn't Exist in Groceries

**The bull's argument:** PEG < 1.0 = undervaluation. KR's 0.56 PEG means it's trading at roughly half its justified valuation.

**My challenge:** The PEG ratio is a heuristic that works well for companies with durable competitive advantages and pricing power. **KR has neither.**

**Why the PEG ratio is misleading for KR:**

1. **The "G" (growth) in PEG is forward earnings growth. But what's the basis for 5-7% earnings growth in a 0% comparable-store sales environment?**

   From the research:
   - **Dollar General and Dollar Tree posted >3% comp-store sales growth** in Q2 2026
   - **Walmart's profit growth exceeded comp sales growth**, indicating cannibalization of shelf grocery
   - **KR's forward guidance has not been provided** (waiting for Sept 11 earnings), but implied guidance assumes recovery that seems increasingly unlikely

   **The market priced in an optimistic earnings scenario. The PEG ratio assumes that scenario holds.** As evidence mounts that it won't, the PEG ratio becomes a **value trap, not a value opportunity.**

2. **PEG ratios are backward-looking in a forward-changing environment.**

   Historical PEG analysis assumes that past growth rates are predictive of future growth rates. But grocery retail is experiencing **structural disruption**:
   - Shift to value channels (DG/DT)
   - Walmart's willingness to sacrifice margin for volume
   - Amazon/Instacart taking delivery market share
   - Regulatory risk (dynamic pricing scrutiny)

   **None of these trends are reversing.** The PEG ratio assumes a return to historical conditions. That's optimistic.
