3. **Guidance raise is a confidence trap—but guidance was likely soft to begin with**

## **III. ON UPSCALE CUSTOMER PENETRATION: THIS IS A WARNING SIGN, NOT AN OPPORTUNITY**

This is where I need to be most direct: **You have inverted the signal.** The increasing penetration of $100K+ households into dollar stores is **not evidence of TAM expansion—it's evidence of consumer stress trickling upward.**

### **Here's Why:**

1. **Affluent consumers don't permanently "trade down" to value retailers unless forced**
   - The macroeconomic context is crystal clear: household budgets are under pressure; consumers are trading down out of necessity, not preference
   - Once inflation moderates or rates decline, these affluent customers revert to their preferred retailers (Whole Foods, regional chains, boutique grocers)
   - This is a **cyclical windfall, not a secular TAM expansion**

2. **You're confusing revenue growth with margin-sustainable growth**
   - Yes, more affluent shoppers = incremental revenue. But here's the problem: **affluent customers expect different things than core DLTR customers**
   - They expect:
     - Fresher produce (DLTR's supply chain is optimized for non-perishables)
     - Wider selection (DLTR's limited SKU count—a strength for core customers—becomes a weakness for affluent customers)
     - Better checkout experience (DLTR's sparse locations and long lines don't appeal to time-rich affluent customers)
   - As the consumer environment normalizes, these customers will leave, and DLTR will be left with **stranded supply chain investments and disappointed margins**

3. **The variable pricing strategy is a knife's edge**
   - You tout multi-price strategy ($1.25 core + $3-5 upscale SKUs) as a margin expansion opportunity
   - But this is **operationally complex and creates customer friction:**
     - Confuses core customer base ("Why is this item $3 and that item $1.25?")
     - Requires sophisticated inventory management (DLTR is strong operationally, but this adds complexity)
     - Risks brand dilution if perceived as "bait and switch" pricing
   - The history of value retailers attempting dual-pricing is mixed at best (see: Five Below, Dollar General's strategic missteps)
