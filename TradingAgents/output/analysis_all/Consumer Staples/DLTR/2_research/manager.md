**Rationale**: Both sides make fair points, but when I weigh them against each other, the bear case holds up better on the hard numbers while the bull case leans heavily on narrative and extrapolation from limited data points. (3) On traffic, the bull's July +4.8% figure is a single data point without YoY seasonal context (back-to-school effect), while the bear correctly notes Q2's +0.4% traffic was ticket-driven, not footfall-driven - a sign of pricing power nearing its ceiling rather than genuine demand strength. (4) The upscale-customer trade-down thesis is genuinely ambiguous - it could be secular or cyclical - but the bear's point that affluent shoppers have different expectations (fresh produce, selection, checkout experience) that DLTR's format doesn't naturally serve is a legitimate structural risk to the bull's TAM-expansion story. (5) On the tariff refund/supply-chain reinvestment argument, the bear's framing that this is largely maintenance capex/keeping pace with peers rather than a moat is more grounded than the bull's assumption of "permanent 30-50bps margin expansion" from an unproven pricing strategy. (6) Technically, the bull's "healthy consolidation" read on subdued RSI/MACD post-crash is optimistic, but the underlying catalyst - a big Q3 guidance miss and margin compression - gives more weight to the bear's skepticism about the recovery being durable.

Given this, I don't think the picture is balanced enough for a Hold. The bear's arguments are more supported by concrete margin and guidance data, while the bull's strongest points (traffic inflection, upscale TAM expansion) rely on extrapolation from thin data and optimistic narrative framing. This tilts toward a cautious stance, but I stop short of a full Sell because the bull does highlight real positives - moving average alignment, a genuine EPS beat on normalized ops, and real market share gains vs DG - that argue against outright abandoning the position. The right call is Underweight: trim exposure and de-risk ahead of the Q3 print, but don't capitulate entirely given the demonstrated relative traffic/market-share strength versus DG.
