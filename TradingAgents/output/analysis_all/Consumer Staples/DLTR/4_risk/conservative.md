## THE TARIFF REFUND ARGUMENT CONFLATES CAPITAL DEPLOYMENT WITH EARNINGS SUSTAINABILITY

The aggressive analyst makes a compelling rhetorical move by saying the tariff refunds are "being weaponized for market share capture" and therefore aren't ephemeral. This is exactly the kind of narrative that gets portfolio managers in trouble. Here's why:

**There is a fundamental difference between deployed capital and recurring earnings power.**

Yes, management stated they'll reinvest the $369 million in supply chain and pricing initiatives. That's capital allocation. But capital deployment does NOT automatically translate to sustainable earnings expansion. It's an investment with uncertain returns, and the data we have suggests those returns are modest at best.

Look at what actually happened: DLTR deployed (or claims it will deploy) $369 million in tariff refunds to fund supply chain improvements and pricing actions. What did that buy them in July? **A 4.8% traffic increase versus Dollar General's 1.9%.** 

Now, here's what the aggressive analyst isn't emphasizing: **that's still anemic traffic growth.** Q2 traffic was 0.4%. July bounced to 4.8%. That's a recovery, but it's not proof of structural market share gains. It's one month of data following a major crash day. One month. You know what happens to one-month traffic comparisons when they follow periods of severe disruption? They bounce. This is mean reversion, not secular acceleration.

More critically: **if you had to spend $369 million in capital to generate a 4.8% traffic increase in a single month, what does that tell you about the underlying unit economics?** It suggests that without continuous capital infusion, traffic reverts. That's not a competitive moat—that's a treadmill. You're running faster and faster just to stay in place.

The aggressive analyst is treating this tariff reinvestment as a one-time catalyst that unlocks new structural efficiency. But the margin data tells a different story. Let me walk you through it:

**Q2 Gross Margin: 36.8% (down from 39.2% in Q1)**
