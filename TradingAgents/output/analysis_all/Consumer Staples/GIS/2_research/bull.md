### **Bear Claim #2: "The debt is too high—Debt-to-Equity of 217.63% is dangerous!"**

**Bull Rebuttal:**
Yes, the debt-to-equity ratio looks scary. But let's contextualize:

1. **This is *normal* for packaged food companies** because:
   - Brands ARE intangible assets (goodwill $14.12B, intangibles $6.72B = $20.84B total)
   - These brands generate *recurring, predictable cash flows* that justify leverage
   - Peer companies (Mondelez, Kraft-Heinz) carry similar leverage ratios

2. **The debt is *manageable* relative to EBITDA:**
   - Net Debt: $13.08 billion
   - EBITDA: $3.45 billion
   - **Net Debt / EBITDA = 3.79x**—well within investment-grade territory (IG ceiling is 4.5x)
   - **Interest Coverage: Secure** (Operating Income $2.84B / Interest Expense ~$700M = 4.0x)

3. **Free Cash Flow is Sufficient to De-Lever:**
   - With $2.31B annual FCF, GIS could pay down debt meaningfully while maintaining dividends
   - Recent restructuring (hence the Q2 impairment charges) likely positions the company to improve FCF going forward

**Bottom Line:** The leverage is *leverage*, but it's *serviceable leverage*—not distress territory. And the fact that GIS *maintains and raises its dividend* despite challenges shows management confidence in cash generation.
