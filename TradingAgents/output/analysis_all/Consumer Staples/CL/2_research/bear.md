### 1. **The "Volume Growth" Narrative is Misleading—Here's Why**

The bull makes a compelling-sounding case: "Three consecutive quarters of organic volume growth proves CL is winning the pricing vs. volume battle." 

**I need to challenge this directly with data the bull is conveniently downplaying.**

#### **The Revenue Growth Story Doesn't Support the Narrative**

Look at what actually happened in Q2 2026:
- **Q2 2026 vs Q1 2026:** Revenue grew only **0.7%** sequentially
- **Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025:** Let me be clear—we don't have explicit YoY revenue growth in the data, but the TTM revenue is $21.05B

Here's the critical point: **If CL is achieving "organic volume growth for a third consecutive quarter," why is sequential revenue growth stuck at 0.7%?**

The answer is that **pricing power and volume growth are canceling each other out, not compounding**. In other words:
- Volume may be up 2-3% in units
- But price realization is flat or slightly negative
- Net result: 0.7% revenue growth

**This is not the sign of a company "winning" the pricing vs. volume battle.** This is the sign of a company stuck in a stasis trap—gaining units but not gaining pricing leverage or revenue traction. That's exactly what happens when:
1. You raise prices aggressively (Q1-Q2 2025)
2. Consumers eventually trade down or reduce quantity
3. You're forced to offer discounts to defend volume
4. Net result: higher unit count, lower effective pricing

**The bull's point about P&G facing an 8% EPS headwind is irrelevant.** Yes, P&G is struggling. But P&G is trading at a premium valuation because it *still has pricing power and brand equity to defend*. CL's 0.7% revenue growth, by contrast, suggests the company is **already at the wall on pricing.**

### 2. **The "Forward EPS Growth" Assumption is Built on Sand**

The bull argues: "Forward P/E of 21.93x is reasonable because the market is pricing in 61% EPS growth."

- **From acquisitions?** The -$866M Q4 write-down suggests management's M&A track record is poor. Why would I assume they'll suddenly execute brilliant accretive deals?

**The forward EPS estimate of $4.11 appears to be a mechanical extrapolation, not a fundamental forecast.**

#### **The Bull is Cherry-Picking the Forward Estimates**

Here's what the bull won't admit: **Forward earnings estimates are frequently wrong, especially for mature consumer staples.**

Analyst estimates incorporate consensus optimism bias. For a stock that's already up significantly (CL is trading in a $89-94 range near historical highs), forward estimates are typically inflated because:
1. Sell-side analysts are bullish on large-cap defensive names (they're "safe" calls)
2. Consensus estimates lag reality—they're backward-looking
3. Once estimates get elevated, they rarely come down until earnings actually miss

**The bull is assuming the market's forward estimate is correct. But what if it's wrong?** What if CL grows EPS at 8-10% instead of 61%? Then the forward P/E of 21.93x becomes a **35-40x multiple on real growth**—and we're back to an expensive stock.
