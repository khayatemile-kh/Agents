## THE TECHNICAL SETUP: RSI DIVERGENCE IS NOT A SELL SIGNAL IN CONSOLIDATION

Now let me address the technical argument, which is where I actually find the most problematic framing.

The Aggressive Analyst points to RSI divergence (69.76 down to 53.32) as proof momentum is exhausted and the stock will break lower "with velocity." This is a classic misinterpretation of what divergence means in a consolidation zone.

**Here's the empirical reality:** When a stock is consolidating between two major moving averages (the 50 SMA at $52.28 and 200 SMA at $56.57), RSI divergence doesn't predict direction—it predicts indecision. RSI at 53.32 is neutral. It's not overbought (>70), which would clearly suggest profit-taking pressure. It's not oversold (<30), which would clearly suggest capitulation and reversal. At 53, RSI is telling us: "The buyers and sellers are balanced. The next move depends on which group has more conviction."

The failed breakout at $56.71 on August 19 is a *legitimate* technical concern, I grant the Aggressive Analyst that. But a failed breakout in a low-volatility environment doesn't prove a breakdown is imminent—it often just reflects profit-taking after a move. The stock fell to $54.26, reestablished support, and is now consolidating. That's not capitulation; that's healthy correction in a longer-term uptrend.

**The Aggressive Analyst's real claim is that low ATR (2.72%) precedes explosive downside moves.** True statement in the abstract. But they're using it to justify immediate selling, which is where the logic breaks down. Low ATR precedes *significant moves in either direction*. Without additional conviction signals, selling into consolidation just because volatility is compressed is equivalent to selling because "something might happen." That's not risk management; that's hypervigilance.
