## **PART 1: THE ORGANIC REVENUE DECLINE ISN'T "NORMALCY"—IT'S A RED FLAG**

The bull wants us to believe that -1.4% organic revenue guidance is just "normalcy" for a mature food company and that COVID comparisons explain everything. **This is fundamentally wrong, and here's why:**

### **"Normalcy" Would Mean Stabilization, Not Continuous Decline**

The bull's own framing reveals the problem: LW has had "disappointing organic revenue **for two years**." Let me be clear—that's not a single-year normalization. That's a **trend**. When a company's core business deteriorates year-over-year for two consecutive periods *and* the guidance suggests a third year of decline ahead, we're not looking at COVID "normalization"—we're looking at **structural demand destruction**.

Here's the critical distinction:
- **"Normalizing after COVID surge"** would mean: 2024 down, 2025 flat-to-up, 2026 stable
- **"Structural headwind"** means: 2024 down, 2025 down, 2026 down

LW is exhibiting the second pattern. This suggests consumer demand for frozen potatoes is not just normalizing—it's **declining**.

### **Why the COVID Comparison Argument Collapses**

The bull claims that foodservice demand temporarily shifted to retail during lockdowns, and now it's "reverting to trend." But let's examine this logic:

**First**, if that were true, we'd expect to see foodservice recovering post-COVID. The data doesn't support this narrative. QSR (quick-service restaurant) traffic has remained structurally challenged in 2026, with chains reporting modest-to-flat growth. If foodservice were recovering, LW's volumes should be improving. They're not.

**Second**, the retail side isn't picking up the slack. Retail frozen potato consumption has not accelerated—if anything, the shift to fresh/healthier alternatives has persisted post-COVID. This is a **secular trend**, not a cyclical one. Consumers have *remembered* that fresh alternatives exist, and they're not going back to frozen in mass quantities.

**Third**, the bull provides no evidence that the -1.4% guidance is "already priced in." In fact, analyst sentiment according to the data is increasingly cautious—Simply Wall St. explicitly questions whether the rebound is "fully priced." If the market truly believed -1.4% organic decline was the floor, the stock would be trading lower, not rallying +30.9% YTD on momentum.

Here's a critical piece the bull glosses over: **Zero StockTwits messages, zero Reddit mentions across WSB, r/stocks, and r/investing in the past week, despite a +30.9% YTD rally.**
