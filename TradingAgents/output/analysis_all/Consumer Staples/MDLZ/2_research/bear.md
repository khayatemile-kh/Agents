## OPENING STATEMENT: THE BULL CASE IS A HOUSE OF CARDS

I appreciate the bull's optimism, but let me be direct: **MDLZ is a value trap masquerading as a turnaround story.** The company is caught between two worlds—it can't abandon its mature, declining cash cow (traditional confectionery) fast enough to offset the structural pressures, while simultaneously taking on massive financial risks to chase protein bars and wellness trends it may not win.

Here's my core argument: **The market didn't misprice MDLZ due to "near-term noise"—it correctly identified that MDLZ is a slow-growth, overleveraged business facing secular volume declines, with a pivot strategy that is both capital-intensive and uncertain.**

Let me dismantle the bull's thesis point by point, and then present why the risks far outweigh the opportunities.

## I. THE "PIVOT" NARRATIVE IS WISHFUL THINKING, NOT STRATEGY

### **A. Grenade Doesn't Solve MDLZ's Core Problem—It's a Distraction**

The bull claims: "Grenade could add $500M+ in revenue within 3-5 years and leverages our distribution network."

**Here's the reality check:**

1. **$500M is Less Than 2% of Current Revenue**
   - MDLZ's current revenue base is $39.7B
   - Even if Grenade hits $500M in five years (aggressive assumption), that's a **1.25% contribution** to total revenues
   - For a $79.7B market cap company, this is statistically insignificant relative to the execution risk and capex required

2. **The Protein Bar Category is SATURATED**
   - Yes, it's growing at 8-12% CAGR. But here's the catch: **everyone is already in this market**
   - Quest Nutrition, RXBAR (now Nestlé), Clif Bar (Mondelez-owned), Perfect Bar, KIND (Mondelez-owned)... MDLZ *already owns multiple protein bar brands*
   - Adding Grenade doesn't create market share—it **cannibalizes internal brands** while facing established competitors
   - The bull essentially concedes that MDLZ is now competing against itself (Grenade vs. Clif vs. Perfect Bar)
   - **Translation: You're paying for financial engineering, not organic growth**

3. **Distribution Network Isn't a Moat Here**
   - The bull brags about "190-country distribution." But here's the uncomfortable truth:
   - **Every major competitor has similar or better distribution**: Nestlé, PepsiCo, Mondelez's own competitors already have direct-to-retail relationships
   - Distribution in protein bars is increasingly **direct-to-consumer and e-commerce** (Amazon, DTC platforms), not dependent on legacy retail networks
   - The real bottleneck isn't getting Grenade onto shelves—it's **capturing market share from entrenched competitors** who have higher brand loyalty and marketing budgets

4. **Capital Efficiency is a Myth**
   - The bull claims this is "capital-efficient" because MDLZ isn't "building distribution from scratch"
   - But protein bars require massive **marketing spend** to drive trial and repeat purchase
   - MDLZ already spent billions acquiring these brands (Clif, Perfect Bar, Grenade). Now it needs to spend billions more on *promotional support* to differentiate Grenade from 50+ competitors
   - This is NOT capital-efficient. It's **overleveraged capital burning through cash to chase a crowded category**

---

### **B. Oreo's "Limited Edition" Strategy is a Margin Killer, Not a Margin Savior**

The bull claims: "Limited-edition Oreo flavors drive retention, pricing power, and mix improvement."

**Let me challenge this directly:**

1. **Limited Editions CANIBALIZE Core Sales**
   - Every "chicken and waffles" Oreo or "strawberry milkshake" flavor is a **replacement purchase**, not an incremental purchase
   - A consumer spending $3.99 on a limited-edition pack is **not spending $3.99 on a regular Oreo AND a limited edition**
   - The quarterly revenue data supports this: Q2 2026 revenue was $10.08B (down 4.0% vs. Q1's $10.50B)
   - If the innovation strategy were working, we'd see **sequential revenue growth**, not decline

2. **Pricing Power is Weakening, Not Strengthening**
   - The bull argues that Oreo innovation "allows MDLZ to maintain premium pricing"
   - But the data shows **net earnings declining** despite holding the line on prices: 
     - Q3 2025 Net Income: $743M
     - Q4 2025 Net Income: $665M
     - Q1 2026 Net Income: $560M
     - Q2 2026 Net Income: $560M
   - This is a **16% decline in net income over two quarters**
   - If the company had real pricing power, net income would be stable or rising. Instead, it's **collapsing**
   - This tells me: **Volume is declining faster than price increases can offset**

3. **The "Mix Improvement" Story Falls Apart with COGS Trends**
   - Look at the cost of revenue trajectory:
     - Q3 2025: $6.05B COGS on $8.98B revenue = 67.4% COGS ratio
     - Q2 2026: $7.28B COGS on $10.08B revenue = 72.2% COGS ratio
   - **COGS as a % of revenue is RISING**, not falling
   - This means **mix is actually degrading**, not improving
   - Ingredient costs, supply chain, and input prices are eating into margins faster than premium positioning can defend
   - The bull's "mix improvement" thesis is contradicted by the actual COGS data
