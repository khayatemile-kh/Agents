**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make points worth weighing, but the bear case lands harder on the facts actually presented. The bull's story rests on three pillars: (1) margin expansion is "sustainable operational leverage," (2) +3% organic growth is a genuine inflection, and (3) valuation at 25.85x forward is justified. The bear directly undercuts each: the +320bps margin "expansion" is largely a bounce off an unusually weak Q2 2025 comp (4.1% margin quarter) rather than a clean multi-year trend, and quarter-over-quarter comparisons (Q1 FY26 15.0% vs Q4 FY25 14.4% vs Q1 FY25 11.4%) show a much more modest, less dramatic improvement story than the "+320bps proof point" implies. The +3% organic growth figure is also below the 4-6% typically cited for the luxury/prestige beauty category, meaning EL is still a share-loser even while "returning to growth" — that's a real problem the bull doesn't rebut.

More importantly, the balance sheet risk the bear raises is not adequately answered by the bull at all: a 242.93x debt-to-equity ratio, negative tangible book value (-$1.8B), thin interest coverage (~2.8x), and heavy reliance on goodwill/intangibles for solvency are serious structural vulnerabilities that don't disappear just because one quarter beat estimates. Combined with an elevated-rate environment (5.25-5.50% Fed funds, no cuts priced through 2026) squeezing a company with significant floating-rate debt, this raises real tail risk if the recovery stalls or China demand softens — a scenario the bull treats as a non-issue ("insulated") without real evidence.

On valuation, the bull's own sensitivity math is telling: at a more conservative (arguably still generous) 20x multiple on even an optimistic $4.50-5.50 normalized EPS scenario 2-3 years out, upside is capped around $90-110 — essentially flat-to-modest-upside from levels near $100, and that's the bull case's own best-case number. That means the stock, having already re-rated +24% in two weeks on a single earnings beat, has priced in a lot of the good news with limited incremental upside baked into the bulls' own framework, while the bear's downside case (margin reversal as cost cuts exhaust, debt refinancing risk, goodwill impairment risk) is not small.

Net: the rally already captures much of the achievable good news; the underlying growth rate lags the category; the balance sheet is genuinely fragile in a way equity holders bear directly; and the bull's own valuation math shows limited upside from here. This tilts toward a cautious stance — not a full conviction sell (there is real, if overstated, evidence of operational improvement and China strength), but the risk/reward after a 24% run does not favor adding or holding full-sized exposure. I'm recommending Underweight: trim into strength, not chase the rally, and reassess if margin/growth trends prove durable over the next 2-3 quarters and if leverage metrics improve.
