**Q2 historically includes strong spring/summer demand onset.** A 61% decline in profitability during what should be an improving season is **not seasonal—it's structural demand failure.**

The bull's TTM profitability is inflated by Q3's seasonal strength. Strip that out, and the run-rate profitability is much weaker. **The market is right to be concerned.**

## THE REAL BEAR CASE: STRUCTURAL HEADWINDS THAT WON'T REVERSE

### Headwind 1: Sector-Wide Demand Destruction (Not Temporary)

The bull dismisses hard seltzer's collapse and argues premiumization is "cyclical." I say: **he's misunderstanding the demand shift that's underway.**

#### Beer Consumption Trends: The Uncomfortable Reality

**U.S. Beer Consumption (Five-Year Trend):**
- Per-capita beer consumption: DOWN 1-2% annually
- Total beer volume: DOWN 3-4% annually
- Low-end/value beer: FLAT to declining
- Premium beer: Gaining volume but at much lower growth rates than 2010s

**The hard truth:** Beer consumption in developed markets is **structurally declining**. This isn't a cyclical rebound waiting to happen—it's a secular trend driven by:

1. **Younger consumers preferring spirits and wine** over beer
2. **Cannabis legalization diverting discretionary spending** from alcohol
3. **Health consciousness reducing alcohol consumption** broadly
4. **Hard seltzers cannibalizing mainstream beer sales** (then collapsing themselves)

The bull points to Corona and Modelo's market share gains. Let me be blunt: **if you're gaining share while the total market shrinks, you're not growing—you're just losing slower than competitors.**

#### Premiumization Has Hit a Ceiling

The bull argues premiumization is "temporary" weakness. The data disagrees:

**What's Happening:** Consumers are trading DOWN, not up.
- Core beer drinkers are facing **higher cost-of-living pressures** (rent, energy, food inflation)
- Consumer discretionary spending is being squeezed
- Premium beer typically has 2-3x markup over value beer
- **Consumers are abandoning premium for mid-tier and value**

**Evidence from the sector:**
- Brown-Forman Q1 2026: Premiumization stalling across portfolios
- Molson Coors: Struggling to maintain pricing power
- Boston Beer: Highly cyclical, showing weakness
- Altria: Narrowing product portfolio (not expanding—consolidating)

**The bull's claim that premiumization is cyclical ignores the real driver: consumer purchasing power is declining.** That's structural, not cyclical.
