**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points, but weighing them, the bear case carries more weight for near-term positioning. The bull's core argument rests on TTM profitability metrics (20.1% net margin, 23.7% ROE) and a valuation gap (10.44x forward P/E vs peers), but the bear correctly identifies that these trailing metrics are being distorted by one strong seasonal quarter (Q3) masking a genuinely alarming sequential deterioration in Q2 FY2026 (revenue down 23% sequentially, net income down 61%, operating margin down 770bps to 27.7%). That's not noise-level volatility; it's a real signal of demand and margin stress showing up in the most recent trailing data, which undercuts the bull's reliance on TTM figures as forward-looking proof of health.

The bull's valuation argument is the strongest part of the case—STZ does trade at a discount to peers and the market on a forward multiple, and normally that would be a value signal. But cheap-for-a-reason is a real risk here: the bear's point that the 19.3% forward EPS growth consensus requires either a volume inflection (which nothing in the beer/hard seltzer data supports, given structural per-capita beer decline and hard seltzer collapse) or margin expansion (which the Q2 data argues against, given commodity/input cost pressure) is a serious, underaddressed gap in the bull thesis. The bull never actually reconciled how 19.3% EPS growth is supposed to materialize, which is the linchpin of the "screaming buy" framing—if that growth doesn't show up, the stock isn't as cheap as the multiple implies.

The bear's institutional-abandonment point (Berkshire trimming the position) is circumstantial but adds to the qualitative case that smart money is not using this dip as a buying opportunity, which should give pause given the bull's core thesis is essentially "everyone else is wrong about the risk, take the value."

That said, I don't think this rises to a full Sell/exit call — the company still generates genuine free cash flow (~$2B TTM), maintains a real dividend, has iconic brands (Corona, Modelo) with actual competitive strength versus pure decline, and the valuation discount is real even if partly justified. This isn't a broken balance sheet or a melting ice cube; it's a quality franchise facing a genuine cyclical/structural demand air pocket that the market has already partially priced in. Given the demonstrated sequential deterioration, uncertain path to the growth priced into consensus estimates, and lack of a clear near-term catalyst to prove the bear thesis wrong, I'm siding with caution over the bull's "screaming buy" framing, but not going all the way to Sell given the cash-generative core business and dividend support.

Net: Underweight. Trim exposure into strength/any bounce toward $140+, avoid adding on the "cheap valuation" argument alone until there's clear evidence (a quarter or two) that volume/margin trends have stabilized and that the 19%+ EPS growth consensus is actually achievable rather than aspirational.
