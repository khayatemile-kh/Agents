## THE BEAR CASE: FOUR CRITICAL REBUTTALS

### **REBUTTAL 1: IMPAIRMENTS AREN'T ONE-TIME CHARGES—THEY'RE SYMPTOMS OF ONGOING DETERIORATION**

**What the Bull Said:**
"Impairments are backward-looking. Management is cleaning house. This is bullish."

**The Bear Reality:**
This is the classic value-trap rationalization. Let me show you why:

**The $1.96 Billion Impairment Reveals a Pattern, Not an Anomaly:**

1. **This Isn't the First Impairment:**
   - Q2 2026: $1.96B impairment
   - Q4 2025: $968.3M impairment
   - Q2 2025: $53.2M impairment
   - **Total write-downs in 18 months: ~$3.0 billion**

   This isn't "cleaning house once." This is **repeated, ongoing asset deterioration**. If management made good acquisition decisions, you don't write down nearly $3 billion in 18 months. This is evidence of *systematic bad capital allocation*, not a one-time error.

2. **Goodwill Depletion Continues:**
   - Feb 28, 2026: Goodwill & Intangibles = $11.92B
   - May 31, 2026: Goodwill & Intangibles = $9.95B
   - **Depleted by $1.97B in 3 months—the pace of destruction is accelerating**

   At this rate of write-downs, the company will have depleted its entire goodwill base within 2-3 years. That's not "clearing the deck"; that's **evidence of failed M&A strategy compounding**.

3. **The "Normalized Operating Income" Argument Doesn't Survive Scrutiny:**

   The bull claims: "Add back the $1.96B impairment, and Q2 shows ~$262M profit."

   But here's the problem: **Impairments exist because those assets aren't generating the cash flows they should be.**

   Think about this logically:
   - Conagra bought brands/companies for $1.96B
   - Those businesses are now worth $0
   - But the underlying operations *within* those businesses are supposedly still generating cash?

   **That's contradictory.** If the cash flows were there, the impairment wouldn't be necessary. The impairment exists *because* those business units are broken, cannibalized by competition, or technologically obsolete.

   **Real-world example:** Look at Hormel Foods (HRL). Have they taken $3B in impairments over 18 months? No. Have their acquisitions gone sideways at this rate? No. CAG's impairment pattern is *not* industry-standard; it's CAG-specific failure.

4. **Operating Cash Flow Is a Mirage (More on This Below):**

   The bull highlights Q2 FCF of $397.3M as proof of health. But let's trace the cash:
   - **Operating Cash Flow: $506.5M** (includes $1.96B non-cash impairment add-back)
   - **Capital Expenditures: -$109.2M** (normal)
   - **Free Cash Flow: $397.3M**
   - **But wait:** Dividend payments ($167.5M) + debt service obligations ($hundreds of millions)
   - **Real question:** After dividends and debt service, is cash position improving or deteriorating?

   **Cash position degradation:**
   - Aug 31, 2025: $698.1M cash
   - May 31, 2026: $218M cash
   - **Decline: 69% in 9 months**

   The company is **bleeding cash** despite the headline FCF. Why? Because the impairment charges are masking the fact that underlying operating performance is weakening.

**Bear Point:** Impairments signal broken asset bases and failed acquisitions. They're warning flags about management quality and strategic execution, not cleanup opportunities. The ongoing pattern of write-downs indicates this problem is *structural and ongoing*, not solved by one charge.

### **REBUTTAL 2: THE VALUATION IS CHEAP FOR A REASON—AND THAT REASON IS STRUCTURAL DETERIORATION**

"CAG trades at 1.33x EV/Revenue vs. peers at 1.6-1.85x. It's 40% discounted and offers 14.4% total returns."

**The Bear Reality:**
The discount exists because markets are efficiently pricing in durability risk. Let me show why:

**The Valuation "Discount" Is Actually Risk Premium:**

1. **The Peer Comparison Ignores Quality Differentials:**

   | Company | EV/Revenue | 5-Yr Returns | Margin Trend | Debt/Equity | Impairment History |
   |---------|-----------|-------------|-------------|------------|-------------------|
   | CAG | 1.33x | -37% | ↓ Deteriorating | 117.6x | $3B in 18 months |

   **The market is telling you:** "We'll pay more for HRL because management executes better and debt is sustainable. We'll pay less for CAG because we don't trust management and debt is unsustainable."

   This isn't mispricing. **This is the market working correctly.**

2. **The "14.4% Expected Return" Calculation Is Based on Assumptions That Won't Hold:**

   The bull's math:
   - EPS growth: ~10%
   - Dividend yield: 4.39%
   - **Total return: 14.4%**

   **But this assumes:**
   - EPS growth will materialize (no evidence yet)
   - Dividend will be maintained (at risk—see below)
   - Debt won't blow up (very risky)

   **A) EPS Growth Assumption Is Unfounded:**
   - TTM EPS: -$4.02
   - Forward EPS (analyst consensus): $1.55
   - That's a ~$5.57 swing—almost entirely from *removing* one-time charges, not operational improvement
   - Where's the 10% growth coming from after that?
   - Margin pressure is industry-wide but hitting CAG hardest
   - Revenue is flat-to-down
   - **Conclusion:** 10% EPS growth is fantasy, not forecast

   **B) Dividend Sustainability Is Questionable:**
   - Annual dividend: $670M
   - FCF (adjusted for the cash drain we identified): ~$400-450M after D&A normalization
   - **Dividend is NOT covered by sustainable cash flow**
   - The company has been maintaining the dividend by *depleting cash reserves*

   - At this burn rate: **Dividend cut within 12-18 months is material risk**

   Once the dividend is cut, the "safety net" disappears and the stock reprices down 20-30% instantly (dividend investors exit).

   **C) Debt Sustainability Is the Real Crisis:**
   - Total Debt: $7.27B
   - Net Debt: $7.05B
   - TTM Net Income (normalized): ~$262M (excluding impairments, still very weak)
   - **Net Debt / Normalized Income: 26.9x leverage**
   - This is **distressed territory**
   - Debt service obligations are eating cash flow
   - Refinancing risk exists if credit rating falls
