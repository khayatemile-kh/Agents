**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Weighing both sides, the bear case lands more hits on the specific, verifiable numbers, while the bull case leans more on narrative and 'this is a feature not a bug' framing. The bull's strongest points are real: KMB generates meaningful FCF, the dividend yield (~4.78%) is attractive in absolute terms, the business is genuinely defensive/non-discretionary, and productivity gains (6.4%) are a real offset to input cost inflation. Kenvue optionality is a legitimate call option on re-rating if the deal closes cleanly.

But the bear's rebuttal exposes real cracks that the bull glossed over or mischaracterized: (1) the bull literally conflated a 1.8x payout ratio with '1.75x coverage'—dividends ($1.7B) exceed TTM FCF ($941M), meaning the dividend is NOT actually covered by FCF today, it's being funded partly through incremental debt (long-term debt +$1.329B in a single quarter cited by the bear). That's a structural red flag, not a rounding error. (2) Balance sheet quality is genuinely poor on the metrics presented: negative tangible book value, sub-1.0 current ratio, negative working capital, and equity that's shrinking QoQ. Even if some of this is a legacy of historical buybacks rather than new deterioration, it removes the margin of safety the bull is implicitly assuming when calling this a 'fortress.' (3) The forward EPS bridge is shaky — normalized quarterly EPS run-rate ($4.60-$6.00 annualized) sits well below the $7.49 forward estimate the bull's whole valuation case (14.6x forward P/E, 'asymmetric opportunity') depends on. If forward EPS is optimistic by 25-35%, the 'cheap forward multiple' argument collapses back toward the more expensive TTM multiple (21x), which is not a bargain for a low/flat revenue growth staples name. (4) Kenvue is being sold by the bull as a clean catalyst, but integration of a J&J consumer-health spinoff onto an already leveraged balance sheet adds execution and financing risk — this cuts against the 'hidden optionality' framing and is closer to the bear's 'amplification of existing problems' view, especially given the company's demonstrated preference for debt-funded buybacks over deleveraging.

Net: this isn't a slam-dunk sell — the yield is real, the category is defensive, and macro backdrop (low recession odds, controlled inflation) is genuinely supportive of staples generally. But the balance-sheet fragility, the dividend coverage math that the bull got backwards, and the EPS estimate risk mean I can't underwrite this as a Buy/Overweight. The stock is down ~11% over 12 months and trading below its short-term moving averages with negative MACD — momentum agrees with the bear. I'm landing on Underweight: trim exposure, don't add on the 'cheap forward multiple' story until (a) FCF visibly covers the dividend without incremental debt, and (b) forward EPS estimates get confirmed rather than assumed, and (c) there's more clarity on Kenvue financing terms and leverage impact.
