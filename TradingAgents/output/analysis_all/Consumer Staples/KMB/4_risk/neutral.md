The aggressive analyst correctly identifies that KMB faces a real dividend sustainability question. Here's the honest assessment: **the 180% payout ratio on TTM FCF basis is mathematically real, not an accounting fiction.** The conservative analyst tries to rationalize this away by pointing to normalized FCF of $2.0-2.1 billion, but that normalized figure is itself a projection, not a current reality.

Let me be precise about what the data actually shows:

**The TTM FCF picture is genuinely weak:**
- TTM FCF: $941 million
- Annual dividends: $1.7 billion
- The math is inescapable: $1.7B / $941M = 180.5%

**But here's where the aggressive analyst goes too far:** They treat this tail risk as imminent destiny rather than a scenario requiring surveillance. They're selling *now* based on *potential* deterioration that hasn't materialized yet. That's not risk management; that's preemptive panic.

But here's the problem: KMB is simultaneously trying to maintain a high dividend *and* fund an acquisition *and* invest in innovation. This pattern suggests the company doesn't have sustainable FCF to both service debt and fund dividends without borrowing. The conservative analyst's framework doesn't quite grapple with that.

**The equity erosion is also material:**
- Stockholders' equity: $1.750 billion (down from $1.796 billion in Q1)
- Tangible book value: -$154 million (negative)
- Debt-to-equity: 348%

The conservative analyst glosses over this because they're focused on interest coverage (which is indeed healthy). But negative tangible book value means the company's brand value and goodwill are *everything*. If KMB hits a brand deterioration (like a product scandal) or if the Kenvue deal craters, the equity base evaporates instantly. There's almost no cushion.

**This is the conservative analyst's blind spot:** They're saying, "The company can service debt, so it's fine," but they're not asking, "What's the company actually worth *excluding* brands and goodwill?" The answer is: very little. That's a concentration risk they're not acknowledging.
