On balance, the picture is not one of imminent collapse (cash generation is still substantial and coverage, while thinning, is not yet in crisis territory), but it is also not a table-pounding buy — the trend lines the bear identifies (margin compression, accelerating volume decline, dividend growing faster than FCF) are directionally against the bull's flat-FCF-forever narrative and represent a real deterioration in the safety margin of the story, even if we're several years from any dividend cut. This is a case for holding existing exposure and collecting the very high, still-covered yield, rather than adding aggressively into a business with confirmed decelerating fundamentals, or exiting a name that still throws off ~8% of market cap in cash annually with investment-grade leverage. The most defensible stance given genuinely balanced but diverging directional signals (attractive valuation/yield vs. deteriorating trend in margins/coverage) is to hold current position size without adding, while monitoring the margin/dividend coverage trend closely for confirmation of the bear thesis before trimming.
