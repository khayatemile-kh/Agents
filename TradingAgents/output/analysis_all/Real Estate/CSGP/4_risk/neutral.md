## Now, Here's What Both of You Are Getting Wrong About the Real Estate Sector Dynamics

The Aggressive Analyst makes a valid point about Fed policy and real estate headwinds. The Conservative Analyst says these are "already priced in." Neither of you is fully correct, and this is where I think the biggest blind spot in this analysis exists.

Here's what the data actually tells us: The Fed has an 89% probability of holding rates flat through 2026. That's real. Prediction markets show 75% probability that US median home values stay below $419K through Q3. That's also real. But here's what neither of you is connecting to CoStar's actual business model: **CoStar's revenue isn't purely transaction-dependent in the way a brokerage or auction platform is**.

CoStar generates roughly 60-70% of revenue from subscriptions and recurring data services, not transaction fees. Yes, transaction volumes matter for new customer acquisition and expansion revenue. But the core subscription base is fairly sticky even during real estate downturns. This is important because it means CoStar has a buffer that a pure transaction-based business doesn't have.

However—and this is critical—CoStar's *growth* is dependent on transaction activity. And if we're in a regime of flat rates through 2026, transaction volumes will be compressed relative to the company's historical growth assumptions. So the question isn't "will CoStar's core business collapse?" The question is "will CoStar grow at 8-12% annually as the market might have assumed pre-rate-hike, or will growth compress to 2-4%?"

That actually matters a lot for the valuation. If the market was pricing in 12% revenue growth and CoStar delivers 4% growth, that creates a repricing opportunity. But we don't yet have confirmation of where growth is actually landing in 2026. The Q2 2026 revenue of $925M against Q2 2025 of $781M suggests growth of about 18%, but that's boosted by Zonda consolidation. Organic growth is probably closer to 4-6%.
