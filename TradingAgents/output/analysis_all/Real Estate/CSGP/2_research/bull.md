## Part 1: The "Compressed Earnings" Problem is Temporary, Not Terminal

**EBITDA is $394M on $3.56B revenue = 11.08% EBITDA margin.** That's healthy for a SaaS/software hybrid. EBITDA margin is where the *operational* story lives, and it's solid.

The gap between EBITDA ($394M) and net income ($74M) is $320M—driven by:
1. **Interest expense** (~$35-40M annually on $1.2B debt)
2. **Depreciation & Amortization** (~$150-160M from the $6.65B goodwill base)
3. **Tax normalization**

**But here's the critical point:** The $6.65B in goodwill and intangibles *includes the Zonda acquisition ($800M deal announced/closed recently).* Zonda integration is in **early innings**. When Zonda's revenue (estimated $120-150M annually) flows through and the integration synergies materialize, net margins will expand *significantly* because D&A won't scale proportionally.

**Margin Expansion Thesis:**
- Zonda adds $120-150M revenue at ~60%+ gross margins
- Modest incremental SG&A due to operational overlap
- Net result: $10-15M+ additional net income within 12-18 months
- That's a **13-20% earnings lift** from a single integration

The bears will cite compressed margins as a red flag. I see them as **a mathematical artifact of integration timing**, not fundamental business weakness.
