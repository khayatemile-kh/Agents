**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bear case lands more decisive blows in this debate. The bull's central thesis rests on three pillars that the bear systematically undermines: (1) the 'integration noise' argument for compressed margins, (2) buybacks as a confidence signal, and (3) forward P/E-based valuation upside.

On margins, the bull argues EBITDA is the 'real' operational story and net margin compression is a temporary artifact of Zonda integration D&A. The bear correctly points out this is circular - the goodwill/intangible base is growing, not shrinking, so D&A is a structural drag, not a one-time noise item. More damning is the quarterly trend the bear lays out: a -3.7% net margin loss in Q3 2025 and only 0.3% in Q1 2026 before a modest 5.9% in Q2 2026. That's a volatile, weak trajectory, not a clean margin-expansion story with 'visible catalysts' already underway.

On cash flow and buybacks, this is where the bear's argument is most powerful and hardest to rebut. The bull frames $972M in buybacks against $413M TTM FCF as 'strategic capital allocation' and a sign of management confidence. The bear's math is simple and compelling: repurchasing at 2.35x the rate of FCF generation, funded by a cash balance that fell from $3.73B to $1.27B (a 66% decline) is balance-sheet liquidation, not strength. Combined with a cited 29% sequential decline in operating cash flow (Q4 2025 $162M → Q1 2026 $152M → Q2 2026 $115M), this raises real sustainability concerns about the buyback program and the 'fortress balance sheet' claim the bull leans on.

On valuation, the bull's forward P/E of 18x and $55-65 'normalized' price target depend on consensus EPS of $1.73 - a number the bear shows requires ~860% earnings growth from the current ~$0.18 TTM EPS run rate. That's an enormous assumption resting entirely on Zonda synergies and margin normalization materializing on a specific timeline, none of which is evidenced in the trailing data (which instead shows a loss quarter as recently as Q3 2025).

The bull does have legitimate points - EBITDA margins are respectable, the Ten-X/Zonda strategic logic is reasonable, and the technical setup (RSI neutral, MACD turning positive, price above 50-day SMA) suggests near-term stabilization. But strategic logic and technical bounce potential don't offset the concrete evidence of cash burn, declining operating cash flow, and a valuation thesis built on unproven forward assumptions. The weight of the actual reported financials favors caution over conviction-buying a name still mid-transformation with observable deterioration in the metrics that matter most (net income volatility, cash reserves, OCF trend).

This isn't a full Sell/exit case - the EBITDA profile, asset-light model, and strategic rationale for Zonda/Ten-X keep this from being a broken business outright, and the technical base-building argument has some merit for existing holders. But the bear's evidence on cash burn sustainability and the fantastical nature of the consensus EPS assumption is strong enough to warrant trimming exposure rather than adding or holding steady.
