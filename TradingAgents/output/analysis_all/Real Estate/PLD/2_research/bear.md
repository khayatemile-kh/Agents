### **FFO/AFFO Multiples Don't Solve the Problem—They Hide It**

The bull claims PLD trades at 15.8x forward FFO and 18.1x forward AFFO, calling these "fair value."

**Let me check those numbers against reality:**

**Problem #1: The FFO Estimates Are Not Conservative**

The bull estimated FFO per share of ~$3.90 and AFFO of ~$3.40 based on "fundamentals." But I have actual guidance data:

- **Reported Q2 2026 Net Income: $1.06B** (quarterly)
- **TTM Net Income: $4.20B** (annualized)
- **Forward EPS guidance: $3.405** (provided in the data)

If forward EPS is $3.405 and there are ~958M shares outstanding, that's total forward earnings of approximately **$3.26B annually.** 

Now, FFO typically adds back depreciation and amortization. For a REIT with $81.23B in property assets, depreciation is likely $2.5-3.5B annually. So forward FFO might be $3.26B + $2.5B = $5.76B, or **~$6.01 per share.**

**That's NOT $3.90 per share.** Either the bull's math is off, or he's being selective with assumptions.

**Problem #2: Even at the Bull's Numbers, 18x AFFO Is Not "Fair"**

Let's accept the bull's $3.40 AFFO per share at face value.

- Current price: $139.68
- AFFO per share: $3.40
- Implied multiple: **139.68 ÷ 3.40 = 41.1x AFFO**

That is **NOT 18.1x.** That's over 2x higher.

Or, working backward from an 18x multiple:
- 18x × $3.40 = $61.20 per share

**At fair value of 18x AFFO, PLD would trade at $61.20, implying 56% downside from current levels.**

The bull's valuation math doesn't add up. And that's a serious problem.
