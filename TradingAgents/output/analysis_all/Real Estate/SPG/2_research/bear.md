## **PART 1: YOUR CASH FLOW ARGUMENT IS MISLEADING**

### **1A: Free Cash Flow is Hiding Deterioration**

Yes, SPG reported $3.2B in FCF in 2025. But let's look at **what that number actually represents:**

| Year | Operating Cash Flow | CapEx | FCF | FCF Margin (FCF ÷ Revenue) |
|------|-------------------|-------|-----|--------------------------|
| 2025 | $4,137M | $(934)M | $3,202M | **50.4%** |

**Here's what the trend shows:**
- FCF margin is **collapsing** (down 8.5 percentage points in 3 years)

- Operating cash flow growth has **stalled** (+8.4% over 3 years = 2.8% CAGR) despite you claiming 8.3% operating revenue growth

**The disconnect is critical:** Operating revenue is growing, but OCF is barely moving. This tells me that:

1. **Tenant quality is declining** (more leverage needed for tenant improvements and concessions to attract/retain them)
2. **Working capital is consuming cash** (receivables collecting slower, current liabilities building)
3. **Operational efficiency is eroding**

This is the opposite of a healthy cash machine. This is a **business where incremental revenue is increasingly low-margin or requires capital investment to achieve.**

Your market size analysis assumes SPG can capture 0.3–0.8% of a $30B retail media market. But that assumes **SPG is a credible, high-ROI advertising platform.**
