**You're adding back depreciation and amortization as if it's a positive adjustment.** But in depreciating real estate, depreciation IS economic reality. It's not "accounting noise"—it's a real claim on future cash flows.

1. **Buildings age and require capital replacement.** A 20-year-old office building requires significant capex to remain competitive with newer, better-designed spaces. Your $336.9M operating cash flow in Q2 2026 assumes minimal capex, but that's misleading.

2. **Depreciation charges reflect economic wear.** When you add back $300M/quarter in D&A, you're essentially saying "ignore the fact that our buildings are becoming less valuable." But that IS the fact that matters.

3. **Real Estate Math:** 
   - Rental revenue = $643M (Q2 2026)
   - Operating expenses = $341M
   - Normalized cash flow ≈ $300M pre-tax
   - But buildings are depreciating, so some of that $300M goes to replacing worn-out assets
   - The "free cash flow" figure assumes you can pocket the depreciation benefit in perpetuity—you can't
