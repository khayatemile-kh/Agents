**Investment Thesis**: The debate converges on a real but non-terminal deterioration story. Bear/aggressive evidence that carries the most weight: (1) dividend coverage has compressed from headline TTM 1.6x to a run-rate 1.13-1.16x based on Q1+Q2 2026 annualized OCF (~$2.08-2.12B), a 22-25% YoY decline — this is arithmetic on the same cash flow statement, not interpretation; (2) stockholders' equity fell from -$1.64B to -$3.27B in two quarters, an accelerating trend per the neutral analyst's own confirmation, and tangible book value is -$9.17B even excluding goodwill, meaning hard assets don't cover liabilities either; (3) revenue (-1.9% TTM) and operating income (-15.8% TTM) are both declining, directly undercutting the bull's inflation-escalator/pricing-power narrative — if lease escalators and tenant demand were working, revenue should not be shrinking; (4) technicals confirm the deterioration is being priced in, not overreacted to: price below both 50 SMA ($76.90) and 200 SMA ($83.79), with all moving averages in a declining trendline, MACD negative, and RSI neutral (49.04) rather than signaling an oversold bounce.

The conservative/neutral pushback earns partial credit but does not overturn the bear case. Negative equity is partly a structural REIT accounting feature and the $22B debt reduction (29.9B->23.4B) provides genuine near-term liquidity breathing room — but the neutral analyst correctly notes this deleveraging came from discontinued-operations asset sales, not organic OCF improvement, making it a one-time bridge rather than a fix. The Q1 ($509M) to Q2 ($531M) OCF sequence shows flat-to-slightly-improving quarterly cash generation, which tempers the "imminent collapse" framing and argues against a binary Sell — dividend cut risk is elevated and tightening, not current or covenant-confirmed.

Net: the preponderance of the freshest, hardest evidence (quarterly run-rate OCF, accelerating negative equity, declining revenue/operating income, and confirming technicals) favors continued de-risking over holding or adding, but the absence of an active covenant breach, confirmed dividend cut, or discredited long-term thesis (satellite disruption was reasonably rebutted) means this stops short of a full exit. This mirrors the NVDA lesson: prospective risks flagged as monitoring triggers should be weighted by probability/timeline rather than escalated to maximum conviction before confirmation — here, unlike NVDA, the trigger metrics (OCF decline, equity deterioration) are already manifesting in real, recent data, which justifies trimming now rather than waiting, but the lesson counsels retaining optionality rather than an all-or-nothing sell given genuine uncertainty about whether the OCF decline is cyclical (capex-cycle related, as the conservative analyst argues) or structural.
