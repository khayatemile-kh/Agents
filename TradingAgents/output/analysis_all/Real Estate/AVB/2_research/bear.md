- 2025: $3,040.7M revenue (+4.4% YoY)
- Q2 2026: $777.8M quarterly (+0.9% QoQ, which annualizes to ~3.6%)

**Even More Concerning—Net Income Dynamics:**

- Q1 2026: Inflated by $179.9M in property sale gains (one-time, not recurring)
- **Strip out the gain:** Q1 2026 normalized earnings were approximately **$150–160M** (rough calculation), implying an annual run rate of **$600–640M**—a **40% decline** from 2025's normalized levels.

The bull argues this is "temporary supply headwinds." **I say it's proof that AVB's rental base is under pressure.** If fundamentals were truly sound, why would the company need to sell properties to maintain earnings?

## II. THE BALANCE SHEET IS NOT "FORTRESS"—IT'S A LIABILITY TIME BOMB

### **The Bull's Deceptive Framing of Leverage**

The bull says: "High leverage is standard for REITs." 

**True. But not ALL leverage is created equal, and AVB's leverage is deteriorating in a dangerous way.**

### **The Specific Vulnerabilities:**

**1. Debt-to-Equity at 75.1x Is NOT Normal—It's Alarming**

Let me put this in context:
- Typical healthy REIT debt-to-equity: **40–60x** (yes, REITs use leverage, but 75x is at the high end)
- AVB's ratio: **75.1x** 
- **Why this matters in a rising-rate environment:** Every 25 basis points of rate increases adds ~$22–25M in annual interest expense on $9.19B of debt

**At current rates of 6.75–7.25%, AVB is paying close to 5.5–6.0% on refinanced debt.** If we model a modest further rate increase or if the company refinances existing debt at current rates:
- Additional $50M in annual interest expense is entirely plausible
- This reduces free cash flow by 3.5% and earnings by 5%+

**The bull dismisses this as "standard REIT practice." I say it's reckless in an environment where rate cuts are OFF the table.**

**2. The Liquidity Picture Is Deteriorating, Not Stable**

The bull claims the $739.6M line of credit drawn is "prudent capital deployment."

**I say it's a red flag that management is resorting to expensive credit lines rather than internal cash generation.**

**Here's the timeline:**
- Q2 2026: $739.6M drawn on credit line
- This represents a **material increase** from typical working capital needs
- Why would a company with $1.67B in operating cash flow need to tap credit lines at 7%+ rates?

**Two possibilities:**
1. **Cash reserves are being drained faster than operations replenish them** (operational stress)
2. **Management is front-running potential future refinancing needs** (preparing for a capital crunch)

Either way, **this is NOT a sign of confidence**—it's a sign of caution and potential stress.

**The bull's framing is backwards:** Drawing credit lines in a high-rate environment is a **luxury you can't afford**, not a sign of "opportunistic" capital deployment.

**Why this matters:**

- But when combined with debt increases AND credit line draws, it signals **desperation for liquidity**
- At $68.14/share, issuing 8.9M shares ($609.8M ÷ $68) is **massively dilutive** to future shareholders

**The math:**
- Pre-issuance shares outstanding: ~142.4M
- Post-issuance: ~151.3M (rough calculation)
- This is **6.2% dilution** in a single quarter
- At that rate, **AVB will have diluted shareholders by ~25%+ annually**

**The bull frames this as "raising capital strategically." I frame it as "destroying shareholder value through dilution while stock is in freefall."**
