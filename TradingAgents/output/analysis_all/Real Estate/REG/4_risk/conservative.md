## The Operating Cash Flow Narrative Needs Serious Qualification

Here's what's actually happening here that gets glossed over in that doomsday framing: REITs have highly seasonal cash flow patterns. Q2 is traditionally one of the strongest cash generation quarters for retail REITs because that's when spring leasing activity settles, tenant improvement allowances get finalized, and rental collections peak heading into summer. Q1, by contrast, is typically weaker because leasing activity was lower in winter months, and it takes three to six months for new leases to generate full-year cash contributions.

Looking at the data provided, we have:

- Q3 2025: $218.7M
- Q4 2025: $203.9M
- Q1 2026: $152.7M

Now, the Aggressive Analyst wants you to see a "cliff," but what I actually see is a **seasonal deceleration pattern moving into the traditionally weak Q1 period, followed by a natural trough that we should expect to recover from as we progress into Q2 2026 and beyond**. This is not a structural collapse—this is a seasonal low.

The TTM operating cash flow sits at approximately $940M. That's not $600-650M annualized run rate that the Aggressive Analyst invented. That's the actual cash being generated across a full business cycle. They reflect what the price *has* already done, not what it *will* do. The MACD being negative at -0.97 tells us that bearish momentum has been present. But technical oversold conditions (RSI at 29.37 on 8/11) followed by a bounce (RSI at 41.79 on 9/01) is a *normal mean-reversion pattern*, not confirmation of further deterioration.
