**Here's the fundamental flaw in that argument:**

REITs don't get to ignore depreciation just because it's a "non-cash charge." Depreciation exists because **assets actually deteriorate.** When REG's shopping centers age, they require:
- Capital expenditure for roof repairs, HVAC replacements, parking lot resurfacing
- Tenant allowances and concessions to maintain occupancy during refurbishment
- Competitive upgrades to compete with newer retail formats (lifestyle centers, mixed-use)

These are **very real cash outlays** that replace the depreciation "magic" the bull cited.

The bull argues: *"REG's portfolio features tenants that are thriving—discount retailers, essential services, grocery-anchored centers."*

1. **These are exactly the tenants that negotiate hard on rent:**

2. **Grocery-anchored centers face their own headwinds:**

3. **The "essential services" argument ignores the real problem:**
