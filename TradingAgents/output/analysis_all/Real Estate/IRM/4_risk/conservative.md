I want to circle back on this because it's the linchpin of my case. The Aggressive Analyst cited 7.73x Total Debt/EBITDA and 6.74x Net Debt/EBITDA. These are not just "elevated." These are distress-level metrics for a REIT.

Let me put this in context. In the 2008-2009 financial crisis, REITs that were levered above 6.0x Net Debt/EBITDA faced:
- Forced covenant testing
- Credit rating downgrades
- Restricted capital access
- Pressure to reduce dividends or asset sales

IRM is currently at 6.74x. And the path forward is *not* toward deleveraging. Here's why:

**Negative free cash flow means the debt book is growing, not shrinking.** If IRM is burning -$436M in FCF annually, and they're using debt to finance operations and dividends, the leverage ratios are *improving in the wrong direction*—i.e., they're getting worse, not better.

The only way out of this is:
1. Free cash flow becomes positive (I don't see evidence this is happening based on margin compression)
2. EBITDA grows faster than debt grows (the debt is growing due to negative FCF, so this is backwards)

None of these are likely in the near term. And if they're not likely, then IRM is on a path toward forced deleveraging through either equity dilution or covenant breaches.
