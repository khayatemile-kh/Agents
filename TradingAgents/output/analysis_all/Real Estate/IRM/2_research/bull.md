## **II. WHY THE BALANCE SHEET "CRISIS" IS LARGELY A REIT ACCOUNTING ARTIFACT**

Now let me address the most aggressive bear criticism head-on: **the negative equity position and sky-high leverage multiples.** The bears will point to:
- Stockholders' equity: **-$1.28B (negative!)**
- Debt/EBITDA: **7.73x (very high!)**
- Net Debt/EBITDA: **6.74x (extremely high!)**

**Here's what the bears don't understand about REITs:**

### **A. REITs Are *Designed* to Carry Leverage**

REITs operate under IRS rules requiring them to:
1. **Distribute 90%+ of taxable income as dividends** (leaving minimal retained earnings)
2. **Finance growth through debt and equity raises,** not retained earnings

**This means negative retained earnings is *normal and expected* for REITs.** It's not a sign of financial distress; it's the *intended capital structure design.*

**Proof:** IRM's dividend yield of **3.03%** is attractive precisely *because* the company returns nearly all cash to shareholders. A healthy REIT distributes capital, not hoards it.

### **B. The Leverage Question: Is 6.74x Net Debt/EBITDA Excessive?**

For specialized asset-based REITs like IRM:
- **Investment-grade range: 5.0x – 8.0x** (typical for asset-backed REITs with long-term contracted cash flows)
- **IRM's 6.74x is in the middle of this range**, not an outlier
- Compare to data center REITs like **Equinix (EQIX)** at **6.5x** — IRM is *in line with peers*

**More importantly:** IRM's debt is supported by:
- **$2.55B in annual EBITDA** (TTM, Q2 2026 annualized: $2.54B)
- **Long-term, inflation-linked contracts** with government and enterprise customers (providing cash flow stability)
- **$12.71B in tangible PP&E** (property, plant, and equipment) backing the debt

The debt is *not* unsecured or speculative; it's secured by mission-critical infrastructure.

**Bull perspective:** A 300 bps margin compression while growing revenue 18.5% YoY is a *favorable trade-off*. As integration synergies materialize and scale kicks in, margins will expand.
