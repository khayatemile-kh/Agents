**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides land real punches, but weighing the arguments carefully tips this toward a modestly cautious/neutral-to-negative stance rather than a confident buy.

Bull's strongest points: AMT genuinely has a wide moat in physical tower infrastructure, contractual escalators (3-5%) provide built-in pricing power, tenant base is investment-grade telcos with high switching costs, and revenue is growing ~6.7% YoY with FCF of ~$2.9B. The satellite-disruption-is-overblown argument has merit on a multi-year horizon — physics and capex intensity do favor terrestrial towers for dense urban capacity, and the technical bounce off June lows with a still-positive MACD is a real, if soft, signal.

Bear's strongest points are more numerically grounded and harder to dismiss: the FCF-minus-interest-minus-dividend waterfall is the crux of the debate. If dividends (~$3.2B) plus debt service exceed organically generated FCF (~$2.9B TTM), the company is structurally reliant on continuous refinancing and/or equity/asset sales to sustain the payout — that's a legitimate capital-structure risk, not just a leverage philosophy point. The 6.4x Debt/EBITDA figure being meaningfully above peers (CCI ~5.5x, SBAC ~5.1x, DLR ~4.2x) is a concrete, checkable red flag the bull never directly rebutted — the bull's response was essentially 'leverage is normal for REITs,' which doesn't address the peer-relative excess. The 2.8x interest coverage ratio is also thin and leaves little margin for a rate or earnings shock, especially with 89% probability of no Fed cuts in 2026 meaning refinancing walls hit at higher rates than legacy debt. Bear's point that CapEx is overwhelmingly maintenance capex rather than growth capex, meaning debt (not operating cash flow) funds expansion, is a serious structural critique of the 'self-funding growth machine' framing.

On the satellite issue, the bear's reframe—that the threat isn't full replacement but marginal displacement and negotiating leverage compression on lease renewals—is the more nuanced and probably more realistic read; the bull's 'physics favors towers, mixed model inevitable' argument doesn't actually rebut the marginal-share-loss / pricing-power-erosion mechanism the bear raises.

On valuation, bull didn't get to finish the point (24.22x P/E), so I can't credit an unstated argument. Bear's overall framing that AMT is over-levered relative to peers and its growth is decelerating (near-term -2% post-earnings, growth capex financed by debt) tips the balance.

Net: this isn't a "sell everything" case—AMT is still a real infrastructure asset with recurring contractual revenue and a moat that isn't disappearing overnight. But the leverage profile is genuinely elevated versus direct peers, coverage ratios are thin, dividend sustainability is not obviously self-funded by organic FCF, and competitive/pricing dynamics from satellite entrants create real (if gradual) erosion risk that the bull didn't adequately counter. That combination argues for trimming/de-risking exposure rather than adding, and definitely not a genuinely balanced 50/50 call — the bear's capital-structure math is the more decision-relevant, quantifiable risk here.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing AMT exposure by roughly 20-30% of the position rather than adding on the recent bounce; use any further strength toward the 200-SMA / prior highs (~$179) as an opportunity to reduce rather than chase.
2) Do not initiate new/incremental positions at current leverage/coverage levels (6.4x Debt/EBITDA, ~2.8x interest coverage) until there is concrete evidence of deleveraging (declining Debt/EBITDA, improved coverage) or a sustained decline in the 10-year yield that meaningfully lowers refinancing costs on the ~$15B of near-term maturities.
3) Monitor next 1-2 quarters of FCF-after-dividend-and-debt-service closely; if the payout continues to exceed organically generated FCF (i.e., growth is debt-funded, not CapEx self-funded), treat this as confirmation to reduce further rather than a signal to average down.
4) Keep a residual/core position (do not go to zero) given the genuine moat, contractual escalators, and investment-grade tenant base — this is a leverage/valuation-timing concern, not a thesis-broken call.
5) Watch satellite-to-phone (Starlink, AST SpaceMobile) commercial rollout news and upcoming lease renewal terms for signs of pricing-power erosion; a negative surprise there would justify moving from Underweight to Sell.
6) Set a stop/review trigger if Debt/EBITDA rises above ~6.5-7x or interest coverage falls below 2.5x in upcoming filings.
