## **SECTION 1: DECONSTRUCTING THE BULL'S REVENUE GROWTH ILLUSION**

### **A. Revenue Growth Is a Mirage Built on Inflation, Not Productivity**

The bull proudly points to **+3.7% TTM revenue growth and +4.9% Q2 YoY growth**. Sound impressive? **Look closer—this is the oldest trick in the REIT playbook: confusing inflation with real operational improvement.**

Here's the critical question the bull won't answer: **How much of this revenue growth is pricing (rent escalations), and how much is from new tenant demand or improved occupancy?**

The data suggests the answer is uncomfortable:

**Revenue Growth Without Tenant Demand = Landlord Desperation**

- If KIM is growing revenue by extracting higher rents from existing tenants without adding new tenants, **this signals landlord pricing power is temporary**
- Tenants accept higher rents only if they have pricing power of their own (which retail tenants increasingly don't)
- When tenants face margin pressure, they will:
  - **Renegotiate leases downward** at renewal (particularly in a high-interest environment where refinancing is painful)
  - **Reduce store footprint** (consolidate locations)
  - **Default or declare bankruptcy** (watch retail bankruptcies spike in Q4 2026-Q1 2027)

**The Bull's +3.7% revenue growth is not evidence of health—it's the death rattle of a landlord squeezing tenants before they break.**

### **B. Occupancy Rates Are the Canary in the Coal Mine—And It's Silent**

**Notice what the bull didn't provide? Occupancy rates.**

This is intentional. KIM's latest earnings materials should include detailed occupancy metrics by property type. The absence of this data in the bull's argument is **deafening.**

Why? Because **if occupancy rates were strong, the bull would have led with them.** Instead, we get vague praise for "grocery-anchored properties" without hard data on:
- Current occupancy rates (my estimate: likely 85-92%, below historical 95%+ benchmarks)
- Occupancy trends quarter-over-quarter
- Tenant renewal rates and renewal spreads
- Bankruptcies and closures among anchor tenants

**The silence is damning.** Revenue can grow while occupancy falls—a classic warning sign that a landlord is compensating for fewer tenants with higher rents per tenant.

### **C. The Grocery-Anchored Defense Is Weakening**

The bull repeatedly cites KIM's focus on "grocery-anchored" and "convenience retail" as recession-resistant. **This is outdated thinking that misses the structural challenges facing grocers in 2026.**

**Grocery retail is under unprecedented margin pressure:**

1. **E-grocery competition is relentless** — Amazon Fresh, Walmart+, Target Circle, and regional players are still aggressively expanding e-commerce fulfillment. Traditional grocers face deflationary pricing pressure online.

2. **Private label erosion** — Grocers' margins are compressed as inflation-weary consumers trade down to private label. When Kroger and Albertsons operate on 2-3% net margins (versus historical 3-5%), they have zero flexibility to pay higher rent.

3. **Consolidation and store rationalization** — The **Kroger-Albertsons merger** (announced 2022, ongoing regulatory battles) signals the sector's desperation. When consolidation happens, store closures follow. KIM properties anchored by Albertsons or Safeway are at **material risk of closure or rent reduction** as the merged entity optimizes its footprint.

4. **The Foot Locker / Bed Bath & Beyond pattern repeating** — Remember when the bull's argument would have defended Bed Bath & Beyond as "stable"? Retail anchors that seem essential can deteriorate **far faster than landlords anticipate.** The grocery sector is not immune.

**My prediction:** Within 12 months, we'll see material anchor closures among KIM's grocery tenants. The bull is betting on historical assumptions about grocery stability that 2026 will disprove.

## **SECTION 2: EXPOSING THE MARGIN ILLUSION & PROFITABILITY MIRAGE**

### **A. 68.9% Gross Margin Is Meaningless Without Understanding Underlying Dynamics**

The bull trumpets a **68.9% gross margin** as evidence of "exceptional operational efficiency." Let me translate what this actually means:

**68.9% gross margin = 31.1% of revenue is consumed by property-level costs (real estate taxes, insurance, maintenance, property management, utilities, repairs).**

Here's the uncomfortable truth: **These costs are rising faster than rents in 2026.**

Why?
- **Property tax inflation** — States and municipalities, facing revenue pressures, are reassessing properties upward. Tax bills on commercial real estate are rising 5-7% annually.
- **Insurance premiums spiking** — Commercial property insurance is experiencing a hard market. Expect 10-15% annual increases for the next 2-3 years.
- **Maintenance capex deferral is ending** — Post-pandemic, KIM deferred significant maintenance. That bill is coming due. Roof replacements, HVAC upgrades, parking lot resurfacing—these are non-discretionary.
- **Labor costs relentless** — Property management, maintenance staff, security—all facing wage inflation of 4-6%.

**The 68.9% gross margin is a trailing number based on 2025 cost structures. Forward margins are compressing, not expanding.** The bull's confidence in this metric is misplaced.
