## **PILLAR 2: NEGATIVE EQUITY IS NOT A FAILURE—IT'S A FEATURE**

### Understanding REIT Capital Structure

Let me address the elephant in the room: **SBAC's -$4.63B negative equity.** The bear will say, *"This is alarming! The company has negative book value!"* 

**Wrong frame.** Here's why:

#### **Why Tower REITs Run Negative Equity**

1. **Intentional Capital Allocation Strategy**
   - Tower operators are designed to **maximize cash distribution**, not accumulate equity
   - Instead of retaining earnings, SBAC returns ~100% of FCF to shareholders via dividends and buybacks
   - Negative equity is the **result of successful capital allocation**, not poor operations

2. **Depreciation & Amortization**
   - SBAC carries $2.87B in goodwill + $7.48B in net PPE on its balance sheet
   - These assets **generate cash** but are written down for accounting purposes
   - The towers themselves are performing; the accounting is conservative
   - Actual tower asset values are higher than book value after accounting depreciation

3. **Historical M&A Funded by Debt**
   - SBAC has grown through strategic acquisitions financed with debt
   - This is **standard practice** for tower operators (Crown Castle, American Tower also run negative equity)
   - The acquisitions created value; the financing structure created negative equity

4. **Dividend Policy**
   - SBAC pays dividends from cash flow, which reduces retained earnings
   - Paying $250M+ annually in dividends while carrying $1B+ FCF is **shareholder-friendly**, not alarming
   - This is exactly what a mature REIT **should** do
