The bear will point to:

- Declining multifamily sales volume (-16% YoY)

#### **Rising Cap Rates = Opportunity, Not Threat**

When cap rates rise in real estate, two things happen:
1. **Pricing normalizes** – Overvalued assets get marked down
2. **Spreads widen** – The gap between cap rate yields and bond yields becomes attractive for institutional capital

CPT, with its **$9.06B in total assets** and fortress balance sheet, is one of the largest operators in multifamily. When capital becomes scarce and deals are fewer, **scale and operational excellence compound**. Smaller competitors will face liquidity pressure; CPT will consolidate.

#### **Declining Sales Volume = Less Competitive Pressure**

A 16% YoY decline in multifamily transaction volume is often interpreted as a negative. But consider the alternative: during frothy markets (2021-2023), every seller was offloading properties, creating competition. Now, with deal flow contracting, CPT faces **less competitive pressure** from desperate sellers trying to exit.

This is a **buyer's market for a large, well-capitalized operator**.

#### **Affordability Crunch = Durable Rent Demand**

The prediction markets show US median home values will likely stay below $419K through September 2026. Translation: **housing affordability is strained**, and renting remains the only option for millions of Americans.

This is a **secular tailwind** for multifamily REITs:
- **Household formation** remains robust despite affordability challenges
- **Rental demand is sticky** because home purchase options are unavailable to the next 5-10 million households
- **Rent growth may moderate**, but **occupancy rates remain elevated**

CPT's Q2 2026 revenue of $388.8M (in line with prior quarters) reflects **steady occupancy and stable operations**. This is not a market collapsing; it's a market normalizing.
