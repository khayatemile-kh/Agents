### **PILLAR 2: Quantum Computing is a Distraction, Not a Revenue Driver**

The bull is excited about the Diraq quantum deployment in Sydney by October 2026.

Let me ask a simple question: **What percentage of EQIX's $9.9B in annual revenue do you think will come from quantum computing in 2027? In 2030?**

The bull throws out "5-10% of revenue at 60%+ margins" as a 5-7 year possibility. Let's reality-check this:

**Quantum Computing Market Reality:**
- Global quantum computing market today: ~$500M-$1B annually (including software, hardware, services)
- Hybrid classical-quantum workloads currently operational: **Measured in dozens, not thousands**
- Commercial quantum computers deployed: **Fewer than 100 globally**
- Revenue-generating quantum services: **Nearly nonexistent at scale**

The bull is essentially saying: *"By 2032, quantum will be 5-10% of EQIX's revenue."*

**This is fantasy.** Quantum computing is still in the research phase. It's not a revenue driver in any meaningful sense. Yes, EQIX will get *some* premium pricing for quantum-grade power and cooling. But we're talking $10-50M annually, not $500M-$1B.

**The bull is pricing in optionality that has <10% probability of materialization.** That's not investment analysis; that's speculation.

### **PILLAR 3: FERC Engagement is Not a "Regulatory Win"—It's an Admission of Constraint**

Here's where the bull's narrative really breaks down.

The bull frames EQIX's FERC filings as *strategic positioning* to secure power supply advantages. But read between the lines: **EQIX is filing regulatory comments because it's terrified of power constraints.**

Why would they file these comments if power wasn't becoming a serious bottleneck? Answer: They wouldn't.

**What the FERC filing actually reveals:**
1. **Power grids are constrained**, especially in key US data center markets (Northern Virginia, Dallas, Arizona)
2. **Large data center loads are creating grid reliability concerns**
3. **FERC is considering rules that could** either favor or **discriminate against** data center operators
4. **EQIX is lobbying to protect itself** against unfavorable outcomes

This is not a win. **This is evidence of structural risk.**

Here's the critical implication the bull missed:

**If power becomes the primary constraint, EQIX's growth is fundamentally limited.** It doesn't matter if demand exists if you can't secure power to serve it.

And here's the kicker: **Competitors know this too.** Other data center operators are doing the same regulatory engagement. The outcome will likely be a leveling of the playing field—not preferential treatment for EQIX.

**What the bull presents as a moat is actually industry-wide competitive mitigation.**
