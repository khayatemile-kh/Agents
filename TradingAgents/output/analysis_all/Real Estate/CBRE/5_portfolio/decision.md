These are present-tense deteriorations, not speculative risks.

The Conservative and Neutral analysts offer a legitimate alternative explanation — that SG&A expansion and the EBITDA normalization gap reflect temporary acquisition-integration costs typical of a consolidator, and that the market may re-rate CBRE upward once integration completes. This is a plausible bull case, but it is exactly that: a forward-looking bet that requires margin recovery and accretive M&A execution that has not yet been demonstrated in the data. The Neutral analyst's key insight — that the central unresolved question is whether CBRE's fee-based, capital-light model can absorb a refinancing shock in a rate-sticky environment — was never adequately answered by the bulls. The Conservative analyst acknowledged the refinancing risk on the $2.54B current tranche but then minimized it as a reason for "conservative risk management" rather than pricing it into position sizing, which the Neutral analyst correctly flagged as hand-waving.

Technically, the setup corroborates rather than resolves the fundamental ambiguity: a bearish MACD crossover (-1.05 histogram), a break below both the 10 EMA and 200 SMA, and RSI declining from 62.49 to 44.83 all point to momentum exhaustion following the August rally. The Conservative/Neutral counter — that price is holding the 50 SMA (143.63) and this is "normal consolidation, not breakdown" — is a fair technical nuance, but it does not offset the fundamental deterioration signals, and it argues for a measured trim rather than either full conviction (add/hold) or panic (full sell).

Given (1) confirmed present-tense margin/leverage deterioration that the bulls could not refute with data, (2) a valuation (33x trailing) that offers no cushion against that deterioration, (3) a material and unhedged refinancing risk landing squarely in a hawkish rate environment, but (4) genuine offsetting assets — real revenue growth, a legitimate data center tailwind, and interest coverage that is stressed but not yet in covenant-breach territory — the appropriate stance is Underweight: reduce exposure meaningfully now, but stop short of a full Sell given the absence of confirmed distress (no impairment, no covenant breach, no confirmed refinancing failure).
