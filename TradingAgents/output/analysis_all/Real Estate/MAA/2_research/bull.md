### **REITs Are Supposed to Look "Levered" on Traditional Balance Sheet Metrics**

Here's why:

1. **Negative Working Capital is Normal:** REITs hold *illiquid real estate assets* (worth $11.6 billion) funded by long-term debt (mostly fixed-rate mortgages on individual properties). A negative working capital ratio doesn't mean financial distress—it means the company is efficiently using leverage to fund income-producing assets.

   **Analogy:** If you own a $10M apartment building funded by an $8M mortgage, your accounting balance sheet looks "levered," but you're generating $600K+ annually in NOI. This is financially healthy, not distressed.

2. **Negative Retained Earnings in REITs Are Immaterial:** REITs are *required by law* to distribute 90% of taxable income to shareholders. This means earnings are *intentionally* not retained; they're paid out as dividends. A negative retained earnings balance simply reflects this tax-advantaged structure. **It is not a sign of financial weakness.**
