A debt-to-FCF of 4.1x against a company whose organic growth is decelerating is not "manageable" in the reassuring way the bull frames it — it's a lever that amplifies downside risk precisely when the stock's technical setup (RSI 81, parabolic 4-day move) already screams overextension.

On valuation-versus-peers, the bull's comparison table asserts CRM is cheap relative to NOW, ADBE, WDAY, DDOG, but doesn't reconcile that those peers' higher multiples come with higher, more consistent growth — the bear was mid-way through dismantling this when the debate cut off, but the growth deceleration data already undercuts the PEG-based cheapness argument, since PEG here is being fed an optimistic forward growth number rather than the messier actual trend.

The AI monetization story is the most legitimate long-term bull argument — Einstein adoption economics are plausible and structural — but it's a multi-year, execution-dependent thesis, not something that justifies chasing a stock up nearly 40% in four days at RSI 81 with a materially weakened balance sheet. Optionality on AI doesn't offset the near-term risk that the market has already priced in a beat-and-raise scenario while the debt/equity picture has deteriorated sharply and growth has been inconsistent.

Net-net: the bear's balance-sheet deterioration and growth-deceleration evidence are concrete and verifiable from the data both sides cite, while the bull's valuation-discount and margin-expansion claims rest on assumptions not yet supported by the numbers. This isn't a full Sell/exit call because the AI optionality and FCF generation ($10B TTM) are real and keep this from being a broken business, but the combination of stretched technicals, deteriorating capital structure, and unconfirmed margin expansion justifies trimming exposure and waiting for either a valuation reset or confirmation that margins/growth are actually inflecting as the bull predicts.
