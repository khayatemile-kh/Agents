The former is a stabilization signal. The latter is confirmation of uptrend strength. We're getting the former, not the latter.

So what does this actually mean for positioning? ## THE RSI ARGUMENT: WHERE THE CONSERVATIVE ANALYST ACTUALLY NAILS IT

Here's where I'm going to disagree with the aggressive analyst and side more closely with the conservative view, but with important nuance.

The aggressive analyst argues that RSI at 43.83 "isn't weak—it's opportunity." The conservative analyst counters that RSI not reaching oversold after a 13% decline means "selling isn't done." The conservative analyst is more correct here, but let me be precise about why.

RSI measures momentum relative to recent price movement. An RSI of 43.83 after a sharp decline from peak RSI of 54.18 down to a low of 38.43 tells us something important: **the stock has bounced off its lows and is back in neutral territory.** This is neither oversold nor overbought. 

But here's what matters: RSI at 43.83 after a 13% pullback without a corresponding move into oversold territory (below 30) is actually a warning sign about the *character* of that decline. When a stock falls 13% sharply but RSI doesn't reach 30, it usually means one of two things:

1. The decline is rolling lower in waves rather than in one capitulative flush. This is bearish because it suggests multiple layers of support being tested and broken.
2. Volume is declining on the way down, which means there's less conviction behind the selling but also less capitulation to signal a bottom.

The data we have shows exactly this: volume declined from the 16.19M share August 6 capitulation event to 5.5M shares recently. So the stock is falling on lower volume, not backing up with force. This is the hallmark of a stock that hasn't found a true bottom yet.

**The aggressive analyst's assertion that "when RSI hits oversold combined with MACD cross, the bounce will be violent" is not supported here.** We may get an RSI reading below 30 and not get a violent bounce—we might just get another test of support at lower levels. The mechanical bounce assumption is exactly the kind of indicator-chasing that blows up portfolios.

So my take: The conservative analyst is right to be cautious about the RSI setup, but the takeaway is not "expect further downside for certain." The takeaway is "don't assign high confidence to a sharp reversal until we actually see capitulation indicators line up properly—oversold RSI *combined with* heavy volume and a confirmed MACD cross."
