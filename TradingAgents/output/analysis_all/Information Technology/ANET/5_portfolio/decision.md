**Investment Thesis**: The bull case is built on realized, not projected, fundamentals: QoQ revenue growth accelerated from 4.7% to 12.1% over four quarters, margins are best-in-class and expanding (62.9% gross, 45.4% operating, 38.4% net TTM), and deferred revenue of $6.86B (+82.8% YoY) represents paid-in-advance customer commitment equal to roughly 2.25 quarters of revenue already secured — as the Aggressive analyst noted, this is "cash locked in a vault," not speculative pipeline. The Neutral analyst's correction that the forward multiple is 36.7x (not the headline 59.8x trailing) against 65.9% forward EPS growth is the single most important valuation fact in this debate: it shows the market has already partially repriced the stock down from its $210.50 peak to $189.26, and the remaining multiple is reasonable relative to peers with slower growth, not a bubble multiple.

The Conservative and Neutral analysts raise risks that are real but probabilistic rather than evidenced: customer concentration in Microsoft/Meta (plausibly 70-85% of revenue), the risk that hyperscalers accelerate in-house networking (Meta's custom silicon spend, Microsoft's networking R&D), and supply-chain execution risk where "consensus assumes parts arrive on time." The Neutral analyst's scenario framework (60% probability robust continuation, 25% moderation, 15% supply-chain break) supports leaning bullish rather than defensive, since the base case remains favorable and the downside scenarios require specific negative catalysts (missed delivery quarters, explicit hyperscaler capex cuts) that have not yet appeared in the data. The insider-selling signal cited by the Conservative analyst (six insiders selling in August) is a soft, ambiguous data point rather than a fundamental impairment, and cuts both ways per the debate.

Technically, the $185 entry sits within a normal consolidation (RSI 50, price below 10-day EMA, bearish MACD crossover) rather than a breakdown, with support at $183.75 and the 50-day SMA at $180.88 providing a defined risk boundary. This justifies staged entry rather than a single lump commitment, sizing the position as a high-growth, single-customer-cluster-dependent hardware name rather than a maximum-conviction bet. Prior lesson from NVDA (2024-05-10) reinforces this approach: prospective risks flagged as monitoring triggers (competitive substitution, margin compression) should not be treated as current impairments until confirmed over an earnings cycle — the same discipline applies here to the Microsoft/Meta in-sourcing and supply-chain risks, which remain hypothetical rather than realized.
