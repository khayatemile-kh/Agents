## PART 3: ADDRESSING THE INSIDER SELLING CONCERN

Now, let's talk about the elephant in the room: **Director Ondrej Vlcek sold ~452,000 shares in August for ~$10.4 million.**

The social media report flags this as bearish, with StockTwits noting "insider selling at rising prices = red flag." I have a different perspective:

### Why Insider Selling Isn't the Smoking Gun Bears Think It Is

**1. Context Matters: Vlcek's Holdings & Motivation**

Without knowing Vlcek's full position size, absolute holdings, or vesting schedule, we're drawing conclusions from incomplete data. Insider selling can reflect:
- **Diversification needs** (especially for directors with concentrated positions)
- **Vesting schedule exercises** (forced liquidity events)
- **Planned capital deployment** (downpayment on property, etc.)
- **Tax planning** (year-end repositioning)

Insider selling does NOT necessarily signal loss of confidence in the business.

**2. The Real Signal: Insider *Buying* Would Be Far More Meaningful**

If Vlcek were *buying* $10.4M in stock at $28-30, that would be a massive bullish signal. The absence of aggressive insider buying doesn't mean selling is bearish—it just means we're not seeing conviction demonstrated via new investment.

**3. Compare to CEO/Board Buying Activity**

The critical question: Are *other* executives and board members buying or holding? If the broader leadership team is holding steady while one director trims position, that's far less concerning than if multiple insiders are heading for the exits.
