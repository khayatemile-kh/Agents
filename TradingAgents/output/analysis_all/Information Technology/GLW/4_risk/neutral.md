## Where the Aggressive Analyst Has a Real Point (But Overstates It)

But they're cherry-picking the historical cases that support this thesis. The data actually shows that volatility compression precedes breakouts in *both directions* with relatively equal frequency. By asserting that "the path of least resistance is typically back through the breakdown point," they're introducing a directional bias that isn't mathematically supported by their own framework.

Where they really lose me is on the forward earnings cliff argument. They cite "Q1 2026 earnings lumpiness with a projected 31% sequential EPS decline." But here's what they're doing: they're conflating a sequential decline with a trend reversal. Q1 to Q2 went from $0.43 to $0.64. That's a +49% sequential increase. Yes, that's volatile. But it's the volatility you'd expect from a manufacturing company with lumpy contract wins and seasonal patterns—not evidence of an earnings cliff. The aggressive analyst is using that lumpiness as justification for the forward PE of 33x being "unproven," but the actual quarterly data shows we're already tracking toward earnings growth. The PEG of 0.87x isn't some mathematical illusion; it's reflecting the fact that earnings are accelerating.

**So yes, trim position size as a risk management discipline. But not because the technicals are "toxic"—they're just neutral-to-slightly-negative after a panic. Trim because volatility creates opportunity, and that's a perfectly legitimate tactical decision without needing to convince yourself that downside is "most likely."**
