## PART II: THE MACRO HEADWINDS ARE STRUCTURAL, NOT TEMPORARY

Bulls dismiss the 89% probability of no Fed rate cuts in 2026 by saying "CDNS has pricing power and FCF yield is defensive."

**That's backwards logic.** Here's the actual mechanism:

1. **High Real Rates Compress Growth Stock Multiples:** When the risk-free rate is 5-6%, investors demand *higher* returns to justify growth stock risk. That typically means *lower* multiples, not higher.

2. **The 1.9% FCF Yield Isn't Defensive—It's Dangerous:** The bull is comparing CDNS' 1.9% FCF yield favorably to S&P 500's 2.5% average. But **that comparison is apples-to-watermelons**:
   - S&P 500 companies have *lower* growth expectations and *higher* dividend yields
   - CDNS has *high* growth expectations already baked into its valuation
   - If CDNS misses growth expectations, the yield becomes irrelevant—the stock crashes

**Scenario Testing: What Happens If Growth Misses?**

| Scenario | Stock Price Impact |
|----------|-------------------|
| Beats guidance, 18%+ growth | $350-380 (modest upside) |
| Meets guidance, 12-15% growth | $313-320 (flat) |
| Misses guidance, <10% growth | $250-280 (17-20% downside) |

**The risk/reward is inverted.** There's limited upside if CDNS executes, but *massive* downside if it misses. That's exactly the profile you want to *avoid* in a high-rate environment.
