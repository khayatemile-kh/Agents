## PART 2: THE "30% UNDERVALUATION" THESIS IS BUILT ON BROKEN ASSUMPTIONS

Now let me address the valuation argument directly. The bull's case hinges on DCF models that assume:

- 5-7% annual FCF growth
- Sustainable 3.6% FCF margins
- 6-8% WACC
- 3-4% terminal growth

**All of these assumptions are deteriorating in real-time. Here's why:**

### Assumption #1: "5-7% FCF Growth" — Contradicted by Recent Operational Data

The bull points to:
- TTM FCF: $1.21B
- Q3 FY26 OCF: $535M

And annualizes this to "$1.4B+ FCF."

**But let's look at what's actually happening in the balance sheet:**

From the fundamental report (May 31, 2026 quarter):

- **Accounts Payable surged 56.1% in ONE QUARTER** from $7.62B to $11.91B
- **Accounts Receivable jumped 22% YoY** from $4.48B to $5.47B
- **Inventory climbed 24% YoY** from $4.78B to $5.93B

**This is not normal operational scaling. This is inventory and receivables deterioration.**

Here's what this pattern tells us:

1. **The 56% payables surge suggests JBL is having trouble paying its suppliers on time.** This isn't "positive working capital arbitrage"—this is cash strain being masked by extended payment terms to suppliers. Suppliers don't extend terms out of goodwill; they do it when a company is desperate.

2. **The 22% receivables jump combined with the 24% inventory jump suggests:**
   - Either customers are delaying payment (red flag on end-market demand)
   - Or JBL is carrying excess inventory that's not converting to cash quickly

3. **If JBL is extending payables while receivables stretch, the actual cash conversion cycle is deteriorating.** The FCF narrative breaks down.

### Let's Do the Math on Cash Flow Quality:

**Q3 FY26 (May 2026):**
- Operating Cash Flow: $535M
- CapEx: $184M
- Free Cash Flow: $351M
- Financing Cash Flow: **-$821M** (massive cash outflow)

That -$821M financing outflow is critical. What's that financing outflow?

- Debt repayment? (Good sign)
- Dividend payments? (But the bull says dividend yield is only 0.11%—minimal)
- Share buybacks? (The research notes treasury stock of $8.85B—JBL is buying back shares)

**Here's the red flag:** If JBL is generating only $351M in FCF per quarter but is shelling out $821M in financing activities, the company is burning cash. The TTM FCF of $1.21B is likely trailing and not representative of the *current* cash generation rate.

### Assumption #2: "These Are Sustainable Margins" — Belied by Gross Margin Pressure

The bull emphasizes:
- Gross Margin: 9.2% (TTM)
- Q3 FY26: 9.5% (+10 bps YoY)

**But let's look at the trend more carefully:**

The gross margin expanded by only *10 basis points* despite 11.8% revenue growth. In a high-growth scenario with pricing power, gross margins should expand more aggressively. The fact that JBL is barely holding margin while scaling revenue suggests:

1. **Intense price competition** from other contract manufacturers (Flex, Sanmina, etc.)
2. **Rising input costs** that JBL can't pass through to customers
3. **Mix shift** toward lower-margin products (basic electronics vs. value-add services)

For a company trading at 37.5x trailing P/E, this margin profile is deeply concerning.
