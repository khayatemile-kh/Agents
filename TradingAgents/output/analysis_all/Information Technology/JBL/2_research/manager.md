**Rationale**: Both sides make points worth weighing, but the bear's evidence is more concrete and harder to dismiss than the bull's optimistic extrapolations.

Cash strain being masked by stretching supplier terms is a more parsimonious explanation than "supplier confidence." The bear's point that a mere 10bps gross margin expansion despite double-digit revenue growth signals pricing pressure/margin compression at a 37.5x trailing P/E is also a legitimate concern - that multiple leaves very little room for error if margins are structurally capped.

If the AI/data-center thesis were compelling to institutions at these levels, we'd expect volume-backed buying, not chronic rejection at declining moving averages. The bull's response to this was essentially deferred/incomplete going into the leverage discussion.

On leverage/cash flow: the bull's attempt to spin negative working capital as "Amazon/Walmart-like strength" doesn't fully hold up for a contract manufacturer with thinner margins and no where near the same negotiating leverage or brand moat; the -$821M financing outflow in a single quarter against only $351M of quarterly FCF is a real red flag on cash burn that the bull hasn't addressed with anything beyond asserting the debt ratios "look fine" on a trailing basis.

Net: the bear's case is grounded in the same underlying fundamental disclosures (payables/receivables/inventory swings, financing outflows, margin trend) and confirmed by the price action (failed breakouts, volume collapse), while the bull's case rests on optimistic multi-year secular narratives and a DCF whose inputs the bear shows are already cracking.
