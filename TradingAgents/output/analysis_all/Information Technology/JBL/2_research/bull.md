## PART 3: FINANCIAL STRUCTURE — LEVERAGE IS MANAGEABLE, NOT CATASTROPHIC

Okay, I know what the bear will say: *"Debt-to-equity of 297x! Negative working capital! This thing is a house of cards!"*

**That's a fundamental misreading of the business model.** Let me explain:

### Why High Leverage is "Normal" (But Requires Understanding)

JBL's business model is fundamentally different from, say, a capital-intensive manufacturer or a software company:

1. **Asset-Light Operation:** CapEx is only 2.1% of revenue. JBL doesn't need to own massive factory complexes; it operates on behalf of OEMs. This means *lower absolute asset base* relative to revenue.

2. **Working Capital Dynamics:** Here's the critical nuance the bear will miss—**JBL's working capital is structured to its advantage:**

   - **Accounts Receivable:** $5.47B (30-45 day terms from hyperscalers—very safe)
   - **Inventory:** $5.93B (managed JIT, part of customer's risk)
   - **Accounts Payable:** $11.91B (60-90 day terms from suppliers)
   
   **This is a *positive* working capital arbitrage:** JBL collects from customers in 30-40 days but pays suppliers in 60-90 days. The $11.91B payables balance isn't weakness—it's evidence of *supplier confidence* and *customer payment strength.*

3. **Negative Working Capital is a Feature, Not a Bug:**
   
   Check this: Companies like Amazon, Walmart, and Dell have *negative* working capital. Why? Because they're so large and creditworthy that they can negotiate extended payment terms while collecting inventory quickly. JBL is in this category. Negative working capital actually *generates* cash.

4. **Net Debt is Manageable:**
   
   - **Total Debt:** $3.38B (LT + current)
   - **Cash:** $1.36B
   - **Net Debt:** $2.02B
   - **Net Debt / EBITDA:** 0.84x (very healthy! <1x is conservative)
   - **Net Debt / Operating Cash Flow:** 1.67x (manageable, under 2x is reasonable)
