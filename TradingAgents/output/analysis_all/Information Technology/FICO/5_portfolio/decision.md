**Investment Thesis**: The debate converged on facts that cut against aggressive accumulation here. The bull (Aggressive) correctly notes elite, durable economics — 85.1% gross margin, 53.8% operating margin, Q2 net income of $237.2M up 30.4% YoY, EBITDA of $378.8M covering debt comfortably at a stated 4.2x Net Debt/EBITDA — and a PEG of 0.77x that looks cheap if 54% forward EPS growth holds. But the Conservative analyst's rebuttal on the sustainability of that growth assumption is the more decisive point, and the Neutral analyst's arbitration confirms it: revenue growth decelerated meaningfully from Q1's 35% YoY pace, and the Q1-to-Q2 sequential net income decline (-10.3%, $264.5M to $237.2M) is abnormal versus the prior year's near-flat sequential pattern ($188.5M to $187.5M in 2025). That is concrete evidence the deceleration is real, not merely optics, and it directly undercuts the PEG math the bull's cheapness argument depends on — as the Neutral analyst states, a 0.77 PEG requires ~25%+ sustained earnings growth, and if that growth normalizes toward 20-25% (the Neutral analyst's stated base case), the "undervalued" framing weakens materially without the stock being obviously broken.

On the balance sheet, the Conservative analyst's core objection — that -$4.10B stockholders' equity reflects cumulative capital returned exceeding cumulative earnings, funded by $5.6B in debt and $2.27B of Q2 buybacks alone — was not cleanly refuted. The Neutral analyst's arbitration sided partially with caution here too: buybacks accelerating precisely as earnings deceleration first appears is a legitimate capital-allocation-timing concern, even though earnings are still growing ~30% YoY (a point the Neutral analyst credits to the bull as evidence this isn't yet "peak-cycle" behavior). Net: leverage is not imminently dangerous given investment-grade profile and strong EBITDA coverage, but it removes any margin of safety if the deceleration continues, and it makes the current 20.7x forward P/E less clearly "cheap" than the bull frames it.

On technicals, the Neutral analyst's tie-breaking read is the most useful: RSI at 45.47 with no capitulation flush, combined with the failed recovery attempt above $1,130-$1,150 on August 27, is consistent with an orderly-but-unresolved downtrend rather than a confirmed bottom — this argues against the bull's case for aggressive tactical buying on weakness, while also not validating the bear's "further leg lower is guaranteed" framing. The prudent read is that price action is genuinely undecided pending the Q3 print, which all three analysts agree is the actual resolving catalyst (bull needs 25-30%+ revenue growth with margin maintenance; bear thesis strengthens on sub-20% growth or margin commentary; Neutral's base case of 20-25% growth with modest margin pressure implies a modest bounce, not a breakout or breakdown).

Given this, retaining full exposure or adding is not justified by the evidence — the deceleration signal and buyback-timing concern are real, not resolved, and not adequately priced given the PEG-dependent bull case. But outright selling ignores that the franchise quality (moat, margins, EBITDA coverage) remains genuinely intact and unrefuted, and that the stock has already absorbed a 42% de-rating without technical capitulation. This supports trimming into strength while retaining a core position sized for the underlying moat — Underweight, not Hold and not Sell.
