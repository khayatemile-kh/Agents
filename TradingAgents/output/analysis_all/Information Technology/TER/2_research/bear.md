## THE BEAR THESIS: FOUR STRUCTURAL WARNINGS

### **PROBLEM #1: CYCLICALITY DISGUISED AS SECULAR GROWTH—WE'VE SEEN THIS MOVIE BEFORE**

Let me directly challenge the bull's central premise: that Teradyne is riding a "5-10 year" secular tailwind in AI infrastructure.

**The Hard Truth About Semiconductor Equipment Cycles:**

Teradyne operates in one of the *most cyclical* industries in the market. The semiconductor industry goes through boom-bust cycles approximately every 4-5 years. We've seen this repeatedly:

- **2000-2001:** Dot-com crash → semiconductor capex collapsed → equipment companies crashed 70-90%
- **2008-2009:** Financial crisis → semiconductor fab construction halted → Teradyne crashed >60%
- **2015-2016:** Memory oversupply → capex pullback → equipment sector corrected sharply
- **2022-2023:** Post-COVID inventory normalization → orders declined, margins compressed

The pattern is consistent: boom cycles peak, inventory builds, capex stops, prices crater. **And we're showing warning signs today.**

**Why the AI Buildout Will Inevitably Overshoot:**

1. **Capacity Addition Accelerates Exponentially Until It Overshoots**
   - Every hyperscaler is racing to build datacenters simultaneously
   - This creates a "prisoner's dilemma"—everyone must invest or lose market share
   - The result? **Massive capex spending compressed into 2-3 years, followed by plateau/decline**
   - Intel, TSMC, Samsung, and new entrants (Intel's foundry, Micron's fabs) are all expanding simultaneously

2. **Demand Assumptions Are Already Priced In—Or Will Be**
   - The AI narrative is no secret. Every investor and CFO knows this story
   - Capex spending surge is *already happening*—it's not a future surprise
   - When reality matches expectations, the stock typically sells off
   - When capex inevitably slows (whether in 2 years or 3), the disappointment will be severe

3. **Historical Precedent: AI Chip Demand Will Create Supply Glut**
   - Look at memory chips: after the 2020-2021 capex surge, DRAM and NAND markets are in *oversupply*
   - Prices have collapsed, and fab utilization is declining
   - The same will happen with AI chips—except with even more invested capital

**The Bull Assumption Is Flawed:**

The bull argues "this is 5-10 years of accelerating demand." But the data doesn't support that:
- AI chip capex is *concentrating* into 2024-2028 window
- After that, growth will *moderate* as capacity matches demand
- Teradyne's revenue will compress when capex normalizes

We're likely in **Year 2-3 of a 5-year capex super-cycle, meaning 60-70% of the upside is already priced in, and 100% of the downside awaits.**

### **PROBLEM #2: VALUATION IS OBJECTIVELY STRETCHED—AND THE BULL'S MATH DOESN'T HOLD UP**

Let me dissect the bull's valuation argument, because this is where the bear thesis gets surgical.

**The Bull Claims a "35x Forward P/E" Is Reasonable**

Here's their logic:
- Q2 2026 annualized EPS: ~$9.50
- Current stock price: $335.46
- "Forward P/E": 35.3x

**This is a dangerous oversimplification. Let me explain why:**

**Error #1: Extrapolating One Quarter Into Perpetuity**

The bull took Q2 2026's exceptional performance ($2.38 EPS) and annualized it to $9.50. But this assumes:
- Q3, Q4, Q1, Q2 will all be identical to Q2
- No quarterly seasonality
- No margin compression
- No demand softening

This is fantasy.

**Real-World Context:**
- TTM EPS: $7.41 (more representative average)
- Q1 2026 EPS: $2.53
- Q2 2026 EPS: $2.38
- **Q2 actually declined 5.9% sequentially from Q1**

If we assume modest normalization, a more realistic forward EPS estimate is **$8.50-9.00**, not $9.50. That puts the forward P/E at **37-39x, not 35x.**

**Error #2: Comparing Forward P/E to Historical Averages**

The bull argues "35x forward P/E is appropriate for a 100% growth company."

Let me provide context:
- Typical semiconductor equipment companies trade at 15-25x earnings during growth phases
- High-growth semiconductors (e.g., NVIDIA during early AI acceleration) traded at 60-80x but had *accelerating* margins and *indefinite* runway
- Teradyne's margins are already *declining* (31.1% Q1 → 28.2% Q2)
- Growth will moderate as capex normalizes

**A 37-39x P/E for a cyclical company with **declining** operating margins is not "appropriate"—it's a **valuation trap.**
