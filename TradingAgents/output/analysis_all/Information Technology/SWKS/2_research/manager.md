**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull case rests on two pillars: (1) the Qorvo acquisition being a low-risk, synergistic transformation, and (2) the current earnings weakness being a cyclical trough that will mechanically resolve into ~$4.97 forward EPS. Both pillars have real cracks that the bear exposed convincingly.

On the acquisition, the bull's 'complementary, not overlapping' framing is optimistic but the bear correctly points out that large RF/analog M&A historically comes with 18-36 months of integration drag - overlapping sales forces, dual fab/foundry relationships to reconcile, and SKU rationalization that creates openings for competitors (Broadcom, TI, Infineon) to poach share while SWKS is distracted. The bear's counter-examples (Intel/Altera, Qualcomm/NXP) are apt illustrations that even well-resourced acquirers struggle to execute semiconductor integrations cleanly, and the timing of a large deal coming right as SWKS's own margins and ROE have collapsed (operating margin ~8.8%, ROE ~5%) reads more like a defensive consolidation play than a confident bolt-on.

On fundamentals, the bull's 'trough stabilizing' story requires accepting that Q3 2025's revenue was an anomalous spike and that flat Q1-to-Q2 2026 revenue is a sign of bottoming. The bear's rebuttal - that Q1 and Q2 2026 revenue is actually down 2-3% year-over-year versus Q2 2025, not just down from an unusual peak - is the more rigorous read of the same data and undercuts the stabilization narrative. A business that's shrinking YoY even at a supposed 'trough' is more consistent with structural share/pricing pressure than with normal cyclicality.

The forward EPS bridge the bull builds ($520-600M SWKS run-rate + ~$300M QRVO net income ≈ $4.97 EPS) is a clean arithmetic exercise, but it embeds several optimistic assumptions stacked on top of each other: that SWKS's core business snaps back to Q3/Q4 2025 profitability levels despite YoY revenue erosion, that QRVO's standalone earnings power fully carries over without dilution, and that integration costs and synergy realization are a wash in year one. Given the bear's evidence of real revenue and margin deterioration, this bridge should be treated as a best-case scenario rather than a base case.

Where the bull is on firmer ground is gross margin resilience (~40%, up ~120bps YoY) and continued R&D investment - these are genuine positives that argue against an outright collapse thesis and against a full Sell. But they don't offset the combination of (a) elevated deal/integration risk arriving at a moment of internal weakness, (b) YoY revenue declines that look structural rather than purely cyclical, and (c) a forward EPS estimate that requires multiple optimistic assumptions to compound simultaneously. That combination tilts the risk/reward to the downside from here, but the margin resilience and dividend support keep this short of a full exit call.

**Strategic Actions**: Trim SWKS exposure to an underweight position relative to benchmark/sector - reduce current holdings by roughly 25-35% rather than exiting entirely, since gross margin stability and the dividend yield (~4%) provide some downside cushion.

Do not add to the position ahead of confirmed post-close integration data on Qorvo; specifically wait for at least two quarters of combined-entity revenue and margin reporting before reassessing.

Set a re-entry/upgrade trigger: (1) two consecutive quarters of YoY revenue growth (not just sequential flatness) for the combined entity, and (2) evidence that QRVO's operating margin is being preserved (not diluted) post-close - either of these would materially strengthen the bull case.

Set a further downside trigger to move to outright Sell: signs of customer/design-win attrition during integration (e.g., disclosed share losses to Broadcom/TI/Infineon) or operating margin compression below current ~8.8% levels.

Hedge remaining exposure where feasible (e.g., pairing against a semiconductor sector ETF or a stronger RF/analog peer) to reduce idiosyncratic integration risk while the deal closes and integrates.

Avoid using forward EPS of $4.97 as a valuation anchor for position sizing; underwrite any residual position using SWKS standalone trough-normalized earnings only, treating QRVO contribution as optionality rather than a certainty.
