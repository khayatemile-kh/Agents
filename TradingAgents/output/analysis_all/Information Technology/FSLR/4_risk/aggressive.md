Yes, First Solar manufactures domestically. Yes, tariffs should theoretically benefit FSLR by raising import costs for competitors. But here's what everyone seems to be glossing over: **the market already rejected this thesis once**. 

Look at what happened when the Trump executive order on bulk-power system equipment was announced. FSLR rallied overnight alongside ENPH and ARRY. But then what? The stock "drifted down" while SolarEdge surged 8% on the inverter curbs. This isn't neutral price action—this is the market telling us that FSLR's tariff beneficiary narrative has real structural problems.

Why? Because the inverter curbs actually matter more to residential installers and system integrators than module tariffs matter to module manufacturers. The market is saying: *we're not convinced FSLR can pass these costs to customers*. And frankly, they might be right. Utility developers have long-term PPAs locked in. Module costs are just one component of project economics. If FSLR tries to expand margins on the back of tariffs, it risks losing volume to offshore suppliers who can absorb the tariff cost and undercut on price.

The research plan explicitly warns against "contrarian bottom-fishing," and I agree. The fact that Canadian Solar (CSIQ) is collapsing with 28.7% revenue declines and wider losses tells us the solar sector has real near-term headwinds. We don't know yet how tariff pass-through will actually play out in practice. That's not caution—that's intellectual honesty.

The Hold position protects us from this margin compression uncertainty. We don't re-enter until we see *sustained* margin proof, not just one quarter of outsized results.
