That said, the bear doesn't dispute the fundamental engine — this isn't a broken business, it's a high-multiple compounder going through a valuation/technical correction amid a broader semiconductor sector pullback (shared by Lam Research, TI, FormFactor per the bull's own admission), which suggests this is at least partly sector rotation rather than an MPWR-specific red flag. So I don't think this rises to a Sell/exit-the-business call — the balance sheet and margin profile are real and defensible, and there's no evidence of fundamental impairment (bookings, backlog, or customer losses aren't cited by the bear).

Netting it out: the weight of evidence favors caution over conviction on either extreme. The bear wins the near-term technical and valuation argument decisively (corrected PEG math, stacked bearish moving averages, negative MACD with no reversal signal), while the bull's fundamental/secular thesis remains intact for the medium term but is already substantially priced in at 75x earnings. This is not a "balanced coin-flip" Hold — the immediate risk/reward is skewed to the downside given technical deterioration and valuation vulnerability to any growth deceleration, so trimming exposure into strength/before further breakdown is the prudent stance rather than adding aggressively as the bull suggests. I'm landing on Underweight: respect the long-term quality of the franchise (don't advocate a full exit) but reduce position size and require confirmation of technical stabilization (MACD turning, price reclaiming 50-day/200-day) and evidence that growth is holding above ~15% before adding back.
