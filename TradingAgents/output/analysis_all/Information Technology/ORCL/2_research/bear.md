A software company with recurring revenue shouldn't see 11.6% sequential growth regularly—that's noise. What matters is YoY growth, which is decelerating if we trend it against earlier 2026 guidance expectations.

## PART 2: THE DEBT LOAD IS NOT "STRATEGIC"—IT'S FINANCIALLY RECKLESS

### Let's Dissect the Bull's Debt Defense

My colleague made four key claims about the debt. Let me address each systematically:

#### CLAIM #1: "The debt correlates with PPE investment—it's rational"

**This is dangerously misleading.**

Yes, debt grew $50B and PPE grew $72B. But here's what the bull obscured:

**Oracle's CapEx is *not* generating adequate returns:**

According to the data provided, Oracle's **free cash flow is negative.** The report specifically notes concern about "negative free cash flow in recent quarters." 

Let me do the math:
- **Operating Income Q4:** $6.96B (annualized ~$23B)
- **Estimated CapEx (from PPE growth):** ~$72B / 2-3 years = $24-36B annualized

**This means Oracle is spending $24-36B on CapEx annually while generating only ~$23B in operating income before interest and taxes.** Even with tax benefits, **the company is not generating positive free cash flow to service $156B in debt.**

This is the opposite of the bull's "rational capital arbitrage" story. **This is a company taking on leverage to fund CapEx that it cannot self-finance—a textbook recipe for financial distress.**

#### CLAIM #2: "Interest coverage ratio of 6-7x is conservative"

**The bull's math is overly optimistic and ignores debt service reality.**

Let me recalculate with actual data:
- **TTM Operating Income:** ~$23B (estimated from Q4 EBITDA of $9.66B extrapolated, adjusted for D&A)
- **Estimated Interest Expense on $156B debt at 3.5-4%:** ~$5.5-6.2B
- **Interest Coverage:** 23 / 6 = **3.8x** (not 6-7x)

Moreover, **interest coverage doesn't account for principal repayment.** Oracle has $7.2B in current debt due within 12 months. With negative free cash flow, **the company will need to refinance or issue more debt to meet principal payments.**

**Real question:** Can you sustain 3.8x interest coverage while your free cash flow is negative? Historical default data says: **No, not for extended periods.** Companies with negative FCF and leveraged balance sheets typically face covenant violations or forced asset sales within 2-3 years.

#### CLAIM #3: "Deferred revenue offsets the debt burden"

**This is a category error.**

Deferred revenue ($9.92B current, ~$28-35B total) is revenue already collected but not yet recognized. It's *operational cash that's already in the bank*—it's a cash benefit that's already reflected in the balance sheet.

**You cannot use deferred revenue to offset debt obligations.** That's double-counting. The proper question is: **Where is the cash from deferred revenue going?**

The answer: **It's being spent on massive CapEx ($72B PPE growth) that isn't generating positive returns.**

This is the opposite of reassuring. It means Oracle is essentially using *customer prepayments* to fund loss-making infrastructure buildout, not to reduce leverage.

#### CLAIM #4: "Leverage improves naturally as profits grow"

**This assumes the company CAN grow into its debt—and the data suggests otherwise.**

Here's the brutal reality:

**For leverage to improve, one of two things must happen:**
1. **Debt stabilizes while EBITDA grows** → Leverage ratio improves
2. **Debt shrinks while EBITDA holds steady** → Leverage ratio improves
