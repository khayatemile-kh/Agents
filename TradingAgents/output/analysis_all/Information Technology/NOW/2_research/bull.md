Now, I'll address the elephant in the bull china shop: the balance sheet does look concerning at first glance. But context matters enormously.

### The Debt Spike Reflects Strategic M&A, Not Financial Distress

- Q2 debt exploded from $2.43B to $8.45B—a 248% increase
- But this corresponds *directly* to **$7.45B in acquisition spending**
- This isn't debt "spiraling out of control"; it's **intentional, strategic leverage deployed for growth**

**The Real Question**: Are the acquisitions accretive? ServiceNow's track record suggests yes:
- **TTM EBITDA: $2.90B (19.7% margin)** remains robust
- **TTM FCF: $5.15B** is healthy and covers debt service comfortably
- **Debt-to-EBITDA: ~2.9x** is elevated but not alarming for a SaaS company in acquisition mode

Compare this to distressed leverage profiles (5-6x+). ServiceNow is in the normal range for a strategic buyer.

### Current Ratio Below 1.0 Is a Red Herring for SaaS Companies

The 0.70x current ratio looks scary in isolation. But:

1. **SaaS companies carry massive deferred revenue liabilities** (future obligations already cash-collected). This inflates current liabilities artificially.
2. **Operating cash flow of $2.26B YTD covers near-term obligations** comfortably.
3. **The $2.08B commercial paper program** is a standard liquidity management tool, not a sign of distress.

For SaaS, **working capital metrics are less relevant than for capital-intensive businesses**. Focus on FCF, not the current ratio.
