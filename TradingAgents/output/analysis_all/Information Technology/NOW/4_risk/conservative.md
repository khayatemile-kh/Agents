Let me walk through what likely happened:

That's why operating margins collapsed—not because the core business is deteriorating, but because the company is absorbing acquisition-related charges.

The debt increase? **That's the financing mechanism for said acquisition.** The company took on debt to fund a strategic purchase. That's not "desperation acquisitioning." That's capital structure management.

Now, here's the critical question: **was the acquisition strategically sound?** If yes, then the margin compression is temporary, and margins will expand as integration costs unwind. The debt load is manageable if the acquired business generates sufficient EBITDA to service it.

What evidence do we have about acquisition quality? Well, the fact that management felt confident enough to deploy $7.45B of capital in Q2, coupled with the company's strong historical EBITDA profile ($2.9B TTM), suggests they're not panicking. If management thought the business was deteriorating, they wouldn't be deploying that much capital.

**The forward EPS forecast is not unrealistic. It's the base case if integration goes smoothly.**

So yes, the margin compression is real. Yes, the debt increase is material. But these are **temporary operational headwinds tied to a strategic acquisition, not signs of fundamental business deterioration.** Trimming the position because of short-term margin pressure is exactly the kind of cyclical decision-making that locks in losses before the recovery.
