Okay, let's address the Fire Ant cyber espionage campaign head-on, because the bears will absolutely hammer this point.

**But let's be precise about what this means:**

1. **This is already public and managed.** Cisco disclosed it. Customers know about it. This isn't a secret ticking time bomb—it's a disclosed vulnerability being remediated.

2. **Vulnerabilities drive upgrade cycles.** When customers learn their infrastructure is vulnerable, they *accelerate* their purchases of patched/updated systems. This is actually revenue-accretive in the near term.

3. **This plays into CSCO's strength in cybersecurity.** Cisco Secure is one of the company's fastest-growing business units. Elevated security threats = elevated demand for Cisco's security solutions.

### **Why This Isn't Existential:**

Compare this to what happened with other infrastructure vendors:
- When Microsoft had Exchange vulnerabilities, did the company collapse? No. Customers bought more security solutions.
- When Amazon AWS had outages, did it lose market share? No. Customers double-downed on redundancy.
- When Juniper had backdoors allegedly inserted by NSA, did it go bankrupt? No.

**Infrastructure providers are trusted precisely because they manage security transparently and remediate effectively.** CSCO has 30+ years of credibility on this front. A single vulnerability disclosure doesn't erase that.
