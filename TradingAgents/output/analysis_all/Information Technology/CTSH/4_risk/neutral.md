Here's the problem: **the Aggressive case confuses uncertainty with danger**.

You state that "nobody can explain why" the debt surged, then use that absence of explanation as evidence of structural problems. But that's not how fundamental analysis works. Absence of disclosure isn't the same as absence of a legitimate business reason. - A refinancing of existing obligations at better terms
- A strategic acquisition in life sciences (perfectly consistent with the AI pivot narrative)
- A cash management optimization ahead of major client payments
- A credit facility drawn for operational flexibility

The fact that we don't immediately know doesn't make it sinister. It makes it *important to monitor*. There's a crucial difference.

The inconsistency matters because it reveals the underlying problem: you're pattern-matching to a narrative ("debt explosion = distress") rather than analyzing the actual financial trajectory.
