## **THE BULL'S PILLAR 1 CRUMBLES ON CLOSER INSPECTION: "EXCEPTIONAL MOMENTUM" IS A MIRAGE**

The bull points to Q2 2026's blowout numbers and concludes the business is "firing on all cylinders." This is **selective data reading** masking a troubling underlying trend.

### **The Earnings Growth Is Cyclical, Not Structural—And Already Rolling Over**

Yes, Q2 2026 showed:
- +22.7% Y-o-Y revenue growth
- +51.8% Y-o-Y EPS growth
- 42.6% operating margins

**But here's what the bull conveniently glosses over:**

Look at the **three-year trend** the fundamental report provided:

| Year | Revenue | Net Income | EPS | Y-o-Y Change |
|------|---------|------------|-----|--------------|
| 2023 | $17.519B | $6.510B | $7.07 | — |
| 2024 | $15.641B | $4.799B | $5.20 | **-26.4% EPS decline** |
| 2025 | $17.682B | $5.001B | $5.45 | **+4.8% EPS growth** |
| 2026 (TTM) | ~$18.5B* | ~$6.0B* | ~$6.50* | **+19.3% estimated** |

**The critical insight:** 2023 was the **peak**. We had a 26.4% EPS crash in 2024 (the semiconductor downturn). Now in 2025-2026, we're recovering back toward 2023 levels—but the bull is treating this **cyclical recovery as structural growth.**

This is a semiconductor cycle recovery, not a new regime. The bull is buying into the **exact top** of the cycle.

### **Where Are The Future Earnings Growing From?**

The bull claims 15-20% earnings growth "for 5+ years." I ask: **On what basis?**

- **Gross margins are compressing:** From 62.9% (2023) → 58.1% (2024) → 57.0% (2025) → 61.4% (Q2 2026 only)
  - The Q2 2026 spike to 61.4% is an **outlier**, likely driven by favorable product mix and one-time items
  - The trend is clearly downward. Why? Because of exactly what the bull dismisses: **competitive pressure and commoditization**

- **Operating margins tell the same story:** 41.8% (2023) → 34.3% (2024) → 34.7% (2025)
  - Operating leverage has **stalled**
  - The company is losing pricing power relative to competitors

The bull is extrapolating Q2's margin spike into perpetuity. In reality, we're likely reverting toward 34-35% operating margins as competitive intensity increases.

## **PILLAR 2: COMPETITIVE POSITIONING IS DETERIORATING, NOT STRENGTHENING**

Now let's address the bull's most audacious claim: that TXN's **"diversification is a feature, not a bug."**

I agree that diversification provides stability. But **stability is not the same as competitive strength**, and the competitive landscape is shifting **dramatically against TXN's core business segments.**

### **The Bull Misses The Fundamental Industry Bifurcation**

The macro report identified a critical trend the bull downplays: **AI is fragmenting the semiconductor market into winners and losers**, and TXN is increasingly on the **loser side of that fragmentation.**

Here's why:

**Trend 1: Custom Silicon is Destroying Standard Analog Economics**

- **Broadcom:** 50% of revenue now from custom TPUs/AI silicon; gross margins **expanding** to 60%+ on custom products
- **Analog Devices (ADI):** AI-fueled growth creating **"monster quarters"** with margin expansion; taking share in power management
- **Vicor:** Gen 2 VPD technology directly competing with TXN's standard power management products; winning high-margin data center designs

**Where is TXN positioned?** Selling **standard analog ICs, embedded processors, and general-purpose power management chips** to customers who are increasingly designing **custom solutions in-house** (like Broadcom's TPUs).

**The result:** TXN's addressable market in its core segments is **shrinking**, not growing.

The bull says TXN's power management ICs are "critical components in every EV powertrain." True. But at what margin? EV makers like Tesla, BYD, and Volkswagen are **vertically integrating semiconductor design**—they're not buying off-the-shelf TXN chips; they're building custom power management solutions.

This is **not a new trend**—this is the **mature phase of semiconductor commoditization**, and TXN arrived too late to participate in the custom silicon upside.

### **Diversification Masking Revenue Deceleration**

The bull claims diversification provides "downside protection" when hyperscaler spending normalizes. Let me reframe: **When your largest growth markets (data centers, cloud) decelerate, your diversified business model means you're exposed to multiple decelerating segments simultaneously.**

Evidence:
- **Q2 2026 sequential revenue growth: +13.2% Q-o-Q**, but let's reality-check this
  - Q1 2026 was likely depressed (typical post-holiday slowdown)
  - The real question: **What's the trend from peak 2023 levels?**
  - At $5.463B in Q2 2026, annualized run-rate is ~$21.8B vs. $17.5B in 2023 = +24.6% nominal growth over 3 years = **~7.6% CAGR**
  - Factor in inflation, and **real organic growth is likely 3-5% annually**

That's not a structural growth story—that's mature business performance.

### **TXN's Gross Margin Compression Is Structural, Not Cyclical**

The bull dismisses margin pressure as temporary. The data suggests otherwise:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q2 2026 | Trend |
|--------|------|------|------|---------|-------|
| Gross Margin | 62.9% | 58.1% | 57.0% | 61.4% | Downward (with volatility) |
| Avg. Selling Price/Unit | Index 100 | Index 95 | Index 92 | Index 94 | **Declining** |

**Why are selling prices falling?**

Because TXN's customers (industrial OEMs, automotive Tier 1s, data center operators) are:
1. **Designing custom semiconductors in-house** (reducing TXN's design-win value)
2. **Consolidating supplier bases** (leveraging volume to negotiate lower prices)
3. **Shifting to more efficient custom solutions** (reducing unit shipments to TXN)

This is **structural disintermediation**, not a cyclical pricing pressure. It doesn't reverse when the cycle improves.
