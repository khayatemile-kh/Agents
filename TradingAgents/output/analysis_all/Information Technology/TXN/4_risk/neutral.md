## **The Aggressive Analyst Is Right About the Structural Headwinds—But Wrong About the Timing and Magnitude**

The Aggressive Analyst has correctly identified that TXN faces real competitive displacement and margin pressure. The 590 basis point decline from 2023 to 2025 is real. Vicor's Gen 2 VPD technology is a genuine threat to TXN's power management franchise. ADI's AI exposure is superior to TXN's. These are not debatable points—they're embedded in the market data and competitive landscape.

But here's where the Aggressive Analyst makes a critical error: they're conflating *recognition of structural headwinds* with *conviction about timing and magnitude of downside*. Just because a structural headwind exists doesn't mean the market hasn't already priced it in. And just because Q2 2026 showed strong sequential performance doesn't automatically make it a "dead-cat bounce." Those are two different claims, and the Aggressive Analyst is eliding between them.

The real question isn't "are there structural headwinds?" The answer is obviously yes. The real question is: **Has the market already discounted those headwinds into the current price of $253, or is there still material downside left?**

This is where the data gets murky, and the Aggressive Analyst glosses over it by pointing to technical weakness as confirmation of further downside. But technical weakness *after a 13% decline in 40 days* is not surprising—it's exactly what you'd expect to see. That doesn't tell you whether another 20-30% decline is coming or whether we're near a tradeable bottom.

## **The Conservative Analyst Is Right About Stabilization Signals—But Wrong About Dismissing Valuation Risk**

Now let me turn to the Conservative Analyst, because they've made some genuinely important observations that the Aggressive Analyst has minimized.

Q2 2026 gross margin of 61.4% is not nothing. If that was a true dead-cat bounce with no follow-through, we'd expect to see deterioration signals in current market data or forward guidance. We haven't seen those yet. The fact that operating margin expanded to 42.6% in Q2 versus 37.8% in Q1 is consistent with operational leverage working through the system, not with a company in structural free fall.

And the Conservative Analyst is absolutely correct that the 2023-to-2025 EPS comparison is misleading. 2023 was a cyclical peak. Comparing current performance to cyclical peaks as if they're the baseline is exactly backward from how you should think about semiconductor cycles. The relevant comparison is 2025 to 2024 (EPS grew 4.8%), and that's directionally positive.

But—and this is critical—the Conservative Analyst then makes a leap that isn't fully supported by the data. They claim that consensus guidance of $10.50-$11.00 in forward annual EPS "is eminently achievable" based on Q2 run-rates and normalized operating margins of 40%. Let me push back on this claim directly.

If Q2 2026 annualizes to $8.56 in EPS but the Conservative Analyst is projecting $10.50-$11.00 for full-year forward guidance, that's an implicit assumption that either (a) margins expand significantly in H2 2026 versus Q2 levels, or (b) revenue accelerates substantially. The Conservative Analyst hasn't proven either of these will happen. They've shown Q2 was strong, but strong Q2 doesn't automatically predict strong Q3 and Q4, especially when the seasonal pattern in semiconductors often shows Q4 strength followed by Q1 weakness.

Moreover, the Conservative Analyst is being too dismissive of valuation risk by anchoring so heavily to consensus estimates. Yes, consensus guides to $10.50-$11.00. But what if consensus is wrong? What if the Q2 margin recovery is actually a cyclical bounce, not the beginning of a sustainable trend? Then the market has overestimated earnings power, and a multiple compression from 24x to 18-20x becomes very likely—not a 25% bear case, but more like a 35-40% downside scenario.
