## THE FUNDAMENTAL CASE IS REAL—BUT IT'S ALREADY PRICED IN, AGGRESSIVELY

Here's what I need to challenge directly: just because LRCX has strong fundamentals doesn't mean it's a *good buy at $290*. That's not investing logic; that's narrative investing.

Yes, $6.72 billion in quarterly revenue is impressive. Yes, 15% sequential growth is solid. And yes, the forward P/E of 24.91x on 102% earnings growth sounds reasonable in isolation. But let's unpack what that actually means.

The market is now pricing in **$11.57 in forward earnings**. That's an assumption that LRCX will nearly *double* earnings in the next 12 months. Do you understand what that confidence level means? It means the market is already fully pricing in a **sustained, uninterrupted acceleration** of the business. There is almost no margin for disappointment built into that valuation.

Now, here's where the risk comes in: what happens when—not if, *when*—capex growth moderates? The Aggressive Analyst says, "Foundries have already committed to capex plans." That's true. But semiconductor capex cycles are notoriously front-loaded. When TSMC or Samsung decides to build a new fab, they order 70-80% of their equipment in years 1-2 of the cycle, then taper sharply. We're likely in years 1-2 of this AI-driven cycle *right now*. 

The forward guidance assumes that acceleration continues linearly. History suggests otherwise. Look at the past three semiconductor equipment cycles (2010-2012, 2016-2018, 2020-2022). Each saw aggressive capex, followed by sharp pullbacks. LRCX's own revenue history shows this: from 2025-09-30 ($5.32B) to 2025-12-31 ($5.34B), growth essentially flatlined. Then 2026 exploded upward. That's not linear; that's cyclical.

So when the market is pricing in 102% earnings growth and there's a measurable risk that growth moderates to 20-30% in the back half of 2026 or 2027, what happens to a stock trading at 24.91x forward? It compresses—hard. We're talking a 30-40% multiple contraction scenario that isn't implausible at all.

**The fortress balance sheet you're touting? Yes, it's fortress-like. But it also means the stock has *already compressed* from higher multiples into 24.91x on the forward basis.** If earnings don't grow at 102%, that multiple won't support current prices. The 27% dividend hike is great for existing shareholders, but it's not a guarantee of earnings growth—it's a statement of *current* cash position, which can deteriorate quickly if capex cycles cool.

When RSI is declining *during a price decline*, it's confirming that the downtrend has legs. We're not there yet. We're in the zone where weak-handed longs are still holding, watching their gains evaporate, waiting for a bounce that never comes. That's typically when capitulation finally hits.

Now, the MACD argument. The Aggressive Analyst said MACD was deeply negative at -16.86 on Aug 3 and rallied to +1.25 by mid-August, so the current -4.09 is "just normalization." 

That's a misreading of the data. That's backwards. When a stock is *testing* a Bollinger Band, especially the lower band in a downtrend, it's testing whether that support will *hold*. We don't know if it will. The stock could breach that band easily if selling accelerates. Moreover, historical LRCX behavior doesn't show a consistent pattern of sharp reversals at the lower band—and even if it did, that would be a *countertrend trade*, not a trend-following position. For a business-driven, secular growth thesis, you want to be buying in *confirmed uptrends*, not fishing for reversals at support bands. That's tactical trading overlaid on a strategic thesis, and mixing the two is dangerous.
