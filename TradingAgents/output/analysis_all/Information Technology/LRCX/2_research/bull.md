**And who supplies the tools to build the fabs where those chips are made? Lam Research.**

When a capital equipment company commits $3 billion to accelerating AI chip manufacturing innovation, that's not bullish posturing—that's a **bet on sustained, multi-year demand.**

### The Capex Cycle is Non-Discretionary

Unlike software companies or consumer discretionaries that can defer spending during uncertainty, **semiconductor manufacturers cannot defer fab capex without losing market share.**

Think about it:
- TSMC can't slow 3nm/2nm ramp without ceding share to Samsung
- Samsung can't pause advanced node investment without falling behind TSMC
- Intel can't defer foundry capex without abandoning its foundry strategy

This means LRCX's equipment orders are backed by *existential competitive pressures*, not discretionary IT budgets. When customers commit to advanced node production, they commit to Lam's tools. Full stop.
