**Investment Thesis**: The bull case is anchored in hard, already-reported numbers that the bear never disputes on a historical basis: 1,400bps gross margin expansion, operating margin swinging from -1.7% to +17.2%, net income +163% YoY, 50% YoY revenue growth, and TTM FCF of $8.84B against just 3.9% capex/revenue. These are realized results, not projections, and the Research Manager correctly weights them as the stronger evidentiary anchor versus the bear's forward-looking conjecture (TSMC price-hike risk, demand saturation, margin "mirage") which the debate confirms has no corroborating hard data yet — no reported margin damage, no FCF deterioration, no confirmed AMD-specific share loss.

The Conservative and Neutral analysts nonetheless raise legitimate, quantifiable risks that argue against maximum conviction: (1) the forward P/E of 29.6x depends on $15.45 forward EPS, which the Conservative and Neutral analysts both show may be overstated — the Neutral analyst's own reconstruction (25-30% revenue growth, 15% operating margin) implies forward EPS closer to $5.50-$7.50, not $15.45, meaning the "cheap forward multiple" argument is unreliable; (2) debt-to-equity of 6.36x combined with $41.1B of goodwill (49% of total assets, largely from the Xilinx deal) creates genuine impairment/de-rating tail risk if growth disappoints, though the Neutral analyst's point that AMD could generate $14-16B of FCF over two years to de-lever even in a slower-growth scenario meaningfully caps the "Intel-style distress" downside the Conservative analyst warns of; (3) Taiwan/TSMC geopolitical risk is a real, non-modeled tail risk that the debate agrees is only partially reflected in the 17% pullback, not a solvency-threatening base case.

On technicals, both sides concede this is close to a coin-flip: price is below the 10 EMA ($471.97) and 50 SMA ($502.90) but +36.2% above the 200 SMA, RSI is neutral-to-weak (43.6), and the MACD histogram is compressing (-1.85 to -0.67) without confirmed crossover. The Neutral analyst's observation that AMD may simply be in a range-bound "between catalysts" phase ($440-480) until the next earnings print is the most balanced read and supports scaling in patiently rather than chasing.

Applying the prior NVDA lesson directly: that experience showed that prospective risks flagged as "monitoring triggers" (there, ASIC/hyperscaler substitution) did not materialize within one earnings cycle, and premature escalation to a downgrade would have been a false signal. The same discipline applies here — the bear's margin-reversal and demand-saturation risks are prospective, not yet confirmed, so they should be tracked as explicit downgrade triggers over the next 1-2 quarters rather than acted on preemptively. This supports Overweight now with defined re-rating checkpoints rather than either a maximal Buy (ignoring real valuation/leverage risk) or a defensive Hold/Underweight (ignoring the fact that the bear's most damaging claims are unconfirmed).
