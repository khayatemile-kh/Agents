**Investment Thesis**: The debate converges on a clear, evidence-based conclusion: operational momentum and balance sheet quality are both deteriorating even as the stock priced in a much rosier narrative. The single hardest data point in the entire debate—Q2 2026 revenue down 0.6% YoY—directly contradicts the bull/AI-tailwind story that Gartner should be capturing outsized demand amid 14.2% global IT spend growth; all three analysts (Aggressive, Conservative, Neutral) acknowledge this figure is real, and even the Conservative Analyst's seasonality defense (10.9% sequential bounce from Q1) does not resolve the YoY comp problem, only contextualizes it as inconclusive rather than reassuring.

On capital structure, the Aggressive Analyst's numbers were not disputed by the other two: equity swung from +$1,532.6M (Q2 2025) to -$167.3M (Q2 2026), a ~$1.7B decline, driven by $1,081.7M of H1 2026 buybacks stacked on top of a $459.6M YoY increase in debt. The Conservative Analyst correctly frames this as partly an accounting/treasury-stock mechanic rather than proof of insolvency, and notes TTM EBITDA of ~$1.38B and a 2.34x Debt/EBITDA ratio that is not immediately alarming for a recurring-revenue business. However, the Neutral Analyst's synthesis is the most persuasive: management chose to accelerate buybacks into a period of flattening/negative revenue growth rather than pause and preserve flexibility, which is a capital-allocation red flag regardless of whether the balance sheet is technically solvent today. This is a leverage-funded EPS-support strategy layered on top of stalled organic growth — a combination that erodes financial flexibility precisely when the AI-disruption risk to advisory economics (explicitly raised in retail commentary: "AI has eliminated these guys yet?") is a live, unresolved question.

Technically, all three analysts agree the setup has weakened: RSI fell from 64.90 to 57.33 in a single session, MACD histogram turned negative (-0.94), and price is sitting on the Bollinger Middle Band ($189.34) after a 33.8% three-week rally to $202.78. The Conservative and Neutral analysts reasonably note this could be normal mean-reversion/profit-taking after an overbought move (RSI 71.40 on Aug 24) rather than a crash signal, and the 50 SMA at $158.81 remains well below current price, arguing against an imminent structural breakdown. This tempers the Aggressive Analyst's "falling knife" framing but does not invalidate the case for trimming — it simply argues against a full exit.

Net synthesis: there is no analyst-presented evidence of revenue reacceleration, debt reduction, or equity stabilization — the three explicit falsifiable triggers the Research Manager set for upgrading conviction. Until at least one of these reverses, the risk/reward favors a reduced, non-core position rather than either full exposure or a full sell of a still cash-generative, high-margin (~70% gross margin), moat-protected business. This is a rating of Underweight, not Sell, because the Conservative Analyst's point about real EBITDA generation ($1.4B TTM) and the Neutral Analyst's point about absence of confirmed operational collapse (versus accounting-driven equity optics) argue against treating this as a business in distress — only as one whose current risk-adjusted setup no longer justifies full-weight conviction.
