And yes, the options market surge in implied volatility is noteworthy. Market-makers don't push IV that hard on pure noise.

Piper cut the price target from $140 to $125 *while* upgrading. You frame this as a "conviction upgrade at better entry point," but that's cherry-picking the narrative that confirms your bias. Here's an equally plausible reading: Piper's analysts saw the Q2 2026 earnings data—negative net income momentum, slowing revenue growth, margin collapse—and realized their old $140 target was predicated on a Compute inflection that's taking longer to materialize than they expected. So they pivoted. They're saying "we still believe in the story, but we're less confident in the timing and magnitude."

That's not the same as "the inflection is imminent."

And when you cite "Compute revenue growing 25%+ YoY," I need you to show me the data. The comprehensive fundamental report shows total revenue at 2% TTM growth. If Compute were truly growing at 25%, even if it's only 15-20% of revenue mix, we'd see headline revenue growth in the 4-6% range, not 2%. That gap tells me either: (a) Compute isn't growing 25%, or (b) the core CDN business is declining, which is even worse than the headline suggests.

You can't just assert segment growth without showing the math. That's not "portfolio math"—that's assumption stacking.

## **WHERE THE CONSERVATIVE ANALYST IS RIGHT—AND WHERE THEY OVERSELL THE CERTAINTY**

You're correct that the fundamentals are deteriorating. Net income down 23% YoY in Q2, operating margin at 7.6% vs. 15.2% four years ago, debt up 64% year-over-year to $9.3B—these are real red flags. And you're right that 5.6x Net Debt/EBITDA is materially elevated for a software company in a high-rate environment.

Your point about the macro backdrop is also valid. Fed rate cuts are priced at 1-4% probability through December. Akamai can't rely on refinancing relief to delever. That's structural pressure.

But here's where you overstate the case: **you're treating the current quarterly snapshot as a permanent state when it might be a transition point.**

You ask, "Will Compute margins expand to 30%+ as it scales?" and respond with skepticism because edge infrastructure is "capital-intensive and operationally complex." That's fair pushback, but it's not a refutation. Yes, edge infrastructure is capital-intensive. But so was cloud computing in AWS's early days. Unit economics improve with scale. And if Compute becomes 25%+ of revenue with even 20% gross margins (not 30%), the blended picture improves meaningfully.

You also cite the 2% TTM revenue growth as evidence the company is "in distress." But growth deceleration and distress aren't the same thing. A 2% growth company trading at 38.6x P/E is expensive, yes. But it's not a distress signal—it's a valuation risk.

Where you really lose me is when you say the market has "already priced in" a Compute inflection. If that were true, the stock wouldn't be down 5-10% from its highs with technicals breaking down. The market is actually *un*-pricing the inflection right now. That's the opposite of a fully valued scenario.
