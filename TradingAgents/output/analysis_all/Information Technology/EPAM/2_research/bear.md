### A. TTM Numbers Are Masking H1 2026 Reality

Here's where the bull argument falls apart: **It relies entirely on TTM (trailing twelve-month) metrics, which are backward-looking and obscuring what's actually happening right now.**

Let's look at the real-time cash flow data:

| Period | Operating Cash Flow | Free Cash Flow | Working Capital Change |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|------------------------|
| **Q2 2026** | **-$2.4M** | **-$17.6M** | **-$182.7M** |
| **Q1 2026** | **-$36.4M** | **-$54.2M** | **-$229.6M** |

| Q4 2025 | $282.9M | $268.1M | $45.2M |
| Q3 2025 | $294.7M | $286.4M | $32.1M |

**This is not a rounding error. This is a structural deterioration.**

The bulls say EPAM generates "$612.7M in annual FCF" and point to the 1.03x FCF multiple as cheap. **But that number is completely stale.** The company is *currently burning cash* in operations. Two consecutive quarters of negative operating cash flow is not a temporary blip—it's a warning signal.

What's driving this? **Accounts receivable surged by $93.4M in Q2 2026 alone.** This signals:

1. **Customer collection problems** – Either clients aren't paying, or EPAM is extending terms
2. **Revenue quality deterioration** – Sales are happening, but cash isn't following
3. **Working capital drain** – The company is financing its customers' operations with company cash

The bulls' response will be: "This is temporary. Operating cycles fluctuate." **But that's exactly what a company in distress says.** Two consecutive quarters of negative OCF in a $5.6B revenue business is not noise—it's a data point that the business is struggling to convert its top line into actual cash.

## II. MARGIN COMPRESSION IS NOT TEMPORARY—IT'S STRUCTURAL

### A. The "Restructuring is Transition-Accretive" Argument is Self-Contradictory

The bull makes a bold claim: JPMorgan acknowledges the North American restructuring is "transition-accretive to margins," therefore margins will expand.

**But that's not what JPMorgan said.** Here's the actual implication from the analyst commentary: The restructuring will be *margin-accretive long-term*, but near-term revenue and margins are at risk. Translation: **We expect pain before gain.**

Let's look at the margin reality:

| Period | Net Margin | Operating Margin | EBITDA Margin |
|--------|-----------|------------------|---------------|
| **FY2024** | 9.61% | ~11.5% | ~15.2% |
| **FY2025** | 6.92% | ~8.9% | ~13.8% |
| **Q2 2026** | 7.28% | 10.8% | TBD |
| **Q1 2026** | 5.89% | 8.3% | TBD |
| **TTM** | 7.15% | ~8.9% | 13.4% |

**Margins have compressed 250+ basis points from FY2024 levels.** The bull argues this is "front-loaded restructuring costs" that will dissipate. **But where's the evidence?**

- Q2 2026 net margin of 7.28% shows *no improvement* from Q1's 5.89%—it's still below the TTM average
- Operating margins are still volatile (8.3% to 10.8% quarter-to-quarter)
- There's **no clear inflection point** where margins are accelerating

And here's the critical point: **The bull is betting on margin expansion, but the company is simultaneously dealing with:**

1. **Revenue headwinds** (only 2.9% YoY growth, with FX drag)
2. **Competitive pricing pressure** in an oversaturated IT services market
3. **Higher labor costs** (EPAM has a global workforce; wage inflation is persistent)
4. **Client budget constraints** (the macro environment is tightening—more on this below)

If revenue isn't accelerating AND margins need to expand to justify valuations, the company must achieve simultaneous revenue stabilization AND margin expansion. That's not a baseline assumption—**that's an optimistic scenario.**

### B. Margin Expansion Will Come From Automation, Not Revenue Magic

The bull argues: "Automation of service delivery drives margin expansion."

**But automation requires capital investment**, and the company has already shown it's capital-constrained:

- CapEx in 2025 was only $42.2M (0.8% of revenue)
- This is extraordinarily low for a company claiming to be investing in AI/automation tooling
- Peers like Accenture and TCS invest 3–4% of revenue in transformation tech

**The contradiction is clear:** How does EPAM automate its way to higher margins without investing in automation? The short answer: **It can't.** And if it does invest, that CapEx comes out of cash flow, which is already negative.
