But look at the actual quarterly breakdown: Q2 2026 was negative $2.4 million and Q1 2026 was negative $36.4 million. That's a combined $38.8 million drag over six months. Now compare that to trailing twelve months operating cash flow: $654.9 million. 

Do you see what's happening here? The TTM operating cash flow is heavily weighted by Q3 and Q4 2025, which generated $294.7 million and $282.9 million respectively. Yes, that's a significant step-down in H1 2026. But this is not evidence of structural deterioration—this is evidence of **seasonal and cyclical working capital timing**. 

The aggressive analyst mentions a $412 million working capital drain. That number needs scrutiny. In a services business—and EPAM is fundamentally a services business—working capital can swing dramatically quarter to quarter based on:
- Client billing cycles and invoicing timing
- Collections cycles (which can extend during periods of client financial tightness)
- Payroll timing and accrual adjustments
- Advance payments from large contracts
- Currency fluctuation impacts (EPAM has global revenue)

The fact that accounts receivable increased by $93.4 million in Q2 could indicate softer collections, OR it could indicate that revenue timing shifted favorably into the quarter-end period with invoicing trailing slightly. Without seeing the days sales outstanding (DSO) metric explicitly, the aggressive analyst is assuming the worst interpretation.

Here's the critical point that gets overlooked: Free cash flow on a TTM basis is still $612.7 million positive, representing 12.5% of TTM revenue. That's a healthy free cash flow conversion rate. If H1 2026 was a temporary working capital dislocation, we should expect Q3 and Q4 2026 to show normalization—and potentially strong recovery as large projects invoice and collect.
