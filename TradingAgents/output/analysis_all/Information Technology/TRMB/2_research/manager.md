**Recommendation**: Hold

**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points that roughly offset each other, which is why this is a genuine Hold rather than a forced pick. The bull's strongest argument is the Q1 2026 print itself: 11.9% revenue growth, 68.8% gross margin, 15.6% operating margin, and $98.9M net income are real, recent, and directionally consistent with a company gaining pricing power and scale. Cash generation is also genuinely strong on an annual basis ($686M FCF, 18% margin), and the valuation (14.3x forward P/E, 1.39 PEG) is not demanding relative to peers, leaving room for the 28% consensus upside if the trend holds.

But the bear's rebuttal lands on the two things that matter most for conviction: quality and durability of that improvement. The TTM net margin is -2.77%, meaning Q2-Q4 2025 absorbed a combined ~$203.6M loss that has never been explained in the evidence provided — no breakdown of one-time charges, restructuring, or write-downs tied to the $6.09B goodwill balance. Without that explanation, we can't distinguish a genuine profitability inflection from a mix-driven or non-recurring beat. The bear's point about operating cash flow volatility is also hard to dismiss — a swing from -$53.5M OCF in Q2 2025 to +$274.7M in Q1 2026 is not the signature of a stable, high-visibility business, and it directly undercuts the bull's "8.8x interest coverage" comfort framing, since that coverage ratio is only as good as the quarter you measure it in. Working capital coverage at ~0.95x with $234M cash against $1.37B current liabilities is thin enough that a single collections hiccup (which we already saw in Q2 2025) could pressure liquidity again.

Neither side provided the data needed to settle the key question: was Q1 2026's margin expansion structural (software mix, pricing power, cost discipline) or transient (one-time credits, favorable mix, easy comp)? Until that's resolved — ideally with a Q2 2026 print confirming margins held or improved sequentially, and disclosure on what drove the prior-year loss — committing capital aggressively in either direction is not well supported by the evidence in this debate. The technical setup (RSI neutral at 53, MACD rolling over) also doesn't offer a tie-breaking signal; it's consistent with either healthy consolidation or the start of a pullback.

Net: bull has the better growth/valuation story, bear has the better quality-of-earnings and balance-sheet-fragility story. That's a genuine stalemate, not a coin-flip resolved by conviction — so Hold is the intellectually honest call here.
