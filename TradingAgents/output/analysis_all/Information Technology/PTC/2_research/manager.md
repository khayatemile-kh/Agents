**Rationale**: Weighing both sides, the bear case lands more decisive blows on the specific evidence presented. On the guidance raise, the bull leans heavily on "encouraging early AI traction" language that the bear correctly flags as unquantified IR-speak, and the bear's quarter-over-quarter data (Q1→Q2 revenue -22.5%, operating margin -10.5pp, EPS -79.3%, with Q1 inflated by $465M in non-operating gains) is a concrete, verifiable red flag the bull never addresses or rebuts. That kind of sequential deterioration in the core business, sitting right behind a superficially reassuring ARR guidance raise, is exactly the kind of disconnect that should worry a trader more than it comforts them. The bull's buyback-as-confidence-signal argument is also weaker than the bear's counter: accelerated buybacks are equally consistent with a company using financial engineering to prop up EPS when organic growth options are scarce, and the bull offers no incremental disclosure (like buyback dollar amounts vs. FCF) to prove this is conviction rather than optics. On the technical setup, the bull's read of "consolidation, not reversal" is plausible but is a secondary technical argument next to fundamental controversy over earnings quality — RSI cooling and a bearish MACD cross after a huge run is at best neutral, not a strong tailwind. Macro argument (rates flat, low recession risk) is a wash — it doesn't favor PTC specifically over any other quality software name, so it doesn't tip the scale. Net: the bear's forensic breakdown of the revenue/margin/EPS trajectory and the unresolved gap between ARR optimism and actual P&L deterioration is the more rigorous, harder-to-wave-away argument. This isn't a slam-dunk short thesis (the balance sheet is fine, cash is up, there's no distress), so outright Sell is too strong, but the weight of evidence suggests trimming exposure and waiting for concrete, quantified proof of AI-driven revenue before adding on the recent rally.
