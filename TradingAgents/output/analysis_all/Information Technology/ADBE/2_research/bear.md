But here's what the bull is conveniently glossing over: **The market isn't skeptical of Adobe's profitability—it's skeptical of whether those forward earnings projections are actually achievable.** And frankly, when I dig into the evidence, I see a company facing significant headwinds that could eviscerate those 60.8% growth assumptions.

Let me be direct: **I'm not debating whether Adobe is profitable. I'm arguing that the bull's growth narrative is dangerously over-optimistic, the Saudi Arabia deal is a revenue trap masquerading as strategy, and the stock's recent 34% rally from $212 to $292 is setting up for a sharp reversal.**

The bull wants you to look at the PEG ratio of 0.70 and see opportunity. I see a ratio that's been artificially deflated by forward earnings estimates that will likely miss.

## **CRITICAL FLAW #1: The Forward Earnings Projection Is Built On Quicksand**

Let me attack the bull's core thesis directly. They're anchoring everything on a **60.8% forward earnings growth assumption**. Let's examine where this comes from and why it's likely wrong.

### **The Math Doesn't Hold Up Under Scrutiny**

Here's what we actually know from the fundamentals report:

| Metric | Q3 2026 | Q2 2026 | Q1 2026 | Trend |
|--------|---------|---------|---------|-------|
| **Net Income** | $1,712M | $1,889M | $1,856M | **DECLINING** |
| **EPS** | $4.25 | $4.60 | $4.45 | **DECLINING** |
| **TTM Net Income** | $7.2B | - | - | - |

**Here's the problem:** Q3 2026 net income actually *declined* sequentially from Q2. EPS fell from $4.60 to $4.25—a **7.6% drop quarter-over-quarter.**

The bull is projecting 60.8% forward earnings growth, which would imply annualized EPS of approximately $27.50. But look at the trend:
- If we annualize Q3 2026 EPS of $4.25, we get ~$17 annually
- That's essentially flat with the current $17.10 TTM EPS
- To hit $27.50, earnings would need to nearly **double in the next 12 months**

**Where is this doubling coming from?** The bull points to:
1. CXO AI-first ARR surging
2. Agentic adoption expanding
3. Future Saudi Arabia monetization

But here's the critical issue: **None of this is reflected in actual quarterly earnings yet.** Q3 shows revenue growth of 12.7% YoY—that's solid but not spectacular—and net income is *contracting* sequentially. If AI-driven growth and agentic adoption were truly "surging," we'd see it already showing up in the numbers. We don't.

### **The Forward Estimates Are Self-Refuting**

Think about what the bull is claiming: The market is pricing in 60.8% earnings growth, yet the stock is only trading at a forward P/E of 10.17x. That's mathematically inconsistent.

Here's why: **If the market genuinely believed in 60.8% earnings growth, the stock would trade at 20-25x forward earnings at minimum.** Growth stocks with that kind of trajectory command premium valuations. The fact that ADBE trades at 10x forward suggests the market is *already heavily discounting* the growth assumptions.

**Two possibilities:**
1. The market is irrationally pessimistic (bull's argument)
2. The forward earnings estimates are inflated (my argument)

I believe it's #2. Analyst forward earnings estimates are historically biased toward optimism, particularly for high-profile tech companies where analysts face pressure from their investment banking relationships. When those estimates miss—and they will—the multiple compresses sharply.

## **CRITICAL FLAW #2: The Saudi Arabia Deal Is A Margin Killer, Not A Growth Engine**

Now let me directly refute the bull's characterization of the Saudi Arabia partnership.

The bull frames this as "strategic market seeding"—comparing it favorably to Spotify's international expansion. That's a false analogy, and here's why:

### **The Economics Are Terrible**

Let's do the math on this $4B "investment":

- **Cost:** $4 billion in foregone subscription revenue over 12 months
- **Benefit:** 27+ million new users with free access
- **Per-user acquisition cost:** $4B ÷ 27M = **~$148 per user for one year of free product**

Now compare this to Adobe's historical unit economics:
- Average Creative Cloud subscription: ~$55/month or ~$660/year per user
- But these are *free* subscriptions to Saudi users who may never pay

**The critical question:** What's the probability that 27 million Saudi users, who've been accustomed to free Adobe products, will actually convert to paid subscriptions at meaningful rates after year one?

The bull claims "higher monetization rates than virgin markets typically achieve." But that's speculative. In reality:

1. **Price sensitivity in emerging markets is extreme.** Saudi Arabia's per-capita GDP is ~$35,000. The pricing expectations for software in that market are dramatically lower than in the US or Europe.

2. **Government-backed initiatives create political complications.** If the Saudi government subsidized user access, they may expect preferential pricing or terms. This could constrain Adobe's pricing power indefinitely.

3. **One year of habitual usage doesn't guarantee conversion.** If users grow accustomed to *free* Adobe, the switching cost argument works *in reverse*—they're less likely to pay later because they're habituated to zero cost.

### **The Margin Impact Is Immediate and Real**

Here's what the bull conveniently didn't emphasize: **This deal immediately pressures Adobe's margins.**

Currently, Adobe's gross margin is 89.4%. When you give away $4B in product to Saudi Arabia, you're absorbing the cost of delivery and support without recognizing revenue. This is a *contra-revenue* transaction that hits profitability in Year 1.

On a base of $25.2B revenue, $4B represents **16% of annual revenue being given away.** Even if the COGS is minimal (it's software, so it is), the lost contribution margin is substantial.

**The result:** Adobe's profitability metrics will *contract* in FY2027 when this deal is fully recognized, not expand. Yet the bull's growth projections assume profitability remains stable or expands.

### **This Is Exactly What Happens Before Guidance Misses**

I've seen this movie before. A company makes a big strategic announcement that sounds impressive (market seeding, long-term optionality). Analysts and bulls extrapolate rosy scenarios. Management initially supports the narrative. Then, when the deal's actual economics materialize, the company quietly revises guidance downward, and the stock craters.

From the market research: *"Adobe's option-implied move range extends well beyond 52-week highs and lows, suggesting the market is pricing elevated volatility and uncertainty."*

**That elevated volatility is because institutional investors are pricing in downside surprise risk.** They're not convinced by the bull's narrative either.
