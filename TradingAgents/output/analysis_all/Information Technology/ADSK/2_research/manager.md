**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: Q2 revenue beat (+16% YoY), Q3 guidance came in well above the prior consensus, but full-year EPS guidance was lowered, and RPO growth (+2%) badly lagged revenue growth (+16%). The bull's rebuttal to this (that it's just integration-cost timing) is asserted, not evidenced, whereas the arithmetic the bear lays out (Q2 beat + strong Q3 + still-cut FY guide => weak implied Q4) is hard to dismiss.

On valuation, the bull's PEG/forward P/E argument is credible and keeps this from being an outright Sell — this isn't an overvalued growth-scare stock, it has real profitability and cash generation. The bear's leverage argument (debt-to-equity ~109%, current ratio 0.855) is a legitimate secondary concern but is overstated as an imminent crisis given net debt of only $234M and $2.67B cash on hand; I don't weight this heavily as a standalone reason to be bearish, but it does modestly diminish comfort with adding aggressively here.

Net: the guidance-quality issue (EPS cut despite revenue strength) plus the RPO deceleration are the two most decision-relevant, forward-looking signals, and neither was convincingly rebutted by the bull beyond asserting "it's just timing." That tips this from a clean Hold toward a cautious, trim-biased stance rather than a full Sell, because the underlying profitability/cash-flow engine and reasonable multiple argue against panic exit. This is not a "balanced, do-nothing" situation — the bear's forward-looking bookings/earnings-quality argument is the stronger, more decision-useful thesis, so I'm calling this Underweight rather than Hold.
