**Investment Thesis**: All three analysts and the research manager agree on the underlying facts: CBOE is a high-margin (26.7% net margin, 38% EBITDA margin), high-ROE (26%) business with a fortress balance sheet (0.82x debt/EBITDA, 38.5x interest coverage), trading at 24x trailing/19x forward earnings, in a clean technical uptrend (above both 50-day $280.37 and 200-day $283.82 SMAs, RSI 57.7, positive MACD, $12.24 of room to the upper Bollinger Band). The dispute is entirely about durability of growth vs. valuation/cyclicality, and neither side actually rebuts the other's core point.

The Aggressive analyst's strongest evidence—tangible book value surging from $0.10B to $1.33B, widening 50/200-SMA gap, tightening Bollinger Bands as a 'coiling' setup—was directly and convincingly challenged by both the Conservative and Neutral analysts: a 1,200% tangible book jump against only 30.2% retained-earnings growth is a mismatch that signals accounting/goodwill noise, not organic capital compounding, and is a red flag rather than a bullish catalyst. Similarly, the Kalshi litigation was reframed correctly by both Conservative and Neutral analysts as evidence the prediction-market catalyst is still unlaunched/unproven rather than a validated growth driver — "if CBOE were already printing money there, Kalshi would be fighting market share, not lobbying for delays." This significantly weakens the aggressive case for accumulation now.

The Conservative analyst's IV-compression argument was itself moderated by the Neutral analyst: sticky elevated rates create hedging demand tied to volatility-spike risk, not the "hedging becomes unnecessary" framing conservative used, and retail volume growth does provide a real (if modest) tailwind to CBOE given its ~30% share of U.S. options volume and smart-order-routing obligations. The Neutral analyst's key factual challenge to the bull case stands unaddressed: revenue grew only ~3.9% sequentially (Q1→Q2 2026), which does not yet support the mid-20s EPS growth priced into the 19x forward multiple — this is the single most important unresolved data gap in the debate. Until quarterly numbers show the data/analytics segment reaccelerating independent of trading volume, the valuation risk flagged by the bear/research manager is live and unaddressed by bulls beyond narrative ("CBOE wins either way").

Weighing the debate: this is a quality business (unanimous agreement) trading at a full multiple that already prices near-perfect execution, with a technical setup that is genuinely neutral-to-constructive (not overbought, not breaking down) rather than a screaming buy or sell signal. The Neutral analyst's synthesis — that the setup is "a textbook hold pattern," with the $315-316 area representing a defined, capped near-term target rather than unlimited upside — is the most evidence-grounded conclusion of the three. Aggressive accumulation is not justified because the bull's best incremental evidence (book value surge, Kalshi complaint) was shown to be either accounting noise or a sign the catalyst is unproven. Outright selling/underweighting is equally unjustified because the business quality metrics are real, balance sheet risk is minimal, and the stock is not showing technical breakdown signs. Hold, with the specific triggers below, best captures this balance.
