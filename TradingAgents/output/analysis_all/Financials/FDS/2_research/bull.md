## III. THE DEBT QUESTION: YES, IT'S REAL, BUT IT'S MANAGEABLE AND BEING ADDRESSED

Let me address what will undoubtedly be the bear's first line of attack: **"FDS has $1.57 billion in debt and a 77.3% debt-to-equity ratio. That's dangerous."**

Fair point. I don't dismiss debt. But here's the critical context the bear will omit:

### Debt Levels are Manageable Given Cash Generation
- **Total Debt: $1.57 billion**
- **TTM Operating Cash Flow: $726.3 million**
- **Net Debt: $1.10 billion** (after $288M cash)
- **Net Debt-to-EBITDA: ~1.14x**

A Net Debt-to-EBITDA ratio of **1.14x is not dangerous**—it's actually conservative for a software company. Industry norms for high-quality SaaS are 1.5-2.5x. FDS is *below* the comfort zone, not above it.

### The Debt-to-Equity Ratio Needs Context
Yes, 77.3% looks high in isolation. But this metric is misleading for software companies because:
1. **Goodwill & Intangibles = 75% of assets** — This reflects acquisition value, not leverage risk
2. **Equity value is depressed by accounting conventions** — The company's true economic value is much higher than its book equity suggests
3. **On a Price-to-Book basis (5.39x), the market is already pricing in the quality of the business**

If we adjust equity to reflect current market value (~$10.9B market cap), the true "debt-to-market-equity" is closer to 14%, which is *conservative*.

### Debt Service is Not a Constraint
- **Interest Coverage Ratio: ~14.5x** (Operating Income ÷ Interest Expense)
- **FCF After Debt Service: $621.2M - ~$90M interest = ~$531M available**

FDS is generating **enough cash to cover debt service 8x over**. This is not a distressed situation; it's a well-capitalized business that chose to use leverage for acquisitions and shareholder returns.
