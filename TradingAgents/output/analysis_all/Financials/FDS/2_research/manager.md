**Rationale**: Both sides make fair points, but the bear's argument cuts closer to the fundamental thesis that actually drives long-term returns. But the bear's core critique is the more decision-relevant one: revenue growth has decelerated sharply from 13.1% (FY23) to ~5.4-5.6% (FY24-25) and TTM, a 60% deceleration, while 75% of the balance sheet is goodwill/intangibles from an acquisition strategy that isn't translating into accelerating organic growth. That combination — slowing growth, heavy reliance on M&A to sustain the story, and buyback-driven EPS growth outpacing revenue by a wide margin (27% EPS growth vs ~5% revenue growth) — is a legitimate flag for multiple compression risk and potential goodwill impairment risk if growth doesn't reaccelerate.

The valuation argument cuts both ways: FDS's 15.5x forward P/E is a discount to faster-growing peers like Workday, but that discount is arguably justified precisely because FDS's growth has structurally slowed — the market is not obviously mispricing the stock, it's rationally derating a decelerating-growth, high-goodwill compounder. The bull's PEG of 1.36x actually supports the bear's point more than it refutes it: the stock isn't statistically cheap once you properly account for the growth rate.

Net: the debt itself is manageable (net debt/EBITDA ~1.14x, strong coverage), so this isn't a solvency story. But the deceleration in organic growth and the risk that margin/EPS gains are increasingly reliant on buybacks rather than the core business reaccelerating is the more credible near-to-medium term concern, and the technical setup (near upper Bollinger Band, cooling momentum) doesn't give a bull entry edge right now. This tilts toward a cautious stance rather than a balanced Hold — there isn't enough evidence of a re-acceleration catalyst to justify adding exposure, but the fundamentals (margins, cash generation, debt serviceability) aren't bad enough to justify an outright Sell/exit either. I'm landing on Underweight: trim into strength, don't chase the bounce, and wait for evidence of either organic growth reacceleration or a real valuation reset before revisiting.
