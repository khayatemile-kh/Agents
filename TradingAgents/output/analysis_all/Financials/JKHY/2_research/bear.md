### **PROBLEM 1: THE "HIGHER RATES = BANKS SPEND MORE ON TECH" THESIS IS BACKWARD**

The bull just argued that 89% probability of zero rate cuts means banks will have higher profitability and thus accelerate tech spending. **This is economically naive.** Let me explain why.

**The Actual Mechanics of Higher-for-Longer Rates:**

Yes, banks *initially* benefit from wider net interest margins. That's true. But here's what the bull conveniently omitted:

1. **Capital Allocation Shifts Under Rate Pressure:**
   - When rates are high and sticky, banks become *more* capital-constrained, not less. They're managing deposit flight, rising deposit costs, and compressed loan demand.
   - A bank CFO's priority hierarchy in a tight-rate environment is: **(1) Shore up balance sheet, (2) Preserve capital, (3) Raise dividend, (4) Do M&A, (5) Discretionary IT spend.**
   - Software modernization gets deprioritized. Banks will defer platform migrations and operate legacy systems longer if it means preserving capital ratios.

2. **Loan Demand Destruction Reduces Bank Urgency:**
   - Higher rates kill origination volumes. Fewer mortgages, fewer commercial loans, fewer consumer installments.
   - With lower transaction volumes, banks don't need new payment processing throughput. They don't need digital banking upgrades to compete on market share—there's less market to fight for.
   - JKHY's payment systems business is *directly tied to transaction volumes*. If banks are originating 15-20% fewer loans, JKHY's growth stalls.

3. **The Historical Pattern Contradicts the Bull:**
   - In 2022-2023, when the Fed rapidly raised rates and banks were dealing with deposit crises (SVB, Signature, etc.), did bank IT spending spike? **No.** Regional banks cut capex and hunted for cost savings.
   - JKHY itself has *underperformed the S&P 500 over the past 12 months* while rates remained elevated. That's not coincidence—it's market pricing in slower bank tech spending.

**The Kicker:** The bull argues that "higher rates = banks remain profitable." True, but profitability ≠ discretionary IT spend. A profitable bank under rate pressure still cuts capex to preserve equity capital. JKHY is competing for a *declining* IT budget, not an expanding one.

**The Bottom Line:** JKHY is priced for 9-10% growth and margin expansion. The data shows 6-7% growth and *stable* (not expanding) margins. That's a valuation gap of 15-20% downside.
