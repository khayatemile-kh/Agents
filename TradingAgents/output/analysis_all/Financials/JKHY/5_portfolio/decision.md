# FINAL TRADING DECISION: JKHY

## Synthesis of the Debate

Three analysts weighed in, and the substantive disagreement boils down to one question: **is the Q1 FY26 8.7% growth print a genuine inflection or noise against a 6.6%→7.2% trend?** The neutral analyst delivered the decisive fact that both the aggressive and conservative analysts missed or underweighted: **Q1 FY2025 also grew 8.7%.** This is not year-over-year acceleration — it's a seasonally strong quarter matching its own prior-year comp. That single data point deflates the core pillar of the aggressive case ("the new baseline") far more effectively than macro speculation about bank IT budgets, which remains unconfirmed by any hard commentary from JKHY itself in this record.

On valuation, the conservative analyst's reframing holds up: 24x TTM P/E, ~21x forward P/E, and 28.6x FCF multiple against a demonstrated 6.6-7.2% two-year growth trend is not "fair value" — it's pricing in acceleration that hasn't been confirmed. The aggressive analyst's FCF yield argument (4.2%) is mathematically accurate but doesn't resolve the fact that we'd be paying a growth premium for growth that is, at best, unconfirmed and at worst a one-quarter anomaly.

The margin data cited by the conservative analyst (43.3%→42.9%→42.9%→42.8%) is real but the neutral analyst is right that one quarter of 50bps compression isn't yet a structural trend — it's a flag to monitor, not a standalone sell trigger.

On technicals, the neutral analyst correctly punctures both sides' overreach: the aggressive analyst's "declining volume = bullish consolidation" and the conservative analyst's "declining volume = distribution" are both unfalsifiable readings of the same ambiguous data. Neither should drive the sizing decision on its own. What is not ambiguous: the stock is up 15.8% off August lows, sitting well above both 50-day and 200-day SMAs, with RSI at 62 — elevated momentum that has already priced in the higher-for-longer rate narrative the aggressive analyst wants to treat as a fresh catalyst.

## Decision

The aggressive case requires believing in an acceleration story built on a single quarter that merely matched its own prior-year comp — that's not evidence, that's hope. The conservative case is better grounded in the data actually presented (valuation multiples, margin trend, competitive positioning unaddressed by bulls) but risks being too absolute in treating one quarter of margin softness as confirmed deterioration. The neutral analyst's scenario-weighting is a useful discipline check, but scenario-planning doesn't change the fact that current price already reflects the optimistic macro case while offering no confirmed fundamental basis for extending exposure.

**This is not a broken business.** Recurring revenue (80%), consistent EBITDA margins (~33%), and no evidence of customer attrition all argue against a full exit. But the risk/reward at $168.10 — after a 15.8% rally, at 24x earnings, with growth trend still ambiguous (not confirmed accelerating) and margin softness emerging (not yet confirmed structural) — does not support holding full size, let alone adding.

The research plan's original framing (Underweight, trim into strength) survives the debate essentially intact. The neutral analyst's contribution is refining *how* to trim, not whether to trim.

## Rating: **Underweight**

## Trading Action

Execute over 2-3 trading sessions rather than a single print, taking advantage of any intraday weakness to improve average exit price — this addresses the neutral analyst's execution critique without abandoning the core rationale.

**Retain the residual 75-80% position** — full exit is not supported by the evidence. No confirmed customer defections, no negative catalyst, recurring revenue base intact. This is a valuation/momentum trim, not a fundamental sell.

**Monitor specifically for:** (a) Q2/Q3 comps that properly account for the Q1 FY25 base effect the neutral analyst identified, (b) any margin trend continuation below 42.8%, (c) competitive win/loss commentary on Temenos/Finastra displacement — none of which bulls addressed in this debate.

FINAL TRANSACTION PROPOSAL: **SELL (trim 20-25%, retain residual position)**
