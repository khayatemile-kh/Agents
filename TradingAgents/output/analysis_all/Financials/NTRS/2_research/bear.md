## PART 2: THE MACROECONOMIC BACKDROP IS ACTUALLY A RISK, NOT A TAILWIND

### The Bull Got the Rate Environment Backwards

My colleague argues: "89% probability of zero rate cuts means NIM protection and earnings stability."

**This is backwards.**

Here's the macro reality:

**1. Higher Rates = Bond Portfolio Losses (Unrealized Pressure)**

NTRS holds **$61.62B in investments**, including:
- **$16.6B in held-to-maturity (HTM) securities** – These are marked at book value, masking real economic losses
- **$34.8B in available-for-sale (AFS) securities** – Already mark-to-market, showing realized losses

When rates stay elevated (the 89% scenario), the unrealized losses on HTM securities grow. If NTRS needs to raise capital or if regulators force mark-to-market accounting, those losses become real.

**2. Client Asset Valuations Fall, Reducing AUM Fees**

My bull colleague says: "57% probability of S&P 500 outperformance and equities will offset bond weakness."

**But here's the problem:** Even if equities outperform bonds, bond portfolios—which represent a massive portion of NTRS's AUM—decline in value in a high-rate environment.

**NTRS's AUM breakdown (approximate):**
- Equities: ~40-45% of AUM (~$600-670B)
- Fixed Income: ~30-35% of AUM (~$450-520B)
- Alternatives: ~20-25% of AUM (~$300-370B)

If the fixed-income portion (30-35% of AUM) declines in value due to higher rates, **NTRS loses basis for fee generation immediately**, regardless of equity performance. You don't need both to fall for NTRS to suffer—one falling is enough.

**3. The 8% Recession Probability Is Too Optimistic**

My colleague cites "only 8% recession probability by end-2026" as proof of a benign macro backdrop.

**I see it differently:** An 8% recession probability means there's still an 8% chance of a severe AUM contraction, credit losses, and asset-quality deterioration. In financial services, tail risks matter. 

And more importantly: **Prediction markets are notoriously overconfident in the "no recession" scenario.** We're 9 months into 2026. Credit card delinquencies, loan loss provisions, and commercial real estate stress could emerge rapidly if economic data disappoints. A single quarter of poor earnings could flip the recession odds to 20-30%.

**For a levered asset manager, a 8% recession tail risk is material—not immaterial.**
