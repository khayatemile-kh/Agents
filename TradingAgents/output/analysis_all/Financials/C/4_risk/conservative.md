Citigroup's stock declined 85% from peak. The bank was on the verge of government nationalization. That's not a victory narrative; that's a catastrophe that happened to be survived through taxpayer capital injection.

1. **Tighten credit standards**: Reducing C's ability to deploy capital profitably
2. **Compete aggressively on deposit rates**: Pushing Citigroup's funding costs higher (which we're already seeing)
3. **Create counterparty risk concerns**: If a mid-sized or regional bank fails, it may have significant exposure to derivatives, swaps, or other counterparty arrangements with larger banks like C. This creates immediate P&L hits and potential capital requirement impacts.

Citigroup has $402.7 billion in total debt outstanding. Its leverage ratio is elevated for a bank. In a stress scenario where funding markets seize (as happened in 2008), refinancing that debt becomes extremely difficult and expensive. This isn't a competitive advantage scenario; it's a regulatory haircut scenario.
