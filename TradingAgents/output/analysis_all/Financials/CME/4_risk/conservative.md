That $40+ move happened *in anticipation* of rising volatility, elevated Fed uncertainty, and increased derivatives demand. Traders were already pricing in the possibility of a hawkish Fed pivot weeks before Warsh actually delivered it on August 28.

Then, when Warsh *actually* spoke, what happened? The stock rallied 2.7% more. That's the market confirming the expectation, not discovering a new catalyst. The aggressive analyst is confusing "confirmation of an already-priced thesis" with "the beginning of a new multi-quarter uptrend." These are not the same thing.

The research plan's "Underweight" rating wasn't based on pre-August 28th data exclusively—it was based on the recognition that front-running risk was elevated. And the data confirms this: a 20.6% rally in one month, with RSI at 72.23 and price clustering at the upper Bollinger Band, are *precisely* the conditions that emerge when markets have already substantially priced in a thesis. The Warsh speech didn't create a new catalyst; it confirmed an existing one that had already been reflected in the price.

Now, here's the real risk: markets don't wait patiently for catalysts to "fully play out" before correcting. They anticipate, they price in expectations, and then they reverse when actual results either confirm or disappoint those expectations. We're now at a stage where the consensus has fully adopted the "higher rates, more volatility, CME benefits" narrative. That's exactly when surprises hurt the most.
