But here's what the Aggressive Analyst is skating over: **valuation attractiveness and downside safety are not the same thing.** A stock can be cheap and get cheaper. A lot cheaper. That's called a value trap, and regional banks have been the poster child for value traps since 2008.

Let me point to the macro data they're conveniently minimizing. The prediction market is pricing a **64% probability of a US bank failure by year-end 2026**. Let me underline that: not a 10% tail risk. Not a 20% possibility. **Sixty-four percent.** And that number *surged 25 percentage points in a single week*, according to the world affairs report.

Now, the Aggressive Analyst says, "CFG is systemically important, so it won't fail." Okay—but systemic importance doesn't make you immune to mark-to-market losses, deposit flight, or forced capital raises. Let me ask them directly: **If systemic banking stress materializes, do you really think CFG's stock holds at 68? Or do you think it reprices down alongside its peers in a crisis contagion scenario?**

This is the critical blind spot in their argument. If deposit flows weaken, if loan growth slows, if charge-offs tick up (which they likely will as credit normalization continues post-pandemic), then $1.40 becomes optimistic, not conservative.

The Aggressive Analyst is essentially betting on a *best-case* scenario—beats, acceleration, multiple re-rating—materializing in the next six weeks. That's not investing. That's hoping.

They say, "That risk is priced in already." 

Is it? Let me check CFG's current valuation relative to its risk profile. The stock is at 68.16, trading at 10.7x forward P/E. (A) Ignoring systemic bank failure risk (in which case it's mispriced on the upside, and CFG should go higher), or
