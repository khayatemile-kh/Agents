**Rationale**: Weighing both sides, I don't think either analyst has a knockout argument, so this lands as a genuine Hold rather than a disguised Buy or Sell.

The bull's strongest points: CFG is genuinely cheap on traditional metrics (10.7x forward P/E, 1.23x P/B), the Q2 print was strong (34.6% YoY net income growth, 13.5% NII growth, positive operating leverage with only 3% expense growth vs 12% revenue growth), the balance sheet is fortress-like (51% YoY cash growth, $26.2B equity), capital return is real ($225M buybacks, 2.7% yield), and the wealth management push into higher-margin fee income is a legitimate strategic diversification away from pure NII sensitivity. The 42.2% forward EPS growth number doesn't reconcile well with the quarterly trend — going from 15.5% Q1-to-Q2 growth to needing a 28% Q3-to-Q4 sequential jump to hit the $6.52 full-year estimate is a legitimate math flag, not just bearish spin. On technicals, the bear's read is fair: price is below the 50-SMA, MACD is negative and not turning, and "oversold" in a confirmed downtrend is not automatically a reversal signal — it can just as easily mean capitulation isn't finished. The 64% bank-failure prediction market probability is a systemic overhang that, whether or not CFG specifically is at risk, is clearly correlated with the recent multi-day selloff and could keep multiples compressed regardless of CFG's own fundamentals.

Net-net: the fundamental business (NII growth, balance sheet strength, buybacks) supports not exiting the position, but the near-term technical damage, systemic bank-sector risk pricing, and the unresolved credibility gap in the EPS growth estimate argue against adding aggressively here. This is a case where both theses have real teeth and the prudent call is to hold the position, let Q3 earnings on 10/16 serve as the tie-breaker, rather than commit fresh capital or reduce exposure on unresolved uncertainty.
