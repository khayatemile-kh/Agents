### **Where Is This Growth Actually Coming From?**

The bull says: *"Higher rates = better NII on $362B investment portfolio."*

Let me fact-check this claim with real insurance math:

**Current State (Q2 2026):**
- Investment portfolio: $362.76B
- Average portfolio yield: ~3.2% (typical for mixed bond/equity insurance portfolio)
- Gross investment income: ~$11.6B annually
- Operating expenses: ~$1.69B (Q2 run-rate × 4 = $6.76B)

**Bull's "Rate Expansion" Scenario:**
- Rates stay at 4.25-4.50% (vs. current ~3.2%)
- Yield improves by 115 basis points to ~4.35%
- Additional NII: $362.76B × 1.15% = **$4.17B**

**Here's where this breaks down:**
1. **Not all $4.17B is profit.** Insurance companies have:
   - Claims costs on annuity products that *increase* with higher rates (duration liability revaluation)
   - Hedge costs to lock in yields
   - Realized losses on existing bond portfolio (marked-to-market accounting)
   - Regulatory capital requirements that tighten as rates rise

2. **The bull cherry-picked the interest rate benefit but ignored the corresponding liabilities.** When rates rise:
   - Present value of long-duration insurance liabilities *decreases* (good for balance sheet)
   - But *current period* earnings face headwinds from annuity surrender value adjustments
   - Net realized gains/losses on portfolio fluctuate wildly

3. **Historical precedent is damning:** From 2021-2023, when rates rose from 1% to 4.25%, did MetLife's earnings double? **No.** TTM EPS moved from ~$4.00 (2021) to $5.33 (2026)—a 33% increase over *five years*, not the 105% the bulls project in *12 months.*
