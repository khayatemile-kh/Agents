**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: AJG has strong multi-year revenue/EPS CAGR, heavy reliance on debt-funded M&A, and a very messy Q2 2026 print (OCF of only $10M vs $957M in Q1, YoY EPS/net income/margin declines vs Q2 2025). Where they diverge is interpretation. The bull says Q2 weakness is seasonal noise and working-capital timing; the bear counters convincingly that the YoY comparison (Q2'26 vs Q2'25, not just sequential Q1→Q2) shows real deterioration — EPS down 10.7%, net income down 12.1%, margin compressed 330bps — which a pure seasonality argument can't explain away, since seasonality should show up similarly each year, not worsen YoY. The bear's point on cash-flow conversion (OCF/NI collapsing from 116% to 3%) plus AR increasing while revenue fell quarter-over-quarter is a legitimate red flag on collections/working capital, not just timing, and the bull didn't fully rebut the YoY comparison, only the sequential one.

On leverage, the bull's reframe to Net Debt/EBITDA (~5.5-6x) versus peers (4-5.5x) is fair context, but it still concedes AJG is more leveraged than peers, with negative tangible equity, and cites 3.3-3.7x interest coverage — leaving relatively little cushion if cash flow deteriorates further, which is exactly what Q2 showed. So the leverage risk is real and amplified by the cash flow problem: less FCF cushion to service $14B+ debt and fund the M&A machine that's the entire bull thesis engine.

Net assessment: the bull's growth and leverage-is-strategic arguments have merit as a long-term structural story, but the bear's rebuttal on the Q2 YoY earnings decline and cash conversion collapse is the stronger, better-evidenced point for the near-to-medium term. This isn't a slam-dunk sell — AJG remains a scaled, serial-acquirer franchise with real long-term growth and manageable (if elevated) leverage — but committing to full Buy conviction given the credible signs of earnings/cash-flow deterioration and stretched balance sheet would be premature. The prudent stance is to trim/de-risk exposure until the OCF and margin trend either confirm reversion (supporting bull) or confirm a structural slowdown (supporting bear). That points to Underweight rather than Hold or Buy.
