## The Valuation "Mirage" Is Actually Risk Asymmetry In Our Favor

You said that 6.42x forward PE is a "screaming sell signal." I'd argue the opposite—it's evidence that **the market has already priced in the bad news**. Let me explain the mechanical difference, because this is crucial for asset protection.

When a stock trades at 6.42x forward PE, there are only two scenarios:

**Scenario A:** The market is correct, and earnings will continue to deteriorate. The stock will compress further to 5x PE, 4x PE, and eventually we see $35-40 per share. That's certainly possible.

**Scenario B:** The market has overshot to the downside, earnings stabilize at current levels or recover modestly, and the multiple re-rates to a more normal 10-12x range for a payments infrastructure business. That gets us to $55-70 per share in 18-24 months.

Now here's the asymmetry that matters for **capital preservation**: If we sell at $52.93 right now and Scenario B plays out, we lock in a loss and give up 30-40% upside while sleeping well at night. If we hold and Scenario A plays out, we lose another 20-25% and feel vindicated for our pessimism. The risk/reward isn't actually as bad as you've framed it—it's actually inverted from your narrative.

But more importantly, selling into capitulation without a clear alternative deployment plan is itself a form of **unmanaged risk**. We're not talking about deploying proceeds into higher-conviction, lower-leverage opportunities. We're talking about selling at market lows to avoid a further decline. That's not risk management; that's performance chasing with regret.
