## **II. THE INVESTMENT BANKING CYCLE IS PEAKING, NOT ACCELERATING**

My colleague cited Goldman Sachs' 52% IB fee increase as evidence of a multi-year bull market. **I'm reading the same data and drawing the opposite conclusion: this is peak cycle, and reversion is coming.**

### **Historical Pattern Recognition: Cycles Peak When Growth is Highest**

Goldman's 52% fee growth is *historically* a peak-cycle indicator, not an early-cycle signal. Here's why:

- **Early cycle:** Backlog builds, fees accelerate from a low base (30-50% growth)
- **Mid cycle:** Growth moderates as backlog depletes but new deal flow replaces it (10-20% growth)
- **Peak cycle:** Desperate growth numbers as firms and PE funds rush to close deals before rates rise or deals disappear (50%+ growth, then a cliff)
- **Downside:** Deal velocity collapses by 40-60% as credit spreads widen and caution returns

**We're seeing peak-cycle desperation numbers, not mid-cycle health.** Once you see 50%+ growth, the next print typically shows a meaningful deceleration or outright decline.

### **The September Fed Decision is More Hawkish Than the Bull Acknowledges**

My colleague brushed off the "50% probability of a +25 bps hike" with: "One hike won't derail the cycle."

**That's naive.** Here's what's actually happening:

**The market is pricing 50% of a +25 bps hike in September because the Fed *wants* to signal ongoing tightness.** The labor market is still tight (unemployment low), inflation stickier than expected, and debt concerns are rising. This isn't a one-off—this is **a signal that the Fed is pausing its easing cycle longer than markets hoped.**

What does this mean for deals?

1. **Debt financing gets more expensive:** LBO spreads widen as rates rise
2. **Strategic buyers get more conservative:** With higher cost of capital, M&A premiums compress
3. **Backlog converts more slowly:** Companies delay acquisitions when financing gets tougher
4. **Fee pools shrink:** Smaller deals = smaller fees, even if deal count stays flat

The bull is assuming the IB backlog converts linearly. **It doesn't.** When rates rise, backlogs clear more slowly, and newly initiated deals decline sharply.

### **Private Equity is Slowing, Not Accelerating**

My colleague mentioned "PE funds with dry powder will accelerate deployments." Let me provide context: 

**PE deployment actually peaked in 2021-2022.** Since then, PE firms have been *cautious* about deployment despite dry powder because:

1. **Valuations are elevated** relative to 2020 entry points
2. **Exit multiples are compressed** due to higher rates
3. **PE firms are cautious about rate sensitivity** in their portfolio companies

Yes, PE will be active, but the days of explosive LBO financing are behind us for this cycle. **Morgan Stanley's IB fees are *already* at a cyclical peak**, and the conversion rates are slowing.
