**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree FIS looks statistically cheap (6.28x P/E, 0.21 PEG), but the bear case does a better job explaining WHY it's cheap and identifies risks the bull never adequately rebuts. Three things tip the scale: (1) Earnings/FCF quality - the bull leans on TTM net income and cumulative FCF, but the quarter-by-quarter trend is ugly: net income collapsed from $2.366B in Q1 to $231M in Q2, and FCF fell from $642M (Q4'25) to $452M (Q1'26) to $238M (Q2'26), a ~63% decline in two quarters. That trend, not the trailing-twelve-month average, is what the market is pricing. (2) Balance sheet fragility - negative retained earnings of -$20.58B, negative tangible book value of -$18.54B, and a current ratio of 0.535 mean FIS has essentially no cushion; it's dependent on continuous refinancing of $4.23B in commercial paper and on FCF that is currently shrinking, not growing. The bull's 'debt-to-EBITDA is fine for infrastructure companies' argument ignores that FCF net of interest leaves very little for actual deleveraging (bear's back-of-envelope ~$150M/yr against $21B+ of debt). (3) Growth quality - revenue growth, while headline-strong at 29% YoY, is decelerating (29%→26%→21% sequentially) and likely inflated by Worldpay-related M&A/organic mix rather than pure organic strength; the bull never disaggregates this. The Ericsson partnership is a legitimate optionality item but is unproven, announced defensively alongside the Wells Fargo downgrade, and shouldn't be underwritten as a near-term earnings driver. Net: the valuation discount is not obviously a market overreaction — it appears to be a rational discount for declining earnings quality, shrinking FCF, and a stressed balance sheet. That said, the business does still generate real cash and has a genuine (if early) growth optionality lever in Ericsson, so this isn't a full conviction Sell — it's a name to reduce exposure to and monitor closely rather than aggressively short or accumulate.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing FIS exposure by roughly 25-35% of the position now rather than adding on 'cheap valuation' alone; do not treat the low P/E/PEG as an entry signal until FCF and net income trends stabilize for at least two consecutive quarters. 2) Set a close watch on Q3 2026 FCF print - if it falls below ~$150-200M (continuing the decline trend), reduce further; if it stabilizes/reverses upward, that would be the trigger to reconsider adding back. 3) Monitor the current ratio and commercial paper rollover activity each quarter as a liquidity stress indicator - any signs of difficulty refinancing the $4.23B CP book or covenant pressure should prompt further de-risking. 4) Keep a small residual position (do not go to zero) to retain optionality on the Ericsson wallet partnership and potential organic-growth re-acceleration, but size it so it's a tracking position rather than a core holding. 5) Use any near-term bounce in the stock (e.g., on positive Ericsson updates or short-covering) as a better level to execute the trim rather than selling into further weakness. 6) Avoid adding leverage-related derivatives or options exposure given the balance sheet fragility - keep this as a straightforward equity underweight.
