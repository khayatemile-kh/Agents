## THE DEBT-TO-EQUITY "RATIO" IS A DELIBERATELY DISTORTED METRIC

**This ratio is mathematically meaningless for FIS, and any financial analyst knows it.**

Here's why: FIS has $34.58 billion in goodwill and intangible assets ($34.58B / $44.11B total assets = 78.4%). This is typical for a financial services technology company that has made strategic acquisitions. When you acquire a payments processor or financial software firm, you're paying for customer relationships, brand value, and recurring revenue streams—all of which show up as goodwill on the balance sheet.

The aggressive analyst is treating this goodwill as if it's phantom value. It isn't. It represents real, recurring cash-generating businesses embedded within FIS's operating structure. More importantly, the debt-to-equity ratio is a terrible metric for assessing refinancing or solvency risk when you should be looking at **debt-to-EBITDA** instead.

FIS's EBITDA (TTM) is $3.52 billion. Total debt is $21.26 billion. That's a debt-to-EBITDA ratio of **6.04x**.

If a company generates $3.52B in EBITDA annually, returns 60-70% to shareholders via dividends and buybacks, and plows the remainder into growth investments, retained earnings will be negative.
