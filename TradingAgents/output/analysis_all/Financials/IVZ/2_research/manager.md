**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points, but when I weigh them the bear case is more disciplined about what the data actually supports, while the bull case leans heavily on extrapolating a single strong quarter and hand-waving balance sheet risk.

Where the bull is strongest: the Q2 2026 numbers are genuinely good (20.5% YoY revenue growth, 70% YoY operating income growth, 42.5% QoQ net income growth), and it's fair to say the Q4 2025 impairment was a non-cash, non-recurring charge that shouldn't be permanently baked into a 'the business is broken' narrative. The Solutions & Custom Strategies platform and rising AUM/flows are real, verifiable positives that support a case that operating momentum has improved sequentially.

Where the bear lands the more important blows: (1) the annualized run-rate from the actual Q2 2026 result ($0.76 x 4 = $3.04) is barely below the forward EPS estimate of $3.26 the bull uses to justify the 'cheap' 9.73x forward P/E — implying the market isn't pricing miraculous growth, it's pricing roughly flat-to-modest growth, which undercuts the 'screaming bargain' framing. (2) The 12.06x debt-to-equity ratio and negative tangible book value are structural realities that the bull dismisses too quickly by analogy to other asset managers, without demonstrating IVZ's specific coverage cushion is safe in a downturn. (3) The bear's point that revenue/AUM growth in asset management is heavily beta-driven to market cycles, and that operating leverage cuts both ways (margins expand in the up-cycle, contract just as fast when flows or markets reverse) is a valid and underappreciated risk that the bull doesn't rebut with balance-sheet-specific stress testing. (4) The bear correctly notes that if the bull's normalized-earnings thesis were obviously correct, the multiple should already be re-rating toward historical/peer averages (12-15x) — the fact that it hasn't done so despite strong recent prints is a signal that the market is discounting the durability of this earnings run, not just being irrationally cheap.

Net conclusion: there is real underlying business improvement and the deep discount valuation gives some downside cushion, but the balance sheet leverage (debt/equity ~12x, negative tangible book value, ~5.3x net debt/EBITDA) combined with the cyclicality of asset management earnings and the fact that forward estimates imply only modest growth over the current run-rate makes this too risky to call an outright Buy. This isn't a balanced 50/50 debate, though — the bear's structural leverage and cyclicality concerns are concrete and quantifiable, while the bull's rebuttal of the balance sheet ("asset managers don't need book value") is more assertion than analysis. That tips me toward a cautious stance rather than a true Hold.
