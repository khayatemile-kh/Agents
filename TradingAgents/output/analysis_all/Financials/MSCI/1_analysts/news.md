## **SECTION I: MSCI-SPECIFIC DEVELOPMENTS**

### **A. Critical Corporate Governance Issue: Strategy Inc. Index Delisting Dispute**

**Issue Overview:**
Michael Saylor's Strategy Inc. has launched a forceful public campaign against MSCI's proposed new delisting criteria that would remove Strategy from MSCI's global indexes in November 2026. This represents a significant reputational and operational challenge for MSCI.

**Key Details:**
- **Magnitude:** Strategy Inc. would be "the largest by some distance" among three companies affected by the proposed screen
- **Timeline:** Implementation scheduled for November 2026
- **Strategy's Response:** Called the proposal "fundamentally flawed" and claims it damages MSCI's reputation
- **Demand:** Strategy has requested MSCI either withdraw the proposal or apply changes only prospectively going forward

**Investment Implications:**
- **For MSCI:** This dispute creates reputational risk and potential legal/client relations challenges. If MSCI proceeds, it invites further criticism; if it backs down, it undermines index integrity and governance credibility.
- **Revenue Risk:** Potential loss of assets from Strategy-focused clients or index trackers if delisting occurs
- **Operational Risk:** Ongoing media and political pressure could distract management and create client uncertainty regarding index methodology stability

### **B. Rising Operating Expense Guidance**

**Key Issue:**
MSCI raised its 2026 operating expense forecast earlier in the year, citing:
- Acquisition costs
- Higher employee incentives
- Stepped-up investment spending

**Analyst Commentary:**
Simply Wall St. reports this elevated cost outlook "has prompted fresh questions about near-term profitability" despite positive long-term prospects.

**Investment Implications:**
- **Margin Compression Risk:** Near-term earnings per share could face headwinds despite revenue growth
- **Strategic Intent:** Investment spending suggests MSCI is positioning for growth in data/analytics, likely in ESG, AI, or climate risk areas
- **Profitability Timeline:** Recovery contingent on revenue offsetting cost increases within 1-2 years

### **C. Emerging Markets Currency Divergence**

**Recent News Development:**

**Market Mechanics:**
- Traditionally, higher Treasury yields drive dollar strength and weaken EM currencies
- Current environment: Higher yields are not translating to proportional dollar strength

**Implication for MSCI:**

- **Currency Hedging Products:** May create demand for MSCI's currency indexing and hedging analytics
- **Diversification Trend:** Supports thesis that global investors are rebalancing toward EM, benefiting MSCI's geographic breadth
