But here's where I push back on the Aggressive Analyst: a margin compression observed in two quarters doesn't automatically mean further compression is inevitable. Asset management firms are cyclical businesses. Margin compression can be temporary, tied to specific transaction or gains cycles. We need more data to determine if this is structural or cyclical.

## The valuation question reveals why both analysts are talking past each other

The Aggressive Analyst keeps pointing to the 63.62x TTM P/E as unconscionable. The Conservative Analyst responds that this is meaningless because Q3 2025 was an exceptional quarter and we should use forward P/E instead. They're both right, and they're both missing the actual problem.

Here's what the 63.62x TTM P/E actually tells us: earnings are compressed relative to the stock price *right now*. Whether that's because Q3 was abnormally good or Q4 was abnormally weak is less important than the fact that the market is pricing in significant earnings recovery. The forward P/E of 19.2x is only reasonable if that recovery actually materializes. But here's the critical question neither analyst is asking: what's the probability that forward earnings actually reach the consensus estimates?

Given that we've just seen revenue decline 13.8% from peak and margins compress 8+ percentage points, the forward earnings estimates might themselves be at risk. If forward earnings prove to be 15-20% lower than consensus, then a 19.2x forward P/E becomes a 22-24x multiple on actual realized earnings, which is materially less attractive. That's not speculation—that's the basic math of what happens when estimates disappoint.

The Conservative Analyst argues that a 19.2x forward P/E is "reasonable" for a mature asset manager with $671B in AUM. I'd say that's only true if two conditions hold: (1) that AUM remains stable or grows, and (2) that margins stabilize at current levels. But the recent data suggests both of these conditions are in question. The Conservative Analyst is assuming away uncertainty rather than pricing it in.
