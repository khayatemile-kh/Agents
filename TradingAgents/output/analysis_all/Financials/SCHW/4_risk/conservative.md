First, context: a 26% rally in 90 days is substantial, and some normalization in momentum indicators is not just expected—it's *healthy*. The MACD rolling over after a sharp 26% surge isn't a red flag; it's textbook mean-reversion behavior in overbought conditions. The fact that the RSI crashed from 73.67 to 51.68 shows that the market itself is doing the work of cooling down the excess. This is *exactly what you want to see* in a healthy correction within an uptrend.

But here's the critical part the aggressive case glosses over: **a MACD histogram turning negative doesn't mean the stock drops precipitously.** Let me challenge the premise directly. We're on September 1. The MACD histogram is at -0.66. The stock is at $108.28. If the aggressive thesis were correct—if this momentum deterioration truly signaled imminent downside—where's the price action? The stock has pulled back 4.7% from the August 24 high. That's a reasonable correction, not a capitulation.

More importantly, **MACD deterioration is most predictive when it's paired with price deterioration**. We don't have that yet. Price is still above the 50-SMA (103.41) by 4.87 points. Price is still above the 200-SMA (96.56) by 11.72 points. The long-term structure is *intact*. A widening negative MACD histogram in an otherwise healthy uptrend often signals consolidation, not reversal.

If MACD deterioration were truly predictive of downside, we'd expect to see technical confirmation: price breaking below the 50-SMA, then the 200-SMA, volume surges, breadth deterioration, etc. We're not seeing that. We're seeing a pullback within a longer-term uptrend. Those are fundamentally different scenarios.

The aggressive analyst makes much of the 10-EMA sitting at 109.78 while price is at 108.28. They frame this as "the bullish structure breaking." I'd argue this framing is imprecise and potentially misleading to risk management.

Here's the reality: the 10-EMA is a very short-term indicator designed to capture intraday and day-to-day momentum. A single crossover below it—especially when price is still 11.72 points above the 200-SMA and 4.87 points above the 50-SMA—tells us almost nothing about intermediate-term direction. It's noise in a larger picture.

Moreover, and this is important: **the Bollinger Bands technical report explicitly notes that price sitting between the middle band and lower band is normal consolidation behavior, not a reversal signal.** Price at 108.28 is 0.78 points below the middle band at 109.50 but 2.88 points above the lower band at 105.40. The aggressive case wants to treat every technical crossover as a death knell. But professional risk management requires looking at the *constellation* of signals, not isolated indicators. And that constellation—the price relative to all three moving averages, the band structure, the lack of volume confirmation—tells a story of healthy consolidation, not breakdown.

That's precisely backwards from a conservative risk management perspective. An RSI at neutral (51.68) after collapsing from overbought (73.67) is *exactly where you want to be* if you're holding a fundamentally sound position in an uptrend. Here's why:

**The RSI normalization is evidence that the correction is working properly.** When a stock rallies 26% in 90 days and gets overbought, RSI needs to cool down. The fact that it crashed to 51.68 without breaking into oversold (<30) tells us the correction is proportionate and healthy. If RSI were to bounce now into the 55-65 range, that would signal renewed strength *without* the overbought extreme. That's the textbook setup for continuation of an uptrend.

The aggressive case seems to be interpreting "RSI at neutral" as "no conviction." But that's a misreading. RSI at neutral isn't a warning—it's a **reset button**. Compare this to a scenario where RSI would have broken into oversold (<30) and then bounced back above neutral. That would suggest capitulation-driven selling followed by reversal—a more dangerous setup. We don't have that. We have orderly deterioration followed by stabilization. That's the conservative outcome, not a mediocre one.
