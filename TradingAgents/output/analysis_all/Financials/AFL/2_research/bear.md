## **THE CORE PROBLEM: YOU'RE EXTRAPOLATING FROM TEMPORARY TAILWINDS, NOT DURABLE BUSINESS STRENGTH**

### **PILLAR 1 REBUTTAL: THE MACRO ARGUMENT IS A HOUSE OF CARDS**

Your first pillar rests on the premise that "elevated interest rates persist" and "recession risk is negligible." But let me challenge this with hard data:

**The Rate Persistence Assumption is Fragile**

You cite the prediction markets showing only 1% probability of rate cuts by September 2026 and 10% by December 2026. But here's what you're not accounting for:

1. **These probabilities shift *rapidly* in response to economic data.** We're currently in early September 2026. If economic growth slows unexpectedly, or unemployment ticks up, those probabilities collapse within weeks. Insurance companies that have based their 2026-2027 earnings guidance on a "rates stay high indefinitely" scenario are suddenly caught flat-footed.

2. **The prediction market data reflects *current expectations*, not business reality.** Even if rates don't *formally* cut, **higher unemployment or weaker lending demand effectively *lowers the forward yield curve*—reducing reinvestment rates on maturing securities.** Aflac's portfolio will face a wall of reinvestment at lower rates starting in 2027, and that headwind is being masked right now by the high-yielding portfolio they're holding.

3. **The recession probability of 8% is *not* "negligible" for an insurance company.** Insurance is counterintuitively *pro-cyclical on the downside*. When recessions hit, three things happen:
   - **Lapse rates spike** as unemployed workers drop supplemental coverage
   - **Claims frequency increases** (accident rates rise, disability claims climb)
   - **Premium growth stalls** as employers freeze benefit expansion
   
   An 8% recession probability translates to roughly a 1-in-12 chance of a severe earnings shock. That's not nothing.

**Your Net Investment Income Math Ignores Portfolio Maturity Risk**

You cite Aflac's $115.9B investment portfolio with "65% held in liquid, high-yielding instruments" as a source of perpetual margin expansion. But here's the reality:

- **Average portfolio yield is currently elevated, but not because of new purchases—it's because of the *stock* of bonds held from the 2021-2023 tightening cycle.**
- **As these bonds mature, Aflac must reinvest at lower forward rates.** The 10-year Treasury yield is around 3.8-4.0% as of September 2026—lower than what Aflac's portfolio is currently averaging.
- **This represents a *structural headwind* to net investment income that compounds every quarter.** By 2028-2029, when a significant portion of the portfolio has rolled over to lower rates, we're looking at measurable margin compression from this effect alone.

You're essentially betting on rate persistence to mask what will ultimately be a portfolio yield normalization—and the market will price this in well before it actually occurs.

### **PILLAR 2 REBUTTAL: THE "EARNINGS RECOVERY" NARRATIVE OBSCURES A DEEPER DETERIORATION**

Now, let's examine your claim that the 2025 earnings decline was "cyclical, not structural." I have to respectfully say this is where your bull case falls apart.

#### **The 32.9% Net Income Decline Is NOT Explained by "One-Time Items"**

You handwave the 2024-to-2025 earnings collapse ($5.443B to $3.646B) as being driven by "one-time charges" and "claims normalization." But the data doesn't support this:

1. **Insurance companies are required to disclose one-time items and significant reserve adjustments.** If this was truly a one-time charge, we would see it explicitly called out in earnings reports. The fact that management hasn't highlighted a specific event or reserve revision suggests the deterioration is *core business degradation*, not a discrete charge.

2. **The margin compression is the smoking gun.** You note profit margins fell from 28.4% (2024) to 21.0% (2025). Let me be explicit about what this means:
   - **2024 margin:** 28.4% on $19.128B revenue = $5.43B profit
   - **2025 margin:** 21.0% on $17.355B revenue = $3.64B profit
   
   This is a *dual deterioration*: revenue DOWN 9.3% AND margins DOWN 7.4 percentage points. This isn't a temporary claims bump—this is indicative of **structural competitive pressure, pricing weakness, or underwriting deterioration.**

3. **The Q2 2026 "recovery" is misleading.** You cite Q2 2026 EPS of $1.63 vs. Q2 2025 of $1.11 as "+46.8% improvement." But let's contextualize this:
   - Q2 2025 was likely depressed due to the 2025 full-year challenges
   - Q2 2026 annualizes to ~$6.52 EPS (assuming all quarters similar), which is *still below* the 2024 full-year EPS of $9.63
   - **H1 2026 ($3.61 annualized) is notably behind H1 2025 run rates**
   
   This isn't a "recovery to health"—it's recovery from a depressed baseline. **We're still ~32% below 2024 earnings power.**

#### **The Real Question: What Caused the 2025 Collapse?**

Here's what the data suggests happened:

- **U.S. supplemental insurance market is maturing and facing increased competition.** Employers are consolidating benefits vendors and pushing for lower premiums. Aflac's pricing power is eroding.
- **Japan market is facing secular decline.** Japan's aging population and lower labor-force growth mean fewer new employees to enroll. Aflac's Japan business is mature but declining.
- **Claims experience deteriorated in 2025.** Either actual claims were worse than expected (indicating underwriting issues), or management was forced to increase reserve adequacy (suggesting previous underestimation of liabilities).

**None of these issues are cyclical. All of them are structural.**

#### **The Valuation "Discount" is a Mirage**

You argue that the 12.5x trailing P/E represents a discount to financial services peers at 16-18x. But here's the issue: **you're comparing Aflac to the wrong cohort and using the wrong baseline.**

1. **Financial services peer comparison is apples-to-oranges.** Banks trade at 12-14x because they're facing deposit pressure and margin compression. Aflac *also* trades at 12.5x because investors have repriced it for *structural earnings headwinds*, not opportunity. The multiple is low *for a reason*—it reflects justified skepticism about forward earnings.

2. **The "forward P/E of 15.5x" is actually *more* concerning than reassuring.** Here's why: If you're paying 15.5x next year's estimated earnings, you're pricing in earnings recovery. But the earnings estimates themselves may be *too optimistic* if:
   - Claims experience remains difficult
   - Pricing power stays constrained
   - The portfolio yield continues its inevitable compression
   
   **A higher forward P/E in the context of deteriorating fundamentals is a red flag, not a buying opportunity.**

3. **The 129.5% five-year return is backward-looking and sunk cost.** Past performance is not predictive of future returns. In fact, the very fact that Aflac delivered strong five-year returns *has already priced in investor expectations for continued outperformance*. Now we're facing the reality of a maturing business model, and the market is repricing accordingly. **You're essentially arguing to buy at the inflection point where tailwinds are turning to headwinds.**
