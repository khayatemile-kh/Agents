**Investment Thesis**: The debate converges on a leverage-and-price dispute over the USI deal that the bulls (Conservative) never fully neutralize. Bear/Aggressive case, largely unrebutted on its core quantitative claim: pro-forma net debt/EBITDA rises from a comfortable 2.36x to an estimated 4.1-4.4x — a near-doubling of leverage — layered onto the balance sheet at the exact moment there is an 89% probability of no 2026 rate cuts, meaning the ~$13-14B of new acquisition debt gets financed at elevated rates (Aggressive estimates $585-700M of incremental annual interest expense, 15-18% of current ~$3.9B net income). The 6x sales multiple paid for USI is demonstrably rich versus sector comps (MMC 2.8x, WTW 3.1x, BRO 4.2x) — a 40-50% premium the Conservative analyst rationalizes only via "proven synergy extraction" and margin quality, not through comparable-multiple justification. Buyback suspension removes structural equity demand for years, a point the Neutral analyst concedes is "cleanly right" and under-addressed by the Conservative case.

Countervailing bull evidence has real merit but is tactical, not strategic: Conservative correctly notes USI's ~$3B revenue at 15-20% margins should generate $450-600M of incremental EBITDA that services the new debt with cushion (pro-forma net debt of ~$13.9-14B against ~$6.4B combined EBITDA), and NFP integration, while delayed, ultimately worked. Neutral analyst validates this EBITDA-coverage math but stresses it depends on integration proceeding "normally," which both NFP and JLT precedent (cited by Aggressive, not rebutted) suggest is optimistic — multi-year elevated integration costs, margin compression, and management distraction are the realistic base case for a 10,500-employee, 200-office bolt-on of this scale. The technical setup (RSI 34, price below lower Bollinger band, 4-5x normal volume) is a genuine oversold signal, but as the Neutral analyst notes, the bounce sequence is not mechanically guaranteed (stock could retest $330-335 with lower highs rather than cleanly reach the $336-351 SMA cluster), and even a successful bounce only resolves a tactical timing question, not the 12-24 month structural leverage/integration risk.

Net assessment: this is not a genuinely balanced Hold — the deal announcement is new, quantifiable information (leverage roughly doubling, premium price paid, structural buyer for the stock removed) that skews forward risk/reward negative relative to pre-announcement levels. But the underlying franchise remains intact (22%+ net margins, $3.19B FCF, investment-grade, recurring fee revenue per Conservative's largely unchallenged points), and the stock has already absorbed a real de-rating (-6.6% on announcement, RSI ~30), so a full Sell/exit is not supported by the evidence — that would be treating a re-risked but still profitable platform as a broken business, which none of the three analysts actually argue. Consistent with the Research Manager's plan and the Trader's proposal, a disciplined partial trim into technical strength is the correct sizing decision, with hedges (collars/puts) on the residual position warranted given the elevated-for-longer rate backdrop and headline volatility risk through integration disclosure.
