**KEY's rising NPLs suggest the bank is *already experiencing credit stress* in pockets of its portfolio—particularly in commercial real estate and commercial lending.** The fact that charge-offs are rising *before* a recession hits suggests the underwriting quality has deteriorated, not that risks are minimal.

Commercial real estate, in particular, is showing stress in 2026 as companies return-to-office mandates have stalled and vacancy rates remain elevated in key markets. KEY's commercial real estate exposure is material, and deteriorating metrics suggest they're experiencing mark-to-market losses on their loan book.

## SECTION 3: THE DEBT SPIKE IS NOT "SEASONAL"—IT'S STRUCTURAL

### The Bull's Weak Excuse Doesn't Hold Up

The bull claims the 55% debt increase from Q4 2025 ($10.988B) to Q1 2026 ($17.026B) is "seasonal liquidity management." Let me dismantle this claim:

**The Actual Debt Composition:**

| Debt Category | Q4 2025 | Q1 2026 | Change | % Change |
|---------------|---------|---------|--------|----------|
| **Current Debt** | $1.071B | $6.149B | +$5.078B | **+474%** |
| **Long-Term Debt** | $9.917B | $10.877B | +$960M | +9.7% |
| **Total Debt** | $10.988B | $17.026B | +$6.038B | **+55%** |
| **Debt-to-Equity** | 0.54x | 0.85x | +0.31x | **+57%** |

**Here's the problem with the "seasonal" excuse:**

1. **Current debt skyrocketed 474%.** This is NOT seasonal borrowing—this is structural refinancing or liquidity raising. Seasonal borrowing fluctuates in the 10-20% range, not 400%+.

2. **Debt-to-equity blew up to 0.85x** from 0.54x, a massive one-quarter deterioration. This significantly increases financial leverage and risk exposure.

3. **Why would they borrow heavily in Q1 2026 if operations were generating cash?** The presence of this debt suggests:
   - **Loan growth outpaced deposit growth**, forcing the bank to source funding externally
   - **Asset-liability management pressures**, likely driven by deposit flight or margin compression
   - **Possible need for capital buffer** ahead of stressed loan portfolio realizations

4. **The Q1 2025-to-Q1 2026 comparison is worse:** Q1 2025 total debt was $14.720B, meaning the company has *increased debt year-over-year* from $14.72B to $17.03B, an increase of $2.31B despite claiming operating leverage from higher rates.

**If "higher for longer" rates were generating such strong earnings**, why does the balance sheet require significantly MORE debt to fund operations? This is a **red flag that operating cash flow is weaker than earnings suggest.**
