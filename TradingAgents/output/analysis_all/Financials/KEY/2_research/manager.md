**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull case rests on a favorable "higher for longer" rate backdrop, strong headline EPS/net income growth (22.8% YoY), attractive valuation (10.2x forward P/E, 1.35x P/B, 3.87% yield), and management signals (Chief Strategy Officer hire, branch expansion). The bear case, however, lands several harder-to-dismiss quantitative points: (1) a clear technical breakdown with heavy institutional selling volume and accelerating negative MACD, which at minimum argues against adding aggressively right now; (2) a persistent, multi-quarter rise in net charge-offs (+27% YoY) and nonaccrual loans (+23% YoY) that directly undercuts the bull's 'asset quality is fine because recession risk is low' argument—credit deterioration is happening in real time, independent of a recession materializing; (3) a debt structure red flag that the bull's 'seasonal' explanation doesn't really hold up to—current debt up 474% in a single quarter and debt/equity jumping from 0.54x to 0.85x is a large, abrupt change that smells more like funding stress or loan growth outpacing deposits than routine liquidity management; and (4) a QoQ decline in stockholders' equity in Q1 2026, reversing the prior positive trend, which is inconsistent with the bull's narrative of unambiguous strength.

Weighing these: the bull's earnings growth and valuation discount are real, but earnings growth alongside rising charge-offs, rising NPLs, ballooning short-term debt, and shrinking equity suggests the reported profitability may be masking underlying balance-sheet stress — possibly credit costs being under-provisioned relative to the deteriorating trend, or funding pressure. Valuation being cheap doesn't protect against a value trap if credit costs continue to climb and leverage keeps rising; cheap multiples on regional banks with worsening credit quality often get cheaper, not the reverse (classic value trap dynamic). The technical picture (institutional-scale volume on the breakdown, MACD swinging hard negative in under three weeks) reinforces that the market is actively repricing this deterioration, not irrationally punishing a fundamentally sound business.

That said, the bear case isn't a clean "collapse" story either — recession probability is still low (8%), NIM tailwinds from higher-for-longer rates are real and NII growth of 11.5% YoY is genuine, and the dividend yield provides some support. This isn't a full Sell/exit case, but the credit quality trend, leverage spike, and technical breakdown are concrete, data-backed concerns that outweigh the bull's more narrative-driven points (leadership hire, branch opening) and make a straightforward "cheap stock, buy the dip" stance too aggressive today. The more balanced, evidence-respecting call is to move to a cautious stance — trim/reduce exposure and wait for confirmation that charge-offs/NPLs stabilize and the debt spike proves genuinely transitory before adding back — rather than aggressively buying the pullback or holding unchanged.

Retain a small residual position (rather than a full exit) to preserve upside optionality from the genuine NII tailwind (+11.5% YoY) and the 3.87% dividend yield, but size it as an underweight relative to benchmark/regional bank peers given the balance-sheet leverage increase (D/E from 0.54x to 0.85x) and QoQ equity decline.
