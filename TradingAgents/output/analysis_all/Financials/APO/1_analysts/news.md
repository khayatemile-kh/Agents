Recent strategic moves—including the Kelvion exit and significant ONEOK backing—reflect a deliberate repositioning toward infrastructure and midstream energy assets.

## **1. APO-SPECIFIC DEVELOPMENTS (Week of August 25–September 1, 2026)**

### **Key Transactions & Portfolio Actions**

**ONEOK Investment & Midstream Exposure:**
- **$9 billion minority equity investment** in ONEOK to support its **$4.425 billion acquisition of Brazos Midstream** assets in the Permian Basin
- This positions Apollo directly in natural gas gathering and processing, tying it to core energy infrastructure
- ONEOK is concurrently executing a **$5 billion debt overhaul**, reshaping capital structure in coordination with Apollo's involvement
- Apollo is exploring a **debt repackaging strategy** to convert the $9 billion equity stake into investment-grade debt instruments for broader distribution to institutional investors—a sophisticated capital markets move that unlocks additional returns and reduces balance sheet exposure

**Kelvion Divestiture:**
- Apollo exited **Kelvion** (cooling solutions provider) to **SLB (Schlumberger)** for **~$4.1 billion**
- Kelvion was originally positioned for data center thermal management demand, but the exit suggests Apollo is prioritizing higher-conviction infrastructure plays over downstream data center supplier positions
- This capital redeployment underscores portfolio optimization and disciplined capital allocation

**Insider Activity:**
- CFO **Kelly Martin disposed of 4,534 shares** (routine transaction per disclosure commentary)—minimal signal of concern

### **Implications for APO:**
1. **Deal volume & fee generation**: Large transactions (ONEOK, Kelvion exit) drive advisory, structuring, and management fees
2. **Asset management growth**: ONEOK stake and ongoing energy infrastructure positioning expand AUM (assets under management)
3. **Capital efficiency**: Debt repackaging of the ONEOK equity interest demonstrates financial engineering capability and may improve returns on invested capital (ROIC)
4. **Sector positioning**: Doubling down on energy midstream + data center infrastructure exposure aligns with secular tailwinds (energy demand, AI infrastructure)
