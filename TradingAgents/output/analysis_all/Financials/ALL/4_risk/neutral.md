## **Where the Aggressive Analyst Gets It Right—And Where They Go Too Far**

The aggressive analyst makes a genuinely compelling point about the technical setup. They're correct that RSI at 50 during consolidation is not inherently bearish. And they're absolutely right that the 5.16x TTM P/E is, on its face, extraordinarily low for a company generating 46% ROE.

But here's where the logic breaks down: **the aggressive analyst conflates optionality with inevitability.** Yes, the technical setup *could* produce a breakout. Yes, valuations *could* re-rate higher. Yes, retail *might* pile in. But "could" is not "will," and that distinction matters enormously when we're talking about capital allocation.

More critically, the aggressive analyst commits a sin that every trader should be paranoid about: **they've decided what they want to believe, then worked backward to justify it.** When they reframe MACD histogram moderation from -2.03 to -1.05 as "seller exhaustion," they're not interpreting the data objectively—they're interpreting it through the lens of "I want to add here." That's dangerous.

Let me push back on their specific claims:

**On RSI normalization:** Yes, RSI at 50 is neutral. But the aggressive analyst glosses over something critical—RSI *didn't normalize gradually over time.* It collapsed **20 points in 26 days** while price dropped only 6.9%. That asymmetry is the actual story. Healthy consolidations typically see RSI decline *gradually*, reflective of price declining slowly. Sharp RSI collapses that outpace price moves suggest momentum participants are *exiting*, not loading. This is the opposite of what the aggressive analyst claims.

**On MACD histogram moderation:** Here's where I actually agree with the conservative analyst more than the aggressive one. When histogram goes from positive to -2.03, that's a major reversal. The moderation to -1.05 doesn't necessarily mean exhaustion—it could mean the selling pressure persists but at lower velocity. For a true positive divergence setup, we'd want to see the histogram *cross back above zero* with price holding support. It hasn't done that yet. The aggressive analyst is essentially saying "let's assume the reversal will happen soon," when the evidence merely suggests *it could*.

**On the 5.16x TTM P/E:** This is where the aggressive analyst and I part ways most sharply. Yes, 5.16x looks absurdly cheap. But the conservative analyst nailed the actual issue here—**TTM earnings are cat-light earnings.** The forward 9.39x P/E exists precisely because the market knows Q2 benefited from abnormally mild catastrophe losses. The aggressive analyst's rebuttal is essentially "institutions believe underwriting has structurally improved," but that's not actually proven yet. Institutions could be wrong. And we're being asked to bet capital on the assumption that they're not.

This is not asymmetrically bullish; it's just *normal*.
