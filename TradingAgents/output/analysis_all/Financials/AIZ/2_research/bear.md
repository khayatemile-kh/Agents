### **VULNERABILITY 3: THE MACROECONOMIC SETUP IS MORE FRAGILE THAN IT APPEARS**

Now let me challenge Pillar 3 with a dose of realism about the prediction market data.

#### **The Interest Rate Narrative Is Vulnerable**

The bull argues: "89% probability of NO Fed rate cuts in 2026 is MASSIVE for insurers."

**Let me flip that completely:**

**Yes, rates have been higher. But here's what the bull ignores:**

1. **Rates have *already peaked* and are normalizing downward**
   - 10-year Treasury yield: ~4.2% (down from 5.3% in late 2023)
   - Fed Funds Rate: Peak at 5.33% in July 2023; currently ~5.00%
   - **The repricing has already occurred.** The "sticky high rates" narrative is ex-post facto rationalization.

2. **The "89% probability of no cuts in 2026" is a backward-looking stat**
   - We're already in September 2026; most of the year has passed
   - This prediction market data doesn't meaningfully differentiate AIZ's risk profile
   - What matters is what happens in **2027 and beyond**, when rate cuts will almost certainly resume

3. **Insurance companies actually suffer when rates *decline***
   - Current investment portfolio reinvestment yields are locked in at elevated rates
   - When rates drop (inevitable), new capital deployed into investments earns *lower* yields
   - **This creates a structural headwind to earnings growth** as the portfolio "rolls down" the yield curve

**The Bull's Error:** Conflating "high rates are good for insurers in the short term" with "high rates are sustainable and beneficial long-term." They're not. **Rates will decline, and when they do, AIZ's earnings will face a headwind that most investors haven't priced in yet.**
