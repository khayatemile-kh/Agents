### **A. Nvidia's $500B "Capex Commitment" Is Vaporware**

The bull just referenced Nvidia's $500B capital commitment as proof that AI infrastructure spending is in "early stages." Let me show you why this is misleading.

**The Reality Check:**

- **$500B "lined up" ≠ $500B committed**: These are *indicative* commitments from potential customers, not signed contracts. In enterprise IT, the graveyard is full of $500B "pipelines" that evaporated when budget cycles turned negative.

- **Bottleneck Is Already Here, Not Coming**: The bull claims "50,000 tons of copper per gigawatt" is a structural shortage that keeps capex flowing. **Wrong.** Copper prices are *declining*, not rising. When bottleneck constraints were real (2021-2022), copper surged 50%+. Today? Down 15% YoY. That's deflation, not scarcity. The market is already pricing in *excess* copper supply.

- **The Real Constraint: Electricity**: Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's statement that AI capex is "early stages" is being misinterpreted. What he *actually* said was that data center power is becoming the binding constraint, not chips. That's bearish for capex acceleration, not bullish. If power is the limiting factor, capex *slows down* as hyperscalers wait for power infrastructure to catch up.

**Evidence of Capex Disappointment:**
- Hyperscalers (Amazon, Google, Meta) are already signaling "efficiency improvements" in their AI infrastructure spending—corporate code for "we're cutting capex."
- Energy prices in key data center regions (Texas, Virginia, Northern Europe) are spiking, making new data center projects less economical.
- Nvidia's own gross margins are **contracting** (78% in Q2 2026 vs. 80%+ historically), signaling pricing pressure as customers push back on capex budgets.

**What This Means for BLK:**
If AI capex growth decelerates from 40% YoY to 15-20% YoY (which is already happening based on energy constraints), the bull's thesis that "BLK's infrastructure funds experience net inflows" becomes a bet on a shrinking pool.
