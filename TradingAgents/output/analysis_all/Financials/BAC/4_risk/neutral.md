Now let me dismantle some of the logical overreach from each side.

That's not how technical analysis works in practice. You're essentially arguing that because we've already fallen 4%, the divergence will resolve upward. But divergences resolve when price catches down to momentum—not the other way around. The fact that the MACD signal line is still declining (0.532 and moving lower) suggests this divergence has more downside to run, not that we're near a reversal. I can't accept your argument that this is a "setup for the next leg higher" without evidence that momentum has stabilized or turned positive. It hasn't.

The Conservative Analyst correctly points out that in a systemic stress scenario, regulatory authorities essentially commandeer that liquidity as capital buffers. But I'd push further—even *before* a systemic stress event, if BAC needs to signal confidence to depositors and counterparties, that cash gets deployed defensively, not offensively. **To the Conservative Analyst:** Your argument that BAC's $242.5B cash becomes a "liability not an asset" in stress is theoretically sound but practically overstated. But I do agree with your core concern: tangible equity contraction plus rising debt plus modest earnings growth should make us cautious about downside scenarios. This isn't a fortress balance sheet; it's a working capital structure under pressure.
