## **Opening Statement: Why JPMorgan Chase's Current Valuation is Dangerously Optimistic**

Let me start by acknowledging what the bull case *appears* to show: strong earnings, favorable macro conditions, and a fortress balance sheet. On the surface, JPM looks like a fortress. But I'd argue we're looking at a carefully curated snapshot of data that masks deeper structural vulnerabilities—and critically, a stock that's priced for perfection in an environment where perfection is increasingly unlikely.

The bull analyst has built an elegant thesis on **three pillars**. I'm going to systematically dismantle each one, using the very data they've presented against them. Let's begin.

## **CRITICAL FLAW #1: THE NII TAILWIND IS A MIRAGE, NOT A STRUCTURAL ADVANTAGE**

### **The Bull's Argument Relies on a Flawed Assumption About Rate Permanence**

The bull says: "89% probability the Fed won't cut rates through end-2026, so NII is locked in." This is where I need to challenge the core logic.

**Here's what's actually happening:**

First, let's look at what the data *itself* reveals. The bull highlighted:
- **NII growth of +9.9% YoY**
- **10-year Treasury at 4.8%**
- **89% probability of zero rate cuts in 2026**

But this tells only half the story. **The 10-year yield at 4.8% is already pricing in a very specific macro scenario.** If anything changes—and markets *always* change—that yield will compress, and JPM's NII expansion will stall.

### **The Real Problem: NII Growth is Already Decelerating**

Look at the sequential data the bull glossed over:

| Period | Q2 2026 NII | Q1 2026 NII | Sequential Change |
|--------|-------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Q2 2026** | $25.5B | $25.4B | **+$0.1B (+0.6%)** |
| **YoY Comparison** | $25.5B | — | $23.2B (+9.9%) |

**This is the critical tell:** While YoY growth looks robust at +9.9%, the *sequential* growth from Q1 to Q2 was only **+0.6%**. That's essentially flat.

What does this signal? **The NII cycle is already peaking.** If deposits have already repriced, if loan yields are already elevated, then the year-over-year comparison is misleading—it's just comparing against a lower base from a year ago. But going forward? The growth rate is already slowing.

### **The Deposit Repricing Problem the Bull Ignored**

The bull claimed: "Deposit costs remain relatively sticky (customers can't easily move large institutional deposits)."

**This is outdated thinking.** In 2026, with technology and regulatory pressure, deposit flight is *real*. We've seen this movie before:
- High-yield savings accounts now offer 4.5%+ returns
- Money market funds are offering competitive rates
- Institutional depositors have sophisticated cash management systems

If JPM is paying close to market rates on deposits (which it must to retain them in a competitive environment), then as yields compress, the margin doesn't stay "sticky"—it *compresses with them*.

**The math is simple:** If the 10-year yield drops from 4.8% to 4.0%, and deposit rates follow (as they always do), NII doesn't remain elevated. It falls.

### **Here's the Uncomfortable Truth About the Rate Outlook**

The bull is anchoring to "89% probability of zero rate cuts by end-2026." But let's think critically:

**What if that 11% probability hits?** What if the labor market softens unexpectedly? What if inflation ticks down faster than expected? (We're already 5 months into 2026—we have only 4 months left in the year.)

The markets have priced in *zero cuts* for the rest of 2026. But if cuts come in early 2027 (which prediction markets might be pricing at much higher probability), then JPM has already guided investors to expect NII compression within the next 12 months.

**The real risk:** The market is pricing in a *sustained* 4.8% 10-year yield for the next 2+ years. If that's wrong—and it likely is—then the entire bull thesis crumbles.

## **CRITICAL FLAW #2: INVESTMENT BANKING MOMENTUM IS A CYCLICAL PHENOMENON, NOT A NEW NORMAL**

### **The Bull's Logic: Goldman Sachs IB Fees Up 52%, So JPM Will Benefit**

Let me challenge this directly. The bull says Goldman's IB fees jumped 52% in H1 2026, and this signals a robust deal cycle that benefits JPM.

**But here's what the bull missed: Goldman Sachs IB fees are already normalized from a depressed baseline.**

Look at the historical context:
- 2022-2023 saw a *collapse* in IB revenues across the sector (worst environment in years)
- 2024 saw a modest recovery
- 2025-2026 are seeing *catch-up* in deal flow as companies that delayed decisions execute them

**This is mean reversion, not a new era of deal-making.**

### **The Deeper Problem: JPM's IB Exposure is Lower Than the Bull Implies**

Here's a critical omission from the bull's analysis: **JPMorgan Chase generates the majority of its earnings from net interest income, not investment banking.**

Let's look at the actual revenue mix from Q2 2026:
- **Net Interest Income: $25.5B** (dominant)
- **Total Revenue: $52.9B**
- **NII as % of Total: ~48%**

That means IB fees represent a *minority* of JPM's revenue. So even if IB fees soften (which they *will* as the deal cycle normalizes), the impact on total earnings is muted.

**But here's the catch:** The bull is citing 52% IB growth as proof of a structural tailwind. That same metric tells us IB revenues are cyclical—they spike, then normalize. And when they normalize, JPM's earnings reset lower.

### **The Recession Risk Isn't 8%—It's Mispriced Lower**

The bull relies heavily on "8% recession probability by end-2026" from prediction markets.

**Let me challenge this assumption.** Prediction markets are useful, but they reflect *current* consensus, not fundamental risk. Here's why I'm skeptical of that 8% number:

1. **Credit conditions are already tightening** – While the bull talks about "strong corporate confidence," the underlying credit data shows stress:
   - High-yield spreads are widening
   - Corporate debt levels remain near record highs
   - Refinancing risks are building for companies that issued debt at low rates in 2020-2021

2. **The labor market is showing cracks** – Unemployment is rising modestly, job growth is slowing, and wage pressures are persisting. This is the setup for either aggressive Fed cuts (which compress NII) or recession.

3. **Consumer credit is deteriorating** – Credit card delinquencies are rising, auto loan defaults are climbing, and mortgage delinquencies are starting to tick up. This signals financial stress, not confidence.

**If recession probability is actually 15-20% (not 8%), then JPM's loan portfolio faces significant loss risk, and earnings estimates are too optimistic.**
