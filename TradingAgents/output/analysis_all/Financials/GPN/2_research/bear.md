## **2. THE BULL'S CASH FLOW ARGUMENT IS BUILT ON QUICKSAND**

### Let's Dissect That "$7.87B Free Cash Flow" Claim

The bull presents TTM FCF of $7.87B as evidence of robust cash generation. **Let me show you what's actually happening:**

**Quarterly FCF Performance:**
- Q2 2026: $426.96M
- Q1 2026: -$288.82M (**negative**)
- Q4 2025: $515.21M
- Q3 2025: $768.73M
- Q2 2025: $817.53M

**Do you see the deterioration?** Q1 2026 was **negative FCF despite $2.97B in revenue.** This is a massive red flag:

1. **Working Capital is Deteriorating:** A negative working capital position of -$2.48B means the company is burning cash to fund operations, not generating it. This typically indicates:
   - Rising payables pressure (potentially straining supplier relationships)
   - Declining receivables collection (customer credit deterioration)
   - Inventory buildup or restructuring inefficiency

2. **TTM FCF is Misleading:** The bull cherry-picks a TTM figure of $7.87B, but this includes Q3 2025's $768.73M outlier. If that quarter normalizes, TTM FCF falls to closer to **$6.5-7.0B**, which is still healthy on surface but hiding the trend deterioration.

3. **CapEx Intensity May Be Increasing:** The bull assumes CapEx is "low" for software. But acquisition integration requires capital deployment. Rising CapEx without proportional revenue growth is a squeeze play.

### The Real Issue: EBITDA-to-FCF Conversion is Deteriorating

The bull claims "176% EBITDA-to-FCF conversion" is exceptional. **It's actually a red flag.**

Here's why: Healthy, mature software businesses typically see 80-100% EBITDA-to-FCF conversion. **Over 100% conversion suggests:**

1. **Aggressive Asset Sales:** GPN is selling assets (we see "gain on sale of business" of $343.89M in Q3 2025) to inflate FCF. This is unsustainable.
2. **Working Capital Extraction:** The company is squeezing suppliers and customers to pull cash forward, which is short-term and damaging to customer relationships.
3. **Deferred Payments:** Integration costs and one-time charges are being pushed into future quarters, deferring cash outflows.

**This FCF figure is not as clean as the bull suggests.**
