**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The debate surfaces a real tension: GPN shows headline revenue and EBITDA scale that looks attractive on paper, but the bear case lands more decisive blows on the quality and sustainability of those numbers. The bull's central pillars—68.5% revenue growth, $7.87B TTM FCF, and 4.9x interest coverage—are each substantially undercut once you look at the quarterly progression rather than the trailing aggregate. The sequential revenue pattern (a -4% decline in Q4 2025 followed by a +54% jump in Q1 2026) is much more consistent with acquisition-driven step-changes than organic demand acceleration, which means the 'explosive growth' framing is likely masking a flat-to-declining core business. That matters a lot for a services company where the bull thesis depends on operating leverage compounding over time.

The balance sheet deterioration is the most alarming piece of evidence and the bull never directly rebuts it: tangible book value swinging from +$1.58B to -$23.28B in two quarters, intangibles now 73% of assets, and negative working capital of -$2.48B are not 'non-operating noise'—they are structural signals of an acquisition-fueled roll-up under stress. Net income swinging from +$635M to -$213.65M to a barely-breakeven +$13M across three quarters directly contradicts the bull's claim that integration costs are 'temporary'; if anything the volatility is getting worse, not better, over successive quarters.

On cash flow, the bear's point that Q1 2026 FCF was negative (-$288.82M) despite $2.97B of revenue is a serious flag the bull simply didn't address—it undermines the claim that EBITDA reliably converts to cash. The bull's own '176% EBITDA-to-FCF conversion' framing, when inverted, is actually evidence of one-off asset sales (the $343.89M gain on sale of business) propping up the TTM FCF figure rather than sustainable core cash generation.

On leverage, both sides agree on the raw numbers (4.05x net debt/EBITDA, 4.9x interest coverage, $915M annual interest = ~20% of EBITDA), but the bear's framing is more consistent with the observed earnings volatility: a company with unstable net income and negative tangible equity does not have much margin for error if growth decelerates or rates move against it. The bull's counter—'debt is serviceable because EBITDA covers interest 4.9x'—doesn't really engage with the working capital and book value deterioration, which are the more forward-looking risk signals.

I do think the bear overstates the case slightly (a 4x leverage roll-up isn't automatically a 'crisis,' and the low EV/EBITDA of ~5.5x does suggest the market is already pricing in a lot of this risk), so I'm not going to full Sell. But the preponderance of evidence—decelerating/inorganic growth, deteriorating net income trend, negative tangible book value, negative single-quarter FCF, and elevated leverage relative to peers—tips this toward a cautious stance rather than a constructive one. The bull's rebuttals rely too heavily on trailing twelve-month aggregates that smooth over exactly the quarter-to-quarter deterioration the bear highlights, and that's a real weakness in the bull argument that I can't ignore as facilitator.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing GPN exposure incrementally rather than exiting outright—the ~5.5x implied EV/EBITDA suggests some of the risk is already priced in, so this is a reduce-and-monitor stance, not a full exit.
2) Do not add to the position or initiate new exposure until at least two consecutive quarters show: (a) positive and stabilizing free cash flow, (b) net income trending consistently positive rather than oscillating between large losses and gains, and (c) net debt/EBITDA moving down toward 3.5x or below.
3) Set a hard risk trigger: any further deterioration in tangible book value, another negative-FCF quarter, or a covenant-related disclosure should prompt further de-risking of the position.
4) Watch the Q3/Q4 2026 sequential revenue print closely to separate organic from acquisition-driven growth—if sequential growth reverts to low single digits without further M&A, reassess upward; if growth stalls or reverses, reduce further.
5) Size any remaining position conservatively (below benchmark weight) given the negative working capital, negative tangible equity, and elevated interest burden (~20% of EBITDA), which collectively reduce the margin of safety versus payments-sector peers.
6) Avoid using GPN as a leveraged/options play given the earnings volatility; if hedging is desired, consider protective puts or reducing notional exposure rather than adding leverage.
