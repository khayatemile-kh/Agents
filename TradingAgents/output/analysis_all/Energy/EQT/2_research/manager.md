**Recommendation**: Hold

**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points, and the disagreement really comes down to whether you believe the last two years of margin/FCF expansion is structural or cyclical. The bull is right that the balance sheet is genuinely healthier than it was — debt down from $9.37B to $5.66B, D/E at 0.22, and even a 'normalized' Q2 2026 print ($211M net income, 11.9% margin) still shows a profitable, well-run operator rather than a company limping along. The data center/natural gas demand narrative and seasonal winter-demand tailwind are real directional catalysts, not fabricated ones, and the technical setup (RSI recovering, MACD turning up, price coiled between the 10 EMA and 200 SMA) supports the idea that near-term downside is limited.

But the bear's rebuttal lands some real blows that the bull didn't adequately answer. The FCF trajectory is the biggest issue: H1 2026 FCF of $2.86B was driven almost entirely by an exceptional Q1 ($2.46B), with Q2 collapsing to just $398M — an 84% sequential drop. Extrapolating H1 to a ~$5.7B annual run-rate, as the bull did, is simply not supportable once you see the Q2 base rate; the bear's more conservative annualization (~$1.6B) is closer to a fair estimate if Q2-like conditions persist. Similarly, the debt reduction story is muddied by the fact EQT issued $2.46B in new debt while repaying $4.64B — this is partly refinancing, not pure organic deleveraging, and that matters in a 'higher for longer' rate environment. On valuation, the bull's P/B upside case leans on trough-to-peak earnings and book value that's overwhelmingly tied to PP&E whose worth is a direct function of commodity prices; the bear's point that normalized EPS is probably closer to $2.00-$2.50 (implying a 22-28x normalized P/E, not the headline 12.8x) is a fair and underaddressed risk.

Given that: (1) the bull's most quantitatively aggressive claims (FCF run-rate, P/B upside) don't survive close scrutiny of the Q1/Q2 divergence, but (2) the balance sheet, margin structure, and secular natural-gas-demand tailwind are real improvements versus where the company was in 2024, I don't think either side has fully proven their case for a large directional move. This is a name where the near-term catalysts (winter demand, potential data-center contracts) could support the stock, but valuation isn't the screaming bargain the bull implies once cyclicality is priced in properly, and the FCF/debt story needs another quarter or two of confirmation before extrapolating out. That's a genuinely balanced setup — not a green light to add aggressively, but not a reason to exit either.
