## THE CONSERVATIVE ANALYST IS RIGHT ABOUT THE FCF VOLATILITY PROBLEM

First, grid emergency probabilities declining doesn't mean demand is flat—it means the grid is managing current demand without emergency conditions. In fact, grid operators getting *ahead* of demand through infrastructure investment is exactly what you'd expect before a sustained demand surge. It's not evidence of weak demand; it's evidence of prudent infrastructure planning.

Second, the StockTwits sentiment data—while anecdotal—does show a 7:1 bullish-to-bearish ratio focused specifically on data-center energy catalysts. The fact that Reddit shows zero posts doesn't negate the StockTwits signal; it just means EQT isn't a focal point for that community. The absence of evidence isn't evidence of absence of a real emerging tailwind.

But here's where I'll push back on the aggressive analyst's interpretation: the retail enthusiasm doesn't automatically validate the thesis. The conservative analyst is right that when retail gets excited about unvalidated catalysts, outcomes are: 20% realization + upside, 60% disillusionment + reversion, 20% partial realization + multiple compression. Those are rough probabilities, but they're historically accurate.

The real question isn't whether the data-center thesis *could* be real—it probably is, structurally. The question is *when* it becomes material enough to drive stock performance.
