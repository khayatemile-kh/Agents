The macroeconomic data shows:

**What does this mean for KMI's debt burden?**

KMI carries $29.7B in long-term debt. If rates rise 100 bps (1.0%) over the next 12-18 months:

- **Annual interest expense increases by ~$297 million** (on variable/refinanced portion)
- **This directly reduces distributable cash flow** that funds the dividend and funds capex
- **Refinancing risk escalates:** KMI must roll over debt at higher rates, compressing margins

**Debt/EBITDA of 3.9x becomes dangerous when:**
1. **EBITDA is cyclical** (energy volumes can contract in recession)
2. **Interest rates are rising** (cost of debt servicing accelerates)
3. **Free cash flow is constrained** (capex is non-discretionary for infrastructure maintenance)
