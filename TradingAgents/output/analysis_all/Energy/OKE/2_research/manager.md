**Rationale**: The bull's case rests on three legs: (1) Brazos delivering $80M in "immediate" synergies that translate into a clean EPS/dividend uplift, (2) the Apollo deal being pure de-risking with zero governance cost, and (3) technicals confirming a breakout. On leverage, the bear's forward-looking recompute (using run-rate EBITDA rather than trailing) shows the bull's leverage-improvement narrative is overstated in the optimistic direction but understated in the pessimistic direction — the true post-deal leverage likely lands in a 3.1-3.3x range under partial synergy realization, which is still a genuine improvement from pre-deal levels, just not as dramatic as the bull claims. Importantly, the bear's point about Apollo's non-voting stake still carrying real economic influence (via the 7% yield cap and implicit investment-grade covenant pressure) is a legitimate governance/flexibility concern that the bull dismissed too quickly by focusing solely on voting rights.

Where I lean bullish: the underlying strategic logic is sound — fee-based, volume-driven Permian midstream assets are a genuinely attractive profile, the dividend has a real support base (~70% fee-based cash flows), and even the bear's "worst case" synergy/leverage numbers still show OKE improving from ~4x to ~3.1-3.3x leverage, which is a credit-positive trajectory, not a credit-negative one. The stock isn't cheap after a 29% YTD run and roughly fairly valued to slightly rich per the data cited (near the Bollinger upper band, ~1% overvaluation noted), so this isn't a screaming "back up the truck" Buy. But the bear's argument doesn't establish a thesis for actively reducing exposure either — it's mostly "the bull's upside math is overstated," not "the downside case is compelling." No one presented a scenario where OKE actually impairs the dividend, breaches leverage covenants, or sees the deal fall apart entirely. Net: the fundamental direction of travel (delevering, accretive bolt-on in a good basin, rising dividend) is intact, just the magnitude of near-term upside is more modest than the bull's headline math suggests, and the stock's current price already reflects a lot of the good news. That argues for staying invested and adding selectively into weakness rather than chasing the breakout at the upper Bollinger Band, and it argues against trimming a legitimately improving credit/dividend growth story on execution-risk concerns that are real but not yet visible in the numbers.
