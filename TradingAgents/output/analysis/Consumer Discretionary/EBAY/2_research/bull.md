### **The Debt Question: Not a Crisis**

The bear will say: "EBAY has $7.1B in debt—that's too much." Let me reframe:

**Debt-to-EBITDA: 2.29x** (EBITDA $3.1B / Net Debt $4.43B after cash)
- This is well within investment-grade territory (BBB-rated companies are typically 2.5-3.0x)
- EBAY is generating $1.58B in FCF annually—enough to pay down $400-500M per year while maintaining capital returns

**Debt-to-Equity: 153%** sounds high, but:
- It's inflated by $54.6B in treasury stock (massive buyback program over decades)
- Adjust for treasury stock improperly classified, and the "true" D/E is ~90%, which is moderate
- The company is deliberately maintaining leverage because **cost of debt is lower than ROE** (current rates ~3-4%, EBAY's ROE 46.6%)

**Bottom line:** EBAY's debt is manageable, serviceable, and declining. It's not a risk factor.
