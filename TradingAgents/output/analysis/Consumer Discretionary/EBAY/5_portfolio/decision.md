**Investment Thesis**: The bear/conservative case wins on fundamentals: sequential revenue growth decelerated sharply (Q4'25→Q1'26 +4.2%, Q1'26→Q2'26 only +1.5%, per the conservative analyst's breakdown), and YoY growth has slowed from 12-13% to 7.8% in the most recent read cited by the research manager and trader. The aggressive analyst's rebuttal citing 14.8% YoY in Q2 doesn't actually refute this—it conflates an easy comp with the sequential deceleration trend, which the conservative analyst correctly flagged as the more telling signal of underlying momentum loss. This matters directly for valuation: the bull's 15.76x forward P/E is only 'cheap' if 41% EPS growth materializes, but that is irreconcilable with 11% revenue growth, 'modest active buyer growth,' and margin expansion that has little runway left off an already-high 73.4% gross margin base. Normalize EPS growth to a more defensible 15-18% and the effective multiple re-rates to 25-28x—not a bargain for a maturing marketplace.

The balance sheet argument reinforces caution rather than dismisses it: the conservative analyst's point that 46.61% ROE is mechanically inflated by a shrunken equity base ($4.665B equity vs. $7.13B debt, 153% D/E) is a legitimate structural concern the aggressive analyst never adequately rebuts—large buybacks funded alongside debt paydown look like defensive capital return in the face of growth uncertainty, not confidence-driven reinvestment. The neutral analyst's synthesis is instructive: the technical bounce (RSI 35.83→47.44, MACD histogram +0.19) is real but unconfirmed—price remains below the downward-sloping 50-SMA at $109.21, and volume during the recovery is unremarkable, meaning this reads as a bounce off oversold conditions within a still-bearish intermediate structure rather than a confirmed reversal.

On catalysts, both the GameStop stake and Depop optionality are speculative rather than evidenced. The GameStop derivative conversion may reflect GameStop's own capital-management needs as plausibly as it reflects 'validation' of eBay's intrinsic value—sentiment data (52% bullish on StockTwits) shows retail enthusiasm but no confirmed operational catalyst. Depop's $2-5B bull case is unsupported by cited GMV or take-rate data and carries real integration/cannibalization risk given eBay's acquisition history, as the bear noted.

Where the aggressive analyst is right, and why this isn't a Sell: FCF generation ($1.58B TTM), meaningful buybacks/debt paydown in H1 2026 ($809M buybacks, $750M debt repaid), and a still-manageable 2.29x EBITDA leverage profile provide real downside cushion and argue against full capitulation. The business is decelerating, not collapsing. This combination—decelerating fundamentals and stretched valuation assumptions on one side, genuine cash generation and balance sheet stability on the other—is precisely the asymmetric setup that supports trimming exposure (Underweight) rather than either adding on the bounce or exiting entirely.
