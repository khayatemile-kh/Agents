**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make fair points, but weighing them, the bear case exposes real cracks in the bull's narrative that tip the balance toward caution rather than balance.

On growth/valuation: the bull leans hard on the "guidance beat" and B2B acceleration, but the bear correctly notes the beat is only ~0.25% above consensus ($40M on $16.01B) - that's noise, not a genuine inflection signal. The bull also never quantifies B2B's actual share of revenue or bookings, so touting it as the primary growth driver is speculative. A forward P/E of 12.70x on 9-10% revenue growth guidance only looks cheap if you assume material margin expansion beyond what's been demonstrated - that's an assumption, not a fact in evidence.

On cash flow: the bull's "fortress FCF" story is undercut by the bear's point that the $3.43B TTM FCF and $15.60B deferred revenue are heavily seasonal and front-loaded. The bear's citation of Q3 2025 FCF at -$686M is a legitimate structural counterpoint that the bull's presentation glossed over - FCF conversion isn't steady, it's a seasonal artifact of prepaid bookings, and issuing $986M in new debt during the seasonally strongest cash quarter is a flag worth taking seriously rather than dismissing as "textbook capital allocation."

On leverage: 1.66x net debt/EBITDA is manageable in a stable environment, but the bear's point that a cyclical travel business should carry more conservative leverage (given demand can contract quickly) is reasonable — a moderate EBITDA decline could push leverage into more uncomfortable territory quickly given the operating leverage in the model.

On macro: the bull's use of PMI and low recession odds is fine as a supportive backdrop, but it's a snapshot, not a trend, and the bear rightly flags that these are leading indicators that can turn quickly - travel bookings/PMI deceleration would show up in EXPE's numbers before broader macro data confirms a slowdown.

On technicals: the 10% pullback from highs after a 54-59% one-year, 58% three-month rally is worth respecting as "just noise" only if the fundamental support is airtight - but given the guidance-beat overstatement and the seasonal FCF distortion, I don't think the bull fully earns that benefit of the doubt. Insider selling, even if partially plan-based, adds to a mixed signal environment rather than a purely bullish one.

Net take: the bull thesis over-relies on rounding-error-level "beats" and seasonal cash flow dynamics to justify a business that has already run up substantially, while the bear raises legitimate, evidence-grounded concerns about the durability and quality of that growth and cash generation. This isn't a full sell case — the business does generate real cash and has decent visibility via deferred revenue, and there's no evidence of imminent fundamental breakdown — but the risk/reward is no longer clearly favorable after such a large rally, especially with the technical pullback underway and leverage/seasonality risks flagged. I'm coming down on the cautious side: trim into strength and wait for either a better entry point or clearer confirmation that B2B/margin durability is real rather than assumed.

6) Avoid using leverage or options to add exposure given the debt issuance timing concerns and elevated valuation relative to single-digit revenue growth guidance.
