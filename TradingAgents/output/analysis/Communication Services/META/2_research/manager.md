**Recommendation**: Hold

**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points, and the evidence provided doesn't clearly favor one direction strongly enough to commit capital aggressively either way. The bull case rests heavily on a single outlier analyst call (Bernstein's $800 PT and 'Meta overtakes Google Search' thesis) that has zero corroborating consensus support and no visible revenue line item to validate it yet - this is a real weakness the bear correctly identifies. The bear's breakdown of the sequential financials is the most concrete, hard-to-dispute evidence in the whole debate: operating expenses grew +32% sequentially in Q2 vs only +8% revenue growth, operating income fell -17.9% QoQ, and net income collapsed -41% QoQ, partly due to non-recurring tax benefits inflating Q1. That's a genuine deceleration signal that undercuts the bull's 'earnings acceleration proves capex ROI' argument - that logic is indeed somewhat circular, as the bear points out. On the other hand, the bull's balance-sheet and profitability argument is solid and unrebutted: 82% gross margin, 34.8% operating margin, $90B cash, manageable net debt at 26% of market cap - this is not a company in distress, and it has ample capacity to sustain the capex cycle without financial stress even if ROI takes longer than hoped. The technical picture (price below both 50-day and 200-day SMAs, failed recovery attempts, distribution-like price action) is a legitimate near-term concern flagged by the bear and is consistent with the fundamental deceleration story - momentum and fundamentals are actually aligned bearish in the near term, which bull dismisses too quickly. However, the bear's dismissal of the entire AI capex program as 'speculative fantasy' likely overstates the case too - Meta's core ads business remains highly profitable and is self-funding the AI investment, and the regulatory tailwind (lighter AI rules) is a real, if modest, positive. Given that neither the growth acceleration thesis nor the capex-failure thesis is proven with the evidence at hand, and given the balance of technical weakness against balance-sheet strength, this is a case where the debate is genuinely balanced rather than clearly tilted. The prudent stance is to maintain current exposure without adding or cutting aggressively until Q3 earnings (October 2026) provide clearer evidence on whether Search/AI monetization is showing up in the numbers or whether margin compression continues.
