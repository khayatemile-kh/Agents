**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points that roughly offset each other, so I'm not comfortable committing capital aggressively in either direction right now. The bull's strongest arguments are real: 13.7% organic growth off a $36B base is genuinely impressive, the raised full-year guidance signals management confidence, and the cash flow profile ($4.63B FCF, 2.13x current ratio, active $1.08B debt paydown) supports the dividend and buyback program while still funding a $700M strategic bet in robotics. However, the bear's rebuttal exposes real cracks that can't be waved away: guidance of 7.25-7.75% for the full year versus 13.7% in Q1 is a meaningful deceleration that the bull doesn't adequately explain away as conservatism—it could just as easily reflect tougher comps and normalizing demand. The margin trend cited (20.5% Q1 vs. high-teens in prior quarters) combined with SG&A at 31.2% of revenue raises legitimate doubt about whether the 71.6% forward EPS growth implied by consensus is achievable without continued outsized margin expansion, which is not obviously structural. On the robotics investment, the bear's framing that $700M is a modest opening bid against an entrenched $2-3B+ scale competitor with 70% share and two-plus decades of surgeon/hospital lock-in is a fair counter to the bull's optimism—Affera's niche success in cardiac ablation doesn't automatically generalize to platform-level general surgery robotics. On the balance sheet, the bear's point that mandatory cash outflows (interest, dividends, maintenance R&D/capex) essentially consume all of the $4.63B FCF is important — it means the robotics investment and any margin missteps have very little cushion, and net debt of ~$26B is a real constraint if growth decelerates as guided. Technically, the stock has already run up nearly 12% in 30 days and MACD is decelerating, suggesting near-term momentum may be cooling even as longer-term SMAs remain supportive. Given credible growth deceleration risk, margin uncertainty, a nascent and unproven robotics bet against an entrenched competitor, and a balance sheet with essentially no slack, I don't think this is a high-conviction Buy. But the underlying franchise remains a large, diversified, cash-generative medtech business with a raised guide, a defensible dividend, and optionality in robotics and ablation share gains — that's not a Sell either. The fair resolution is to stay balanced: maintain current exposure, don't chase the recent run-up, and wait for the next 1-2 quarters of data (organic growth trajectory vs. guided deceleration, margin trend, and early robotics/Affera traction) before shifting the stance meaningfully in either direction.
