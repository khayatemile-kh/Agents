In a market where the Fed maintains rates at elevated levels (89% probability per the macro analysis), **growth stocks are supposed to compress in valuation, not expand.** Higher rates mean:

1. **Lower discount rates on future cash flows** — WRONG. Higher rates mean HIGHER discount rates. This is Finance 101. A dollar earned in year 5 is worth less in a 5% rate environment than in a 2% rate environment. Higher rates compress valuations, not expand them.

2. **Defensive stocks should trade at lower multiples during rate-hiking cycles, not higher.** Why? Because defensive stocks are chosen when investors are *risk-off* and retreating to safety. That rotation itself is usually accompanied by multiple compression as growth expectations reset.

**Let's do a real valuation comparison:**

- **JNJ P/E: 31.87x**
- **S&P 500 P/E: ~20-21x**
- **Premium: 52% above market**

**If the Fed is holding rates high for structural reasons (inflation, fiscal deficits, labor market), then:
- Beta DOESN'T expand multiples; it reduces required return, which is priced in via capital structure, not multiple expansion

**The real reason JNJ trades at 31.87x P/E is NOT justified fundamentals—it's momentum and dividend-hunting.** Retail and income-focused funds have bid the stock up in a low-rate environment (2022-2024), and that valuation hasn't reset now that rates are staying high.

**Here's the risk: The moment rates stop being "expected to hold" and instead move higher (which a 0.231 beta suggests is possible), or the moment dividend funds rotate to higher-yielding alternatives (Treasuries at 4-5%, junk bonds at 6-7%), JNJ's multiple compresses quickly to 20-22x.
