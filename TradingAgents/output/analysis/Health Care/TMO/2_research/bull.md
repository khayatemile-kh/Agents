## III. THE COMPETITIVE MOAT: WHY RIVALS CAN'T CATCH UP

This is where I think most investors fundamentally undervalue TMO.

**Thermo Fisher has built something rare: a vertically integrated, sticky ecosystem** that competitors simply cannot replicate quickly.

### **The Switching Cost Fortress**

When a pharmaceutical company selects TMO's instruments for drug development, what actually happens?

1. They train their scientists on the system
2. They integrate the instruments into their lab workflows
3. Their data output is locked into TMO's software and informatics platforms
4. They build protocols and methodologies around the hardware

Now, can they switch to a competitor's equipment? **Theoretically, yes. Practically? It's extraordinarily expensive and disruptive.** It means retraining, re-validating results, potentially re-running experiments. For a pharma company with a drug in clinical trials, that's not acceptable.

**This is why TMO's customers have a 90%+ retention rate and continue to buy consumables for years.** It's not because they love the brand; it's because the business model makes switching prohibitively expensive.

Compare this to a commodity competitor like Avantor (AVTR)—they're getting margin-pressured exactly because they lack these switching costs. Their customers can shop around. TMO's customers are, in effect, locked in.

### **The Portfolio Breadth Defense**

TMO's latest product launches—EMPAD G2 detector and Orbitrap Isora mass spectrometer—aren't one-off innovations. They're part of a **systematic strategy to expand the ecosystem** into new customer verticals:

- **Material science** (EMPAD G2)
- **Environmental monitoring** (Orbitrap Isora)
- **Food authenticity** (Orbitrap Isora)
- **Geoscience** (Orbitrap)
- **Bioscience research** (traditional installed base)

This **multi-platform strategy makes TMO the one-stop-shop** for advanced research infrastructure. When a company can buy from one vendor that integrates seamlessly, they do it. It reduces vendor management burden, improves interoperability, and leverages existing relationships.

Competitors trying to compete on a single product face an uphill battle against a platform.
