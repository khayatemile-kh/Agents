## THE BOLLINGER BAND "TRAP" ARGUMENT CONFLATES SUPPORT/RESISTANCE WITH REVERSAL MECHANICS

The Aggressive Analyst argues that BMY trading $1.76 below the upper Bollinger Band ($68.68) sets up a "mean reversion kill zone." This is where their thinking becomes genuinely dangerous for portfolio management.

Let's be precise about what Bollinger Bands actually measure: they're volatility envelopes, not deterministic reversal points. The upper band represents approximately +2 standard deviations from the 20-SMA. A stock touching or exceeding the upper band doesn't automatically trigger mean reversion; it signals *elevated volatility*, not exhaustion.

In fact, in strong uptrends (which the technical report confirms BMY is in—price sits 8.19% above the 50-SMA and 17.61% above the 200-SMA), price frequently *rides* the upper Bollinger Band for extended periods. This isn't weakness; it's confirmation of strength. The band widens, price stays elevated, and the trend continues.

The Aggressive Analyst's prediction of a 7.6% pullback to $61.83 (the 50-SMA) is presented with false precision. The technical report shows the 50-SMA has been *rising consistently* throughout August (from $57.91 on 8/3 to $61.83 on 9/1). A rising moving average in an uptrend is typically *support*, not a reversal target. Price tests it, bounces, and continues higher.

We're not in mean reversion setup territory. We're in a market that's consolidating at resistance before breaking through. The proper portfolio management response here is to hold through consolidation, not to exit into the consolidation and pray for a re-entry opportunity that may never come.
