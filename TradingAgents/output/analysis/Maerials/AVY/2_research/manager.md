**Recommendation**: Hold

**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points that roughly offset each other, which is why I'm not comfortable committing strongly in either direction. The bull is right that the sequential operational trends are genuinely good: Q1-to-Q2 revenue, gross profit, operating income and net income all accelerated, margins expanded ~70-100bps, and the company throws off close to $1B of annual free cash flow while still funding dividends, buybacks and bolt-on M&A. A 31% ROE and 2.3x net debt/EBITDA are not signs of distress in a benign environment, and the valuation (19x P/E, trading near/below moving averages with neutral RSI) doesn't look stretched for a company showing margin leverage.

That said, the bear raises the more important structural questions rather than just nitpicking. First, the sequential acceleration story is undercut once you frame it year-over-year and against pre-downturn levels — this looks more like a bounce off a depressed 2024-2025 base than genuine share-gaining growth, and margin expansion could just as easily be inventory/cost normalization as durable pricing power in a business that is largely commodity-like and price-taking. Second, and more concretely, the 'debt repayment' line in the cash flow bridge is essentially zero ($1.7M) — nearly all FCF is going to buybacks, dividends and M&A, meaning leverage is not being worked down despite the bull's claim that management 'could' delever if it wanted to. Third, the ROE argument cuts both ways: 31% ROE on a comparatively thin $2.32B equity base versus $3.68B of debt is mechanically a leverage effect, and the bear's stress test (a mild 25% EBITDA decline pushing net debt/EBITDA to ~3.1x) is a reasonable illustration of how quickly the cushion could disappear if the recession probability moves off its current low base. Given the Fed holding rates elevated and no debt paydown occurring, the balance sheet has less margin for error than the bull's framing suggests.

Because the bull's operating momentum argument is credible but not yet proven out over multiple quarters/years, and the bear's leverage-and-cyclicality risk is real but contingent on a downturn that hasn't yet materialized (only 8% recession probability priced), I don't think the evidence clearly favors upgrading or downgrading conviction right now. The prudent stance is to hold the existing exposure, monitor the next 1-2 quarters for confirmation of whether margin gains are durable and whether management starts directing more FCF to deleveraging, and treat any further multiple compression as an opportunity to reassess rather than react.

**Strategic Actions**: 1. Maintain current AVY position size; do not add or trim materially until the next 1-2 quarterly prints clarify whether margin expansion persists ex-inventory/cost normalization and whether YoY (not just sequential) growth holds up.
2. Set a specific trigger to upgrade to Overweight/Buy: two consecutive quarters of margin expansion accompanied by visible debt paydown (net debt/EBITDA trending toward/below 2.0x) rather than 100% FCF deployment to buybacks/dividends/M&A.
3. Set a specific trigger to downgrade to Underweight/Sell: any confirmed uptick in recession probability, a covenant-relevant leverage move toward 3x+ net debt/EBITDA, or evidence that gross margin reverts back toward the 28-28.5% pre-expansion range.
4. Use options collars or modest hedges (e.g., protective puts financed by covered calls) if the position is large, given the asymmetric downside highlighted by the bear's leverage stress test, without giving up exposure to the bull's upside case.
5. Reassess valuation entry points if the stock falls further toward/below the 200-day SMA ($171.88) on no fundamental deterioration — that would be a better risk/reward add point than adding into current strength.
6. Continue monitoring debt repayment line item and capital allocation mix each quarter as the single most important leading indicator of whether the bull or bear leverage narrative is playing out.
