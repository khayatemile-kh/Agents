Impairments occur when **management was wrong about the value of past acquisitions.** IFF overpaid for businesses or assets that underperformed. The 2023-2025 impairment cascade ($2.59B in 2023 alone, plus $1.15B in 2025) signals **repeated capital allocation failures.**

**The data tells the damning story:**

| Year | Impairment Charges | Revenue | EBITDA | What This Means |
|------|-------------------|---------|--------|-----------------|
| 2023 | $2.59B | $11.48B | $779M | Management destroyed shareholder value through failed M&A |

**The critical insight:** Despite claiming operational improvements, **EBITDA in 2025 was $779M—identical to 2023.** That means **real cash generation hasn't recovered; it's just being masked by lower depreciation after the write-downs.**

The bull wants you to believe we're past the impairment phase. **I'm not convinced.** With the India antitrust investigation now live, what's the probability of further restatements, penalties, or forced divestitures that trigger *more* write-downs? ### **Claim #4: "Cash Generation Is Strong"—But It's Masking Deterioration**

The bull cites deleveraging (debt down $450M in 6 months) as evidence of "positive and accelerating cash generation."

**This is misleading.** Let me show you why:

**What actually generates cash:**
- Operating cash flow from net income: **Must verify against cash flow statement** (not provided in the bull's argument)
- Asset sales or working capital optimization: Can reduce debt *without* operational improvements
- Reduced capex or R&D: Another form of financial engineering

**The key metric the bull omits:** Free cash flow (Operating CF - Capex). Without this, we can't assess whether debt reduction reflects genuine operational strength or financial trickery.

**Here's the risk:** If IFF reduced debt primarily through:
- Deferring capital investments (weakens future competitiveness)
- Cutting R&D (critical for fragrance/flavor innovation)
- Asset sales (one-time, non-recurring)
- Working capital harvesting (pulling forward receivables, delaying payables)

**Then the "deleveraging" is illusory, and debt reduction will reverse once operational cash generation weakens.**

Given IFF's need to innovate in a competitive market (Givaudan, Firmenich), **cutting capex/R&D is not a sustainable strategy.** The bull hasn't addressed this risk.
