**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make points, but the bear's argument is more rigorous and better supported by the hard numbers presented. The bull's thesis rests almost entirely on the assumption that the -$730M H1 FCF and 580bps gross margin compression are "temporary/seasonal," but the bear correctly points out this reframing doesn't reconcile with the facts in evidence: (1) OCF didn't just dip, it collapsed 80% YoY ($2.55B to $507M) - this is a magnitude shift, not typical seasonal noise, and the bull's own comp ("H1 2025 had the same pattern") is factually undermined since H1 2025 Q2 was robust, not weak like H1 2026. (2) Q1 2026 posted an actual net loss and negative operating income - that's not consistent with "seasonal mix" alone, it signals real operating deterioration. (3) The leverage picture is concerning and the bull doesn't really address it: $19.8B total debt against $3.08B cash, net debt ~$14.9B, D/E ~78%, with OCF at depressed run-rate barely covering estimated interest expense, leaving little room for capex/dividends/debt paydown before drawing down cash or facilities. That's a real solvency-adjacent risk if the cash generation trough persists another quarter or two, not merely "noise." (4) The bull's margin recovery story requires taking on faith that commodity deflation, not competitive/demand erosion, is the driver - but revenue fell 10.6% YoY and margins still compressed by more than mix alone would suggest, which is more consistent with volume-driven demand weakness and/or pricing concessions than a benign commodity story. The valuation argument (16x P/E, discount to peers) is the bull's most defensible point, but valuation alone doesn't offset a business showing an actual quarterly net loss and severe cash conversion deterioration - cheap can get cheaper if the fundamental deterioration is real and ongoing. The infrastructure/public-works secular tailwind argument has some merit and shouldn't be dismissed entirely, but it doesn't yet show up in the numbers (margins/cash flow), which is what a market pricing on trailing/current data will react to. Given the extent and multiplicity of red flags (cash flow collapse, an actual quarterly net loss, elevated leverage against a shrinking cash-generation base, and margin compression exceeding what a benign mix/commodity story would produce) I'm siding with the bear thesis as carrying more weight this round. This isn't a strong "Sell/exit" case because there is real optionality in the infrastructure/public-works backlog and the stock is statistically cheap versus peers, so I don't think it warrants full capitulation - but the risk of continued deterioration, leverage strain, and a further guidance cut is high enough that adding to or holding a full position isn't prudent either. Net: lean bearish, trim exposure, and preserve capital until there's clear evidence (a quarter with positive FCF conversion and margin stabilization) that the trough thesis is actually playing out rather than assumed.
