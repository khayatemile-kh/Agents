**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points, but weighing them carefully: the bull's macro argument (stable rates, low recession risk) is a generic backdrop that supports many fintech names, not a company-specific catalyst. This debt profile amplifies risk if the profitability volatility continues.

On technicals, the bear's read of accelerating negative momentum (MACD collapsing 41% in a single day, RSI at 43 with room to fall further before oversold, potential lower Bollinger Band break targeting July lows near $76.49) is a more coherent near-term risk picture than the bull's "healthy pullback" framing. The bull's argument that 50/200 SMA support remains intact is fair, but it doesn't invalidate the point that short/intermediate momentum is deteriorating and could pressure price toward the 50 SMA or lower in coming weeks.

Net-net: the fundamental profitability instability plus elevated leverage plus deteriorating short-term technical momentum outweigh the bullish points about macro stability and one institutional buy. This doesn't rise to a full "Sell" because there are genuine long-term supports (200 SMA, restaurant vertical traction, net cash position, stable revenue in $6-6.6B range) and the bear case doesn't demonstrate structural business decline, just volatility and thin margins. Given the balance tilts toward caution but isn't a clear conviction sell, an Underweight rating recommending trimming exposure while retaining some position for optionality is the most defensible call.
