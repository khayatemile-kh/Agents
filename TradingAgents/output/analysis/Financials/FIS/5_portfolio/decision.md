No analyst produced a credible rebuttal to why the market ignored the catalyst and reacted negatively to the downgrade instead — that asymmetry is a meaningful, evidence-based tell.

Where the analysts diverge is on urgency and sizing, not direction. The Aggressive Analyst wants a moderate 20-30% trim on a bounce; the Conservative Analyst wants a heavier 30-50% cut immediately, citing cascading-downgrade risk; the Neutral Analyst correctly notes the internal tension in both stances (if solvency risk were truly acute, a 70-80% residual holding is inconsistent) and instead recommends a staged 25-35% reduction now rather than waiting on a bounce, since liquidity/bid support may not persist if downgrades cascade. The Neutral Analyst also rightly notes the benign macro backdrop (8% recession probability) argues against imminent systemic default, meaning this is more likely a value-repricing/deleveraging story than a bankruptcy-watch situation — which argues against a full Sell/zero-position stance, but does not argue for holding or adding.

Technically, price sits below all three key moving averages (10 EMA, 50 SMA, 200 SMA) with a negative, widening MACD histogram and RSI at 44.60 (neutral-to-bearish) — confirming the fundamental caution rather than offering a contradicting signal. Net: the quantifiable balance-sheet and liquidity risk, corroborated by the Wells Fargo downgrade and the market's rejection of the Ericsson catalyst, outweighs the speculative, unconfirmed upside case. This supports a disciplined Underweight — meaningful de-risking now, not full capitulation — since there is no disclosed covenant breach, default, or confirmed credit-rating cascade yet, and the debt did coincide with real (if value-destructive) asset expansion.
