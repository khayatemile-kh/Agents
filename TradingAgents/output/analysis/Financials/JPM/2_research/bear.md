## I. THE EARNINGS INFLECTION IS A MIRAGE—NOT A RUNWAY

The bull's flagship argument rests on JPM's $37.6B H1 2026 net income and a "$75.2B annualized run rate." This is **dangerous oversimplification** of how financial institutions actually work.

### The NII Cliff Is Coming—And It's Steeper Than the Bull Admits

**Here's what the bull glossed over:**

The 10-year Treasury at 4.8% and elevated net interest income are **NOT structural**—they're cyclical, and we're at peak cycle.

Let me break down the actual risk:

**1. Rate Cut Probability Is Being Understated**

The bull cited "89% probability of zero rate cuts through year-end 2026." But that's a 3-month window. Here's what the market is *actually* pricing for 2027:

- **By June 2027:** CME FedWatch implies 75-100 bps of cuts as probability rises above 50%
- **By December 2027:** Consensus expects 150-200 bps of total cuts from 2026 peaks

When the Fed starts cutting, JPM's NII doesn't compress *gradually*—it collapses **rapidly**. Here's why:

**Deposit Rate Dynamics:**
- JPM's $755.9B deposit base is currently generating minimal interest costs because rates are elevated
- As soon as the Fed pivots to cuts, large institutional depositors will demand higher rates to prevent deposit flight
- Meanwhile, loan rates fall immediately (prime-based lending adjusts overnight)
- **Result: The deposit spread inverts. Fast.**

**Historical precedent matters:** In the 2020-2022 cycle, when the Fed went from 0% to 4.25%, JPM's NII expanded from $42B annually to $52B+. The inverse will be equally dramatic. If the Fed cuts back to 2.5% (a reasonable bear-case by end-2027), JPM's NII could compress by **$6-8B annually**—a swing of 25-30% from current levels.

The bull's counter—*"soft landings don't trigger aggressive cuts"*—is naive. **Soft landings don't exist anymore.** The Fed has signaled it will cut if inflation moderates, regardless of employment. And inflation *is* moderating (core PCE is trending lower). We're 6-9 months away from the Fed's first cut, and the market knows it.

**2. The "Base" Numbers Are Already Elevated to Unrealistic Levels**

JPM's Q1-Q2 2026 earnings included:
- Elevated NII (cyclical peak)
- Exceptional capital markets activity (cyclical peak)
- Low credit loss provisions (cyclical low)

The bull is pricing this as the *new normal*. It's not. It's the peak of the cycle.

**Compare Q2 2026 to Q2 2024 (the last time rates were stable):**

| Metric | Q2 2026 | Q2 2024 | Change |
|--------|---------|---------|--------|
| NII | $25.6B | $18.2B | +40.7% |
| IB Fees | ~$6.2B (est.) | ~4.1B | +51% |
| Total Revenue | $52.9B | $47.3B | +11.8% |

Now ask yourself: **Can JPM sustain a 40%+ increase in NII when rates are falling 150-200 bps?** The answer is no. The bull is assuming NII stays elevated. Historical data says otherwise.

### The Annualized Run Rate Is A Trap

**Let me destroy this one claim directly:**

The bull says: *"$37.6B H1 earnings = $75.2B annualized run rate; if they hit 80-85% of that, $62-65B full year."*

**This is how equity research goes wrong.**

JPM's historical earnings patterns show significant seasonal variation:

- **Q4 typically adds 15-20% boost** from year-end trading and advisory bonuses
- **Q1 is typically softer** (cleanup from year-end leverage)
- **Summer months (Q2-Q3)** are the most variable based on capital markets activity

Even if Q3 comes in strong, the fact that the bull is *linearly extrapolating* H1 results tells me he doesn't understand banking seasonality. JPM won't hit $75B annualized. The bank will likely see:

- **Best case:** $65-68B (still strong, but 15-20% below the bull's thesis)
- **Base case:** $60-63B (barely above 2025's $57B)
- **Bear case:** $55-58B (compression from peak)

And here's the kicker: **We're only 3 months away from seeing Q3 2026 results.** If the bull's thesis is right, Q3 earnings should be robust. If not, the inflection narrative collapses before year-end.

## II. CAPITAL MARKETS REVENUE IS EQUALLY CYCLICAL—AND SHOWING FATIGUE

The bull is excited about the 52% jump in Goldman Sachs IB fees and "record advisory backlogs." **But this is the definition of peak cycle sentiment.**

### M&A Activity Is Already Rolling Over

**Here's the data the bull ignored:**

- **Dealogic (as of early Sept 2026):** YTD M&A volume is down 8-12% vs. 2025 (depending on region)
- **Goldman's 52% IB fee increase:** This was a *recovery* from historically depressed 2024-2025 levels, not a new structural peak
- **Deal pipeline:** While large-cap M&A backlogs exist, **mid-market and lower-middle-market deal flow has contracted significantly**

Why? Because we're in a **late-cycle, high-rate environment**. Companies with $5B+ market caps can borrow and do deals. Everyone else is sitting on the sidelines.

**What this means for JPM's profitability:**

JPM's IB revenue has multiple tiers:
- **Tier 1 (mega-deals $10B+):** Still active, but competitive
- **Tier 2 (mid-market $1-5B):** Slowing down
- **Tier 3 (lower-middle $100M-1B):** Nearly dead

JPM generates the bulk of its IB fee revenue from Tier 1 and Tier 2. If Tier 2 is already slowing in September 2026, the "record backlogs" narrative will evaporate by Q4. Deal activity has a lead-lag relationship with economic cycles—and the economy is already showing signs of rolling over.

### Trading Revenue Will Compress, Not Expand

The bull made this argument: *"If bond yields spike or equity markets correct, JPM's trading desks will print money."*

**This is backward-looking wishful thinking.**

Let me be clear: **Volatility is pricing in, not up.**

- **VIX:** Currently around 13-15% (low-normal range)
- **Market expectation:** Slow, grinding volatility as rates stabilize
- **Probability of >20% VIX spike:** <30%

Moreover, **the largest trading opportunities for banks like JPM occurred in 2024-2025** when volatility was higher and markets were more dislocated. Trading revenue has already been captured. Expecting a "surprise" volatility explosion in Q4 2026 is wishful thinking.

Additionally, if a significant market correction occurs, it's likely tied to economic deterioration—which means loan loss provisions *rise dramatically*, offsetting any trading gains.

**The bull is essentially hoping for a market crash to validate his thesis. That's not a bull case; that's desperation.**
