- Full year 2025: $57.0 billion
- Full year 2024: $58.5 billion

**Why is this happening?**

### Net Interest Income Surge: The $25.6B Tailwind

The 10-year Treasury yield has risen to 4.8%, and JPM's net interest income has expanded 10% to $25.6 billion. This is not a temporary blip—**this is structural support for the bank's profitability**. 

Here's why this matters for valuation:

When the Fed holds rates steady (89% probability of zero rate cuts through year-end 2026), NII remains elevated. JPM's deposit base of $755.9 billion gives the bank enormous pricing power. Higher rates on loans + sticky low-cost deposits = wider spreads. This is Economics 101, and it's working beautifully for JPM.

**Bear might say:** *"But if rates fall later, NII contracts."*

**My response:** 
- Current consensus is for a **soft landing** (58% probability), not a recession. Soft landings don't trigger aggressive Fed cutting cycles; they trigger slow, measured cuts.
- Even if the Fed cuts 75-100 bps over 18 months (a dovish scenario), JPM's NII would compress gradually, not collapse.
- Meanwhile, JPM is capturing **record capital markets revenue** right now—and that's stickier than you think.

## IV. BALANCE SHEET STRENGTH: THE FORTRESS FOUNDATION

Let me address the elephant in the room: **Yes, JPM's net debt rose to $223.1B from $156.6B at year-end 2025.** 

But here's the critical context:

### Why Rising Debt Isn't a Problem

1. **It's intentional and measured:** The debt-to-equity ratio is 1.42x, which is *moderate* for a diversified global bank. JPM's rating agencies and regulators are comfortable with this leverage.

2. **It's funding growth, not funding deficits:** JPM's loan portfolio grew $37.7B in Q2 alone. The bank is deploying capital into **higher-yielding lending activities**. That's a *positive* use of leverage.

3. **Total assets just hit $5.015 trillion**, up from $4.90 trillion. The bank is growing balance sheet, not shrinking it. And when you're growing into a $5T+ balance sheet, leverage ratios naturally adjust.

### Capital Adequacy is Fortress-Like

- **Stockholders' Equity:** $374.6 billion (+2.9% QoQ)
- **Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) Ratio:** Well above regulatory minimums (est. 12-13%)
- **Tangible Book Value:** $289.3 billion

JPMorgan can weather a serious market correction. The bank isn't levered to the hilt; it's appropriately leveraged to generate returns.
