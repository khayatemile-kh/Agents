## **The Tax Rate "Permanent Structural Improvement" is Wishful Thinking**

The aggressive analyst claims the tax rate drop from 23.1% to 16.7% is a "permanent structural improvement" that validates the earnings narrative. This is precisely where aggressive analysts often get trapped by their own optimism.

Tax rates are NOT permanent. They're contingent on:
- Regulatory and legislative shifts (Congress could eliminate favorable tax provisions)
- The geographic mix of earnings (if NDAQ's growth comes from higher-tax jurisdictions, the rate rises)
- The composition of income (capital gains treatment vs. ordinary income)
- Audit exposure and repatriation rules

Calling a 620 basis point tax rate improvement "permanent" without hedging that language is risk management malpractice. The research team correctly flagged that 60% of NDAQ's net income growth in 2025 was *attributable to* the tax rate drop, not operating performance. That means if tax rates normalize just 3-4 points higher, earnings growth rates drop from 60% to 25-30% instantly.

And here's what really concerns me: the market hasn't fully priced in the tail risk of tax normalization. If a new administration or Congress reverses these provisions, NDAQ has a 15-20% downside surprise embedded in current valuations. We'd be adding to that tail risk if we increased exposure now.

Higher rates reduce IPO volumes (negative for NDAQ's capital access segments), reduce trading volatility (negative for market services), and increase the discount rate applied to financial earnings. The forward P/E could easily expand to 22-24x if rates persist or rise further. At that multiple applied to lower-than-expected earnings, we're looking at 25-30% downside.

The PEG ratio of 1.55x that the aggressive analyst cites as "supporting" aggressive positioning actually does the opposite. A PEG above 1.0 means growth is not cheap on a relative basis.
