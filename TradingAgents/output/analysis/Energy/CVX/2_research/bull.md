## **PART 2: THE ENERGY MACRO BACKDROP IS SUPPORTIVE**

### **Low Recession Risk = Sustained Oil Demand**

The prediction markets are pricing in only an **8% recession probability by end of 2026.** This is significant because:

- Oil demand is correlated with economic activity
- A resilient economy supports $150+ oil prices (the baseline implied by market pricing)
- With low recession risk, we're unlikely to see demand destruction from economic collapse

For CVX, this means **forward cash flows are more predictable and likely higher** than during recessionary scenarios.

### **OPEC Fragmentation Creates a Supply-Constrained Upside**

Here's where I'll challenge the pessimists head-on. Yes, there's a **38% probability of OPEC member exits by end of 2026.** But—and this is critical—**fragmentation is likely to *tighten* oil markets, not loosen them.**

Consider the dynamics:
- If OPEC fractures, discipline breaks down → smaller producers flood the market with discounted oil
- Larger OPEC members (Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Iran) respond by **cutting production to support prices**
- Net result: **Tighter global supply curve** and **structural support for oil prices above $140/bbl**

CVX *benefits* from this scenario. As an integrated producer with global optionality, Chevron can:
- Increase production at their ultra-low-cost fields (Guyana, Kazakhstan)
- Maintain capital discipline (no need to chase production with weak commodity prices)
- Return cash to shareholders via dividends and buybacks

The bear case (OPEC collapse = oil price crash) is **less likely** than the bull case (fragmentation = supply discipline through higher marginal costs).
