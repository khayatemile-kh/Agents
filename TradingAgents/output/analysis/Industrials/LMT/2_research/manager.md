**Recommendation**: Hold

**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points that roughly offset each other, which is why I'm not comfortable committing capital aggressively in either direction right now.

On the bull side, the forward EPS growth of 20.6% (TTM $27.12 to forward $32.72) is real and reflects genuine visibility into Pentagon interceptor demand and international expansion via the Rheinmetall partnership. The stock is sitting right at its 200-day SMA support ($548 vs current $544.50), and RSI at 40 is weak but not capitulation-level oversold. The structural case for elevated defense spending tied to persistent Iran tensions (89% probability of no diplomatic resolution by Sept 30) is a legitimate near-term tailwind, and LMT's position as one of few qualified interceptor suppliers is a real moat.

But the bear's pushback lands hard on valuation and execution risk. The PEG ratio of 1.04x shows there's no actual discount once you adjust for growth - the "cheap forward P/E" argument is largely an illusion. The P/B ratio of 13.95x is extreme and signals the market has already priced in a lot of optimism. Forward EPS of $32.72 is a consensus estimate, not guidance, and fixed-price contract structures mean margin compression is a real risk if interceptor production costs overrun - which is common in defense manufacturing ramps. The bear's point about binary geopolitical risk is the most important one: the bull's entire structural demand thesis rests on Iran tensions persisting, but that 10-11% probability of de-escalation is a real tail risk that could unwind the defense trade quickly, and defense stocks have historically corrected sharply on exactly this kind of news. The sector rotation evidence (smaller vendors outperforming LMT YTD) is also a genuine red flag suggesting smart money isn't rewarding the large-cap incumbent narrative right now.

Technically, the stock has broken its 10 EMA, MACD has turned negative, and it's down 8.6% from August highs. The bull calls this "accumulation" but the bear's alternative read - distribution - is at least as plausible given the volume spike and momentum breakdown. Neither side has provided a decisive tiebreaker: the earnings growth story is credible but not yet a screaming bargain given elevated P/B and full PEG, and the technical picture is genuinely mixed rather than clearly oversold or clearly broken.

Given that the debate is balanced - real earnings growth and moat vs. full valuation, execution risk, and binary geopolitical tail risk - I'm recommending Hold rather than committing to either Buy or Sell conviction. This isn't a case where one side's argument is clearly weaker; it's a legitimate toss-up between structural growth and valuation/risk concerns.

**Strategic Actions**: Maintain current LMT position size without adding or trimming meaningfully at this level. Do not chase the dip as a "gift" per the bull thesis, but do not exit or short given the credible earnings growth and moat arguments either.
