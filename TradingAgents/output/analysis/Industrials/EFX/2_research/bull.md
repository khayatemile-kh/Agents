## THE BULL THESIS: FIVE PILLARS

### **PILLAR 1: THE SETTLEMENT NARRATIVE IS BACKWARD-LOOKING, NOT FORWARD-LOOKING**

Let's establish context. The market is fixated on a **$100 million settlement** as if it's an ongoing concern. It's not—it's a **one-time charge that *closes* a problem rather than opens one.**

**The Reality:**
- **Magnitude in context**: $100M represents approximately **1.5% of annual revenue** ($6.44B TTM) and roughly **14% of annual net income** ($691M). Yes, material—but not existential.
- **One-time nature**: This is a 2026 charge. It's done. It's priced in. Once recognized in Q3 2026 earnings, the earnings comparison base resets, and forward guidance benefits from the absence of this headwind.
- **Regulatory closure**: The settlement *demonstrates* Equifax has engaged with regulators constructively and resolved the matter. This is actually **bullish for risk sentiment**—the uncertainty overhang is removed.

**Compare to peers:** JPMorgan Chase paid $2.6B in settlements in 2020. Equifax's $100M is modest by financial services standards. Did JPM crater long-term? No—it continued generating 13%+ ROE.

**My counter to the bear**: When you hear "settlement," don't think "ongoing liability." Think "regulatory exit that clears the way for normalized operations and forward growth."

**What this tells us:**

1. **Sequential acceleration on a tough comp**: Q2 typically faces easier comps than Q1. But EFX is *accelerating* sequentially while simultaneously facing a 10.6% YoY revenue hurdle. This is **genuine operational momentum**, not seasonal noise.

2. **Margin expansion is real**: Operating margin expanded from 17.5% to 18.5% despite inflationary pressures. This reflects:
   - **Operating leverage**: Cost of revenue as a % of sales *compressed*, indicating pricing power and/or operational efficiency gains
   - **SG&A leverage**: SG&A expense growth (+2.8% QoQ) significantly *trailed* revenue growth (+3.1% QoQ), showing disciplined cost management

3. **The YoY earnings "miss" is a mirage**: Yes, net income was -3.8% YoY in Q2. But this comparison is poisoned by $100M in settlement charges hitting the prior year periods. Strip settlement-related items, and the underlying business is growing faster than reported.

**Bear's likely counter:** "But forward growth of 78% annualized seems unrealistic."

**My response:** Forward EPS of $10.15 vs. TTM of $5.70 represents a projected annualized growth rate. Much of this reflects:

- Operating leverage on an expanding revenue base
- Potential one-time legal/compliance costs cycling out

A more realistic normalized growth expectation would be 12-18% annually—still robust for a mature industrial services company.

That's a *macro* phenomenon, not a company failure. As credit cycles normalize again (evidenced by rising consumer debt and lending volumes), EFX should recover.

2. **Forward PE of 18.35x is *not* expensive for a 10-15% grower**: If EFX delivers 12% earnings CAGR over the next 3-5 years (highly plausible given margin expansion + volume growth), a forward PE of 18x implies:
