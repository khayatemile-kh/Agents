**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: RTX has a massive $289B backlog, is riding genuine defense demand tailwinds (Tomahawk contract, allied rearmament), and delivered strong 2025 earnings growth (+41% net income, +240bps margin expansion) - but the stock trades at a rich 35x TTM P/E (25.6x forward) and has fallen ~9% in two weeks since the Tomahawk announcement peak.

The bull's strongest points: the backlog is diversified across commercial and defense, giving multi-year revenue visibility that's rare among industrials; margin expansion is already showing up in actual reported numbers (not just guidance) across multiple quarters, which is empirical evidence, not speculation; and the forward PEG framing, while a bit generous, does suggest growth is not fully priced given the size of the EPS growth guided.

The bear's strongest points land harder on scrutiny: (1) the post-announcement stock decline is a real, verifiable data point that undercuts the bull's 'gift-wrapped opportunity' framing - if the market wanted to reward this news, it would have sustained the rally instead of fading it 9% in two weeks; (2) the Tomahawk ramp from 60 to 1,000 units/year is an extraordinary manufacturing execution challenge, and fixed-price defense contracts historically compress margins during ramp phases (F-35 precedent is a fair analogy) - the bull's $2-3B EBITDA estimate assumes flawless execution with no basis given; (3) backlog composition matters - conflating low-margin commercial aerospace backlog with high-margin defense backlog overstates the earnings power of the $289B figure, and commercial aerospace backlog is genuinely exposed to Boeing/Airbus production delays which are a well-documented, ongoing risk; (4) geopolitical demand, while currently strong, is inherently less predictable than the bull's 'structural' framing implies - defense budgets have historically retrenched after de-escalation, and this is a real historical pattern, not a strawman.

Net assessment: the bear's rebuttal is more rigorous because it directly confronts the bull's key numerical claims (the $2-3B EBITDA estimate, the PEG-based undervaluation claim) with concrete execution-risk and margin-compression counterarguments grounded in precedent, and it uses the actual observed price action as evidence that the market itself is skeptical. The bull's case relies on optimistic extrapolation of announced contract value into earnings without acknowledging ramp risk, and treats a diversified backlog as unambiguously bullish without addressing margin-mix issues raised by the bear.

That said, I don't think this rises to a full Sell / exit call - the underlying business is not broken, revenue growth is real, backlog is real, and management has genuine tailwinds; the risk is much more about entry price and near-term execution than a structural business impairment. Given the stock is already down 9% pricing in some skepticism, and given a 35x trailing multiple leaves little room for error if Tomahawk ramp execution disappoints or commercial aerospace backlog conversion slips, the prudent stance is to lean cautious without calling for a full exit. This points to Underweight: trim/reduce exposure and wait for either a better entry price or clearer evidence of successful Tomahawk ramp execution and backlog conversion before adding back.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing RTX exposure by 20-30% into current strength/any bounce toward $210-215, taking some risk off the table given the rich 35x TTM P/E and recent 9% post-Tomahawk-announcement decline signaling market skepticism about flawless execution.
2) Do not initiate new/incremental long positions at current levels ($205); the bull's EBITDA uplift estimates for the Tomahawk ramp assume best-case execution that historical fixed-price defense ramps (e.g., F-35 precedent) argue against - wait for confirmation of margin trajectory through at least 2 more quarters of Tomahawk production data before adding back.
3) Set a re-entry watch list trigger: reconsider adding exposure if the stock retraces toward the $180-190 range (better risk/reward against 2026 EPS guidance of $7.86) or if RTX reports clean evidence of Tomahawk margin expansion without cost overruns in upcoming quarterly prints.
4) Monitor Boeing/Airbus production and delivery cadence closely as a leading indicator for commercial aerospace backlog conversion risk - any further supply chain disruption or delivery delays from these OEMs should trigger further trimming.
5) Maintain a residual core position (do not fully exit) to retain exposure to the genuine structural defense demand and backlog optionality, but size it conservatively (e.g., reduce to 60-70% of prior position weight) reflecting the balance of real fundamental strength against valuation and execution risk highlighted in the bear case.
6) Use options (e.g., covered calls against retained shares) to generate income and partially hedge downside while awaiting more clarity on Tomahawk ramp execution and geopolitical demand durability.
