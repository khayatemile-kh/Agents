The bulls point to $1.147B in TTM free cash flow and say *"debt pays down in 2.2 years!"* But this is mathematically naive:

1. **LDOS doesn't get to keep all FCF for debt paydown** — they have:

### **The Leverage Trap: Why 2.76x Debt-to-EBITDA is Dangerous for LDOS**

The bulls say "5.7x leverage on FCF is reasonable for defense contractors." **No, it's not.** Here's why:

**Defense contractors SHOULD operate at 1.5x-2.0x Debt-to-EBITDA because:**
- Government contracts are TIGHT margin (LDOS's operating margin is 11.7%, not spectacular)
- Contract delays or cancellations cascade into cash flow crises
- Refinancing risk exists if rates rise or credit markets seize
- LDOS has ZERO pricing power (government is the customer — they dictate terms)

At 2.76x Debt-to-EBITDA, LDOS is operating near the upper bound of acceptable leverage. **Any operational hiccup becomes a credit event:**

- Miss one major contract renewal? EBITDA drops 10-15%, and you're at 3.2x leverage (covenant territory)
- Government budget cuts? EBITDA compresses, and suddenly refinancing becomes difficult
- Interest rates rise unexpectedly? Refinancing costs blow up

### **The Cash Flow Quality is Questionable**

Let me dig into the cash flow composition:

**Q2 2026 Operating Cash Flow: $793M**
- But **Q1 2026 was only $301M** 
- And **Q2 2025 was only $486M**

**Free Cash Flow is NOT earnings.** If LDOS's operating cash flow bounces around this much, the underlying business profitability is **volatile and dependent on government payment timing**, not stable recurring revenue.

Compare this to true recurring revenue businesses (e.g., SaaS companies) that deliver predictable FCF quarter after quarter. LDOS does not have that profile.
