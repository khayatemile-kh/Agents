**Recommendation**: Overweight

**Rationale**: Weighing both sides, the bear case lands some fair hits but doesn't overturn the core thesis. On leverage: yes, net debt of $5.28B and debt/EBITDA of 2.76x deserve monitoring, and the bear is right that quarterly FCF is lumpy (Q1 $270M vs Q2 $761M) rather than a clean $3B run-rate. But even using the bear's own conservative $1.98B annualized FCF figure, LDOS still comfortably services and pays down debt over a reasonable horizon while funding dividends/buybacks — this is not a distressed balance sheet, especially with a 1.63x current ratio and debt already down $366M quarter-over-quarter. The 'negative tangible book value' point is standard for services roll-ups with goodwill and isn't a distinguishing red flag versus peers.

On contracts: the bear correctly notes the $301M cyber deal is small in isolation (~0.34% of revenue) and that backlog-to-revenue conversion isn't guaranteed. That's a fair discount to the bull's more excitable framing. However, the bear doesn't actually refute the $5B in Q2 awards or provide evidence of margin decay — the bear cites historical (2018-2022) execution issues, which is stale and doesn't reflect the current print (Q2 beat, strong awards, SAIC peer confirming sector demand). Flat 11-12% operating margin is consistent with the mature government-IT model, not a warning sign of deterioration.

Valuation is the deciding factor: a forward PE near 10.5x against ROE of ~28%, positive award momentum, a peer (SAIC) re-rating higher, and a benign macro backdrop (low recession probability, rate stability supportive of government contracting) skews risk/reward to the upside. The bear's technical narrative ('bull trap') is a reasonable caution but the bulls' reading of consolidation after an overbought RSI move is equally plausible and the stock remains well above its 50 SMA.

Net: the bear raises legitimate balance-sheet and backlog-quality caveats that argue against a full-conviction 'Buy' or aggressive sizing, but doesn't present evidence of impairment, margin collapse, or contract losses that would justify Underweight/Sell. This nets out to a constructive but risk-aware Overweight rather than an unqualified Buy.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Increase LDOS position size gradually rather than establishing a full position at once — scale in on weakness toward the $130-135 support zone (near the 10 EMA/recent consolidation low) to respect the bear's 'bull trap' risk.
2) Size the position at a moderate overweight (e.g., 1.25-1.5x benchmark weight) rather than a maximum conviction bet, reflecting the legitimate leverage and backlog-quality caveats raised in the bear case.
3) Set a monitoring trigger on debt/EBITDA — if it moves toward/above 3.0x on the next print, or if quarterly FCF reverts sharply below the ~$500M/quarter average, treat as a signal to pause further adds and reassess.
4) Track margin trend explicitly each quarter (currently flat ~11-12%) — sustained expansion would validate the bull backlog-conversion thesis and support adding further; continued flatness caps upside and argues against upgrading to full Buy.
5) Use SAIC's multiple as a relative valuation checkpoint — if LDOS's forward PE gap to SAIC narrows meaningfully without fundamental deterioration, take partial profits into the re-rating.
6) Maintain a stop-loss/review level below the 50 SMA (~$120) to protect against a broader thesis break if the pullback extends beyond normal consolidation.
