## SECTION 2: THE OPERATIONAL "STRENGTH" ARGUMENT IGNORES THE REAL PROBLEM

### 78.7% Gross Margins Mean Nothing When Revenue is Decelerating

The bull makes a big deal of gross margins. Yes, they're 78.7%. That's nice. But here's what gross margins don't tell you:

**Gross margin is NOT a profit.** It's the money left after direct costs. It means nothing if you can't convert it to operating income efficiently. Let me show you the real problem:

| Period | Revenue | Gross Profit | Gross Margin | Operating Income | Op. Margin |
|--------|---------|--------------|--------------|------------------|-----------|
| Q2 2026 | $925M | $728M | 78.7% | $76M | 8.2% |
| Q1 2026 | $897M | $701M | 78.2% | $3M | 0.3% |
| Q4 2025 | $900M | $707M | 78.6% | $49M | 5.5% |
| Q3 2025 | $834M | $661M | 79.3% | -$51M | -6.1% |

**What this actually shows:**

The company is struggling to convert gross profit to operating income. In Q3 2025, despite 79.3% gross margins, they had an operating *loss* of $51M. In Q1 2026, despite 78.2% gross margins, they barely broke even operationally.

**This isn't a "pricing power" story—it's an operating expense problem.** The company is bloated. It can't efficiently manage its $2.7B cost base relative to revenue generation. When revenue softens (which it is), the operating leverage works backward. We saw that in Q1 and Q3.

**The YoY comparison is a rear-view mirror that obscures the windshield.**
