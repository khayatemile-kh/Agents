**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points that roughly offset each other, which is why I'm not comfortable committing to a strong directional call here. The bull is right that EQIX sits at the center of a genuine secular trend—AI/interconnection demand—and the top-line numbers (13%+ revenue growth, 22%+ EBITDA growth YoY, expanding gross margins) show real operating momentum, not just narrative. The Net Debt/EBITDA framing (rather than D/E) is also the correct lens for a REIT, and 3.8-4.1x is not an emergency level in isolation.

That's a meaningfully different picture from "deleveraging on track," and it directly undercuts the bull's Part II conclusion. If the company is funding growth capex with debt while FCF stays negative, the leverage ratio's improvement is coming mechanically from EBITDA annualization math rather than actual balance-sheet delevering, which is a weaker foundation than the bull presented.

At the same time, the bear's regulatory risk claim (72% probability of state moratoriums) and competitive-threat concerns were asserted but not substantiated with the same rigor as the FCF/debt discrepancy, so I don't weight those as heavily. Net effect: the fundamental growth story is intact and this is not a "sell the business" situation, but the combination of stretched valuation, negative FCF, and rising net debt outpacing EBITDA growth means this is not the moment to add or press the position either. The two theses cancel out enough that I'd rather sit at balanced exposure and demand cleaner FCF/deleveraging evidence before upgrading, or a clear multiple compression / guidance miss before downgrading.
