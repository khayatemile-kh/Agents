## The "Rate-Neutral Environment" Is Not Supportive—It's a Trap

Now let's address his macro argument, because this is where conservative discipline needs to override aggressive conviction.

The aggressive analyst correctly notes that an 89% probability of no Fed rate cuts through end of 2026 is "supportive for REITs." His logic: Stable rates mean stable cost of capital, which removes downside surprise risk. **But this misunderstands how REITs trade in a tightening cycle aftermath.**

Here's the actual mechanism: REITs trade on two axes—(1) discount rate (which is pinned by the risk-free rate and credit spreads) and (2) growth expectations. When rates are rising and stay elevated, option 1 is *locked in*. The REIT has limited cap rate compression upside because the Fed isn't cutting. So all of the return has to come from *earnings growth.* And that's where DLR's 63x multiple becomes a vulnerability.

Let me point to the exact evidence in the macro report: **Only an 8% probability of US recession by end of 2026.** The aggressive analyst treats this as "supportive for capex spending." But he's ignoring the base case of what happens *after* a non-recessionary 2026. Economic cycles don't just flatline. If we avoid recession in 2026, you're actually *increasing* the probability of a slowdown in 2027–2028 as the Fed potentially shifts to a rate-cutting cycle (based on growth moderation, not crisis).

**And when that happens, REIT multiples compress because the high-growth narrative unwinds.**

I'm not predicting a recession. I'm saying that betting on a 63x multiple today assumes that hyperscaler capex spending will remain at current elevated levels through 2027–2028 and beyond. That's a *long* growth visibility window for a company whose near-term revenue contribution from strategic wins is immaterial.

The fundamentals report shows DLR's TTM EBITDA at $3.21B and net income at $758M. At 63x forward PE, the market is pricing in earnings per share growth that requires either (a) significant margin expansion, (b) continued capex acceleration, or (c) multiple multiple expansion *itself* (i.e., the stock gets re-rated higher). None of these are guaranteed.
