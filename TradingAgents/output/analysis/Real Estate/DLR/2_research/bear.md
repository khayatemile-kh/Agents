## **1. THE VALUATION IS INDEFENSIBLE—EVEN BY REIT STANDARDS**

### The Bull Wants to Reframe the PE Multiple. Don't Fall for It.

The bull argues: *"The 63x Forward PE isn't expensive—it's pricing in scarcity value."*

**This is nonsense, and here's why:**

#### A. **Compare DLR to Actual Peers—It's Trading at a Bubble Premium**

Let me show you the reality:
- **DLR Forward PE: 63.24x**
- **Equinix (EQIX, largest competitor): ~25-28x**
- **American Tower (AMT, tower REIT): ~20-22x**
- **Prologis (PLD, industrial REIT): ~18-20x**
- **Average REIT Multiple: 12-16x**

DLR is trading at **2.4x the multiple of its direct competitor (Equinix)** and **3.5x broader REIT averages.** The bull wants to explain this away by saying *"but DLR has better moats!"* 

**Except it doesn't.** Equinix operates in the same geographies, serves the same hyperscaler customers, and has comparable operational metrics. Yet it trades at half the multiple. Why? **Because the market is being irrational about DLR specifically.**

#### B. **PEG Ratio of 11.84x Reveals the Real Problem: Growth Doesn't Justify Valuation**

Here's the data:
- **PEG Ratio: 11.84x** (typically, <2.0 is considered reasonable)
- **Implied growth rate priced in: ~5-6% annually**

But look at DLR's actual recent revenue growth:
- **Q2 2026: $1.635B**
- **Q1 2026: $1.635B** (flat)
- **Q4 2025: $1.577B** (~3.7% growth QoQ)
- **Q3 2025: $1.493B** (~5.6% growth QoQ)

**Average recent growth: 3-4% quarterly, which annualizes to roughly 12-16% only when outliers are included.** But normalized, it's **closer to 4-5%.** The market is pricing this in, sure—but then charging a **2.4x premium on top of that** for "scarcity."

**The math doesn't work.** A stock growing at 5% annually shouldn't trade at 63x earnings. Period.

#### C. **The "Regulatory Moat" Argument Backfires When You Read the Fine Print**

The bull points to the **72% probability of data center moratoria** as a *positive* for DLR, arguing it creates scarcity and pricing power.

**I'll turn this around:** Moratoria are a **regulatory guillotine waiting to drop on DLR's growth plans.**

Here's what actually happens when moratoria pass:

1. **Existing capacity becomes stranded.** Yes, prices might rise short-term. But if demand is restricted (because customers can't build nearby and must sign longer leases elsewhere), revenue growth *plateaus*.
2. **DLR's expansion plans get blocked.** The Singapore and Swiss wins are *future* capacity. If moratoria accelerate in the US, Europe, or Asia, DLR's pipeline of new builds gets **strangled**. The bull is celebrating wins that might never reach profitability.
3. **Regulatory arbitrage becomes the game.** Instead of building globally, DLR gets forced into compliance negotiations. Compliance = higher CapEx, lower margins.

**The "scarcity moat" is actually a regulatory bear trap.** When supply constraints bite hard enough, customers get desperate and *lobby for deregulation*—or they move workloads to less-regulated geographies (cloud regions in Middle East, Africa, etc.). DLR's stranded capacity becomes a liability, not an asset.

## **2. THE SINGAPORE WIN IS MARKETING FLUFF—NOT MATERIAL REVENUE**

### Let's Do the Math the Bull Wants to Ignore

The bull argues: *"Singapore validates demand and proves execution momentum."*

**Let's count the actual dollars:**

#### A. **The Revenue Math Doesn't Add Up**

The bull claims:
- 50MW facility at 80% utilization = $20M+ annual revenue
- 15MW Swiss facility = $6M+ annual revenue
- Total: ~$26M incremental annual revenue

**DLR's current revenue base: $6.54B annually**

**$26M incremental = 0.4% revenue growth from headline wins.**

That's... not material. That's **noise.** For context, DLR needs to grow revenue by $130M+ annually (2% growth on its base) just to maintain valuation multiples as it scales. Two "strategic wins" add 0.4% and the market celebrates like it's a game-changer?

#### B. **These Facilities Won't Be Profitable for 2-3 Years**

Here's what the bull glosses over:
- **Singapore 50MW: Not operational until 2027-2028** (likely)
- **Swiss 15MW: Not operational until 2028**
- **CapEx required to build: $150-200M** per facility (data centers are expensive)
- **Ramp period: 18-24 months to reach profitability** post-completion

**Translation:** DLR is spending $200-300M+ today to add $26M in annual revenue that won't materialize for 24+ months. The ROI is **8-12% eventually**—which is *below* DLR's cost of capital (~6-7% on leveraged debt).

**These "wins" are value-destructive on a present-value basis.**

#### C. **These Wins Were Already Priced In**

The stock popped 2% on the Singapore news. That's a **$1.38B market cap increase** on news of $20M annual revenue addition (assuming a 70x multiple). The market immediately **over-capitalized** the value of this deal.

**Where's the upside now?** It's already reflected in the stock price. The bull is asking you to buy on confirmation of news that's already been priced in.
