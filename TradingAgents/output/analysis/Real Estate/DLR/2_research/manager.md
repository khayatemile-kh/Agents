**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points that roughly offset each other, so I can't commit to a strong directional call here. The bull is right that DLR sits on real secular demand: the Singapore and Zurich wins demonstrate the company can still win competitive RFPs against top-tier peers, take-or-pay hyperscaler leases provide revenue visibility, and cash generation (TTM FCF ~$3.77B, EBITDA margin ~47.5%) comfortably covers the dividend. Regulatory moratoria cutting both ways is a fair point too — restricted supply can support pricing on the existing footprint even if it caps new development. Technically, the pullback from $200 to $183 with RSI easing from 63 to ~41 and price sitting near the lower Bollinger Band ($184.82) does look more like a digestion phase than a breakdown, and the risk/reward on a bounce isn't unreasonable.

But the bear lands the harder blow on valuation math. A 63x forward PE and an 11.84x PEG against actual quarterly revenue growth that's decelerating toward the mid-single digits is a real disconnect — you don't get to wave away a 2.4x premium to Equinix, DLR's closest direct comp, just by invoking 'scarcity.' The bear's revenue arithmetic on the Singapore/Zurich wins is also more rigorous than the bull's: $26M of incremental annual revenue against a $6.54B run-rate is genuinely immaterial today, the facilities won't be online for 2+ years, and the market already capitalized the news with a same-day pop, meaning the good news is priced in while the capex outlay is real and near-term. The bear's point that hyperscaler capex commentary is already showing signs of moderation is a legitimate risk to the 'AI capex is early-innings' thesis, even though it wasn't fully substantiated with hard numbers in this debate.

Net-net: the bull's operational and demand-side arguments are credible but the stock's valuation already assumes a lot of that good news, and the bear's arithmetic on near-term profitability from the new wins and the peer-relative multiple gap is harder to dismiss. Neither side landed a knockout — this is a case of a quality, structurally-advantaged REIT that is priced for near-perfect execution, with technicals offering a plausible but unconfirmed bounce setup. Given genuinely balanced conviction, I'm calling this a Hold rather than forcing a directional bet.
