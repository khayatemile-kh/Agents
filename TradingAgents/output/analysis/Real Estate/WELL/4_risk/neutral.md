## WHERE BOTH OF YOU ARE GETTING IT WRONG: THE REAL QUESTION ISN'T "BULL OR BEAR"—IT'S "WHAT'S THE PROBABILITY WEIGHTING?"

Here's what I think is actually happening, and neither of you is modeling it correctly:

**The Morgan Stanley occupancy thesis is probably real, but the timeline is uncertain.** This isn't binary—bull or bear. It's a probability distribution. Morgan Stanley says occupancy is rising and will drive 2027 growth. That's credible. The base case is probably 60-70% odds that EBITDA does reaccelerate by late 2026 or into 2027. But here's the thing: there's also a 30-40% scenario where the timeline extends, or the conversion to EBITDA is slower than expected, or refinancing costs eat more margin than modeled.

Given that distribution of outcomes, what's the right position sizing? Not "reduce 20-30% and wait for confirmation" (which is what the trader recommends and the conservative analyst reinforces). Not "hold full weight and ride it to $251" (which is what the aggressive analyst wants). The right approach is something closer to a barbell: **reduce tactical exposure slightly now, but maintain a meaningful core position, and redeploy if we get either confirmation of EBITDA inflection OR a dip below technical support that gives better entry.**

**Why this barbell approach makes sense:**

One, you're hedging the timing risk. If EBITDA does reaccelerate in Q3 2026, you don't want to be holding only 70-80% of your original position. You'll regret it. But if the timeline extends into 2027, you've already trimmed some exposure, which reduces your drawdown if the stock tests 225-230.

Two, you're maintaining optionality. By holding a core position and keeping dry powder, you can add on weakness without overpaying. This is the best of both worlds—you capture some of the upside if the bull thesis works, but you have flexibility to rebalance if conditions change.

Three, you're being honest about what you don't know. The leverage is elevated, the EBITDA timeline is uncertain, and the technical momentum is weakening. Those are real risks. But the Morgan Stanley occupancy thesis and the 42.9% forward earnings guidance are also real upside catalysts. Acknowledging both simultaneously means positioning for a broader range of outcomes.
