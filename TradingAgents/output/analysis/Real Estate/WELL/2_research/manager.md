**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull leans heavily on a demographic megatrend and a single sell-side price target/EPS number ($3.18) to argue for a 42% earnings inflection and multiple re-rating, while dismissing leverage concerns by pointing to Net Debt/EBITDA and 'deleveraging' from YE2025 to Q1 2026. The bear's rebuttal is more grounded in the actual reported trend data given in this debate: EBITDA fell ~16.8% YoY (2024 $2.78B to 2025 $2.31B) even as revenue grew 35.8%, net debt/EBITDA has climbed structurally from ~3.0x (YE23) to ~6.3x (YE25) before a small seasonal dip to 5.74x (Q1 26), total debt is up 52% since YE23, and the company funded growth with $8.9B of new equity issuance (roughly 10.9% dilution), which undercuts the FCF/dividend-coverage story the bull cites. The bull never reconciles how EBITDA can be shrinking while EPS guidance implies 42% growth — that gap is the crux of the disagreement, and the bear's explanation (one-time gains/asset sales masking operating deterioration) is more consistent with the reported margin compression than the bull's assumption that occupancy gains alone will manifest by 2027.

Also, forward P/E of 7.56x versus a market multiple of 18-20x is not obviously cheap for a REIT with rising leverage and deteriorating EBITDA — it looks more like the market correctly pricing in credit and execution risk than an unrecognized opportunity, as the bear argues. The demographic tailwind is real long-term but doesn't resolve the near-term problem of fixed-rate leases against operators discounting to fill occupancy, which pressures WELL's effective revenue quality even if headline occupancy rises.

Net: the fundamental trend evidence (EBITDA decline, leverage trajectory, heavy equity dilution funding growth) is more concrete and verifiable than the bull's forward-looking assumptions built on a single analyst upgrade and guidance the company hasn't yet delivered on. That tips this debate toward the bear side, but the demographic backdrop and current cash position/dividend coverage are real enough that I don't think this warrants a full Sell — it warrants trimming exposure and waiting for hard confirmation (an actual EBITDA reacceleration, leverage decline sustained over multiple quarters, guidance beat) before adding back.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing WELL exposure modestly (e.g., reduce position size by 20-30%) rather than fully exiting — the demographic backdrop and current dividend coverage argue against an outright Sell, but the deteriorating EBITDA trend and rising leverage trajectory argue against holding at current levels.
2) Do not add to the position on the Morgan Stanley upgrade alone; treat the $3.18 forward EPS and $251 price target as unconfirmed guidance until at least one quarter of reported EBITDA growth and margin expansion is delivered, given the 2024-2025 EBITDA decline of ~16.8% despite 35.8% revenue growth.
3) Monitor Net Debt/EBITDA trend closely over the next 2-3 quarters — require confirmation that the Q1 2026 dip to 5.74x is genuine deleveraging (not seasonal) before treating balance sheet risk as contained.
4) Watch equity issuance/dilution levels — continued heavy share issuance (as in 2025's $8.9B) to fund acquisitions should be treated as a red flag that erodes the FCF-per-share and dividend coverage story the bulls cite.
5) Set a re-evaluation trigger: if next 1-2 quarterly reports show EBITDA reaccelerating alongside occupancy gains and leverage genuinely declining (not just at a single data point), be prepared to upgrade back to Hold/Overweight. Conversely, if EBITDA declines further or dilution continues at pace, consider further trimming toward Sell.
6) For income-focused holders, the dividend is currently covered by FCF, so an outright exit is not necessary, but position sizing should reflect the elevated leverage and margin uncertainty — keep WELL as a smaller, monitored position rather than a core holding until the operating thesis is confirmed by hard numbers.
