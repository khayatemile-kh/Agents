## THE FLORIDA REGULATORY PROBLEM IS REAL, NOT THEORETICAL

Let me address what I suspect conservative analysts will try to minimize: the SB 484 compliance signal from Florida's Public Counsel. Some will say, "Regulatory processes take time; it's priced in already." That's dangerously naive.

Here's what's actually happening: Florida has passed a law (SB 484) addressing how utilities treat large-load customers—primarily data centers. Duke Energy Florida filed a proposal to defer decisions on rate treatment for these high-value customers, and Public Counsel Walt Trierweiler explicitly stated the proposal "doesn't attempt to comply with the most basic provisions" of the law. Translation: Duke's proposal was *rejected on the merits*, not just delayed.

What does this mean for shareholder value? Data centers are the most profitable load segment utilities can serve—high-margin, steady consumption, minimal churn. If Duke can't secure favorable rate treatment for these customers, it loses a critical growth lever precisely when competitors (Constellation Energy, NextEra) are *winning* in this space. Worse, if Florida regulators force Duke to subsidize data center rates or allocate costs unfavorably, the company's earnings guidance gets revised *downward*, not upward.

The aggressive read here is simple: the market has partially priced in regulatory risk (hence the 13% discount per Simply Wall St.), but the *direction of risk is downward*. The regulatory momentum in Florida isn't toward "favorable resolution"—it's toward *tighter scrutiny*. That's a vector, not a snapshot. And I'd rather sell into the discount than hope regulators suddenly become generous.
