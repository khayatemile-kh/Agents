That makes the bear's regulatory argument more grounded in the actual evidence presented. On the FERC/PJM intervention, the bull calls it 'proactive,' the bear calls it defensive/losing-ground — neither side has hard evidence either way, so this point is roughly a wash and shouldn't be weighted heavily. On leverage comparisons to NEE/XEL, the bull has a fair point that regulated utilities run high leverage structurally, but this doesn't resolve the FCF concern, which is the more decision-relevant metric for dividend safety. Overall, the debate leans bear: the concrete, sourced regulatory pushback in Florida combined with the negative free cash flow and accelerating debt/interest burden are more compelling and better-evidenced than the bull's structural 'utilities always recover' framing and unproven data-center margin upside. This doesn't rise to a full Sell — DUK remains a large regulated utility with a long dividend history, a low beta, and rate-base growth optionality if regulatory outcomes normalize — but the incremental evidence favors trimming exposure rather than adding or holding pat. I'm landing on Underweight: acknowledge the yield and defensive characteristics, but recognize that negative FCF, rising leverage/interest costs, and specific adverse regulatory signals in Florida create asymmetric downside risk that outweighs the 13% 'discount to fair value' framing the bull relies on.
