**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: NEE has a massive 35.1 GW data center/hyperscaler backlog, trades at 18.64x P/E and 3.03x P/B, carries 1.62x D/E on a $110.2B debt pile, and posted -$17.8B TTM FCF while issuing $6.9B of debt in a single quarter (net debt issuance of $21.3B trailing). The bull frames this as growth-funding by a well-rated (A-stable) regulated utility that will convert backlog into 10-15 years of contracted earnings, insulated from moratorium risk by rate-recovery mechanisms, with a fair valuation versus AEP/CEG and a defensible 3.01% yield versus a stable-rate macro backdrop (89% odds of no 2026 cuts, only 8% recession risk).

The bear's rebuttal lands harder on three points that the bull never adequately answers: (1) the timing mismatch is real and quantifiable — capex and leverage are rising today (D/E up from ~1.45x to 1.62x) while the backlog's earnings accrue over a decade, so near-term credit metrics deteriorate before the payoff shows up; (2) the regulatory-moat claim is actually inverted — regulators can and do disallow recovery on stranded/controversial capex (the coal-retirement precedent is a real analog), and a 71% probability of at least one state moratorium is a base case, not a tail risk, especially concentrated in the Virginia/Texas corridor where NEE's backlog is heaviest; (3) the dividend/FCF sustainability point is the most damaging — a company with -$17.8B TTM FCF funding both growth capex and dividends almost entirely through debt issuance has a fragile capital structure. The bull's counter that operating cash flow minus capex is +$1.2B ignores that dividends still have to be layered on top of that, and that figure doesn't survive contact with the actual net debt issuance number ($21.3B) which the bull itself cites. That's a self-inflicted admission that the current dividend and growth program is debt-dependent, not FCF-supported.

On valuation, the bear's comp comparison to AEP is more apt than the bull's comparison to CEG — AEP is the more similar regulated-utility peer, and NEE trades at a premium to it despite a ROE differential that's regulator-determined rather than a function of superior execution. The bull's forward-CAGR assumption (11.4% persisting) versus the bear's more skeptical 4-6% forward estimate is unresolvable with the data in front of us, but given the acknowledged capex-intensity mismatch and leverage trajectory, the burden of proof should sit with the bull to justify why the premium multiple persists — and that burden isn't met here.

Net: the bull's macro and backlog-scale arguments are directionally correct and support a defensive floor, but the bear's leverage/FCF/dividend-sustainability critique and the regulatory-disallowance risk are the more rigorously argued and less rebutted points in this round. That tips the balance toward caution rather than aggressive accumulation, but it's not a full sell case — NEE is still an A-rated regulated utility with a real backlog, not a distressed company. The right call is Underweight: trim/avoid adding exposure at current valuation given the deteriorating leverage trajectory and dividend-funding-via-debt concern, without recommending a full exit given the credit rating and structural demand tailwind.
