### **PEG Ratio of 1.66 Masks the Real Problem**

The bull says 1.66 is "reasonable" and shows fair pricing.

**Here's the problem with using PEG to justify a utility valuation:**

- **PEG assumes the growth rate guidance is achievable and sustainable.** It doesn't validate whether the guidance is *realistic*.
- **For utilities, PEG can be misleading** because much of the growth comes from rate base expansion (regulated capex) rather than operational/competitive improvements
- **The real issue:** A PEG of 1.66 is defensible *only if* the company delivers the >8% growth. **We have no evidence it's on track to do so.** Q2 YoY earnings down ≠ accelerating growth.

A more honest framing: **"PEG of 1.66 is fair *assuming* the >8% guidance holds. But given current earnings momentum is negative, risk is asymmetric to the downside."**
