**Rating**: Underweight

**Executive Summary**: Trim AMD by 20-30% into current strength ($460 area) to lock in the YTD doubling, retaining a reduced core position to keep exposure to the genuine AI infrastructure buildout (Saudi Arabia deployment, ROCm ecosystem, margin mix-shift). Protect the retained core with trailing stops or collars given the $450 support is deteriorating and next test is $420-430; do not add fresh capital at 84x FCF/116x trailing P/E. Time horizon 3-6 months, with a re-entry/add-back trigger if forward P/E compresses toward the low-20s without growth deterioration, or a non-fundamental pullback of 25-35% occurs.

**Investment Thesis**: The debate converges on a shared factual base that both bulls and bears accept: AMD's fundamentals are genuinely strong ($8.84B TTM FCF, net cash, 49.5%→53.7% gross margin expansion, real revenue-generating AI deployments like Saudi Arabia Instinct, not vaporware). None of the three analysts dispute business quality or balance sheet safety — this is explicitly not a "broken company" call, which rules out Sell.

However, the risk-reward asymmetry at current pricing is the decisive factor, and it is corroborated across all three debate voices, not just one side. The conservative analyst's stress-test of the PEG/valuation math (116x trailing P/E, 84x FCF requiring net income to nearly triple to $18-20B within 2-3 years via either 120% revenue growth or margin expansion beyond already-elite 53.7% gross margins) was never fully rebutted — the neutral analyst's fact-check actually showed the PEG assumption requires deceleration from AMD's current ~30-34% revenue growth trend to hit the "50% earnings CAGR" baked into the 0.48x PEG, meaning the market is pricing something between optimistic and merely-consistent, with zero room for a miss. That's a thin margin of safety at this multiple.

The technical picture reinforces caution: MACD collapsed from +23.25 to -8.47, RSI at 43.6 sits in the "deteriorating without capitulation" zone per both aggressive and conservative analysts, and price is 8.6% below the 50-day SMA with repeated bounce failures at $514-518. The neutral analyst's counter — that declining volume on the decline could reflect orderly institutional profit-taking rather than panic — is a fair point, but it argues for *some* continued exposure, not for holding the position unchanged; it doesn't rebut the case for trimming.

Critically, all three analysts implicitly or explicitly endorse the trim structure itself: the aggressive analyst frames it as locking in 130% gains while retaining upside ("house money"), the conservative analyst argues it's insufficient in isolation but never argues for zero reduction, and the neutral analyst's own math shows the trim converts a 130% gain into a still-solid 75% gain even in a $350 downside scenario — a real cost, but not portfolio-threatening, and one that buys meaningful protection against the export-control/margin-normalization tail risk that no one in the debate can price precisely. The neutral analyst's probability recalibration (bearish case closer to 20-25% rather than 35%, largely because AMD's core revenue base is not China-dependent) tempers the conservative analyst's most extreme "sell it all" instinct, supporting Underweight rather than Sell.

Net: the long-term AI secular thesis is intact and no analyst disputes it, but current pricing embeds near-flawless execution with no room for margin normalization, export-control shocks, or a single soft quarter — exactly the scenario the trader flagged with 84x FCF and 116x trailing P/E next to a deteriorating technical setup. Trimming into strength while retaining a smaller core position for the AI buildout thesis is the risk-appropriate response; this is a valuation/risk-reward call, not a fundamental-impairment call, so Underweight (not Sell) is correct.

**Time Horizon**: 3-6 months
