The bull says:

**Here's the problem: that forward EPS estimate is pure speculation with massive execution risk baked in.**

Let's deconstruct it:
- AMD's TTM net income: $6.469 billion
- Bull's 2027E net income: $6.9 billion
- That's only **6.7% growth** from TTM to 2027E—not 27%

Wait, the bull said 27% YoY growth. Let me re-read. The bull is extrapolating from "25-30% annually for the next 3 years" based on margin expansion and mix shift. But here's where the model breaks:

**AMD's Margin Expansion is Not a Free Perpetual Pass**

The bull correctly identifies that AMD's gross margin expanded from 49.5% (FY2025) to 53.7% (Q2 2026). But let me ask a critical question: **What happens to that margin when:**

1. **NVIDIA and custom silicon competitors begin scaling and competing on price?**
2. **Export controls actually bite and segment markets, forcing AMD to discount in open markets?**
3. **The AI capex cycle moderates (which it inevitably will) and supply normalizes?**

The bull's answer is implicit: "Growth will be so strong that margins won't compress." That's a bet on perpetual supply shortage, perpetual pricing power, and perpetual market share gains. History says that's not how semiconductors work.

Look at the comparables the bull cited:

Do you see the problem? AMD is being valued above NVIDIA at peak (when NVIDIA had superior competitive positioning) and **nearly 2x above NVIDIA's current valuation**, while AMD is the #2 player in its market competing against a stronger incumbent.

## **PART II: THE GROWTH NARRATIVE IS ALREADY PRICED IN—Here's Why The Bull is Wrong About Runway**

Now let me tackle the bull's core thesis: that AMD is "in the second inning of a nine-inning game" with 7-10 years of growth ahead.

### **The Bull's Math Doesn't Hold Up**

The bull argues:
- Current AI accelerator installed base: 20-30 million units
- Required capacity by 2030: 300-500 million units
- Therefore: 10-15x growth needed

**This is misleading in three critical ways:**

#### **1. This Math Doesn't Drive 25%+ Annual EPS Growth**

Even if the installed base grows 10x over 7 years, that's **42% annualized growth in unit demand**. But AMD doesn't get 42% annual revenue growth from 42% annual unit growth because:

- **Price compression happens**: As supply normalizes, price per accelerator falls. A 42% unit growth + 15-20% ASP compression = only 15-20% revenue growth
- **AMD's market share gets pressured**: Right now, AMD is capturing a disproportionate share because of supply constraints. As supply normalizes and NVIDIA production ramped, AMD's share will compress. Custom silicon adoption (which the bull dismisses) will also take volume.
- **Margin compression is inevitable**: The 53.7% gross margin today is a peak, not a floor. When demand >= supply normalizes to supply > demand, pricing power evaporates. Expect margins to compress to 40-45% by 2028-2029.

**Let me model this realistically:**

| Year | Revenue (Bn) | Gross Margin | Gross Profit | OpEx (% of Rev) | Operating Income |
|------|--------------|--------------|--------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| 2026E | $45 | 51% | $22.95 | 15% | $4.2B |
| 2027E | $52 | 48% | $24.96 | 16% | $4.6B |
| 2028E | $59 | 45% | $26.55 | 17% | $4.6B |
| 2029E | $63 | 43% | $27.09 | 18% | $4.5B |

**This scenario shows revenue growing 15% annually (still solid, still better than market average) but operating income growing only 3% annually by 2029 because margins compress.**

With a 25x forward P/E, that's an stock price in the $90-100 per share range by 2029. Today's price is $459. **That's a 80% downside over 3 years, even in a bullish scenario.**
