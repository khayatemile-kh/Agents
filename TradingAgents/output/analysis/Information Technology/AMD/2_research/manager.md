**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the underlying fundamentals: AMD is generating real, growing cash flow ($8.84B TTM FCF, net cash position, strong liquidity) and is genuinely participating in a large AI infrastructure buildout with live deployments (Saudi Arabia Instinct) and margin expansion (49.5%→53.7% gross margin). The bull's strongest points are the fortress balance sheet, real revenue-generating AI deployments (not vaporware), and the argument that switching costs (CUDA/ROCm, time-to-market) protect AMD and NVIDIA from custom silicon for several years. The bear's strongest points are the valuation math: 84x FCF and 116x TTM P/E are historically extreme even next to NVIDIA, and the bear correctly flags that the bull's growth assumptions require unit growth to translate one-for-one into revenue/margin growth without any ASP or margin compression — an assumption that is very aggressive given how cyclical and competitive semiconductors have historically been. The bear's scenario table showing operating income growth stalling as margins normalize (43-45% by 2028-29) is a plausible mean-reversion case that the bull never directly rebuts; the bull's PEG-ratio-as-bargain argument is also weak because it's built on the same optimistic forward EPS estimate the bear specifically deconstructs and shows may be inconsistent with TTM net income trends.

Net take: the growth story is real and AMD is not a broken company, but the debate's strongest, most concrete quantitative argument is the bear's point that current pricing (84x FCF, 116x trailing P/E, doubled YTD) already embeds several years of flawless execution and sustained peak margins. That asymmetry — modest further upside if everything goes right, versus a "one bad quarter/guide-down/export-control shock" causing a 40%+ multiple compression — tilts the risk/reward unfavorably at current levels even though the long-term AI thesis stands. This isn't a "sell the company" bear case (no fundamental impairment, no balance sheet risk, no evidence of demand collapsing), so Sell is too extreme. But committing to Buy/Overweight when the stock has already doubled and trades at a premium even to NVIDIA's current multiple, with no discussion in the bull case of margin durability under competitive/export-control pressure, is not justified either. The disciplined call is to become more cautious on adding exposure here — bank some of the re-rating gains / trim into strength — while acknowledging the long-term AI secular growth thesis remains intact for whatever core position is retained.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim the position modestly (e.g., reduce by 20-30% of current AMD exposure) into current strength to lock in gains from the YTD doubling, rather than adding fresh capital at 84x FCF/116x trailing P/E.
2) Keep a reduced core position to retain exposure to the genuine AI infrastructure buildout (Saudi Arabia deployment, ROCm ecosystem, data-center mix shift) rather than exiting entirely — this isn't a broken-thesis call, it's a valuation/risk-reward call.
3) Set a re-entry/add-back watchlist trigger: get more constructive (move back to Hold/Overweight) if forward P/E compresses toward the low-20s without a deterioration in the growth outlook, or if a pullback of 25-35% occurs on non-fundamental (multiple-compression) grounds.
4) Use options collars or trailing stops on the retained core position to protect against a "guide-down" or export-control shock given how richly priced the stock is versus even NVIDIA's current multiple.
5) Closely monitor two specific bear risk factors flagged in the debate: (a) gross margin trend in the next 2 quarters — any sequential decline from the 53.7% peak should be treated as an early warning of margin normalization; (b) any incremental export-control tightening headlines, which directly threaten the addressable market assumed in bullish revenue models.
6) Do not chase further strength; avoid initiating new/incremental Buy-sized positions until valuation multiples de-rate closer to historical growth-adjusted semiconductor norms (e.g., PEG closer to 1.0x on more conservative EPS assumptions than the bull's).
