## **PART III: COMPETITIVE POSITIONING—AMD'S MOAT IS REAL**

Now, the bears will trot out NVIDIA's $3.5B MediaTek investment and claim AMD faces extinction from custom silicon. **This argument is backwards.**

### **Why NVIDIA is Investing in MediaTek, Not Why It Threatens AMD**

NVIDIA is funding MediaTek's custom silicon because **NVIDIA recognizes that custom silicon takes 3-5 years to ship, requires massive R&D investment, and often underperforms or gets canceled**. Examples:
- Google TPU is still mostly confined to Google's infrastructure
- Amazon Trainium and Inferentia have limited adoption outside AWS
- Meta's custom chips remain niche

NVIDIA's $3.5B MediaTek play isn't a threat—it's NVIDIA **hedging against the possibility** that hyperscalers build custom silicon faster than historically. But here's the reality: **most hyperscalers will continue buying AMD and NVIDIA accelerators because:**

1. **Time-to-market risk:** Custom silicon takes years. Hyperscalers can't wait.
2. **R&D burden:** Building competitive AI chips requires world-class chip design talent (scarce).
3. **Software ecosystem:** CUDA, ROCm, and other software stacks take years to mature. Custom silicon lacks this.
4. **Flexibility:** Off-the-shelf accelerators allow hyperscalers to pivot as AI workloads evolve.

**AMD benefits from this dynamic because:**
- AMD has the **second-largest AI software ecosystem** (ROCm) after NVIDIA
- AMD's Instinct accelerators offer **25-40% cost savings** vs. NVIDIA H100s for many workloads
- AMD can pivot faster than custom silicon teams
- AMD is shipping in volume NOW, while custom alternatives remain roadmap items

### **Broadcom's VMware AI Push: Not a Threat, a Validation**

Broadcom acquired VMware and is investing in AI infrastructure software. This actually **validates AMD's position** because:
- VMware (now Broadcom) is focused on software/orchestration, not chip design
- Broadcom will likely standardize on NVIDIA and AMD accelerators for VMware AI workloads
- This expands the serviceable market for AMD's Instinct chips
