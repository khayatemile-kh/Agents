**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make valid points, but the bear case rests on hard, verifiable balance-sheet and income-statement deterioration in the most recent quarter, while the bull case leans heavily on narrative (SaaSpocalypse reversal, moat durability) and technical/sentiment signals that are inherently more speculative. The debt explosion from $2.43B to $8.45B in a single quarter, a current ratio of 0.70, negative working capital of -$3.64B that worsened by $2.09B QoQ, and a debt-to-equity ratio of 67.5x are concrete, quantifiable red flags the bull never addressed. Layered on top of that is a genuine operating margin collapse (13.4% to 4.1% QoQ) and gross margin compression (76.6% to 70.7% over four quarters) with SG&A and R&D growing 2-3x faster than revenue. These are not narrative-driven concerns; they are measurable deterioration in unit economics and capital structure occurring in the same quarter the stock rallied 59.6%, which is a dangerous disconnect.

The bull's rebuttal on valuation (27.3x forward P/E being 'rational' given margins) is undermined by the bear's evidence that those margins are actively compressing, not stable. The bull's technical argument (institutional accumulation, RSI cooling into a 'healthy' range, near upper Bollinger Band) is a soft, sentiment-based read that can just as easily be interpreted as distribution/climax-buying given the 50 SMA sitting below the 200 SMA (a bearish crossover signal the bull dismissed too quickly). The bull's competitive-moat argument (switching costs, platform extensibility) is a longer-term structural case, but doesn't address the near-term risk of refinancing $2.08B in commercial paper in a higher-for-longer rate environment, which is a real liquidity/solvency vulnerability, not just an interest-expense drag.

Given that the debate has hard, quantifiable red flags on the bear side that went essentially unrebutted (the bull's write-up cuts off before addressing balance sheet leverage or margin compression at all), I'm siding with the bear thesis for now. This isn't a full conviction sell — the long-term competitive positioning and AI-platform narrative have some merit, and the stock's price action shows real institutional interest — but the magnitude and suddenness of the leverage increase and margin collapse are serious enough to warrant trimming exposure until there's clarity on (a) what the debt was used for, (b) whether margins stabilize in the next 1-2 quarters, and (c) refinancing risk on the commercial paper is resolved. This warrants an Underweight rather than a full Sell, since the top-line growth and platform moat still provide some support and the deterioration, while alarming, is only one quarter old and could reverse.
