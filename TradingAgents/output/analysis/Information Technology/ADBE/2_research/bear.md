## PART 1: THE "CHEAP" FORWARD VALUATION IS A MIRAGE

### The Forward EPS Projection Is Embedded With Risk My Colleague Is Ignoring

Let's start where the bull's thesis lives: the forward P/E of 10.21x and the "57% EPS growth" narrative.

Here's the problem: **That forward EPS number of $27.50 is not a consensus lock; it's a projection contingent on execution risks that are actively materializing right now.**

**Let me walk through the actual earnings trajectory:**

- **TTM EPS (trailing):** $17.48
- **Q3 FY2026 (most recent quarter) Diluted EPS:** $4.25
- **Annualized Q3 run rate:** ~$17.00
- **Forward projection:** $27.50

**But here's what the data actually shows:**

Q3 net income **fell 9.4% quarter-over-quarter** ($1.89B → $1.71B). The bull is correct that buybacks masked this in EPS (-7.6% only), but that's the *problem*, not the solution. **Real earnings are decelerating, and buybacks are papering over the weakness.**

If we extrapolate Q3 net income run-rate forward ($1.71B × 4 = $6.84B annually), we get TTM net income of ~$6.8B—which, with a current share count of ~290M, yields EPS closer to **$23.45**, not $27.50.

**That's an 11% gap between the forward projection and the implied run-rate.** The bull is asking us to believe Adobe suddenly re-accelerates to 40%+ net income growth in the next two quarters. Let me show you why that's not credible.

### The Earnings Headwinds Are Real and Expanding

**1. Operating Margin Compression is Accelerating, Not Reversing**

Look at the Q3 operating margin: **33.8%**, down 400 basis points quarter-over-quarter (from 37.8% in Q2). Year-over-year, operating margin fell 210 basis points (from 35.9% in Q3 2025).

The bull would have you believe this is temporary. **The data suggests it's structural.**

Why? Look at the expense line:
- **Q3 SG&A:** $2,430M, up **11.9% QoQ** and **15.7% YoY**
- **Q3 R&D:** $1,198M, up **7.9% QoQ** and **16.8% YoY**

Adobe is spending faster than it's growing revenue (12.7% YoY). This is a profitability treadmill. The Saudi Arabia initiative? It's increasing R&D spend on localized AI, not decreasing it. Sales & Marketing is ramping to distribute Firefly. Every "strategic initiative" comes with a cost structure attached.

**Here's the critical insight:** In a high-rate environment (89% probability of no Fed cuts in 2026), investors don't pay for *potential* growth—they pay for *current* profitability. Adobe's operating margin is trending the wrong direction, and the bull's thesis of a 57% EPS expansion requires this trend to reverse sharply. **It won't.**

**2. The Saudi Arabia Deal is a Revenue Headwind, Not a Tailwind—at Least Near-Term**

The bull characterized the $4B Saudi initiative as "market capture, not dilution." I respectfully disagree—it's actually **both**, but the dilution is front-loaded and the capture is back-loaded.

**The near-term math:**
- 27+ million free users for 12 months
- If even 10% of those convert to paid subscriptions at an average annual value of $120/user, that's $324M in annual ARR eventually
- But for the next 12 months, these are **zero revenue** users, while Adobe is incurring localization R&D costs

**The opportunity cost:** Adobe could have deployed that $4B in Saudi investment capital toward (a) accelerated shareholder buybacks (which would have been accretive to EPS immediately), or (b) dividend increases, or (c) organic R&D in higher-margin existing segments.

Instead, Adobe chose to seed a market that may or may not convert at the rates management projects. **This is a long-dated, speculative bet disguised as strategic positioning.**

And here's the kicker: **Analyst commentary explicitly flags this as a 2026 headwind.** Citi noted "the near-term setup looks easier than what comes next"—a euphemism for "Q3 beats are masking downstream guidance challenges." The Saudi deal is *part* of those downstream challenges.

### The Valuation "Discount" Reflects Real Risk Premia, Not Irrational Market Pessimism

My colleague says Adobe is trading at a "transition discount" due to September seasonality bias and macro uncertainty. I'd argue that **discount is rational and earned.**

**Here's why:**

**1. September Seasonality is Predictive, Not Noise**

The bull dismissed the historical September pattern as "overblown." But let's examine the data:

- ADBE has finished higher in September **2 times in the past decade** (2 out of 10 years)
- That's an **80% downside bias** historically
- The pattern repeats consistently, which suggests systematic factors (summer vacations, tax-loss harvesting windows, quarterly rebalancing) disproportionately pressure Adobe in September

Now, does this mean ADBE will fall in September 2026? Not necessarily. But **does it mean the market's pricing of a near-term September risk premium is justified?** Absolutely. And the bull hasn't provided a compelling reason to believe this year will be different (it won't be).

**2. The Macro Environment is Actively Hostile to Adobe's Valuation**

The bull noted that 89% of prediction market odds suggest no Fed rate cuts in 2026. But then the bull said **"this suggests sector-specific pessimism is misplaced."**

This is **backwards logic.** Let me explain:

In a **high-rate, no-cut environment**, the discount rate applied to future earnings rises. Adobe's valuation is a function of:

**P/E = (1 - g) / (r - g)**

Where:
- r = discount rate (currently ~6.5% for tech given 10-year rates at 4% + equity risk premium)
- g = long-term growth rate (Adobe's is slowing, as we've shown)

If rates stay high (89% probability), and Adobe's growth rate compresses (from 12.7% to mid-single digits as scale increases), then the denominator (r - g) **widens, and multiples compress**. This is not "sector-specific pessimism"—it's **mechanical valuation math.**

Adobe's forward P/E of 10.21x is low precisely *because* the market is pricing the reality that growth is decelerating and rates are staying high. The market isn't wrong; it's just forward-looking.
