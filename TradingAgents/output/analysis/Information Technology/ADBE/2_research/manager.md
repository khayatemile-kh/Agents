**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make real points, but the bear case lands more decisively on the specific numbers presented. The bull's central pillar—10.21x forward P/E on 57% EPS growth—unravels under the bear's run-rate math: annualizing Q3's $4.25 diluted EPS gives ~$17.00, and even adjusting for the QoQ net income decline (-9.4%) implies EPS closer to $23.45, not $27.50. That's an ~11% gap between the bulge-bracket forward number and what the trailing trend actually supports, and it means the "cheap" multiple is partly an artifact of an optimistic EPS estimate rather than a true market mispricing. Margin trends corroborate the bear case: operating margin fell 400bps QoQ (37.8%→33.8%) and 210bps YoY, while SG&A (+11.9% QoQ, +15.7% YoY) and R&D (+7.9% QoQ, +16.8% YoY) are growing faster than the 12.7% YoY revenue growth. That's a profitability treadmill, not a temporary blip, and it directly undercuts the bull's claim that buybacks alone can sustain EPS growth — buybacks are offsetting, not reversing, a real earnings deceleration (net income -9.4% QoQ).

The bull's qualitative moat arguments (Firefly's vertical integration, CXO stickiness, distribution advantages vs. pure-play AI startups) are reasonable and shouldn't be dismissed, but they're forward-looking and largely narrative-based, whereas the bear's rebuttal on margin compression and EPS run-rate is grounded in the same dataset the bull used, and it exposes a real crack in the bull's arithmetic. The Saudi Arabia initiative is genuinely a two-sided story — I don't think it's purely dilutive as the bear frames it, but the bear is right that it's a near-term cost/revenue-recognition drag with speculative long-run payoff, and management commentary (Citi's "near-term setup looks easier than what comes next") supports caution about guidance beyond the current quarter.

On macro: the bear's r-g valuation logic is basically correct — in a higher-for-longer rate regime, decelerating growth compresses multiples further, and there's no evidence in the debate that Adobe's growth deceleration (12.7% YoY, moderating toward "mid-single digits" per bear, or 8-10% per bull's own admission) is offset by re-acceleration catalysts strong enough to justify holding the stock as a high-conviction growth compounder right now. The September seasonality point (2-of-10 years higher) is weak/noisy in isolation but the bear correctly notes the bull didn't have a compelling reason to expect this year to break the pattern, and it aligns directionally with the margin and EPS-run-rate concerns rather than contradicting them.

Netting it out: the bull's cash-generation and buyback story is legitimate but is being used to obscure a real deceleration in core profitability (net income falling QoQ, margins compressing, and forward EPS resting on an aggressive assumption that isn't yet showing up in the trend data). That's a classic case of financial engineering supporting the headline number while the underlying business slows. I don't think this rises to a full Sell — Adobe still has genuine competitive moats in creative workflows, strong FCF conversion (128%), a healthy balance sheet, and optionality from Firefly/CXO that could reaccelerate growth if execution improves. But the risk/reward skews cautious into the next 1-2 quarters given margin trends, the EPS gap, Saudi-related near-term drag, and a hostile rate backdrop. This warrants trimming/de-risking rather than adding, while keeping some exposure to the long-term moat story.

4. Set a re-evaluation trigger: revisit toward Hold/Overweight if next quarterly report shows (a) operating margin re-expansion, (b) SG&A/R&D growth decelerating toward or below revenue growth, and (c) net income growth turning positive QoQ rather than relying solely on buyback-driven EPS support.
