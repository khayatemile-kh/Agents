**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the fundamentals: CDNS is a high-quality, high-margin, cash-generative business (23% net margin, 27.6% FCF margin, 0.48x net debt/EBITDA) riding genuine AI-driven design demand, with management raising FY2026 guidance off record backlog. Where they diverge is on durability and price. The bull's structural super-cycle argument is credible directionally—custom silicon design activity is real and EDA tools are genuinely mission-critical—but the bear's math on forward EPS is the more rigorous, harder-hitting point: getting from ~$5.04 TTM EPS to $9.54 forward EPS implies net income growth (~83%) wildly out of step with the guided 12-14% revenue growth, unless there's a huge one-time item or share count assumption I can't verify from the evidence given. That gap is a real red flag on guidance quality/consensus estimate reliability that the bull never adequately reconciled. The bear's point on Synopsys taking share and posting faster growth is also a legitimate competitive risk that undercuts the "CDNS is winning the AI cycle uncontested" framing. On top of that, the stock's own price action (7.6% single-day drop, down since earnings despite a guidance raise) is the market's real-time verdict, and it's siding with skepticism, not with the bull's "noise" framing. The rate-environment argument (60x trailing multiple against a persistently higher-for-longer discount rate backdrop) is a genuine structural headwind for the multiple even if the business itself doesn't deteriorate. That said, the bear's boom-bust historical analogy is somewhat speculative and doesn't fully account for the recurring, multi-year nature of chip design re-spins tied to model iteration cycles — so I'm not fully buying the "it's just cyclical and will vanish" framing either. Netting it out: this is a good business trading at a valuation that is pricing in near-flawless execution on numbers that don't cleanly reconcile with guided revenue growth, in a macro backdrop that punishes exactly this kind of multiple, with a credible faster-growing competitor nipping at share. That combination — quality company, but stretched price, murky forward-EPS math, and a real competitive threat — tips this toward trimming exposure rather than adding, but it's not a full sell given the balance-sheet strength and genuine AI tailwind, so I'm landing on Underweight rather than Sell or Hold.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing CDNS exposure by roughly 20-30% into any near-term bounce rather than adding on the post-selloff dip — don't chase the bull's "buy the noise" framing until the forward-EPS math is reconciled with guided revenue growth. 2) Do not initiate new/incremental positions at current 60x trailing / ~32x forward multiples; require either a multiple reset (mid-40s trailing or lower) or explicit management clarification on how ~83% implied net-income growth squares with 12-14% revenue guidance before re-engaging. 3) Watch Q3/Q4 2026 prints closely for backlog-to-revenue conversion and gross/operating margin trend — any miss or deceleration versus the raised guide should trigger further de-risking. 4) Monitor relative performance vs. Synopsys (SNPS) as a share-shift signal; sustained CDNS underperformance on revenue growth vs. SNPS would be a incremental negative confirming bear thesis. 5) Keep a residual core position (don't fully exit) given genuine balance-sheet strength (0.48x net debt/EBITDA, strong FCF) and real AI-design tailwinds — size that core position to reflect a Hold-to-Underweight blend rather than zero exposure. 6) Use options (covered calls or collars) on the remaining position to harvest premium and cushion downside while multiple compression plays out.
