## The Margin Normalization Illusion

Let's start with the core valuation defense. The Aggressive Analyst argues that NVIDIA's 17.9x P/S is justified because the company has 63.7% net margins versus TSMC's 30-35% and Broadcom's 15-20%. This is backwards reasoning, and here's why: **those high margins are precisely what create the cliff risk.**

A 63.7% net margin is not a stable structural feature—it's a *peak-cycle phenomenon* built entirely on scarcity pricing in AI accelerators. NVIDIA currently faces zero meaningful competition in the highest-margin GPU segments because of supply constraints and architectural lock-in, not because the company has discovered permanent superior economics. The moment competition arrives (and it *will*—AMD is shipping Instinct chips, custom chips from Meta and Google are accelerating), that 63.7% margin gets compressed rapidly.

Look at the historical precedent: Intel dominated with 60%+ gross margins in CPUs for fifteen years. When AMD released Zen, those margins compressed from 62% to 48% within three years. The same pattern played out in gaming GPUs when AMD returned to competitiveness. **Semiconductor margins are cyclical, not secular.**

When NVIDIA's net margin normalizes to even 40% (still exceptional by industry standards), the current 17.9x P/S multiple becomes 28.5x on normalized earnings—not 14x forward. That's not "priced for dominance." That's priced for perfection *and* assumes margin stability that history suggests is temporary.
