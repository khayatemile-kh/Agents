## OPENING STATEMENT: WHY NOW IS THE TIME TO BUY KHC

Let me start by cutting through the noise: **Kraft Heinz is undergoing a genuine operational turnaround, trading at a compelling valuation, and positioned to surprise investors to the upside over the next 12-24 months.** 

Yes, the Q2 impairment charge of $7.4 billion looks ugly on the surface—but that's precisely the point. That **non-cash write-down represents corporate housekeeping from the Berkshire Hathaway / 3G Capital era**, acknowledging prior overvaluation. What matters now is that **normalized EBITDA of $8.6 billion in Q2 demonstrates solid underlying operational performance**, and management is demonstrating capital discipline by reducing net debt while investing in innovation.

Let's break down the bull thesis:

---

## PILLAR 1: OPERATIONAL TURNAROUND IS REAL & ACCELERATING

### The Q2 Beat & Guidance Raise Signal Genuine Momentum

Look at the facts:
- **Q2 2026 beat** both earnings and sales estimates
- **Management raised 2026 organic sales guidance** — this is not a small signal
- **Normalized EBITDA remained strong at $8.6B**, confirming cash generation capability
- **Operating margins, while under pressure, remain healthy at 14.7%**

**Here's what the bears will try to tell you:** "But volume is weak in North America!" 

**My response:** Yes, North American volumes are under pressure—but this is *expected* during a portfolio optimization phase. Management is strategically deprioritizing low-margin, high-volume SKUs in favor of premium, higher-margin products. This is exactly what happened at Smucker's when they made their turnaround—volume softness initially, followed by margin recovery and re-acceleration. **Smucker just raised guidance**—proving the packaged foods sector can win when it executes.

The real insight is this: **KHC is trading at a 15-20% discount to Smucker and General Mills on EV/EBITDA, despite comparable operational metrics.** If KHC even approaches peer valuation multiples, the stock has 30-40% upside.

---

## PILLAR 2: EUROPEAN PLAYBOOK = MASSIVE UNPRICED UPSIDE

### A High-Conviction Strategic Initiative

This is the aspect of the bull case I find most compelling. Here's what management is doing:

**Europe Success Story:**
- Kraft Heinz holds **~50% of the European ketchup market**
- European operations are significantly more profitable than North America
- Consumer health trends (GLP-1 drugs, wellness focus) are *accelerating* in Europe, not decelerating

**The Strategic Play:**
Management is using Europe as a **testing laboratory** for health-conscious, reformulated products (lower sugar, lower sodium, better-for-you positioning). Early results are strong. The plan is to **systematically replicate this European playbook into North America**, where similar health trends are gaining traction.

**Why This Matters:**
- Packaged foods is traditionally seen as a "sin stock" — but KHC is preemptively addressing this narrative
- The weight-loss drug trend (Ozempic, Mounjaro adoption) is creating a **tailwind for reformulated products**—and KHC is ahead of competitors in this space
- This initiative could drive **2-3 years of volume re-acceleration and margin expansion**

**The Math:**
If KHC successfully drives a modest 2-3% organic sales growth from European product innovation (net of volume declines), combined with 100-150 bps of margin expansion, EBITDA could grow from $8.6B to $9.5B+ within 24 months. At current valuation multiples, that's a **$3-5 per share upside**.

## PILLAR 3: BALANCE SHEET IS BEING FIXED—DELEVERAGING IS UNDERWAY

### The $7.4B Impairment Was a Feature, Not a Bug

Let me address the elephant in the room directly: **The impairment charge is fundamentally positive for equity holders.**

**Here's why:**

The prior balance sheet (inherited from Berkshire's ownership) carried $59.5B in goodwill and intangibles—much of it from the 2015 Heinz / Kraft merger, which was widely viewed as overpriced at the time. That overhang has been **finally corrected**, reducing goodwill to a more realistic $19.7B.

**The Real Signal:**
- New management is being transparent about past mistakes
- They're writing down assets to reflect current market realities
- This *removes future impairment risk* and sets a cleaner foundation for valuation

**Deleveraging Progress:**
- Net debt down from $18.6B (Q4 2025) to $16.6B (Q2 2026) — a **$2B reduction in 6 months**
- Net Debt/EBITDA improving: 3.38x → 3.02x (best in years)
- Management is executing a disciplined debt paydown while maintaining dividend

**Peer Comparison:**
- General Mills: Net Debt/EBITDA of 3.2x
- Mondelez: Net Debt/EBITDA of 2.1x
- Smucker: Net Debt/EBITDA of 3.4x

**KHC is now within sector norms and improving trajectory.** By 2027, if management maintains current debt reduction pace, Net Debt/EBITDA could hit 2.5-2.7x, creating a meaningful refinancing opportunity and upside optionality.
