**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull's case rests on three pillars: a genuine turnaround backed by hard numbers, China as a tailwind, and a constructive technical setup, with debt and valuation framed as manageable. The bear systematically dismantles the profitability claim by pointing out that the celebrated 11.2% operating margin is a non-GAAP, adjusted figure that excludes $813M in restructuring charges — the actual reported operating margin is 5.2%, which is *lower* than FY2024's 6.2%. That is a critical, fact-based rebuttal: it means the 'operational leverage' story the bull leans on doesn't hold up under GAAP scrutiny, and net margin of 1.21% is roughly a seventh of defensive peers (P&G, Colgate, Unilever). The bear's balance sheet argument is also compelling and grounded in the same dataset the bull used: debt-to-equity of 243%, negative net tangible assets (-$1.88B), and de-leveraging pace so slow (~$590M/year against $9.25B total debt) that it would take over a decade to normalize leverage at this rate, while dividends ($508M) continue to be prioritized over debt paydown. The bear also correctly notes that FY2026 FCF improvement was substantially aided by a 24% YoY cut in capex rather than a genuine profitability improvement, casting doubt on the bull's extrapolation of FCF to $1.7-1.8B by FY2028. Revenue itself is still below FY2024 levels ($15.05B vs $15.61B), undermining the 'growth is back' narrative.

On China, the bull's argument that recession risk is 'already priced in' conflates US-market-implied recession odds with China-specific risk — the bear is right to call this out as a logical mismatch; China's slowdown and competitive pressure from domestic beauty brands is a distinct, underappreciated risk to the 30-35% of revenue tied to that market.

The bull's technical/momentum argument (golden cross, RSI cooling, volume confirmation) is the most defensible part of the bull case as a near-term trading signal, but technicals are backward-looking and don't offset fundamental balance-sheet and earnings-quality concerns for a research/investment (not trading) recommendation.

Weighing both sides: the bull is essentially asking the market to pay up for adjusted, non-GAAP metrics and to underwrite continued margin/FCF improvement from a company that (a) still has negative tangible equity, (b) is barely deleveraging, (c) had a one-year FCF pop driven by capex cuts rather than durable margin gain, and (d) faces asymmetric China risk that isn't fully priced. The bear's rebuttal is more grounded in the literal, unadjusted numbers presented and exposes real fragility beneath the headline turnaround narrative. That said, the stock has already re-rated sharply (+24% in August) on the earnings beat, and there is real, if early, evidence of organic growth returning and FCF improving in absolute dollar terms — so this isn't a full-blown solvency crisis, but the risk/reward is no longer attractive after the rally given how much good news is now priced in against a shaky balance sheet. The stronger, more rigorously supported argument here is the bear's: the reported (not adjusted) financials show margin deterioration, dangerously thin net margins, negative tangible book value, and unsustainable capex-driven FCF improvement — this is not a "balanced" debate, the bear wins on factual grounding. I'm recommending Underweight rather than outright Sell because the bull's technical setup and modest early signs of organic growth/FCF improvement in dollar terms deserve some benefit of the doubt, and a full Sell would ignore the sequential improvement in OCF and the de-leveraging (however slow) that is occurring. But conviction should clearly lean bearish/cautious given the balance sheet fragility and the reliance on adjusted non-GAAP metrics to support the bull thesis.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing EL exposure by roughly 25-35% into the recent rally strength (stock is up ~24% in August and technically extended, per bull's own RSI/MACD data), using the 50 SMA (~$87) as a technical reference point for any final scale-down if support fails.
2) Do not add to the position based on the 11.2% 'normalized' operating margin narrative — underwrite position sizing off the reported 5.2% operating margin and 1.21% net margin, which are dramatically weaker than consumer defensive peers (P&G/Colgate/Unilever at 8-10%+ net margin).
3) Set a balance-sheet trigger for further de-risking: if net debt reduction slows below the current ~$590M/year pace, or if another restructuring/impairment charge is announced (following the $813M FY2026 and $1,286M FY2025 charges), reduce further given the negative net tangible asset position (-$1.88B) and 243% debt-to-equity.
4) Treat China as a binary swing factor, not a confirmed tailwind — hold off increasing exposure until at least two consecutive quarters of consistent (not just 'momentum language') China organic growth are confirmed, given 30-35% revenue concentration there and rising competitive/macro risk.
5) Retain a residual position (do not fully exit) to preserve optionality on the bull's margin-normalization and FCF-growth thesis, but size it as a starter/watch position rather than a core holding, revisiting the rating once FY2027 guidance ($3.10-$3.35 EPS) is tested against a full quarter of reported (not adjusted) margin data.
6) Use any further technical strength into the $95-100 zone (near 10 EMA resistance/prior highs) as an additional trimming opportunity rather than a fresh entry point, given valuation is not cheap and the operational turnaround remains unproven on a GAAP basis.
