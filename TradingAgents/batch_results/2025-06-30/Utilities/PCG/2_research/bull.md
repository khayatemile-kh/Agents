The 189x debt-to-equity ratio terrifies the uninformed. I get it. But let's have the mature conversation:

### Why Utility Debt Is Different

PG&E's **$59.7B debt load** is *structurally different* from corporate debt because:

1. **It's backed by regulated cash flows.** Every customer who turns on a light pays a rate that's guaranteed by the CPUC to cover debt service. This isn't Apple competing in smartphones—this is mandatory electricity.

2. **It's refinanced perpetually, not matured.** Utilities operate in a perpetual refinancing cycle. As long as rates are set to cover debt service (which they are), maturing debt gets rolled over. The company has been operating this way for 100+ years.

3. **Equity investors aren't responsible for it.** The debt finances the asset base. Equity holders benefit from the spread between the regulated return on assets (typically 7-10%) and the cost of debt (currently ~5-6%). That spread is **equity returns**.

**Let me put numbers on this:**

- Long-term debt: $54B
- Annual interest expense (Q2 annualized): ~$2.4B
- Regulated rate base return: ~7-8% (industry standard)
- Rate base value: ~$95B (from PPE + working capital)

**The math:** A 7.5% return on $95B in rate base = $7.1B in regulated operating profit. Debt service of $2.4B leaves **$4.7B for equity holders after debt payments.** 

With $31B in market cap, that's an **implied earnings yield of 15%+** on regulated cash flows—before accounting for growth.
