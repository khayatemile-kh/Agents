**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Weighing both sides, the bear case lands more solid punches than the bull manages to parry. The bull's central thesis rests on three unproven assumptions: (1) that Q2 2025 capex ($1.33B, ~16% of invested capital) represents a peak that will normalize downward, (2) that the ~$976M quarterly operating cash flow annualizes cleanly to $3.9B, and (3) that leverage optics are purely an artifact of utility accounting. The bear dismantles all three convincingly using the bull's own numbers. Most damaging: the bull's naive annualization of Q2 OCF ($3.9B) and Q2 capex ($5.3B) implies TTM FCF of roughly -$1.4B, yet the actual reported TTM FCF is -$3.09B — a ~$1.7B unexplained gap that reveals the Q2 quarter is not representative and that FCF trends are volatile rather than steadily improving. That undercuts the entire "cyclical, not structural" recovery narrative used to justify the valuation call. The bear also correctly flips the depreciation argument: a 10.7% depreciation rate on revenue signals an aging/heavy asset base requiring continued elevated maintenance capex, not an imminent capex decline — and this is reinforced by the bull's own emerging-market growth thesis, which requires MORE generation/distribution capex to capture 4-6% demand growth, not less. On leverage, the bear's cross-sectional comparison is the killer point: Duke, NextEra, and Dominion run debt-to-equity in the 1.3-2.0x range, while AES sits at 257x. This isn't 'leverage by design' common to utilities broadly — it's an outlier even within its own sector, meaning AES carries idiosyncratic balance-sheet fragility on top of normal utility risk. Combined with a macro backdrop where Fed cuts are unlikely in 2026 (93% probability of no cuts), refinancing costs stay elevated precisely when the company is burning cash and has minimal equity cushion. The bull's counterarguments (minority interest inflating capitalization, interest coverage of 2.1-2.6x being "tight but serviceable") are reasonable observations but don't offset the core problem: negative and volatile FCF combined with extreme leverage in a higher-for-longer rate environment is a genuine solvency-risk combination, not simply a statistical mirage. The valuation (5.55x P/E, 4.75% yield) looks cheap only if you believe the cash flow trajectory inflects soon, and the evidence for that inflection is thin and internally inconsistent per the bear's math. That said, I'm not going to full Sell because the bull raises legitimate points — regulated cost-of-service revenue, a genuinely elevated growth-capex phase tied to real demand growth, and a dividend yield that provides some carry — that keep this from being a pure short. This warrants a cautious, risk-reducing stance rather than full capitulation.

**Strategic Actions**: Trim existing AES exposure to underweight relative to benchmark/sector — reduce position size by roughly 25-40% of current holding rather than exiting entirely, preserving some exposure to the regulated cash-flow base and dividend yield while reducing capital at risk to the leverage/FCF volatility. Do not add to the position at current levels; the bull's 'peak capex' and 'FCF inflection' theses are unverified assumptions, not confirmed catalysts — wait for at least two consecutive quarters of narrowing FCF deficit and explicit management capex guidance before considering re-entry. Set a hard risk trigger: if TTM FCF deteriorates further from -$3.09B or interest coverage falls below ~2x, cut the remaining position further. Avoid adding on dividend-yield attraction alone (4.75%) given the negative FCF makes the dividend's sustainability questionable without continued debt issuance. Watch Fed rate-cut probability and any AES refinancing activity/debt issuance spreads as a leading indicator of stress — widening spreads or opportunistic equity issuance would be a negative signal reinforcing further de-risking. If bullish capex-peak thesis is confirmed via next 1-2 quarterly reports (declining capex/revenue ratio, positive FCF inflection), revisit toward Hold/Overweight; until then, maintain underweight stance with a defined stop-loss below recent technical support (~200-day MA area) to manage tail risk from the extreme leverage profile.
