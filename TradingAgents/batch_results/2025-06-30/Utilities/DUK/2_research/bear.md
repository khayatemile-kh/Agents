## **3. REGULATORY RECOVERY IS NOT GUARANTEED—AND IT'S GETTING HARDER**

Let me destroy the central pillar of the bull's argument: the claim that debt is "contractually supported by regulated rates."

**This is naive. Regulatory proceedings are *political*, not mechanical.**

Here's what's actually happening in utility regulation in 2025:

### **A) Regulators Are Becoming More Hostile to Rate Increases**

- Across the Southeast (where DUK operates—Carolinas, Florida), **state regulatory commissions are under pressure from legislatures and constituents to moderate rate increases**.
- In North Carolina and South Carolina, there's been documented pushback on DUK's proposed rate increases, particularly around cost recovery for grid modernization projects.
- Florida's PSC (Public Service Commission) has become *increasingly* scrutinous of utility capex ROI, sometimes rejecting or delaying cost recovery.

**The bull assumes a 9-10% ROE is "guaranteed" because it's been the historical norm.** But utilities are facing a new political reality: **voters hate rate hikes.** Regulators respond to voters. The result: **contested rate cases, delayed cost recovery, and lower-than-historical ROE outcomes.**

### **B) The Capex Bill Is Going Up, But Recovery Timelines Are Stretching**

DUK's capital expenditure program is massive—likely $15-18B+ annually for grid modernization, renewable integration, and EV charging infrastructure. The bull presents this as a *plus*:

> *"These investments are rate-recoverable, meaning regulators permit ROI above the base case."*

**This is partially true, but dangerously incomplete.**

Here's the reality:

- **Rate recovery is *delayed*, not simultaneous with capex spend.** Utilities invest $1B in grid modernization, then file a rate case 12-24 months *after* the investment is complete, requesting to add it to the rate base.
- **Regulators frequently reject or scale back requested recovery amounts.** Full cost recovery is not guaranteed; typical utility cost recovery is 70-90% of requested amounts, with the remainder absorbed by shareholders.
- **The "regulatory lag" is lengthening in many jurisdictions**, not shortening. This means DUK is spending capital *now* (when interest rates are high) but not recovering returns until *later* (when rates may be even higher, compressing returns).

### **C) Energy Transition Capex ROI is Speculative, Not Certain**

The bull claims EV charging and renewable energy buildout will drive "high single-digit to low double-digit growth in the rate base."

**This is fiction.**

Here's why:

- **EV charging networks have *terrible* initial economics.** Utilization rates are typically 20-30% in nascent charging networks. Regulators are increasingly *skeptical* about cost recovery for stranded EV charging assets that don't generate returns.
- **Renewable energy investments face cannibalization risk.** As renewables penetration increases, wholesale electricity prices fall during solar/wind generation hours. This *compresses* utility margins, not expands them. The more renewables DUK builds, the lower the returns on that asset base.
- **Battery storage ROI is uncertain.** Grid-scale battery storage is nascent, costs are falling faster than expected, and regulatory frameworks for cost recovery are still being developed. This is *speculative*, not rate-recoverable capex.

The bull is assuming benign regulatory outcomes on speculative capex. **History suggests otherwise.**
