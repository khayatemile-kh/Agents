## **1. THE REVENUE INFLECTION: UNSUSTAINABLE GROWTH RATES EMBEDDED IN A NARROWING MARKET**

The bull analyst just threw impressive numbers at you:
- 2024: +32.1% YoY growth
- Q2 2025 annualized: +38% vs 2024

Here's what they *didn't* tell you: **This level of acceleration is not sustainable, and the market knows it.**

### The GLP-1 Market is NOT a $100B Opportunity—It's a Narrowing TAM Problem

Let me challenge the bull's core thesis directly. They claim the GLP-1 market will grow from $20B today to $100B+ by 2030. **I call that fantasy.**

Here's the reality:

**1. Market Saturation is Already Here**

- Novo Nordisk (NVO) has *first-mover advantage* with semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy), launched in diabetes in 2013 and obesity in 2021
- By 2025, NVO has already penetrated the easiest-to-reach patients: high-net-worth individuals, early adopters, healthcare workers with access
- LLY entered the market *later* with Mounjaro (diabetes 2022, obesity 2023)
- The low-hanging fruit is already picked

**2. Payer and Reimbursement Pressure is Mounting**

Look at what's happening *right now*:
- **Medicare negotiations on drug pricing** are explicitly targeting high-cost biologics, with GLP-1s squarely in the crosshairs
- **Private payers** are implementing tighter prior authorization requirements
- **Step therapy protocols** are increasingly limiting first-line access to GLP-1s, forcing step-down to older, cheaper alternatives (metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors, DPP-4 inhibitors)
- **State Medicaid programs** are restricting coverage to patients with specific BMI thresholds or comorbidities, not blanket coverage

The narrative that GLP-1s represent "standard of care" for all obese patients is *already* collapsing under payer pressure. This isn't a 2027 problem—it's a *2025 problem*.

**3. The $100B Addressable Market Assumption is Based on Fantasy Penetration Rates**

The bull's argument rests on the assumption that:
- 150+ million people globally with obesity will gain access to GLP-1s
- Penetration rates will reach 30-40% over time
- Pricing will remain in the $12,000-15,000/year range (current U.S. rates)

**This is not realistic.** Here's why:

- **Developing markets won't pay U.S. prices.** India, Southeast Asia, Latin America—these regions will demand generic/biosimilar alternatives at 70-80% discount to current U.S. pricing
- **Obesity is a lifestyle issue, not a disease mandate.** Unlike diabetes, which is chronic and managed by doctors, obesity treatment relies on patient *compliance*. Real-world data already shows 40-50% discontinuation rates within 12 months due to cost, side effects, or behavioral issues
- **Supply chain constraints are real.** The FDA has already flagged shortages of tirzepatide; LLY is scrambling to expand capacity. This caps growth *regardless of demand*

**The actual addressable market for high-priced GLP-1s:** Probably $40-50B globally by 2030, not $100B. And that's *shared* across multiple competitors (NVO, LLY, Amgen, AstraZeneca, others entering soon).

### Growth Will Decelerate Sharply—The Numbers Prove It

Here's the key insight: **LLY's revenue growth *already peaked* in Q2 2025 at the 38% annualized rate.**

Why? Because:

1. **GLP-1 market adoption follows an S-curve, not a linear growth curve.** The initial ramp (2023-2024) captures the "easy" patients. The next phase (2025-2026) will see incremental adoption, but at *decelerating* rates as payer restrictions bite
2. **Q2 2025's outsized growth was driven by inventory fill and pent-up demand.** When the Q3 and Q4 2025 results come in, you'll see the growth rate normalize from 38% down to 18-22% as comparisons get harder and payer pressures intensify
3. **Patent cliffs are coming.** Mounjaro's patent expires in 2032-2033 (depending on extensions). But biosimilar approval pathways for GLP-1s are accelerating—we could see tirzepatide biosimilars 2-3 years *earlier* than traditional pharma timelines suggest

**The bull's forward EPS projection of $47.26 for 2025 assumes these growth rates persist.** They won't.

### These Margins are Structurally Unsustainable

Here's the brutal truth: **The company has printed an unusually high margin in a single quarter due to temporary favorable conditions, not a structural business improvement.**

Let's break down what's actually happening:

**1. Cost of Revenue is Artificially Depressed**

- Q2 2025 CoGS: $2.45B (15.7% of revenue)
- 84.3% gross margin is *not normal* for pharmaceutical manufacturing

Why are COGS so low?
- **Manufacturing scale-up inefficiencies are hidden.** LLY is ramping tirzepatide production, which means they're running newer, more efficient facilities at high capacity utilization. This *temporarily* looks great on the margin line
- **Drug pricing in the U.S. market commands premium rates.** But once international markets ramp (where pricing is 50-70% lower), and once volume mixes shift away from pure U.S. revenue, this COGS ratio will deteriorate significantly
- **Mix is biased toward high-margin specialty drugs *right now*.** Once GLP-1 becomes more commoditized and payers demand discounts, gross margin *will compress*

**2. R&D Spending Will Need to Increase, Not Decrease**

The bull noted that R&D is 21.5% of revenue, "down from prior trends." This is *misleading*.

- **LLY is in a "harvest" phase for current products, not a sustained growth phase.** To maintain growth beyond 2027-2028, they *must* invest heavily in next-generation products

- **The 21.5% figure is a *trough*, not a new baseline.** The bull is confusing temporary operational efficiency with structural improvement
