## THE VALUATION TRAP: WHEN GROWTH EXPECTATIONS BECOME LIABILITIES

The aggressive analyst leads with "38% revenue growth justifies 37.91x trailing P/E." That's seductive logic, but it's fundamentally flawed reasoning. Here's why:

First, let's be precise about what we're actually seeing. Yes, LLY has grown revenues from $28.54 billion in 2022 to $45.04 billion in 2024—that's a 26% CAGR. The annualized Q2 2025 run-rate suggests $62 billion, which extrapolated would be another 38% bump. But here's the critical distinction: **Q2 2025 is one quarter.** One. Single. Quarter.

A single quarter of exceptional performance does not establish a sustainable growth trajectory. When you annualize a single quarter, you're implicitly assuming that quarterly revenue run-rate continues unabated for the entire year. That is not how pharmaceutical companies operate. That is not how GLP-1 markets mature. And frankly, that's exactly the kind of backward-looking extrapolation that gets investors caught holding overvalued positions when reality normalizes.

Look at the fundamentals more carefully. The operating margin expansion from 30.2% in 2023 to 45.1% in Q2 2025 is presented as evidence of operating leverage. But I'm reading this differently: **this is the margin profile of a company ramping up production of a brand-new, high-margin product category that's currently experiencing artificial scarcity-driven pricing power.** 

When GLP-1 supply chains normalize, when biosimilar competition intensifies—and it will, because this is a multi-hundred-billion-dollar market and competitors are all racing to capture share—those margins compress. That's not pessimism; that's pharmaceutical market dynamics. We saw this with statins, with antiretrovirals, with every blockbuster category in history.

The aggressive case wants to argue that the forward P/E of 23.9x is "reasonable" for 30%+ growth. But they're assuming that growth rate holds. Let me present an uncomfortable scenario: What if LLY delivers only 15-18% earnings growth next year instead of 30%? That's still excellent growth. That's still outperforming the broader market. That's not catastrophizing—that's basic valuation math.

**PBM Pressure:** Pharmacy benefit managers are consolidating and increasingly pushing back on specialty drug pricing. They have leverage because they control formulary access.
