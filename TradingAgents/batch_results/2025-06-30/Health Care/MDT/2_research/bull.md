## **PILLAR 1: DEFENSIVE STRENGTH IN A RECESSIONARY ENVIRONMENT**

### The Macro Backdrop: Recession Risk is Rising (Quietly)

The prediction market data tells a story that mainstream financial media hasn't fully amplified: **soft-landing probability has collapsed from ~57% to 48% in just one week**. That's a 9-percentage-point repricing of recession risk in 7 days. More telling: unemployment expectations ≥5.0% in 2026 have climbed to 10% (up 2.1pp week-over-week).

**This is important for MDT because healthcare/medical devices are structurally different from cyclical equities.**

### Why MDT is a Recession Hedge Within Healthcare

Here's the critical distinction most investors miss: **Not all healthcare is created equal in a downturn.**

- **Elective procedures** (cosmetic surgery, some orthopedic work) DO get deferred
- **Essential procedures** (cardiac interventions, diagnostics, neurostimulation) do NOT

Medtronic's core portfolio is **heavily skewed toward essential, high-acuity care:**
- Cardiovascular devices (defibrillators, pacemakers, valve therapies)
- Oncology and minimally invasive surgery platforms
- Neurostimulation (spinal cord stimulation, deep brain stimulation)
- Monitoring and diagnostic equipment

**These aren't discretionary.** A patient with atrial fibrillation doesn't defer their pacemaker implant because unemployment ticked up. A cancer patient doesn't postpone their surgical intervention because the economy softened.

In fact, **hospital systems often prioritize essential procedure revenue during downturns** because it's predictable, reimbursed, and drives volumes when discretionary electives decline. This creates a **natural hedge** for MDT's installed base.

Compare this to, say, a cosmetic surgery company or elective orthopedic-focused player—those would see immediate volume pressure. MDT's portfolio is fundamentally more resilient.

### The PEG Ratio Tells a Different Story

The PEG ratio of 1.69 has come up in bear discussions as "elevated," but this misreads the data:

- A PEG of 1.0 = stock is fairly valued relative to growth
- A PEG of 1.5–2.0 = stock is fairly to slightly undervalued
- A PEG >2.5 = stock is overvalued

**MDT's PEG of 1.69 implies the stock is fairly valued assuming ~10% annual EPS growth.** Is that aggressive? Let me show you why it's not:

**FY2025 EPS: $3.61**  
**FY2024 EPS: $2.76**  
**YoY Growth: +30.8%**

Now, 30.8% isn't sustainable—but even if we normalize to **10% EPS growth** (which is **entirely achievable** given revenue growth + margin expansion + continued buybacks), the PEG of 1.69 implies MDT is *fairly valued, not expensive.*
