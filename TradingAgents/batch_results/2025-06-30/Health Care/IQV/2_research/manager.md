**Rationale**: Weighing both sides, the bear case lands more punches on the facts actually presented. The bull's central pillar—"CRO market growing 5-7% while IQV grows 2.8%"—is self-defeating: if true, it means IQV is losing share in its own core market, not gaining it, which undercuts the "compounder" narrative rather than supporting it. The bull's deleveraging math also doesn't hold up once you annualize the weaker Q2 FCF ($292M -> ~$1.17B) against last year's $2.1B FCF and net out dividends/buybacks/capex; the realistic delever pace is far slower than claimed, and interest expense (~$670M) eats most of the incremental FCF. The bull's technical setup (RSI, MACD, EMA) is real but describes a short-term bounce, not a fundamental re-rating catalyst, and shouldn't override balance-sheet and margin deterioration. On macro, the bear's reframe is more consistent with the data: a soft landing removes the urgency for pharma to outsource aggressively and increases the risk that CRO clients push back on pricing, which is exactly what the margin compression shows. The bull's growth-acceleration argument (H1 2025 run-rate of +4.1%) is a fair point and keeps this from being a full sell, but it doesn't offset the credibility problem in the market-share argument or the levered balance sheet trending the wrong way in the most recent quarter. Net: bear arguments about margin compression, leverage trajectory, and market-share loss are grounded in the actual reported numbers and outweigh the bull's technical momentum and multi-year growth-compounding thesis, but the growth re-acceleration and cash generation aren't dire enough to justify an outright Sell.
