## **PILLAR 1: STRUCTURAL DEMAND TAILWINDS ARE UNDENIABLE**

### The Healthcare Device Market Isn't Discretionary—It's Essential

First, let's establish the macro backdrop. GEHC operates in diagnostic imaging, healthcare IT, and patient monitoring. These aren't luxury goods. When a hospital needs a CT scanner, an ultrasound system, or a cardiology monitoring platform, that capital expenditure gets approved regardless of the economic cycle. This is **counter-cyclical demand**—precisely what we want in uncertain times.

**Here's the demographic math:**
- Global population aging is accelerating
- The 65+ population will grow by 2%+ annually through 2035
- This directly drives imaging volume, diagnostic test frequency, and monitoring needs
- GEHC's addressable market is *expanding* by definition

This isn't speculation—it's demographic destiny. And it's baked into that 24.7% forward EPS growth expectation. If the market was truly worried about demand, would forward earnings be expected to grow nearly 25%?

### Hospital Capital Spending Remains Resilient

I want to address what might be a bear concern head-on: *"But what if hospital budgets get squeezed?"*

Here's the reality: Hospital capital spending is prioritized differently than discretionary corporate capex. When administrators face budget constraints, they cut staff overtime or defer maintenance—they don't defer imaging equipment upgrades. Why? Because diagnostic accuracy directly impacts patient outcomes and revenue. A hospital can't afford a non-functional MRI suite.

**Additionally**, GEHC has significant recurring revenue through service contracts and software subscriptions (their healthcare IT and clinical care solutions). This recurring revenue stream stabilizes cash flows and reduces sensitivity to cyclical capex patterns.
