## **The Deferred Revenue Red Flag That Nobody's Talking About**

Here's a number that should make any serious investor uncomfortable: **deferred revenue represents only 12.8% of TTM sales.**

Deferred revenue is the lifeblood of recurring revenue narratives. It's cash already collected for services/products to be delivered later—it's sticky, predictable, and it converts to high-margin revenue in future periods. For healthcare IT companies and SaaS-adjacent business models, this ratio typically sits at 25-35% of annual sales. 

At 12.8%, GEHC is demonstrating **weak recurring revenue characteristics.** This means the business is far more transactional and project-based than the growth narrative suggests. Every quarter, the company has to fight for new orders rather than benefiting from a revenue backlog that's largely pre-sold.

Why does this matter for *right now?* Because if the economic environment shifts—if hospital capex budgets tighten, if elective procedures decline, if healthcare providers face margin pressure—GEHC has **no buffer.** There's no substantial backlog to sustain earnings through a demand shock. The 12.8% deferred revenue ratio means the company is exposed to cyclicality.

And guess what? If forward EPS growth assumes *continued* strong order flow in a potentially softening environment, that thesis is vulnerable. The trader is right to trim into this rally strength, because the risk/reward is skewed toward disappointment.
