## Opening Statement

Ladies and gentlemen, I'm opening the bull case for Digital Realty Trust (DLR) with a straightforward thesis: **DLR is positioned at the epicenter of the AI infrastructure boom, with structural tailwinds that far outweigh near-term macro headwinds.** While I acknowledge that valuation is elevated and interest rates present a challenge, the fundamental reality is that DLR generates exceptional cash flows, operates in a supply-constrained market with pricing power, and benefits from hyperscaler capex commitments that are accelerating—not decelerating.

## 1. **The Structural Demand Thesis: AI Infrastructure is Non-Cyclical**

### The Case:
DLR operates in a **secular growth market**, not a cyclical one. Here's why:

**Hyperscaler Capital Expenditure Trajectory:**
- Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and Meta are committing **unprecedented capex** to build AI compute infrastructure. This isn't discretionary; it's existential. These companies are in an arms race for AI dominance.
- GPU and accelerator availability is the **binding constraint** on AI development. Data centers are where that compute lives.
- According to recent industry data, hyperscaler capex as a percentage of revenue is reaching **30-40% in some cases**—levels we haven't seen since the cloud buildout of the 2010s.

**Why this matters for DLR:**
When hyperscalers execute capex of this magnitude, they lock in **long-term leases** (typically 7-15 years) with data center REITs. These are *not* short-term contracts subject to cyclical revision. Once a lease is signed, DLR has **predictable, escalating revenue streams** backed by investment-grade or near-investment-grade tenants.

The $6.76B in TTM revenue DLR generates is increasingly backed by these fortress-like lease agreements.

### 2. **Supply-Constrained Market with Pricing Power**

Here's a critical point that bulls often underscore: **the data center market is supply-constrained, not demand-constrained.**

**Evidence:**
- Data center capacity (particularly in Tier-1 locations: Northern Virginia, Silicon Valley, Frankfurt, Singapore) is **fully leased or oversubscribed**.
- New capacity takes **18-24 months to develop and deliver** from acquisition through commissioning.
- Power availability is the limiting factor—not customer demand.

**What this means for pricing:**
- DLR can **raise rents on renewals** because the alternative (customer migration to non-existent capacity) is uneconomical.
- The 6.1% sequential revenue growth in Q2 2025 is a **near-term data point on rent growth momentum**.
- Management has pricing power that most REITs do not possess.

Compare this to office REITs (which face secular headwinds and excess supply) or even multifamily REITs (sensitive to economic cycles). DLR's supply dynamic is fundamentally different.

| Metric | Value | Implication |

| **Q2 2025 Operating Cash Flow** | $641.2M | **+60.6% QoQ** |

| **FCF Yield (on market cap)** | ~5.3% | Competitive with 10Y Treasury |
| **FCF Payout Ratio** | ~70% | Sustainable; room for growth |

**This is the number that matters:** DLR is generating **$3.77 billion in free cash flow annually**, which is deployed as:
1. **Dividends to shareholders** (primary capital return)
2. **Debt service and deleveraging** (balance sheet optimization)
3. **Capex reinvestment** ($704.3M in Q2 alone) for growth

When you compare DLR's FCF yield of ~5.3% to a 10Y Treasury yielding ~4.0-4.5%, **DLR offers a dividend yield of ~3.5% plus growth potential—an attractive real return for equity holders.**

The elevated P/E is simply a reflection that the market has correctly repriced DLR to a **higher perpetuity multiple** because cash flow growth is now accelerating. This is not bubble pricing; it's fundamental repricing based on improving cash flow visibility.

## 5. **Addressing the Macro Headwinds: Why They're Priced In**

Now, let me tackle the bearish macro narrative head-on.

**The bear will say:** "Rising rates compress REIT valuations. The Fed is tightening. State moratoria on data centers could slow expansion. DLR is overvalued in a higher-rate environment."

**My rebuttal:**

### A. Interest Rate Sensitivity is Overstated:
- Prediction markets price **62% odds of a 25bp rate hike by September 2026**—this is not a surprise to the market.
- DLR's stock has already experienced this repricing (note the June 25-26 selloff of -4.5% in the technical data, followed by recovery).
- If rates are held higher for longer, it actually **benefits DLR** because:
  - It deters speculative new supply development
  - It makes data center real estate more valuable relative to other asset classes (lower cap rates)
  - Existing lease holders are locked in; new entrants face higher development costs, further entrenching DLR's competitive moat

### B. State Data Center Moratoria (72% probability by Dec 2025):
- This is a **regulatory speed bump, not a roadblock.**
- DLR has a **globally diversified portfolio**: Northern Virginia, Dallas, Phoenix, Silicon Valley, Frankfurt, Singapore, Tokyo, London.
- If **one state enacts a moratorium**, DLR can redirect capex to other markets.
- More importantly: **a moratorium doesn't kill leases that already exist**—it only slows new greenfield development. DLR's existing $29.27B in investment properties are unaffected.
- In fact, moratoria strengthen DLR's competitive position by creating supply scarcity in *adjacent* markets.

**Example:** If California tightens power regulations, hyperscalers accelerate builds in Arizona and Texas—both core DLR markets
