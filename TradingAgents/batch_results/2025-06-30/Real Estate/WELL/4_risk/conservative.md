## THE DEBT-TO-EQUITY RATIO IS A MISLEADING SCARE TACTIC

Let's start with the elephant in the room that the aggressive analyst is weaponizing: the 41x debt-to-equity ratio. Yes, it's high. But here's what you're not hearing: **this number is nearly meaningless for REITs without proper context, and frankly, the aggressive analyst knows it but chose inflammatory language anyway.**

Here's why. REITs are statutorily required to distribute 90% of taxable income to shareholders. That's not optional—it's regulatory. The tax code essentially forces REITs to maintain elevated leverage because they can't retain earnings to build equity buffers like industrial companies. So when you calculate D/E for a REIT using traditional balance sheet equity, you're comparing apples to oranges.

The proper metric for REIT leverage analysis is **debt-to-EBITDA** or **loan-to-value (LTV) on the asset base.** We don't have LTV disclosed in the current report, but we can calculate debt-to-EBITDA: $17.31 billion in long-term debt divided by $947.72 million in Q2 EBITDA times four (annualizing) equals approximately **4.6x debt-to-EBITDA.** 

That is not reckless. That is textbook REIT leverage. Industry standard for quality healthcare REITs ranges from 4.0x to 5.5x. WELL is *in the middle of the pack*, not in some exotic risk territory. The aggressive analyst cited the 41x D/E ratio and called it a "death trap." It's not. It's a number that looks scary if you don't understand the REIT operating model.

## THE "105x P/E" IS A STATISTICAL ARTIFACT, NOT A VALUATION CONCERN

Now let's talk about the P/E multiple that the aggressive analyst used as exhibit A for "pricing in perfection." Here's the problem: we're looking at a trailing P/E of 105x based on TTM net income of $1.55 billion against a market cap of roughly $169.6 billion.

But that TTM figure is distorted. Let me walk through why. REIT net income is NOT the right earnings metric for valuation. Funds From Operations (FFO) is. Why? Because REIT net income includes the depreciation of real estate assets, which is a non-cash charge. It also includes gains and losses on asset sales that distort the run-rate operating power.

We don't have the exact FFO figure disclosed in this report, but we can estimate it. Using a more realistic FFO-based multiple would put WELL at somewhere in the **12-14x FFO range**, which is reasonable for a quality healthcare REIT with stable occupancy and strategic properties. That's not "pricing in perfection." That's fair valuation.

The aggressive analyst cherry-picked the P/E metric precisely because it's the scariest-looking number on the page. That's not rigorous analysis—that's scare mongering.
