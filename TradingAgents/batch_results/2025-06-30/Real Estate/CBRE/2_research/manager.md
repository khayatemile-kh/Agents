**Rationale**: Both sides make reasonable points, but when I weigh the strongest arguments against each other, the bear case exposes real cracks that the bull glosses over, while the bull's technical case is built on a rally that's already happened and shows signs of losing steam.

On technicals: the bull's "institutional accumulation" story is undercut by the bear's own data - volume declined from 5.35M (May 30) to 2.67M (June 27) to 1.90M (June 30) as the stock approached the upper Bollinger Band. That's a textbook low-conviction rally. RSI at 66.33 is in the upper half of the momentum range, not "early-stage," and the stock has repeatedly tested $140-143 resistance without a clean breakout. The bull's counter - that MACD is still widening - is fair, but declining volume into resistance is the more decisive tell here; rallies that can't attract fresh volume near highs are vulnerable to mean-reversion toward the middle Bollinger Band (~$133), which is roughly a 5% pullback from current levels.

On fundamentals: the bull's operational leverage story doesn't hold up on closer inspection. The bear correctly flags that TTM EBITDA margin (5.1%) is actually below the full-year 2024 margin (5.4%), meaning the most recent stretch of business has seen margin compression, not expansion. Similarly, the Q2 2025 annualized run rate ($38.8B) is below the TTM revenue figure ($43.7B), suggesting deceleration rather than the "accelerating momentum" the bull claims. The 2023-to-2024 revenue acceleration also benefits from an easy trough-year comparison, which the bull didn't address.

Where the bull is on firmer ground is valuation (sub-1x P/S is genuinely cheap for a business with double-digit EBITDA in dollar terms) and the debt picture - net debt/EBITDA of 2.6x is manageable and investment-grade rated, so this isn't a solvency story. But cheap valuation without a credible near-term acceleration catalyst is a value trap risk, not a clear buy signal, especially with technicals showing waning momentum.

Net-net: the balance of evidence tilts bearish-to-neutral. The best-supported near-term view is that the stock has run up on fading volume into resistance while underlying margin and growth trends are softening, not accelerating. That doesn't scream "sell everything" given the reasonable valuation and non-distressed balance sheet, but it does argue against adding exposure here and favors trimming into strength given the technical exhaustion signals and the fundamental deceleration the bear identified that the bull couldn't adequately rebut.
