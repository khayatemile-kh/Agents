## **PART 1: THE CYCLICAL BOTTOM IS VISIBLE – THIS IS A CYCLICAL BUSINESS, NOT A BROKEN COMPANY**

### **Context Matters – Where Are We in the Cycle?**

DOW operates in **basic chemicals**, one of the most cyclical sectors in the market. The current weakness is not unique to DOW; it's a **sector-wide trough** driven by:

1. **Excess global chemical capacity post-2022 energy crisis**
2. **Elevated input costs that have only recently begun moderating**
3. **Weak downstream demand as manufacturing indices contracted in Q1-Q2 2025**

But—and this is critical—**cyclical troughs create opportunity, not permanent destruction.**

The macro data suggests we're at an inflection point:
- **Inflation moderating to 3.4% by August 2025** (per prediction markets): This means **feedstock costs are stabilizing and input price tailwinds are fading**
- **No active recession is being priced by prediction markets**: This indicates soft-landing expectations, meaning industrial demand will recover, not collapse
- **Energy markets have stabilized**: Oil and natural gas volatility has compressed, which is **direct margin support for chemical producers**

**What does this mean for DOW?** As input costs normalize and demand stabilizes, the company's massive installed asset base ($23.72B in net PPE) becomes a competitive **moat, not a burden**. You cannot build new chemical capacity quickly—DOW's plants are operational assets that will generate returns as the cycle turns.
