## **3. THE ACCOUNTS RECEIVABLE SURGE IS A FEATURE, NOT A BUG**

I want to address the elephant in the room: **A/R jumped 39.1% to $5.33B.** 

The bear might argue this signals customer stress or collection issues. **I'd argue the opposite.**

**Here's why:** When A/R grows faster than revenue, it *usually* signals one of two things:
1. **Sales acceleration ahead of cash collection** — a timing lag in a growing business (bullish)
2. **Deteriorating credit quality** — customers struggling to pay (bearish)

**The context here supports interpretation #1:** 
- Q2 2025 revenue is running at +14.6% YoY — accounts receivable growth of +39% is consistent with that sales acceleration
- CRH's DSO (Days Sales Outstanding) likely increased from ~55 days (reasonable for the industry) to perhaps 65-70 days, which is manageable
- The company is *profitable* (generating $1.3B in net income in Q2) and *generating positive operating cash flow* — if there were credit issues, FCF would be collapsing. It's not.

**The real takeaway:** This is a working capital timing issue in a high-growth environment. As sales normalize or cash collections catch up in H2, A/R growth will decelerate and this will be a non-issue. In fact, **managing working capital efficiently will unlock additional free cash flow, providing another upside lever.**
