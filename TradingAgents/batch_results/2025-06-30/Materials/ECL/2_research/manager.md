The bear's pushback is legitimate on nuance but doesn't overturn the bull thesis on the merits actually in evidence.

Where the bear scores real points: revenue growth has decelerated (3.94%→2.75% YoY), buybacks are heavily driving EPS growth (much more than net income growth alone would suggest), and CapEx/Sales at 6.3% is on the lower end for the sector, raising a legitimate reinvestment-versus-margin-durability question. These are valid risk flags to monitor, not thesis-breakers.

Where the bear overreaches: the argument leans on speculative future scenarios ("if raw material costs rise 10%... margins compress 4-5pts") rather than data already in evidence — margins have been expanding for three consecutive periods, not compressing, and there's no evidence in the debate of actual raw material or competitive pressure materializing yet. The bear also implies the EPS math doesn't add up ("net income grew 23.5% but EPS grew 54%??") — this is flagged as a "gotcha" but is actually explained cleanly by combining ~20%+ net income growth with a meaningfully lower share count from buybacks; heavy buybacks are a stated, transparent capital allocation choice (91% of FCF returned), not evidence of manipulation. A 36.5% dividend payout ratio and 1.52x leverage give ample cushion to keep returning capital even if growth normalizes further.

Net verdict: the bull case is grounded in trailing, verifiable financial performance — margin expansion, robust and growing FCF, sound balance sheet, and confirmed technical uptrend. The bear's most credible point (deceleration in top-line growth, lower reinvestment rate) tempers full conviction but is not enough to flip the picture to negative, especially with margins still expanding in the most recent quarter and technicals intact above key moving averages. This supports a constructive but not maximum-conviction stance — Overweight rather than outright aggressive Buy, allowing room to add on further margin/FCF confirmation while being mindful of the deceleration/reinvestment risk the bear raised.
