**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: AVY generates solid FCF (~7.7% margin), has a high ROE (31%) that is partly leverage-inflated (ROIC closer to ~20.7%), trades at 15x forward P/E, and carries meaningful leverage (2.23x net debt/EBITDA, D/E 1.61x). The bull's strongest points are that cash generation is real, capital returns are substantial (100% of FCF), the business has structural e-commerce/labeling tailwinds, and current multiples aren't stretched on an absolute basis. The bear's strongest points are more compelling on balance: this is a consumer-cyclical business with leverage that looks fine in a benign environment but turns dangerous in a downturn (recession-stress test pushes net debt/EBITDA toward 3x+), the 100%-of-FCF payout leaves no cushion to delever, technicals show a bearish MACD crossover and price below the 200-day SMA, and the valuation premium versus lower-levered peers (SEE, WRK) isn't clearly justified given the higher leverage AVY carries. The bull's rebuttal on inflation resilience and macro pricing-in is reasonable but doesn't fully address the structural fragility highlighted by the bear's recession scenario and the capital allocation critique (buybacks/dividends funded entirely by FCF with no debt paydown, in a rate environment where cuts are not expected until 2026+). The interest coverage bear correction actually understates their own point (7.2x is decent), so bulls retain some strength there, but the overall risk/reward skews cautious given cyclical exposure and lack of margin of safety on the balance sheet.

Net take: the debate is not perfectly balanced — the bear's macro/leverage/technical argument is more evidence-based and directly addresses forward risk, while the bull's case leans on backward-looking margin stability and hopes that pricing power persists. Given the elevated leverage in a rate environment that won't ease until 2026, and technical confirmation of downtrend (below 200-SMA, bearish MACD), the reasonable stance is cautious — trim/underweight rather than add, but not an outright sell since the business is still profitable, cash-generative, and not in immediate distress (interest coverage is healthy, EBITDA still solidly positive, and dividend well-covered on a current basis).
