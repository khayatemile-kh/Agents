**Investment Thesis**: The debate converges on one key forensic fact that survives scrutiny from all three analysts: the Q2 deleveraging ($2.4B debt repaid, net debt down to $6.85B) was funded by a non-repeatable $2.7B asset sale, not organic cash generation. Even the Conservative and Neutral analysts, who otherwise pushed back hard on the Aggressive analyst's most extreme EBITDA-stripping math (arguing normalized EBITDA is closer to $600-700M quarterly rather than $412M, and TTM Net Debt/EBITDA closer to 2.4-2.6x rather than 3.2-3.4x), concede the core vulnerability: Q2 operating cash flow of only $241M and FCF of $146M against $6.85B of remaining debt leaves "limited margin for error" (Neutral) and cannot be extrapolated into a 15% forward ROE without relying on non-repeatable continuing-operations income (Conservative's own estimate was rebutted by Neutral, who notes normalized operating income is likely only $150-200M quarterly, not $550M). This directly corroborates the Research Manager's finding that TTM Net Debt/EBITDA (~2.8x) and TTM interest coverage (~7.8x) are the honest denominators, not the annualized single-quarter figures the bull case leans on.

The technical picture reinforces rather than drives the fundamental case: price is below both the 50-SMA ($73.96) and 200-SMA ($82.59), MACD has completed a bearish cross with a widening histogram (-0.256), and RSI at 42.04 is trending down without yet reaching oversold — consistent with what the Aggressive analyst calls "controlled institutional selling" rather than capitulation. The Neutral analyst's critique of the trader's stop-loss placement is well-founded, however: a stop at $73.96 sits at a level price has already broken, which is poor risk calibration; the lower Bollinger Band ($70.66) or Keltner channel is a more sensible risk boundary that avoids selling into a level already breached.

The Conservative analyst's rebuttal has real merit on liquidity (2.06x current ratio, $816M cash, manageable debt maturity ladder with only $500M current) and prevents this from becoming a full Sell — this is not imminent distress. But their optimism overreaches materially on two fronts: (1) treating Q2's continuing-operations net income as representative of a "new normal" ROE trajectory ignores that goodwill/intangibles are 56% of assets, meaning any further write-down risk (a repeated pattern per prior IFF cycles) could compress book equity sharply; and (2) their EBITDA normalization, while more credible than the Aggressive analyst's floor, still leaves leverage in the "elevated but not distressed" 2.4-2.8x band — a zone where, per the Aggressive analyst's unrebutted point, further asset-sale optionality is genuinely limited once the "crown jewels" are sold, and the base business's 3.2% normalized operating margin offers little cushion against input-cost or consumer-demand shocks flagged in the macro backdrop.

Consistent with the explicit lesson from the March 2025, December 2024, and September 2024 IFF cycles — that splitting the difference between deteriorating fundamentals and a constructive-looking technical setup underperforms decisive de-risking, and that fundamentals (earnings quality, leverage, ROE) have proven the more durable predictor of forward alpha than technical bounce narratives in this name specifically — the weight of evidence again favors continued reduction rather than holding full size. This is not a Sell: interest coverage remains solidly positive on a TTM basis, liquidity is adequate, and gross margins (42.9%) reflect real product differentiation that the bear case doesn't fully discredit. But the reliance on a non-repeatable cash inflow to fund the "turnaround," negative TTM ROE (-5.55%), and unresolved technical breakdown together argue for trimming into weakness and setting a hard organic-cash-flow confirmation gate before re-accumulating.
