## **OPENING STATEMENT: The Bull Case Is Built on Fragile Assumptions and Ignoring Critical Risks**

Look, I respect the bull's enthusiasm, but let's be honest: **the market is pricing in perfection, and ServiceNow is a company facing structural headwinds that could derail this narrative in the next 12-24 months.** The data isn't as clean as the bull presents it—there are red flags everywhere if you look beyond the headline growth numbers. Let me walk through why NOW is significantly overvalued and why the risks are asymmetric to the downside.

## **PART 1: VALUATION IS UNJUSTIFIABLE DESPITE FORWARD-LOOKING METRICS**

### **A. The TTM P/E of 82x Is Absolutely Indefensible**

Let me start with the elephant in the room: **NOW trades at an 82x trailing P/E.** Yes, the bull mentions the forward P/E of 26x, but let's not cherry-pick metrics.

**Here's the problem:**

The **TTM P/E of 82x** reflects what the market is **actually paying today** for current earnings. The forward P/E of 26x is a **prediction about future earnings**—and predictions are frequently wrong, especially in technology.

Let me break this down:
- **Current stock price:** $205.62
- **Current quarterly EPS:** $0.368 (Q2 2025)
- **Annualized EPS (at current run rate):** ~$1.47
- **TTM P/E of 82x means the market is paying $82 for every $1 of current earnings**

**For context:**
- The S&P 500 trades at ~20x P/E
- Mature SaaS leaders like Salesforce trade at ~35-40x
- Adobe trades at ~45x

ServiceNow at 82x is in territory **typically reserved for high-growth startups**, not a $10.9B revenue company with maturing growth rates. This is not "reasonable pricing"—it's speculative pricing.

**The bull's response:** "But the PEG ratio is 1.02, which is fair."

**My counter:** **The PEG ratio is mathematically misleading here.** Here's why:

- PEG assumes the current growth rate (22%) will continue indefinitely
- **But growth rates decelerate as companies scale**—this is inevitable
- If NOW's growth decelerates from 22% to 15% over the next 3-5 years (a realistic assumption), the **PEG-adjusted valuation becomes materially expensive**
- A company with decelerating growth trading at 82x TTM earnings is a value trap waiting to happen

### **B. The Bull Is Conflating Operating Margins with Valuation Justification**

The bull says: "Operating margins are expanding, so earnings will grow faster than revenue, justifying the valuation."

**This is where the bull's logic breaks down completely.**

Let me show you the actual numbers:

| Year | Revenue Growth | Operating Margin | Operating Income Growth |
|------|-----------------|------------------|-------------------------|
| 2022 → 2023 | +23.9% | 4.9% → 8.5% | +114.6% |
| 2023 → 2024 | +22.4% | 8.5% → 12.4% | +78.9% |
| 2024 → Q2 2025 | ~22% | 12.4% → 11.1% | ⬇️ DECLINING |

**Here's the critical insight the bull is missing:**

**Operating margin expansion is slowing down.** In 2023, margins expanded 360 basis points. In 2024, they expanded 390 basis points. But in Q2 2025, we see margins **contracting to 11.1% from 12.4%**—a 130 basis point decline **year-over-year on the same quarter** (Q2 2024 was likely ~12.5%).

**Why is this happening?**

1. **CapEx is increasing** — The company spent $892M in 2024 CapEx (8.1% of revenue), and this is likely to increase further as they invest in infrastructure for AI/automation features
2. **Sales & Marketing efficiency is plateauing** — As the company matures and customer acquisition becomes tougher, S&M costs as a percentage of revenue are likely to tick up
3. **Product mix is shifting** — Expansion into lower-margin segments (platform services, custom development) is cannibalizing overall margins

**The real story:** Margin expansion is the **primary driver** justifying the current valuation. Once this inflection point is reached (and Q2 2025 suggests it's already starting), the valuation case **collapses.**

If margins plateau at 12-14% (a reasonable assumption given competitive pressures), and revenue growth decelerates to 15-18% (also reasonable at scale), **the forward P/E doesn't justify 82x TTM earnings.**

## **PART 2: GROWTH DECELERATION IS INEVITABLE AND ACCELERATING**

### **A. 22% Revenue Growth at Scale Is Not Sustainable**

The bull presents 22% YoY growth as evidence of "sustainable, profitable growth." **This is denial.**

Here's the hard truth about SaaS companies:

| Company | Revenue | Growth Rate | TTM P/E |
|---------|---------|-------------|---------|
| Twilio (TWLO) | $3.6B | 15% | ~60x |
| Datadog (DDOG) | $1.8B | 28% | ~120x |
| Palantir (PLTR) | $2.2B | 26% | ~220x |
| Shopify (SHOP) | $6.3B | 25% | ~65x |
| ServiceNow (NOW) | $10.9B | 22% | **82x** |

**The pattern is clear:** ServiceNow is not exceptional in its growth profile. It's growing slower than peers like Datadog and Palantir, yet trading at comparable or **higher multiples.**

**More importantly:** Look at what happened to each of these companies as they crossed $5B+ in revenue:

- **Shopify:** Started at 50%+ growth, now at 25%
- **Datadog:** Started at 70%+ growth, now at 28%
- **Twilio:** Started at 100%+ growth, now at 15% (and stock crashed)

**The bull might say:** "But NOW's market is different—digital transformation is secular."

**My response:** Digital transformation is secular, but **NOW's penetration of that market is already substantial.** ServiceNow is already the market leader in IT Service Management. The greenfield growth story is largely behind them. Future growth will come from:

1. **Upsell/cross-sell** to existing customers (increasingly competitive and low-margin)
2. **International expansion** (slower growth, more price-sensitive markets)
3. **Platform/custom dev services** (lower-margin than core offerings)

This is a **mature company's growth profile**, not a growth company's growth profile. Yet the valuation is still pricing in startup-level multiples.
