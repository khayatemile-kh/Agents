The bear case will hammer the 110.4x debt-to-equity ratio. But here's what they're not telling you:

**This ratio is distorted by:**

1. **Negligible Shareholder Equity Base Due to Aggressive Buybacks**: Salesforce has treasury shares of 104 million, indicating a disciplined capital return program. When a company aggressively repurchases stock, shareholder equity shrinks, artificially inflating the D/E ratio while NOT increasing actual financial risk.

2. **Software Companies Have Inverted Capital Structures**: Unlike manufacturing or financial services, SaaS businesses operate with minimal hard assets and massive intangible value. A traditional D/E ratio is the wrong metric for evaluating them. 

3. **The Relevant Metric is Debt-to-EBITDA**:

This is **well within normal ranges** for stable, cash-generative software companies. For reference:

- Adobe: ~1.4x
- Workday: ~1.6x

**Salesforce's leverage is fundamentally sound when measured appropriately.**
