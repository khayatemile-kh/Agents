1. **Margins compress** (as they inevitably do when markets mature and competition intensifies)
2. **Revenue growth decelerates further** (we're already at 8-11%; what happens at 5%?)
3. **Customer acquisition costs rise** (enterprise software cycles are cyclical; acquisition efficiency won't remain constant)
4. **Credit conditions tighten** (rising rates make refinancing more expensive)

The aggressive analyst dismisses the debt uncertainty range of $14-22B as already "pricing in the worst case." But that uncertainty *itself* is a red flag. And more importantly, if refinancing becomes necessary in a higher-rate environment, that uncertainty becomes existential.

The FCF story—$17.73B annually, 40.4% margins—is genuinely strong. But here's what the aggressive case glosses over: **Free cash flow and share buybacks are not indefinite**. When management deploys all of this cash into buying back shares rather than reducing leverage, you're witnessing a firm that's optimizing for earnings-per-share metrics *instead of reducing risk*.
