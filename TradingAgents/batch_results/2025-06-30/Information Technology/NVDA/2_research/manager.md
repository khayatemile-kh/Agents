**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points, but weighing them carefully the bull case rests on stronger, more verifiable ground while the bear case, though directionally correct on risk, overstates its case and relies on speculative extrapolation.

Bull strengths: The financials are not in dispute—$303B TTM revenue, 63.7% net margin, $192.9B net income, $60.9B FCF, minimal leverage, and a fabless model with low capex intensity are real, reported numbers, not projections. The forward P/E of ~14x versus consensus growth expectations does imply the market itself is pricing substantial deceleration already; that's a meaningfully different starting point than a typical late-cycle bubble stock. CUDA's switching-cost moat and NVIDIA's dominant share of training workloads are well-documented structural advantages that don't unwind quickly even if custom silicon nibbles at inference share.

Bear strengths: The margin-sustainability argument is the most credible part of the bear case—allocation-economy pricing power tied to TSMC capacity constraints is a real risk, and history (Intel, Apple, TSMC) does show margins compress when supply catches up to demand. The point about law-of-large-numbers deceleration is directionally correct and worth respecting for position-sizing purposes. However, the bear's TAM math ($90-120B "realistic" ceiling) is asserted without real support and is already contradicted by NVIDIA's current $303B run-rate—this weakens the rest of that argument. The inventory buildup point is a reasonable flag but is not yet confirmed as channel-stuffing versus legitimate scaling to meet backlog. The claim that forward EPS assumes "zero multiple compression" misreads the PEG argument; PEG is about earnings growth versus multiple, not a claim of no compression at all.

Net assessment: The bear correctly flags real risks—margin normalization, competitive share erosion in inference, and deceleration—but these are medium-term risks to monitor, not evidence that the current price is irrational today. The overbought RSI is a legitimate near-term technical caution, but panic-selling a structurally dominant, wildly profitable business on RSI alone has a poor track record (as the bull noted with MSFT/AAPL analogs). Given the extraordinary current profitability, fortress balance sheet, and the forward multiple already discounting substantial deceleration, I'm siding with the bull's fundamental thesis while acknowledging the bear's margin/competition risks warrant disciplined position sizing rather than aggressive full-conviction buying at the top of a technical extension.

This nets out to a constructive but not maximum-conviction stance: Overweight rather than outright Buy, since the RSI extension and legitimate margin-normalization risk argue against loading up on new capital at this exact price versus phasing in.
