## **1. THE MARGIN MIRAGE: 63.7% NET MARGINS ARE A STATISTICAL ANOMALY, NOT A BUSINESS MODEL**

Let's start with what the bull is leading with: those staggering margins.

Here's the fundamental problem: **Those margins are not normal. They are not sustainable. And the bull is hoping you won't ask why.**

### **The Real Story Behind the Numbers**

NVIDIA's 63.7% net profit margin in TTM is built on a house of cards:

**a) Unprecedented Supply Constraints Creating Artificial Pricing Power**

The bull claims NVIDIA has "pricing power" because supply is constrained. Correct. But here's what that means: **The company is operating at artificially inflated margins because it's selling GPUs in an allocation economy.**

When supply is scarce and demand is unlimited, you're not selling to the buyer willing to pay the most—you're selling to whoever gets the allocation. Prices don't reflect fair market value; they reflect monopoly rent.

**History lesson:** When supply eventually normalizes (and it *will*), margins collapse. This happened to:
- **Intel** during the data center monopoly (80%+ gross margins compressed to 50%+ when competition arrived)
- **Apple** when iPhone supply normalized (margins compressed from 45% → 35%)
- **TSMC** when foundry competition intensified (gross margins from 60%+ → 50%)

NVIDIA's margins are *not* a reflection of a superior business model—**they're a reflection of a temporary supply bottleneck.** The moment TSMC brings new 3nm capacity online (which is *literally scheduled for 2025-2026*), or when competitive alternatives become available, those margins evaporate.

**b) The Revenue Base is Built on a Bubble**

Look at the revenue explosion: $27B (FY2023) → $60.9B (FY2024) → $130.5B (FY2025) → $303B TTM.

This isn't just growth. **This is unsustainable hypergrowth.** 

Here's the critical question the bull won't answer: **What's the TAM (Total Addressable Market) for AI GPU chips?**

Let's do the math:
- Global semiconductor market: ~$600B annually
- AI accelerators are a subset of that (~15-20% is being generous)
- **Realistic TAM for AI GPUs: $90-120B at saturation**

NVIDIA is already at $303B TTM. That means the company is either:
1. Cannibalizing non-AI semiconductor segments (which it is)
2. Selling to the same customers multiple times in inventory buildup (which is happening)
3. Running at an unsustainable revenue rate that will compress dramatically when capex cycles normalize

**The bull conveniently ignores that we're in Year 2 of a 5-7 year capex cycle, not the perpetual growth phase.**

### **The Inventory Trap**

Look at the balance sheet data: **Inventory is $10.1B (up 91% YoY).**

That's not normal. That's not healthy. That's a sign that:
- Hyperscalers are hoarding GPUs against supply uncertainty
- NVIDIA is pushing product through distribution channels to meet revenue targets
- The company is building inventory *in customers' hands*, not selling it

When that reverses—and it will—NVIDIA's revenue will crater for 2-3 quarters as customers work through excess inventory.

## **2. THE FORWARD P/E SLEIGHT OF HAND: 14x Forward P/E IS ONLY "CHEAP" If You Believe in 44% EPS Growth**

The bull's centerpiece argument: "Forward P/E of 14x with a PEG of 0.59 is undervalued."

Let me dismantle this systematically.

### **The Bull is Betting on a Forecast that's Already Priced In—And Likely Too Optimistic**

The forward EPS of $15.57 assumes:
- Continued 40-50% earnings growth from FY2025 to FY2026
- **Zero multiple compression as the stock matures**
- Margins that remain at 63%+ levels
- No competition, no capex cycle normalization, no macro slowdown

Here's what's actually likely:

**a) Revenue Growth Will Decelerate Sharply**

The law of large numbers exists for a reason. When you're doing $303B in annual revenue, growing 40-50% means you need to add $120-150B in incremental revenue *next year alone.*

Where does that incremental revenue come from?
- Data center: Already penetrated with Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta. These four companies represent probably 60%+ of current demand. They're not going to *quadruple* spending year-over-year indefinitely.
- Gaming: Flat to declining (already saturated)
- Professional Visualization: Minuscule growth
- Automotive: Promising but years away from material revenue

**The reality: NVIDIA's revenue growth will decelerate from 114% to 35-40% in FY2026, then to 15-20% by FY2027.** This is a natural progression for a company at scale, not a pessimistic assumption.

**b) Margins Will Compress as Competition Materializes**

When the bull says "custom silicon represents only 10-15% of demand," he's right. But he's also *underselling* the threat.

Here's what's actually happening in real time:

- **Google** is deploying Trillium TPUs for inference (the profit pool of AI chips)
- **AWS** is deploying Trainium and Inferentia across Amazon's workloads
- **Meta** is deploying custom MTIA chips
- **Microsoft** is working with OpenAI on custom chips
- **AMD** is shipping MI300X at more competitive pricing

These aren't nibbling at the edges—they're targeting the highest-margin segments. **Inference is where the profit is**, and that's exactly where custom silicon is making its move.

When these custom chips mature and scale over 2-3 years, NVIDIA's TAM doesn't expand—**it compresses.** The hyperscalers will take 20-30% of the market that NVIDIA currently owns, reducing pricing power and forcing margin compression.

**Forward EPS of $15.57 assumes this doesn't happen. I'm betting it does.**

**c) The Bull's PEG Calculation Assumes Perpetual High Growth**

A PEG of 0.59 only makes sense if you believe NVIDIA will grow earnings at 40%+ for a *decade.*

Let's be realistic about the growth trajectory:
- FY2025-2026: 40-50% growth (aggressive but possible)
- FY2026-2027: 25-30% growth (law of large numbers)
- FY2027-2028: 15-20% growth (market saturation)
- FY2028+: 8-12% growth (mature growth rate)

**If you model out a realistic growth profile, NVIDIA's fair value PEG should be 1.5-2.0, not 0.59.**

That means at current prices, NVIDIA is trading at a **significant premium to its true normalized growth rate.**
