## The "Supply-Demand Imbalance" Argument Ignores Inventory Reality

The Aggressive Analyst claims AI infrastructure demand is so constrained that "price doesn't come back down because RSI says so—price stays elevated until supply catches up with demand." This is a dangerous oversimplification that ignores what's actually happening in NVIDIA's inventory levels.

Let me read directly from the fundamental data:

**Inventory increased 91% year-over-year to $10.1 billion.** This isn't a sign of crushing supply constraints. This is NVIDIA and its partners building inventory ahead of anticipated demand. That's prudent supply chain management, but it also suggests the company *expects* it will need to satisfy demand without supply-side constraints.

More critically, if demand were truly so constrained that chips were flying off shelves and customers couldn't get allocation, we wouldn't see inventory build. We'd see inventory depletion. The fact that inventory grew 91% while revenue grew 114% tells me that NVIDIA is positioning for potentially softer demand ahead, or at minimum, ensuring supply isn't a constraint that prevents customers from buying.

This inventory dynamic is a *red flag* for margin compression. If demand softens and NVIDIA has 91% higher inventory to turn over, they'll face pricing pressure, promotional activity, or write-downs. That's how inventory overhang works in tech cycles.

Suddenly the rewriting of software stacks doesn't look like such a brutal tradeoff. It looks like capital-efficient R&D investment.

And here's the kicker: **the Aggressive Analyst assumes hyperscalers are trying to replace NVIDIA entirely. They're not. They're trying to diversify.** Once AWS, Google, and Meta have viable alternatives for 30-40% of their workloads, NVIDIA's pricing power deteriorates significantly. Moving averages are *lagging indicators*—they follow price lower, they don't act as magical support. So when price breaks below 150.81, we're not buying "support"—we're buying *momentum failure*.
