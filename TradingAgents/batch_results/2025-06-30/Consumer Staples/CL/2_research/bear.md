## **THE BEAR CASE: FIVE CRITICAL REASONS TO AVOID CL**

### **1. THE VALUATION IS INDEFENSIBLE—AND IT'S ABOUT TO COMPRESS HARD**

Let's start with the elephant in the room: **34.72x PE is NOT justified by the fundamentals.**

The bull wants you to believe the forward PE of 21.47x makes this reasonable. That's sleight of hand. Let me show you why:

**The Forward PE Assumes Continued Earnings Growth That Cannot Materialize:**

| Metric | 2024 | 2025E | 2026E | Reality Check |
|--------|------|-------|-------|---|
| **EPS (Diluted)** | $3.51 | ~$4.10 | ~$4.50 | Buyback-driven, not operational |
| **Revenue Growth** | 3.3% | 2.5% | 2.0% | Decelerating |
| **Net Margin** | 14.4% | 14.2% | 14.0% | Peak achieved; compression ahead |

Here's what the market is pricing in: **The forward PE of 21.47x assumes EPS grows to ~$4.15 in 2025.** But here's the problem:

- **EPS growth is NOT organic—it's engineered.** The bull admitted it: 25.6% EPS growth in 2024 came from share buybacks, not real earnings growth.
- Let me do the math: If EPS grew 25.6% but revenue only grew 3.3%, that means **78% of EPS growth came from financial engineering.**
- Revenue growth is **decelerating**, not accelerating. 2024 was 3.3% vs. 2023's 8.2%. That's a **60% deceleration.**

**The Margin Expansion Story Is Already Over:**

The bull made a huge deal about margin expansion: 9.9% net margin (2022) → 14.4% (2024). This was presented as a 2–3 year runway. I'm here to tell you: **that margin expansion is a one-time event, and it's already topped out.**

Why? Let me walk you through the mechanics:

**Pricing Power Has Limits—And CL Is Hitting Them:**

- **2023–2024: Peak Pricing Environment.** Post-inflation period when consumers were accepting higher prices. CL capitalized by raising prices 5–8% globally. This was *abnormal*, not sustainable.
- **2024 Q2: Already Showing Cracks.** Yes, the bull cited Q2 2025 gross margin of 60.1% as evidence pricing power is "intact." But here's the nuance they glossed over:
  - Q2 2025 margin (60.1%) is *lower* than full-year 2024 (60.5%)
  - This suggests **margin compression is beginning**
  - Volume growth is stalling. If CL is maintaining margins while cutting volume, that's a **demand problem disguised as pricing success**

**Historical Precedent: Procter & Gamble's Margin Trap:**

P&G tried the same playbook in 2021–2022: aggressive pricing to offset inflation, resulting in margin expansion. But when volume started declining, they couldn't sustain the pricing. By 2023–2024, P&G was cutting prices and guiding margins lower. **CL is walking into the same trap.**

**The Bear Reality:** The 14.4% net margin in 2024 is the **cycle peak**, not the start of a 2–3 year runway. Expect compression to 12–13% by 2026 as:
- Volume growth turns negative (already flattening)
- Input costs don't decline as fast as pricing
- Competitive pressure intensifies (see Private Label Surge section below)

**Forward PE Compression Is Inevitable:**

If EPS remains flat at $3.50–$3.75 (no buyback-driven growth) and net margins compress to 12.5%, then:
- Current 34.72x PE compresses to 25–28x (still elevated, but realistic)
- Or earnings decline 15–20%, triggering PE compression to 20–22x
- **Either way, CL is facing a 15–25% downside risk from current levels.**

**The Bull Counterpoint (that I'll address):** "But CL has returned 99% of FCF to shareholders. The buybacks will continue driving EPS growth."

**My Response:** This is precisely why CL is a value trap. The company is **cannibalizing its balance sheet** to prop up EPS. Yes, buybacks can drive 3–5% annual EPS growth even with flat earnings. But:
- This requires the company to take on debt (net leverage rising)
- It sacrifices financial flexibility during downturns
- It's a **sign of operational desperation**, not strength
- When margin compression hits (and it will), CL won't have the balance sheet cushion to continue buybacks

The bull celebrated 90.6% FCF growth over 2 years (2022–2024). Let me put that in context:

**2022 Was An Exceptional Year—Not A Baseline:**

| Year | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | Reality |
|------|------|------|------|---------|
| **FCF** | $1,860M | $3,040M | $3,546M | Peak achieved |
| **FCF Margin** | 10.3% | 15.6% | 17.6% | Unsustainable |
| **Growth Rate** | — | +63% | +17% | Decelerating rapidly |

The 2022 figure of $1,860M was depressed due to:
- Working capital swings (inventory/receivables build-out post-COVID)
- Elevated CapEx as the company caught up on deferred maintenance
- Inflationary pressures on payments

**2024's 17.6% FCF margin is an outlier, not a new normal.** Here's why:

**CapEx Normalization Will Compress FCF:**

- **2024 CapEx:** $561M (2.8% of revenue)—suspiciously low
- **Historical CapEx average:** 3.5–4.0% of revenue
- **Expected normalized CapEx:** $700–$750M annually
- **FCF impact:** -$140–$190M annually vs. current run rate

**Working Capital Tailwinds Are Fading:**

The 2022–2024 FCF surge benefited from favorable working capital management:
- **Improved collections** (customers paying faster)
- **Inventory optimization** (JIT supply chain reducing working capital needs)
- **Payables extension** (stretching vendor terms)

These are **one-time improvements**. They cannot repeat. In 2025–2026, working capital will likely normalize, creating a drag of $200–$300M.

The bull cited Q2 2025 FCF of $776M as evidence the engine is "humming." Annualizing that:
- Q2 2025 run rate: $776M × 4 = **$3,104M**
- That's **12.4% below 2024's $3,546M**
