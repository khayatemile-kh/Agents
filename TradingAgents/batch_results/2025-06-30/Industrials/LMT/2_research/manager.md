The bear's strongest points that I can't dismiss: (1) the Q2 2025 cash-flow deterioration is real and multi-dimensional—negative FCF, a 78% sequential drop in operating cash flow, and a working-capital swing where receivables rose, inventory rose, and payables fell simultaneously. (2) The company issued $1.3B of new debt in the same quarter, which is a tangible red flag about self-funding capacity for dividends/buybacks. (3) The technical picture is unambiguously deteriorating on the objective indicators cited by both sides: price below both 50- and 200-day SMAs, deeply negative and widening MACD histogram, no RSI reversal signal yet. The 'capitulation exhaustion' read on declining volume is a plausible but unproven interpretation—bear's alternative (sellers already exited, thin support, vulnerable to further downside) is equally consistent with the same data and arguably more conservative.

Where the bull is stronger: the Net Debt/EBITDA of 2.09x is a legitimate, commonly used leverage metric for capital-intensive industrials, and it does look reasonable in isolation; the extreme D/E ratio is indeed an artifact of goodwill/intangibles compressing the equity base, which is a fair technical correction to make. The forward P/E argument (13.7x on forward EPS growth) is also a reasonable valuation point if the growth actually materializes. But the bull's rebuttal on cash flow essentially asks investors to trust that Q2 is a one-off trough without providing forward evidence (no cited backlog growth in new awards, no guidance reaffirmation on cash flow specifically), while the bear's critique that TTM FCF is backward-looking and could be skewed by a stronger prior-year base is a valid caution that the bull doesn't fully rebut.

Net assessment: the debate is not balanced 50/50. The near-term fundamental (cash flow deterioration, new debt issuance funding shareholder returns) and technical (price below both major moving averages, negative and widening MACD, no reversal confirmation) evidence currently favors the bear case. The long-term structural defense demand story is real and caps how bearish I want to be — this is not a broken business, and the leverage metric that matters most for credit health (Net Debt/EBITDA ~2.1x) is still investment-grade. But given the concrete, data-supported near-term deterioration in cash generation and the confirmed technical breakdown with no reversal signal, this is not the moment to add or even hold at current sizing — it warrants trimming exposure while monitoring for either (a) confirmation that Q2 cash flow was truly transitory via Q3 results, or (b) further technical deterioration that would argue for a larger cut.
