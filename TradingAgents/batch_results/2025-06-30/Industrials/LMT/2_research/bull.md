## PART 2: CASH FLOW "PROBLEM"—BEAR BAIT, NOT A REAL ISSUE

Now, the bear will absolutely hammer the **negative free cash flow in Q2 2025 (-$150M)** and the **elevated debt levels (234.24x debt-to-equity).** Let me dismantle this proactively.

### Why Q2 FCF Was Negative (And Why It Doesn't Matter)

**Working Capital Timing:**

The cash flow statement shows a **-$2.28 billion working capital swing** in Q2 2025. Here's what happened:

- Receivables up $1.74B (customers purchasing more, paying on terms)
- Inventory up $336M (production ramp for increased orders)
- Payables down $268M (paying suppliers faster, likely due to accelerated spending)

This is **classic business growth cash flow dynamics**, not operational failure. When a company scales production to meet demand, it:

1. Takes on more receivables (customers don't pay same-day)
2. Increases inventory (needs materials for higher production)
3. Pays payables faster (suppliers want cash from growing contractors)

**But here's the key:** This working capital *investment* is *temporary*. Once the revenue cycle normalizes and customers (primarily the U.S. government) pay their invoices, this working capital reverses into *massive* cash inflows.

### TTM FCF Still Healthy at $5.6B

Despite the Q2 weakness, **LMT generated $5.6 billion in free cash flow over the trailing twelve months.** That's real money. That's what matters for:

- Debt service (interest expense of $1.1B annually is easily covered: 5.1x FCF interest coverage)
- Dividend payments ($3.1B annually—*yes*, it exceeds Q2 FCF, but it's well-covered by TTM FCF)
- Debt reduction
- Strategic acquisitions

The Q2 quarter is a *trough*, not a trend. By Q4 2025, as government payments cycle through and working capital stabilizes, we'll see FCF normalize back to $1.3-1.5B per quarter.
