**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both analysts agree on the core facts: EFX is a high-quality, high-margin data/analytics business with genuine cash generation and a leveraged balance sheet, but they disagree sharply on interpretation. The bull's strongest point is that FCF has grown materially (2022→2024: $133M→$516M→$813M), gross margins (56.8%) and operating margins (~20%) are healthy, and net debt/EBITDA has already improved from 2.81x to 2.44x, evidence of real (if gradual) deleveraging. The bear's strongest rebuttal is that a large chunk of the 2024 FCF improvement came from capex normalization rather than core operating acceleration (OCF only grew 18.6% vs. the 58% FCF headline), that the debt paydown math is slow (~10 years at run-rate free cash after dividends/buybacks), and that the macro setup (sub-trend GDP growth, elevated rates, weak refi volumes) is more likely to pressure transaction volumes than to be a pure analytics-demand tailwind. The bear also correctly notes net margin is only 10.7%, meaningfully below process implied by gross margin, largely due to ~$400M annual interest expense, and that regulatory/compliance risk (FCRA, prior breach settlements, CFPB scrutiny) is a live risk not currently priced in.

Weighing this: the bear's critique of the FCF growth being partly a capex-timing artifact and the slow pace of real deleveraging is quantitatively sound and hard to dismiss — it directly undercuts the bull's "hidden earnings re-rating" thesis, which depends on rapid deleveraging that the math doesn't support. At the same time, the bull is right that this remains a structurally advantaged, high-margin, cash-generative business with real (if slow) deleveraging progress, not a broken company. The valuation (29x trailing P/E, ~30x EV/FCF) already prices in a fair amount of quality and growth, leaving limited margin of safety if the macro headwinds the bear describes (weaker originations, refi slowdown, credit stress on data quality/pricing) materialize. Technical weakness (down 7.4% from highs, negative MACD) reinforces that the market is currently discounting some of these bear-case risks.

Net: this isn't a broken business, so an outright Sell is too aggressive — the bear's case is about valuation/pace-of-improvement risk, not fundamental impairment. But the bull's re-rating narrative rests on optimistic extrapolation of a single strong FCF year and probably overstates near-term deleveraging speed, while ignoring genuine volume-driven revenue risk in a below-trend growth macro backdrop. That argues for trimming/not adding here rather than being constructive or aggressively bullish. I'm landing on a cautious, Underweight stance: respect the quality of the franchise but recognize that at current valuation and leverage levels, the risk/reward skews unfavorable until we see either clearer deleveraging progress (net debt/EBITDA sustainably below 2x) or valuation compression that better reflects the slower, more volume-sensitive growth path the bear describes.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing EFX exposure by roughly 20-30% into current strength/any bounce, rather than adding on the pullback the bull is calling a "gift." 2) Do not initiate new full-size positions at ~29x trailing P/E / ~30x EV/FCF given the bear's credible case that FCF acceleration is partly a capex-timing artifact (OCF grew only ~19% vs. 58% FCF headline) and that real debt paydown capacity (~$460-500M/yr after dividends/buybacks) implies a much slower deleveraging path than the bull assumes. 3) Set a re-entry/upgrade trigger: revisit constructive stance if net debt/EBITDA falls sustainably below ~2.2x with OCF growth (not just capex normalization) confirming durable FCF improvement, or if valuation compresses meaningfully (e.g., EV/FCF toward mid-20s) to build in margin of safety against macro/regulatory risk. 4) Hedge/monitor macro sensitivity: track mortgage refinancing volumes, credit origination trends, and GDP growth data as leading indicators — deteriorating volumes would be a signal to cut further rather than average down. 5) Keep a residual core position (not a full exit) to retain exposure to the durable data-moat/pricing-power quality of the franchise, sized small enough that further multiple compression or leverage-driven stress does not meaningfully impair the portfolio. 6) Avoid leverage-adjacent instruments (e.g., avoid adding via options that assume near-term mean reversion) until technical trend (MACD/RSI) stabilizes and confirms bottoming rather than continued breakdown.
