## **1. CASH GENERATION IS THE REAL STORY — AND IT'S ACCELERATING**

Here's what jumps off the page when you look at the fundamentals: **Equifax generated $813 million in free cash flow in 2024, up 57.6% year-over-year from $516 million in 2023.** That's not a typo. In just one year, FCF growth nearly doubled.

**Why does this matter?** Because in a capital-intensive business like data services, cash flow is the ultimate truth. And the trend is undeniable:

- **2022 FCF:** $133M
- **2023 FCF:** $516M (+287% YoY)
- **2024 FCF:** $813M (+58% YoY)

In Q2 2025 alone, the company generated $239M in free cash flow on a quarterly basis—annualizing to roughly **$950M+**. That's a run rate that suggests 2025 FCF could exceed $900M, representing yet another year of robust cash generation.

**The bear will say:** "But debt is $4.9 billion! You're drowning in leverage!"

**I say:** At $813M in annual FCF, EFX can pay down $2+ billion in debt over the next 3-4 years while maintaining dividends and modest share buybacks. The company isn't drowning—it's paying down the mortgage while refinancing at favorable terms. The net debt/EBITDA ratio already **improved from 2.81x in 2024 to 2.44x** (as of June 30, 2025). That's **real deleveraging in action**, not wishful thinking.

## **2. REVENUE GROWTH IS STEADY AND SUSTAINABLE — THE MACRO BACKDROP SUPPORTS EQUIFAX**

Let me address the elephant in the room: the macroeconomic environment. 

The market is pricing in **68% probability of US GDP growth of 2.0%–2.5% in 2026**—modest, but stable. Here's the critical insight that bears miss: **this is exactly the environment where Equifax thrives.**

**Here's why:**

- **Higher rates persist** → Consumer credit stress remains elevated → Lenders tighten credit standards → **Demand for advanced credit analytics, fraud detection, and risk decisioning tools surges**
- **Below-trend growth** → Lenders become more cautious → **They lean harder on Equifax's AI-powered scoring models and identity verification solutions to reduce credit losses**
- **Economic uncertainty** → Compliance and regulatory scrutiny of lending increases → **Equifax's compliance and regulatory solutions become mission-critical**

Equifax's revenue grew from $5.1B (2022) to $5.3B (2023) to $5.7B (2024). That's **consistent 7-8% organic growth** in a mature, defensive industry. And here's the kicker: **this growth is happening during a period of tightening credit conditions**, which historically boosts demand for Equifax's services.

**Yes, debt is high. But:**

1. **The company has investment-grade debt** (not junk status) with access to capital markets
2. **Debt is on favorable terms** relative to the company's credit profile and cash generation
3. **The company is actively paying down debt** — net debt declined by $370M in 2024 alone

This is a **feature, not a bug**—it means the business generates cash *faster* than traditional working capital metrics suggest. The company's operating cash flow of $1.3B+ proves the business converts revenue into cash efficiently.

The bear will say, "But tangible book value is negative!"

**Exactly right—because Equifax's value is in its intangible assets:** its credit data, algorithms, brand, customer relationships, and network effects. The $8.2B in goodwill and intangibles is essentially the market's recognition that **Equifax's data moats and analytical capabilities are worth billions**. You can't replace 100+ years of credit history data overnight. That's defensibility.
