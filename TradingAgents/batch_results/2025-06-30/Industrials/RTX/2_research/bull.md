## **PART 2: DECONSTRUCTING THE CASH FLOW "CRISIS"**

This is where I suspect the bear argument will focus, so let me get ahead of it.

### **The Q2 Cash Flow "Problem" Is a Red Herring**

**The headline:** RTX posted -$194M in free cash flow in Q2.

**The context you're missing:**

**1. Working Capital Timing Cycles (The Real Story)**

Look at the cash flow statement again:
- **Operating cash flow: $458M** (actually decent for a quarterly run-rate)
- **CapEx: -$652M** (necessary investment in manufacturing capability)
- **But working capital adjustment: -$2.176B outflow**

Here's what that working capital swing actually represents:
- **Receivables spike (-$1.249B):** RTX isn't collecting receivables slowly—this is normal for government contracts, which often lag delivery by 30-60 days. The company is shipping products ahead of cash receipt.
- **Payables decline (-$538M):** This suggests RTX paid down supplier balances—a sign of healthy supplier relationships, not distress.

**This is NOT a sign of operational distress. It's a sign that RTX is ramping production to fulfill $19.2B in deferred revenue.**

The working capital cycle will normalize. This is temporary.

---

**2. CapEx Investment Thesis: Capacity Building for Revenue Growth**

RTX is investing $652M in Q2 quarterly CapEx ($2.6B annualized) because:
- **Defense contract demand is accelerating** (those deferred revenues are coming due)
- **Supply chain normalization requires manufacturing capacity investment**
- **Modern missile/aircraft systems require cutting-edge manufacturing**

This is **not wasteful spending—it's capacity building for a revenue surge**. As RTX executes these contracts, working capital will normalize and free cash flow will inflect positively.

**3. Dividend Is Sustainable, Not a Problem**

Yes, RTX paid $910M in dividends on -$194M FCF in Q2. Annualized, that's ~$3.6B in dividends on ~-$800M FCF (if Q2 is representative).

**But here's the nuance:**
- Q2 is historically a *weak cash flow quarter* for RTX (seasonal timing of contract payments)
- **The company has $4.78B in cash** and access to bond markets (investment-grade rated)
- Defense contractors are *allowed* to carry higher leverage than other industries because of contract visibility
- **RTX's $43.6B debt is backed by $19.2B in visible deferred revenue** and another $70-90B in multi-year contract backlog

The dividend is sustainable because:
1. TTM free cash flow (when normalized across the full year) is positive
2. Q2 is a cash flow trough, not indicative of full-year performance
3. Management has proven ability to manage working capital cycles
