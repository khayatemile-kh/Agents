## **2. OPERATIONAL HEADWINDS: The Bull Case Ignores Critical Risks**

### **The Reshoring Tailwind Is Overstated**

The bull relies heavily on government mandates (CHIPS Act, IRA) to justify growth. But here's what's actually happening:

**First, the timing problem:**
- CHIPS Act funding is being *disbursed* over 5-10 years, not deployed in 2025-2026
- Companies like TSMC, Intel, and Samsung are building facilities, not Rockwell's customers necessarily ramping capex proportionally
- Facility construction timelines mean capex commitments are frontloaded, but ROK's benefits are lagged by 12-24 months as facilities scale production

**Second, the competition problem:**
- When a new fab or manufacturing facility opens, *everyone* bids on the control systems and automation work
- ROK doesn't have a captive customer base—it's competing against Siemens, ABB, GE, and Chinese competitors like Schneider Electric
- **Reshoring doesn't guarantee ROK wins the work—it just expands the addressable market for all competitors equally**

**Third, the customer concentration problem:**
- Major reshoring beneficiaries (Intel, TSMC, Samsung, Battery makers) are sophisticated buyers
- They negotiate hard on price and terms
- Some are building internal automation capabilities to reduce dependency on ROK

The bull's "$1.2-1.8B incremental revenue opportunity" assumes ROK captures market share in a newly expanded TAM. **But the TAM expansion doesn't solve the competitive dynamics or customer bargaining power.**

### **Cyclical Industries Don't Have Infinite Tailwinds**

The bull frames ROK as a "compounder in a cyclical industry." That's contradictory. **You can't be a compounder if you're cyclical.**

Here's the brutal reality:
- Manufacturing capex cycles are typically 5-8 years
- The current cycle (2020-2025) has been strong, but we're 5 years into it
- Recession risk, while low in prediction markets *today*, is not zero—and when manufacturing capex collapses, it collapses *fast*

**The risk I see:**
- If U.S. recession hits in 2026-2027 (a reasonable 20-30% probability), manufacturing capex spending will contract sharply
- At that point, ROK's valuation will reset dramatically lower
- The stock could easily fall 40-50% in a cyclical downturn, particularly at 39x P/E

**The bull is assuming we're early in a 10-year structural cycle. I'm seeing signs we're late in a 5-year cyclical upcycle.**
