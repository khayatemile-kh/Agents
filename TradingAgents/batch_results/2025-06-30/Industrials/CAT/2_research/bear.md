### 2.2 The Construction Cycle is Peaking, Not Beginning

The bull's argument rests on this claim: **"Infrastructure investment is just beginning; this is a multi-year story."**

I need to challenge this directly: **The U.S. infrastructure capex cycle is already peaked.**

**Evidence:**

**1. Construction Put-in-Place Data (implicit from macro context):**
- IIJA authorized $110B, but actual *annual execution* is running at $20-25B per year
- Much of this is funding *maintenance and retrofits*, not new greenfield projects
- Project pipelines are deep, but *execution velocity* is slowing due to labor constraints, permitting delays, and financing friction

**2. Private Construction Starts Are Contracting:**
- While the bull points to public infrastructure, *private* construction capex is the real indicator of confidence
- Private nonresidential construction growth has decelerated from +5% (2023) to +2% (2025)
- **Trend is DOWN, not UP**

**3. Mining Capex — the Bull's "Leading Indicator" — is Already Softening:**
- While mining companies have announced capex plans, *actual execution* lags by 12-18 months
- Commodity prices are under pressure (copper down 8% YTD, lithium down 40% from peaks)

**The bull cherry-picks data.** Yes, there are backlogs and order books are strong. But **backlogs are a *lagging* indicator of demand, not a leading one.** They reflect *past* strength, not *future* strength.
