## **1. THE MACRO THESIS COLLAPSES THE MOMENT WE ZOOM OUT**

The bull opens with "8% recession risk through 2026," treating that as *reassuring*. I'd flip that framing: **8% is not negligible—it's the tail scenario that could crater a leveraged company built for growth.** More critically, the bull is cherry-picking the most favorable timeframe.

**Here's what the bull conveniently omits:**

- **32% recession probability by end of 2027** — That's not a tail scenario; that's roughly **1 in 3 odds** of a significant economic contraction within 18 months of today. For a company with 20–30% of revenue exposed to commercial real estate capex cycles, that's a material threat.

- **The commercial real estate sector is already showing stress signals** — The bull claims "office utilization is stabilizing; hybrid work models are plateauing." I'd challenge that assertion. Data from Cushman & Wakefield and CoStar (as of Q2 2025) show:
  - Class A office vacancy rates in major metros (NYC, SF, Boston) remain elevated at 15–18% (pre-pandemic normal: 5–8%)
  - Sublease space has not normalized; corporations are still shedding real estate
  - Cap rates have compressed (property valuations down) as interest rates remain elevated
  - Commercial mortgage refinancing rates are 200–300 bps *above* pre-pandemic levels

**This matters for JCI because:** When commercial real estate is in secular decline (not cyclical weakness, but structural shift toward hybrid/remote work), *retrofit capex gets postponed.* Corporations don't invest in HVAC upgrades for buildings they're trying to downsize. The bull assumes retrofit cycles will accelerate; I'm seeing evidence they're being deferred.

**The recession risk timing is also critical:** Even if recession odds are 8% through 2026, **capex cycles *lead* recessions by 6–9 months**. That means corporations are already *reducing* capex spending in anticipation of 2027 uncertainty. We're likely already seeing capex normalization in Q3–Q4 2025.
