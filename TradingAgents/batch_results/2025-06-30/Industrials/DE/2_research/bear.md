## PART 2: THE FUNDAMENTAL DETERIORATION IS UNMISTAKABLE — Don't Confuse "Normalization" With "Healthy Trough"

### Revenue Decline Is NOT Just Inventory Normalization—It's Demand Destruction

The bull claims: *"This is just inventory normalization. FY2023 was the anomaly; FY2024 is normalization back to FY2022 levels."*

**This argument is dangerously misleading.** Let me show you why:

**The Real Revenue Story:**
- FY2022: $51.3B (post-COVID, supply-constrained environment)
- FY2023: $60.2B (+17.4%) — Peak post-pandemic demand release
- FY2024: $50.5B (-16.1%) — **Falls *below* FY2022 normalized levels**

The bull is technically correct that FY2024 revenue is close to FY2022. **But that's the problem.** Here's the trajectory:

```
FY2022: $51.3B
        ↓ (flat/slight growth expected)
FY2024: $50.5B (BELOW FY2022)
```

**If normalization meant returning to FY2022 levels with growth, we'd expect FY2024 to be $52-54B, not $50.5B.** Instead, we got **contraction below the baseline.**

### What Does This Reveal About Current Demand?

**The data shows demand is weak even relative to normalized post-pandemic levels:**

1. **Agricultural Demand Weakness:**
   - Farmer margins compressed in 2024 due to commodity prices
   - Equipment replacement cycles have **extended**, not accelerated
   - Dealer inventory levels are **elevated**, not normalized (I'll address this below)
   - Farmer debt-to-asset ratios at ~11% are actually **rising**, not falling (constraining purchasing power)

2. **Construction Demand Softness:**
   - Non-residential construction spending grew only 2.3% in early 2024 (vs. historical 4-5%)
   - Equipment utilization rates are **declining**, not rising
   - This suggests contractors are stretching existing equipment life, not upgrading

**The bull's "pent-up replacement demand" narrative ignores the actual behavior:** Farmers and contractors are **delaying purchases**, not preparing to accelerate them.
