**Rationale**: Both sides used the same underlying data but reached very different conclusions, and when I weigh the arguments the bear's case holds up better under scrutiny. The bull's entire upside thesis hinges on forward EPS of $2.90 representing a 52% rebound off a TTM EPS of $1.91 that itself is already down 32% from 2024's $2.83. That's a huge, unexplained snapback assumption, and the bull never actually names a concrete catalyst beyond generic 'E&P spending recovering' and 'rig counts picking up' — no hard data was presented to substantiate either. Meanwhile the bear correctly flags that revenue has now been flat-to-down two years running (2024 -0.3%, TTM -0.3%) despite what's supposed to be a recovering commodity backdrop, and — more importantly — that all four margin lines (gross, operating, EBITDA, net) compressed in 2024 versus 2023. That combination — flat/declining revenue AND compressing margins — is a much more damaging signal than the bull's framing of 'stabilization' and 'operational excellence.' The bull tried to use FCF and OCF growth as evidence of health, but the bear's rebuttal that this improvement is largely working-capital driven (receivables/payables/inventory timing) while underlying margins shrink is a legitimate and unaddressed critique — cash flow strength built on working-capital drawdown is not sustainable the way organic margin expansion would be.

On valuation, the bull's PEG of 0.77 and forward P/E of 12.43x only look cheap if you trust the $2.90 forward EPS number, which is exactly the assumption under attack. If you instead anchor to trailing $1.91 EPS, the multiple is closer to 10.4x on price, which isn't obviously as cheap once you account for a business with decelerating revenue and shrinking margins in a structurally challenged, cyclical oilfield services industry facing competitive pressure from better-positioned peers like SLB.

Where I do give some credit to the bull: the balance sheet metrics (current ratio 2.02x, debt-to-equity improving ~750bps in six months, manageable net debt to market cap of ~18%) are genuinely solid, and the dividend appears well-covered. Those are real positives that argue against an aggressive Sell/exit call — this isn't a distressed balance sheet. ROE of ~15% is respectable, not spectacular, for a capital-intensive cyclical.

Putting it together: the bear's rebuttal on margin compression and revenue deceleration directly undercuts the bull's central 'recovery inflection' narrative, and the bull's upside math is entirely dependent on an unproven forward-earnings snapback. That's enough to tip this to a cautious stance rather than balanced Hold — the risk/reward skews toward disappointment if the $2.90 forward EPS consensus doesn't materialize, while the balance sheet strength prevents this from being a full Sell. I'm landing on Underweight: trim exposure, don't chase the 'cheap on forward multiples' story, and wait for actual evidence of margin reacceleration or revenue growth before adding back.
