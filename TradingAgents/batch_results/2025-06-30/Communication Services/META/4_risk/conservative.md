I call it a capital intensity risk that has not yet been stress-tested in a genuine downturn.

Here's what concerns me most: capex as a percentage of revenue has *increased* to roughly 29% of TTM revenue if we annualize Q2 2025 spending ($66B/$228B). The aggressive analyst's response is: "The market is already proving this thesis correct—margin expansion from 24.9% (2022) to 42.2% (2024) occurred while increasing capex." This is cherry-picking the most favorable scenario. What I see instead is a company that benefited from the 2023-2024 cost-cutting cycle and ad market recovery, *and simultaneously* increased capex in the same period. These are not necessarily causal—they may be independent trends that will eventually collide.

## The Valuation Red Flag They Dismissed Too Quickly

The aggressive analyst claimed META is "reasonably valued" at 6.34x book value. Let me interrogate that claim more carefully. A 6.34x price-to-book multiple on a capital-intensive business that is *increasing* capex intensity is not "reasonable"—it's elevated relative to the company's historical range and relative to what free cash flow metrics would support during a high-rate regime.

Here's what the macro data tells us: **93% probability of zero Fed rate cuts through 2026.** The aggressive analyst dismissed this as "already priced in." That's a dangerous assumption. What we actually know is that rates *have been* elevated, but rate expectations can shift rapidly based on inflation prints, labor market data, or geopolitical events. If the Fed does eventually cut rates starting in late 2025 or 2026, equity multiples may expand further. But if rates remain sticky at elevated levels *and* inflation proves more persistent than consensus expects, META's valuation multiple is at risk of compression, not expansion.

Why? Because META's substantial capex requirements ($66B annually) are financed at current rates. If the company has issued debt at 4-5% rates assuming eventual rate cuts, and those cuts don't materialize, the effective cost of that capex rises. Moreover, from a discount rate perspective, if the risk-free rate remains elevated, the discount rate used to value META's future cash flows increases, which mathematically depresses valuation multiples.

The aggressive analyst made a subtle error when they said: "if rates stay elevated for an extended period, that *favors* profitable, cash-generative mega-caps like META." This is only true if you assume the capex buildout delivers proportional returns and doesn't cannibalize margins. In a high-rate environment, the hurdle rate for capex ROI projects upward. Projects that made sense at 2% risk-free rates may not clear the bar at 4-5% rates. META may be forced to moderate capex growth, which would disappoint the market and trigger a re-rating lower.
