**Recommendation**: Overweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: FDS is a high-margin, recurring-revenue business with strong FCF (~$228.6M/quarter, ~39% FCF margin), a leveraged balance sheet (~$1.62B debt, net debt ~$1.07B, debt/EBITDA ~1.75x), a weak current ratio (0.683), and a forward P/E (13.74x) that sits well below trailing P/E (17.84x) on consensus expectations of ~29.7% EPS growth. The disagreement is about how to interpret these same numbers.

The bull's strongest points: the business model is genuinely moaty (high switching costs, recurring subscriptions, embedded in institutional workflows), cash generation is real and substantial, and management is actively using that cash to both delever and return capital — that's a sign of confidence, not distress. The 13.74x forward multiple is objectively not expensive for a company with mid-to-high-single-digit organic growth plus buybacks, and a PEG near 1.3x is reasonable, not screaming overvaluation. Technicals showing a bounce off the 50-day SMA with positive MACD are a real, if soft, supporting signal.

The bear's strongest points: leverage is not trivial (debt/EBITDA ~1.75x, current ratio well below 1.0), and the whole valuation support hinges on hitting a full 29.7% EPS growth number — a figure that looks unusually high relative to FactSet's historical high-single-digit organic growth cadence, which raises real doubt about whether that number is mix/comp driven (e.g., prior-year comparison, buyback-driven EPS accretion) rather than pure fundamental acceleration. The bear's point that P/E compression from 17.84x to 13.74x can reflect either "cheap growth" or "market unease about hitting the number" is a legitimate two-sided read, and the bear is right that the bull hasn't proven which one it is. However, the bear's downside scenarios (20% or 10% growth) are somewhat arbitrarily constructed without evidence they're more probable than the bull's base case, and the "73.9% intangible assets" point is boilerplate for any acquisitive software/data company and isn't itself a red flag absent impairment or goodwill risk evidence.

Weighing this: neither side lands a knockout blow. The bear correctly flags real balance-sheet leverage and liquidity ratios that warrant caution, and correctly notes that the growth assumption embedded in the "cheap forward multiple" argument is unproven and carries real risk if it doesn't materialize. The bull correctly notes the moat, cash conversion, and capital-return discipline are genuine positives that offset headline leverage concerns, and that current ratio for a subscription business with deferred revenue is a less alarming signal than for a typical industrial/retail balance sheet. Given the stock has already pulled back ~11% from highs without any negative fundamental catalyst per the sentiment data (no earnings miss, no competitive loss, no regulatory hit), and cash flow/capital-return trends remain intact, I lean toward the bull side being more evidentially grounded for a modest constructive stance — but the unresolved uncertainty over whether 29.7% forward EPS growth is realistic, plus real leverage/liquidity metrics, keeps me from a full Buy conviction. This nets out to a moderate, not aggressive, constructive stance: Overweight rather than Buy or Hold. This reflects "lean bullish on quality/cash generation and lack of negative catalysts" while respecting the bear's legitimate point that the valuation re-rating depends on an unverified growth assumption and leverage is real, meaning position sizing should be measured rather than maximal.
