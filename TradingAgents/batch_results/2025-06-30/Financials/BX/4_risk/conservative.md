But these aren't passive holdings that drop in value if markets decline. These are *actively managed* positions within their funds. When portfolio companies within their private equity funds experience economic stress, yes, valuations compress. But do you know what happens next? Blackstone typically *deepens* their operational involvement, improves margins, and prepares for exit. That's literally their business model.

More importantly—and this is critical—Blackstone's fee income isn't entirely correlated with AUM changes. During a market downturn, yes, AUM might decline. But management fees are still collected. Co-investment gains might be realized. And critically, their *alternatives-focused* positioning means they benefit from being a contrarian buyer when public markets stumble and private asset prices become attractive.
