## **THE BEAR THESIS: DECONSTRUCTING THE BULL'S FOUR PILLARS**

### **PILLAR 1 REBUTTAL: "HIGHER-FOR-LONGER" IS A TICKING TIME BOMB**

The bull makes a seductive argument: *"93% probability of zero rate cuts in 2026 means NIM expansion continues."* This is where the analysis breaks down spectacularly.

**Here's what the bull missed:**

#### **A. THE NIM COMPRESSION PROBLEM IS ALREADY EMBEDDED**

Let me walk through the data the bull himself provided:

| Period | Interest Income | Interest Expense | NIM (Implied) |
|--------|-----------------|------------------|---------------|
| 2022 | $92.8B | $26.1B | 2.52% |
| 2024 | $193.9B | $101.4B | 2.47% |

**Wait—NII grew 39% but NIM actually *compressed* by 5 basis points (252 bps to 247 bps).** How is this possible if rates have been "higher-for-longer"?

**The answer:** Deposit costs have exploded. While JPM earned more interest, it paid out nearly *4x more* in deposit interest expense ($26.1B to $101.4B—a 288% increase). This is the dirty secret the bull glossed over:

- **Deposits are NOT as sticky as the bull claims.** Customers are rate-sensitive. Even JPM's "ecosystem lock-in" has limits. When money market funds yielded 5%+, retail customers voted with their feet—and JPM had to *raise deposit rates substantially* to compete.
- **The margin is being squeezed from both sides.** Loan yields are already priced in. But as deposit competition intensifies (especially from non-bank competitors like money market funds, Treasury direct access, and fintech challengers), JPM will face further pressure on its cost of deposits.

#### **B. THE "93% NO RATE CUTS" ARGUMENT IS A TRAP**

The bull leans heavily on prediction markets pricing zero rate cuts in 2026. But here's the critical flaw in this logic:

**Prediction markets are backward-looking, not forward-looking.** They price what the Fed is *saying today*, not what will actually happen. Here's the reality:

- **We are currently in June 2025.** Prediction markets pricing "zero cuts in 2026" are extrapolating from today's Fed stance.
- **But economic conditions change.** If labor markets weaken (unemployment ticks up), inflation stays sticky, or credit stress emerges (remember: 66% probability of bank failures by end-2026!), the Fed *will* cut, and it will cut aggressively.
- **The bull's argument assumes a 2-year forecast horizon with perfect visibility—a fantasy.** JPM needs rate stability, not just rates that stay elevated through 2026.

#### **C. HERE'S THE REAL DANGER: NIM IS NOW A LIABILITY, NOT AN ASSET**

JPM's current profitability is *entirely dependent on rate persistence.* If rates decline even modestly:

- Forward EPS of $24.99 is derived substantially from elevated NII
- Any meaningful rate-cut cycle will compress NIM **immediately**
- Unlike 2023, when JPM had reserves and pricing power, the bank now has **limited room to re-optimize** once rates fall

**The bull priced in NII strength and is now betting on no mean reversion. That's not investing; that's speculation.**

#### **A. THE EARNINGS GROWTH STORY IS MASKING DETERIORATING BUSINESS FUNDAMENTALS**

**What the bull is hiding:** The 55% cumulative growth from 2022-2024 is almost entirely attributable to a **one-time windfall from the interest rate cycle.** The Fed hiked rates aggressively from 2022-2023, which inflated JPM's NII. This is NOT recurring, structural earnings growth—it's a temporary benefit that **is already rolling over.**

Notice the implied deceleration: 32% (2023) → 18% (2024) → 3-6% (2025E). This is a **collapsing growth rate**—a red flag that the earnings boom is exhausting.

#### **B. OPERATING LEVERAGE ISN'T REAL; IT'S TEMPORARY**

The bull celebrates Q2 2025's 50.4% operating margin as proof of "optimization." **This is a misreading of the data.**

Here's what's actually happening:

- **Investment banking revenue is cyclically elevated in 2025.** This is a high-margin business, but it's volatile. If M&A pipelines slow (which they do during economic downturns), IB revenue crashes and margins compress.
- **Loan-loss provisions are at cyclical lows** (because credit is pristine and we're near full employment). When the economy slows and credit deteriorates, provisions will spike, hitting the margin immediately.
- **The "cost discipline" narrative is oversold.** JPM's expense base is large and growing. Compensation costs, regulatory compliance, and technology investments are all rising.

**The 50.4% operating margin is a *cyclical peak*, not a new structural level.** When the cycle turns, margins will revert toward historical 40-42% levels.

#### **C. CASH FLOW GENERATION IS COLLAPSING**

Here's the most damning evidence the bull ignored:

| Year | Operating Cash Flow | Free Cash Flow | Trend |

| 2022 | $107.1B | $107.1B | Strong |

| 2024 | $(42.0)B | $(42.0)B | **NEGATIVE** |
| Q2 2025 | $29.5B | $29.5B | Quarter-to-quarter only |

**JPM is destroying cash on an annual basis.** The 2024 negative $42B operating cash flow is a **massive red flag** that the bull completely ignored. This suggests:

- Working capital is expanding (unlikely for a mature bank)
- The bank is taking larger balance sheet positions (taking on risk)
- Or the accounting is masking deteriorating underlying cash generation

**Q2 2025's positive $29.5B quarterly OCF does NOT offset the annual deterioration.** Annualizing Q2 would give ~$118B, but that ignores seasonal patterns and the reality of 2024's disastrous cash generation.

**Bottom line: Earnings growth is illusory if cash flow is collapsing.**
