**Recommendation**: Overweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: FIS trades at a very low headline P/E (~5.9x) and EV/EBITDA (~5.6x), generated a GAAP net loss of -$470M in Q2 2025 alongside positive operating cash flow (~$590M) and FCF (~$372M), carries ~$13B of debt with net debt/EBITDA around 3.5x, and has negative tangible book value once you strip out ~$21.4B of goodwill/intangibles. The debate hinges on how to interpret these facts.

The bull's strongest point is that the cash-generation profile (TTM EBITDA ~$3.5B, consistent FCF) doesn't look like a company in distress, and that a 5-6x earnings/EBITDA multiple for a recurring-revenue, mission-critical financial infrastructure business is unusually cheap versus peers—even after haircutting for normalization. The dividend coverage and continued market access for refinancing support the idea that the market isn't pricing outright distress.

The bear's strongest point is that the bull hasn't actually substantiated the "one-time charge" claim for the Q2 loss with any cited evidence—it's an assumption, not a fact from the filings referenced. The bear also correctly flags that stripping out D&A to "prove" cash strength is circular when a meaningful chunk of that D&A is amortization of acquired intangibles that were bought at premium prices (Worldpay-related); that's a real economic cost, not a pure non-cash quirk. The receivables build and refinancing-treadmill argument are more speculative and less well evidenced in the prompt data (we don't have full detail on whether the $148M receivables increase is seasonal or structural), so I discount that specific point somewhat. But the leverage argument is solid: 3.5x net debt/EBITDA is at the higher end for what's supposed to be a stable infrastructure business, and EBITDA visibility is genuinely uncertain given the recent GAAP loss and lack of management clarification in the record provided. Negative tangible equity is a real balance-sheet weakness that the bull's "it's just goodwill" dismissal doesn't erase — it does raise the risk profile if refinancing terms tighten or if the company needs to divest.

Netting it out: the valuation is genuinely cheap on a cash-flow basis, and the recurring-revenue business model has real defensive characteristics, so this isn't a "Sell" situation. But the bear raises legitimate, unrebutted concerns — the unexplained GAAP loss, elevated leverage relative to "mature infrastructure" peers, negative tangible equity, and refinancing dependency — that the bull did not adequately extinguish (mostly asserted away rather than proven with hard numbers from the record). Given that the debate materials don't give us the specific breakdown of the loss, I can't confidently underwrite the "it's all one-time" bull narrative, which caps my conviction. This is not a balanced 50/50 situation either — the cash flow and valuation support owning/adding to the name, but the debt and earnings-quality concerns argue against aggressively building the position. That points to a constructive-but-cautious stance: Overweight rather than outright Buy, reflecting the valuation opportunity while acknowledging the bear's unresolved risks around leverage and earnings quality warrant a measured position size rather than a maximum-conviction add.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Add to FIS gradually rather than making one large purchase — scale into the position over 2-3 tranches to manage the earnings-quality uncertainty (the Q2 GAAP loss is unexplained in the record; treat "one-time charge" as a working hypothesis, not a confirmed fact, until the next earnings release/10-Q clarifies).
2) Size the position to reflect elevated leverage risk: keep FIS as a moderate-conviction overweight (not a maximum-size Buy) given 3.5x net debt/EBITDA is at the high end for an "infrastructure" business and tangible book value is negative (-$7.2B) — this caps how much balance-sheet cushion exists if EBITDA softens.
3) Set a monitoring checklist for the next 1-2 quarters: (a) confirm/quantify the source of the Q2 net loss (impairment vs. operating deterioration), (b) watch receivables/DSO trend for continued deterioration beyond the $148M Q2 build, (c) track refinancing spreads/costs on the next debt rollover, (d) watch for any EBITDA guidance cuts that would push leverage past ~4x.
4) Use the cheap valuation (5.6-5.9x earnings/EBITDA, ~4.6% dividend yield) as the basis for a value-oriented entry, but hold trims of the position in reserve rather than a strict stop, and be prepared to downgrade to Hold/Underweight if the working-capital or leverage trends worsen in upcoming filings.
5) Reassess the rating to Buy if management explicitly breaks out and confirms the Q2 charge as non-recurring and normalized EBITDA/leverage trends hold at or below current levels; reassess toward Underweight/Sell if net debt/EBITDA trends toward 4x+ or tangible equity deteriorates further without offsetting cash flow strength.
