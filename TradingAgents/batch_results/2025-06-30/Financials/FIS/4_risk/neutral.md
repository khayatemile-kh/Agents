The aggressive analyst points to prediction markets showing 50% overheating / 48% soft landing and concludes this is tailwind for FIS. I need to push back hard on that framing.

First, the prediction markets showing a 50/50 split between two scenarios is *exactly the definition of uncertainty*. If the market were genuinely confident in an overheating scenario, we'd see 70-80% probability. Instead, we see maximum uncertainty. That's not a tailwind—that's ambiguity.

Second, and more critically, **neither scenario is particularly benign for a highly-leveraged financial services infrastructure provider.** Here's why:

- **Overheating scenario:** Yes, transaction volumes increase. But overheating typically brings interest rate volatility, credit spread widening, and potential financial system stress. FIS would need to grow revenues faster than its cost of debt increases. There's no evidence they can do that reliably.

- **Soft landing scenario:** Yes, the financial system stays healthy. But soft landings typically involve *stabilization* of transaction volumes, not acceleration. FIS is essentially pricing in flat to declining growth under this scenario.

The conservative analyst is right that macro uncertainty is a risk factor, but the aggressive analyst is wrong that it's a tailwind. It's a wash—possibly negative given FIS's leverage.
