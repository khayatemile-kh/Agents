**Operating Cash Flow (TTM annualized from Q2): ~$52 billion**
**Less: Capex (TTM annualized from Q2): ~$34.4 billion**
**Equals: Reported Free Cash Flow: ~$17.6 billion**

- Dividend payments: ~$1.3 billion annually

This means GM is *barely generating enough FCF to cover interest expense, dividends, and recent buyback run rates*. There's virtually no room for actual debt reduction. More critically, if capex needs to *increase* (which it likely will as EV ramp accelerates and legacy plant closures occur), FCF gets squeezed further.

This is the recursive problem neither analyst fully grappled with: **GM is trapped in a capex-heavy cycle where FCF barely covers servicing debt and returning capital, while debt itself remains at dangerous levels for any cyclical downturn.**

The aggressive analyst claims GM is "delevering through FCF." The data suggests the opposite: GM is maintaining debt levels while funding the EV transition and shareholder returns. That's not delevering—that's refinancing and hoping the transition completes before demand shocks.
