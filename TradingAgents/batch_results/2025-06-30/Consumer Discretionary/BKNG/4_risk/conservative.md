## The RSI "Feature Not Bug" Argument Misses The Critical Point

The Aggressive Analyst says that RSI at 72 in a strong uptrend is normal and not a warning sign. They're partially right—RSI can remain elevated in sustained uptrends. But they're cherry-picking history to support a predetermined conclusion. Yes, BKNG rallied in early June with RSI above 70. But let's look at what actually happened next: **the stock declined 4.7% to $209.68 by June 18.** That pullback happened *while RSI was still elevated*, which means RSI didn't protect them from drawdown.

Here's what the Aggressive Analyst glosses over: RSI at 72 doesn't mean the stock won't go up. It means the probability of near-term consolidation or pullback *increases materially*. The early June high of $223.36 was reached with RSI at 72.50–72.58. Did the stock immediately blast higher? No. It consolidated for days, then declined. That's the pattern we should expect here.

But here's where it gets more sophisticated—and where the risk really lives. The RSI hasn't just stayed above 70; it's *re-entered* overbought territory from a position of capitulation weakness (June 20: 47.67). That rapid re-acceleration from oversold to overbought in just ten trading days is exactly the kind of exhaustion pattern that precedes mean reversion. The market moves fast up, then it corrects fast down. We're seeing the mechanical signature of that pattern right now.

The Aggressive Analyst wants us to believe this is "strength." I'd argue it's velocity without conviction. And velocity without conviction is how you get sharp reversals.

## The MACD "Rebounding From Capitulation" Argument Conflates Recovery With Confirmation

The Aggressive Analyst is correct that MACD has climbed from its June 20 low of 1.58 to 3.65 by June 30. They're also correct that MACD lags price in strong moves. But they're wrong about what this means operationally, and here's why:

**A divergence becomes actionable when price makes new highs while momentum fails to confirm—and stays failed.** The Aggressive Analyst says "no actual divergence has confirmed yet because we haven't seen reversal." That's technically true but operationally dangerous. Here's what they're missing:

The divergence has *formed*. Price is at 30-day highs ($229.63). MACD is still 43% below its early-June peak (3.65 vs. 6.52). The fact that MACD is climbing doesn't erase the divergence—it just means we're in the early stages of a potential breakdown, not the completion. Divergences are *leading indicators*. They warn you before the reversal happens, not after.

The real risk the Aggressive Analyst is glossing over: if price rolls over here or consolidates sideways for a few weeks, MACD will continue to compress and potentially roll over entirely. At that point, the divergence becomes confirmed, and you've got a classic technical breakdown on your hands—price at highs with momentum rolling. That's when the sharp reversals happen.

Moreover, I want to push back on the narrative that silence in MACD recovery is normal and bullish. It's not—it's a warning. In healthy, explosive uptrends, MACD typically reaches or *exceeds* previous peaks as price continues higher. The fact that MACD peaked three weeks ago and hasn't recovered to that level despite price making new highs is telling us something: **momentum is not accelerating; it's decelerating.** That's the opposite of what we want to see in the early innings of a major rally.

## The Earnings Catalyst Argument Is Built On Speculative Timing

The Aggressive Analyst is betting on earnings to bail out a stretched technical position. They point to Q2 2025 fundamentals showing $6.80B in revenue, 33.1% operating margins, and conclude that earnings will be a catalyst that drives the stock higher. Here's the problem with that logic:

**Earnings catalysts work when valuations are depressed and expectations are low.** The Aggressive Analyst themselves noted that BKNG is trading at 14.19x forward earnings. They called it "cheap." But let me put that in context: over a full cycle, travel services companies typically trade at 12-16x forward earnings. We're *not* at the bottom of that range. We're at the top. That means most of the good news about fundamentals is already priced in.

The silent sentiment environment they highlight as bullish? I read that differently. Silent markets typically mean people aren't paying attention—which means when earnings come, the market reaction could swing either direction without the protective floor of accumulated bullish positioning. There's no army of retail buyers ready to catch a 3-5% dip because nobody's watching closely.

Here's the real risk: if BKNG reports solid Q2 numbers (which they likely will, based on the fundamentals), the market's response might be "that was expected." Stock goes sideways or drifts lower. If they report anything less than perfection—slight guide-down on Q3, cautious commentary on international travel, mention of macro headwinds—the stock could gap down hard. We're at $229.63 with elevated technical conditions. A 5% earnings miss combined with a technical reversal puts you at $218 in a day, with no pullback to buy.

The Aggressive Analyst is essentially asking us to hold a stretched technical setup and pray that earnings lift us off a cliff edge. That's not risk management; that's speculation disguised as fundamental conviction.

## The Capital Allocation Story Proves The Opposite of What They're Claiming

The Aggressive Analyst points to $6.51B in buybacks in 2024 and $1.50B in Q2 2025 as evidence that management thinks the stock is undervalued. This is one of the most dangerous misinterpretations of buyback data I see regularly. Let me be direct: **buyback programs are fixed, pre-announced capital allocation tools. They don't represent real-time management conviction about valuation.**

BKNG's buyback program is ongoing and predictable. It's part of their capital return policy. What management *actually* thinks about current valuation can be gleaned from much more recent data: insider selling. And guess what? I don't see insider buying at $229. If management truly believed the stock was massively undervalued at these levels, we'd see executives loading up personal positions. We don't see that consistently happening at $229.63.

Furthermore, the Aggressive Analyst seems impressed that BKNG carries only $877M in net debt. But let's contextualize that: the company has $18.99B in gross debt against a market cap of $131.99B. That's a 14.4% debt-to-market-cap ratio—which is reasonable but not exceptional. And critically, the company maintains that debt level *specifically to fund buybacks*. They're not reducing debt; they're using cheap debt to fund capital returns. That's not fortress-like; that's optimized for a growth environment. When growth stumbles or rates stay elevated longer, that debt structure becomes a liability, not an asset.

The fortress narrative breaks down when you look at the actual debt maturity schedule and refinancing risk in a higher-rate environment. Prediction markets show 93% probability of *no rate cuts in 2026*. That means BKNG's cost of capital stays elevated. The company can afford it today—but when you layer a slower macro environment onto higher refinancing costs, suddenly that debt load is less benign.
