## THE BEAR CASE: FOUR CRITICAL COUNTERARGUMENTS

### **COUNTERARGUMENT 1: THE VALUATION IS NOT JUSTIFIED BY GROWTH—IT'S A PEAK EARNINGS MIRAGE**

My colleague points to spectacular EPS growth (50.3% CAGR over 2022-2024) and argues the 14.19x forward PE is reasonable. This is where the bulls reveal their analytical blind spot: **they're confusing a temporary earnings acceleration with normalized, sustainable growth.**

**Let's Deconstruct the EPS Magic:**

| Component | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025E | Reality Check |
|-----------|------|------|------|-------|---------------|
| Revenue CAGR | - | +24.9% | +11.0% | ~8-10% | **Growth is DECELERATING** |
| Revenue per User | Growing | Growing | Flattening | Slowing | Saturation signals |
| Operating Margin | 29.8% | 27.3% | 31.8% | ? | Cyclical bounce, not structural |
| Share Count | 805M | 726M | 678M | ~650M | **Share buybacks inflate EPS** |
| Reported EPS Growth | - | +54.0% | +47.0% | TBD | But revenue growth is HALF that rate |

**Here's the inconvenient truth the bulls won't admit:**

1. **Revenue growth has decelerated from 24.9% (2023) to 11.0% (2024).** That's a 55% slowdown in the growth rate. Yes, *absolute* revenues are still growing, but the *rate* of growth is halving.

2. **EPS growth (50.3% CAGR) is being artificially boosted by share buybacks.** If BKNG had held share count constant, EPS growth would be closer to **20-25%**, which is still decent but **far less impressive**. The company is using financial engineering to mask underlying operational deceleration.

3. **Operating margins expanded to 31.8% in 2024—but this is partially a cyclical rebound, not a structural improvement.** Note the 2023 dip to 27.3%. Margins are mean-reverting, not permanently elevated.

**What Does This Mean for Valuation?**

- **Forward PE of 14.19x assumes continued strong growth.** But if revenue growth normalizes to 8-10% and EPS growth (excluding buybacks) settles around 15-20%, then BKNG should trade at 12-13x forward PE, not 14.19x.
- **That implies 8-15% downside from current $229.63 levels**, even in a base case scenario.

**The Bull's Rebuttal Will Be:** "But BKNG has high margins and cash flow; it deserves a premium multiple!"

**My Response:** Yes, high-quality businesses deserve premiums. But **the premium compounds over time only if growth remains elevated. If growth is decelerating (which it is), the multiple must compress to reflect the new reality.** This is how markets work—growth stocks get re-rated lower when growth disappoints.

### **COUNTERARGUMENT 2: THE MACRO BACKDROP IS NOT AS SUPPORTIVE AS THE BULLS CLAIM—AND IT'S DETERIORATING**

My colleague cited prediction markets suggesting 93% probability of no Fed rate cuts through 2026, 68% probability of 2-2.5% GDP growth, and only 4% recession risk. He then claims this is a "supportive backdrop" for travel demand.

**This is a masterclass in selective data interpretation. Let me reframe it:**

**The Harsh Macro Reality:**

1. **"No Rate Cuts Through 2026" = Persistent Consumer Financial Stress**

The bull framed this as "reducing uncertainty." I'll reframe it: **it means interest rates remain elevated, real purchasing power remains suppressed, and borrowing costs stay high for consumers and businesses.**

Here's what this means for BKNG:
- **Higher financing costs for travel partners** (hotels, airlines, car rental companies) → they reduce marketing spend on Booking → BKNG's commission revenue growth slows
- **Consumer budget constraints** → discretionary travel spending is prioritized lower → bookings per user decline
- **Credit card interest rates remain high** → fewer consumers financing vacations → lower average booking values

The prediction market data doesn't say rates are *falling*—it says they're *flat and elevated*. That's a headwind, not a tailwind. The bull's characterization of "stability" as positive is precisely backwards. Elevated rates *are* a drag on consumer discretionary spending.

2. **"2-2.5% GDP Growth" = Below-Trend, Below Historical Expectations**

The bull tried to spin this as "steady, moderate growth" and "perfect for BKNG." Let's be precise:

- **2-2.5% is below the historical US average of 2.8-3.0%**
- **It's well below the pre-pandemic trend of 2.5-3.0%**
- **It's barely above the recessionary threshold**

What does sub-trend growth mean for travel demand?
- **Weak consumer real wage growth** → less discretionary income available
- **Corporate profit margins compress** → business travel budgets are cut
- **International travel is more sensitive to weak US growth** → a 2% US GDP scenario coincides with global slowdown

The base case of 2-2.5% growth is the *weakest* scenario that still avoids recession. It's not a bullish setup—it's a *barely bullish* setup that leaves zero room for disappointment.

3. **"4% Recession Probability" = Tail Risk Is Non-Trivial, Not Negligible**

Let's reframe this: **There's a 4-6% combined probability of recession or severe slowdown.** For a $132B market-cap company heavily exposed to discretionary consumer spending, a 4-6% recession tail risk is material.

In a recession scenario:
- BKNG's revenue could decline 15-25%
- Margins compress from 31.8% to 20-25% as the company absorbs fixed costs
- EPS could fall 40-60%
- The stock would re-rate to 10-12x earnings = -30-40% downside

**The bulls are ignoring tail risk by focusing on base-case probabilities.**

**The Real Macro Headwind Nobody's Discussing: International Exposure**

BKNG generates ~60% of revenue internationally. A strong USD (likely in a higher-rate, higher-real-yield environment) makes international travel more expensive and reduces bookings by non-US residents.

- **USD strength = International travel demand falls**
- **International guests booking US accommodations decline**
- **Currency headwinds for BKNG's international revenue** (when converted back to USD)

The prediction markets say nothing about currency dynamics. The bulls have glossed over a material risk factor.
