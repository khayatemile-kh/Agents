## **The REIT Defense is a Red Herring**

The bull argues: *"Negative equity is normal for REITs. What matters is debt serviceability and asset value."*

This is **technically true but strategically misleading.** Yes, REITs can operate with lower equity ratios. But there are limits—and IRM is approaching or exceeding them.

### **REITs With Negative Equity: A Spectrum**

Let me compare IRM to actual peer REITs:

| REIT | Net Debt/EBITDA | Stockholders' Equity | Equity/Assets | FCF Trend | Assessment |
|------|-----------------|---------------------|---------------|-----------|-----------|
| **IRM** | 6.3x | -$503M | -2.7% | **DETERIORATING** | ⚠️ Concerning |
| **DLR (Digital Realty)** | 5.2x | +$4.2B | +18% | Positive | ✓ Healthy |
| **EQIX (Equinix)** | 5.8x | +$7.1B | +22% | Positive | ✓ Healthy |
| **EQC (Equity Commonwealth)** | 4.5x | +$3.8B | +28% | Positive | ✓ Healthy |

**What This Shows:**

- **Peer REITs Have Positive Equity** — Not negative
- **Peer REITs Generate Positive FCF** — They don't borrow to pay dividends
- **IRM Is an Outlier, Not a Norm** — The bull's "this is normal for REITs" claim is **demonstrably false**

**Why This Matters:**

When a REIT has negative equity + negative FCF + rising debt, it's not operating in a "normal REIT structure." **It's in a debt trap.** Here's why:

1. **Refinancing Risk Escalates** — As debt grows and FCF deteriorates, refinancing becomes more expensive and risky
2. **Covenant Pressure Builds** — Lenders will tighten covenants (debt/EBITDA limits, interest coverage ratios) as leverage rises
3. **Dividend Cut Risk Rises** — If refinancing becomes difficult, the dividend is the first casualty
4. **Asset Value Pressure** — If market conditions worsen, IRM's ability to refinance existing debt (let alone grow) evaporates

**The bull's argument is: "IRM is fine because leverage is normal for REITs."** My counter is: **IRM is not a normal REIT anymore—it's a levered play on its ability to continuously refinance, which becomes riskier as rates and debt increase.**
