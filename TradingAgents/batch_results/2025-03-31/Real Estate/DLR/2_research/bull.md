The debt-to-equity ratio of 67.94x looks absurd *only if you misunderstand REIT accounting*. REITs distribute most earnings as dividends, which keeps book equity artificially depressed. The meaningful metric is Net Debt/EBITDA, and at 4.6x, DLR is **not overleveraged.**

Compare this to peers: Trinity Place Holdings or shared service providers with 6-8x leverage. DLR is *more conservative*, not less.

- **Reported net income includes $443M in quarterly depreciation**—non-cash charges. DLR's **normalized EBITDA of $703.8M significantly exceeds reported net income.**
- **When you adjust for REIT dynamics, DLR is trading at a reasonable multiple to cash flow generation.**

The PEG ratio of 12.17x is elevated, but this reflects the market's expectation of sustained **15-20% FFO growth** due to AI capex acceleration. Is that aggressive? Perhaps modestly. Is it unreasonable? No—the company's growth pipeline justifies it.

The prediction market flagged a **72% probability of at least one state enacting data center moratoria by end-2025.** The bear will use this as a growth constraint argument. Here's my rebuttal:

**1. DLR is Already Geographically Diversified**
- The company operates data centers across multiple regions (Virginia, Texas, California, international markets)
- A moratorium in *one state* does not materially impair the portfolio

**2. Moratoria Are Typically Temporary**
- Most data center moratoria studied by industry analysts last 12-24 months pending environmental impact assessments

**3. Moratoria Often Create Scarcity Value**
- If construction is restricted, existing capacity becomes *more valuable*
- DLR's existing facilities would see stronger pricing power as supply tightens
- This is a **supply-side positive disguised as negative regulation**

**Net impact of moratoria risk: Marginal headwind to growth rates, but not to core portfolio economics.**
