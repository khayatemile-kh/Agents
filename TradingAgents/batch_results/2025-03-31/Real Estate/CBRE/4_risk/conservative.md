## THE EBITDA SLEIGHT OF HAND ISN'T WHAT HE THINKS IT IS

Let me decompose what's happening in CBRE's P&L:

EBITDA grew 13.6% while net income was essentially flat. That's a $233 million EBITDA increase that somehow translated into near-zero net income growth. Where did those dollars go? Into:

1. **Depreciation and amortization**: These increased from $621 million in 2023 to $674 million in 2024—a $53 million jump, or 8.5%. That's normal for a growing company, but it tells us that CBRE is carrying increasing asset loads on the balance sheet.

2. **Interest expense**: With total debt jumping from $4.826 billion to $5.694 billion, interest costs are rising. When debt grows 18% but EBITDA grows only 13.6%, debt service as a percentage of EBITDA is deteriorating, not improving.

3. **Tax provisions and other charges**: These are absorbing the remaining profit expansion.

The Aggressive Analyst frames this as "temporary" and points to the gap as evidence of strategic investment. I see it differently. **When EBITDA growth doesn't flow through to earnings growth, it suggests the company is struggling with operational leverage**—that is, it's having to spend more to generate each dollar of incremental revenue.

This is the opposite of what you want to see in a business with pricing power and scale. In a healthy scaling company, incremental EBITDA flows substantially to net income because fixed costs are absorbed over a larger revenue base. In CBRE, we're seeing incremental EBITDA leak away into depreciation, amortization, and financing costs.

That's not investment mode. That's margin compression disguised as growth.
