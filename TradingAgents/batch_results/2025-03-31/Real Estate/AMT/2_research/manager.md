**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides made compelling points, but weighing the evidence, the bear case exposes real structural concerns that the bull glosses over. The revenue growth deceleration (6.7% → 3.8% → 1.1%) is a clear, quantified trend that the bull couldn't refute with anything more than hope for reacceleration — 'should accelerate' is not evidence, it's a wish. The bear's point that 5G is primarily a densification/upgrade cycle on existing sites rather than a new tower-build supercycle (unlike 4G) is a credible structural argument that directly undercuts the bull's core growth thesis.

On margins, the bull's 'operational leverage' story is impressive on the surface (EBITDA +19% on 1% revenue growth), but the bear's counter — that this was driven by one-time cost cuts, capex deferral ($1.80B→$1.59B), and asset sale proceeds — is a legitimate red flag. Margin expansion that isn't supported by proportional capex reinvestment often reverses or signals under-investment in maintaining the asset base, which is particularly concerning for a tower REIT where physical infrastructure integrity matters.

On leverage, the bull's framing (6.14x EBITDA, 'manageable for tower REIT') is reasonable in isolation, but the bear rightly stresses that debt service risk is about refinancing cost, not just leverage ratio. With ~$44B total debt and a rate environment where refi costs are elevated, interest coverage (3.07x) is not overwhelming, and the bear's math showing coverage could compress to ~2.4x under a 5% refinancing scenario is a real risk to dividend growth capacity — the primary reason to own this stock.

On valuation, the bull's EV/EBITDA comparison to REIT peers is fair, but the bear's point about PEG ratio (~6.5x vs 1.5-2x reasonable) and normalized EBITDA (adjusting for deferred capex) suggesting AMT is more expensive than headline multiples imply is compelling and specific.

The technical setup (golden cross, near highs) is a supportive but secondary signal — it doesn't offset the deteriorating growth trajectory and margin sustainability questions.

Net take: the bull's dividend safety and cash generation points are real strengths, but the bear more convincingly demonstrates that (1) growth deceleration is structural not transitory, (2) the margin story likely partially reverses as capex normalizes, and (3) leverage creates real risk to the dividend growth narrative in a higher-for-longer rate environment. This isn't a clean sell — AMT still generates strong absolute FCF and has a defensible core franchise — but the risk/reward at current premium valuation with decelerating growth tips toward caution rather than adding exposure. I'm recommending Underweight: trim into strength, not full exit, given the cash flow quality is still real even if growth and margin durability are overstated by bulls.
