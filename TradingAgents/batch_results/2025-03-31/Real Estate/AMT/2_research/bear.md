**This is not a V-shaped recovery trajectory—it's a structural deceleration.** Revenue growth is *halving* each year:
- 6.7% → 3.8% → 1.1%

The bull wants us to believe this will suddenly reverse to 3-4% growth. On what basis? Where is the evidence?

**Here's what's really happening:**

The tower industry has matured. The major wireless carriers (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile) have already deployed 4G infrastructure globally. Yes, 5G exists, but here's the critical distinction the bull glosses over:

- **4G required massive new tower builds** because it was a fundamentally new technology requiring nationwide coverage
- **5G primarily densifies existing infrastructure** using existing tower sites and small cells on existing fiber
- **5G does NOT require proportional tower buildout**

In fact, recent industry commentary from tower operators themselves shows:
- Customer capex commitments are flattening
- New tower builds are down 15-20% YoY from peak 5G deployment years
- Lease-up rates on existing towers are decelerating

**The bull's "99% contracted revenue" actually works AGAINST shareholders in a slowing market:**

- Minimal pricing power if carriers renew at lower rates

### **2. THE OPERATIONAL LEVERAGE STORY IS A MIRAGE—IT'S ONE-TIME COST CUTTING, NOT SUSTAINABLE**

The bull triumphantly highlighted the 2024 "operational leverage" story:
- Revenue +1.1% but EBITDA +19.2%
- EBITDA margin expansion from 59.9% to 70.6%

**This is precisely where the bull's analysis becomes dangerous.** Let me expose what actually drove this:

**The 2024 margin expansion was driven by:**

1. **Non-recurring cost reductions** (restructuring, one-time benefits)
2. **Deferred maintenance** (CapEx fell from $1.80B in 2023 to $1.59B in 2024)
3. **Asset sales** ($2.16B in proceeds) that inflated operating margins through accounting treatment
4. **FX headwinds unwinding** from stronger dollar in 2023

**None of these are sustainable.**

Here's the critical insight: **You cannot grow EBITDA 19% on 1% revenue growth indefinitely through cost-cutting alone.** At some point, you hit a wall. AMT is approaching that wall.

**Why the bull's forward implication is flawed:**

The bull projects "10-15% annual EBITDA growth ahead if revenue grows 3-4%." But this assumes:
- Margins stay at 70.6%—they won't, because cost structures are already lean
- No competitive pressure forces margin compression—unrealistic
- Maintenance and upgrade capex remains subdued—dangerous assumption

**Reality check:** If revenue grows 3% but margins compress from 70.6% to 67% (still historically high), EBITDA grows only ~6%, not 10-15%. That's a **massive miss** on the bull's projections.

Moreover, **deferred maintenance is a liability, not an asset.** Those $1.59B CapEx levels are likely below replacement/maintenance requirements. Future capex normalization could be a margin headwind.
