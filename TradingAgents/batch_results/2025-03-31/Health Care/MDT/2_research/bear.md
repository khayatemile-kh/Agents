You claim hospitals have "astronomical switching costs" due to training and workflow integration. Let me challenge that:

**1. The Reality of Hospital Economics**

- **Price competition in medical devices has intensified dramatically over the past decade**

Evidence: Look at the cardiac stent market. Fifteen years ago, drug-eluting stents had 40%+ margins. Today? Mid-20s. Why? Because **switching costs don't prevent commoditization when procurement teams get competitive bids.**

**2. Your Regulatory Moat is Crumbling**
- You cite "5-10 years and $50M-$500M to get FDA approval"

- **Emerging competitors from China and India are flooding the market with lower-cost alternatives**
- Example: Orthopedic implants and spinal devices (MDT's key categories) now face brutal Chinese competition at 30-50% price discounts

Your moat isn't widening; it's eroding. MDT's pricing power is evaporating against lower-cost competitors.

**MDT's emerging market business is:**
- Lower margin (15-20% EBIT margin vs. 22%+ in developed markets)

More importantly: **MDT's 30% emerging market mix hasn't driven growth acceleration.** If emerging markets are truly your growth engine, why isn't MDT growing faster than 3.6%?

The answer: **Emerging markets have lower margins and face local competitors. They're dragging on overall profitability, not lifting it.**
