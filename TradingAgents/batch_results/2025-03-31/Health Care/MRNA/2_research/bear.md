## PART 1: THE COVID CLIFF EXCUSE DOESN'T HOLD UP TO SCRUTINY

**The Bull Says:** "The market has already priced in the COVID revenue cliff. That decline was expected."

**My Response:** Yes, it was expected. **And the market is telling you the company won't successfully replace it.**

### **Here's the Critical Math the Bull is Glossing Over:**

Let's trace Moderna's actual execution against its own guidance:

| Year | Revenue | Management Implied Expectation | Reality |
|------|---------|--------------------------------|---------|
| **2022** | $18.88B | Peak COVID year | Reality: Peak |
| **2023** | $6.75B | Decline to "normalized" level | Shortfall: Further 64% drop in 2023 |
| **2024** | $3.20B | Stabilization toward non-COVID base | Shortfall: Further 53% drop in 2024 |

**The Real Story:** Moderna's non-COVID business is collapsing *faster* than anticipated, not stabilizing. If management expected the "base business" to be $5-6B annually by now (their implication), and it's actually $3.2B, then **the pipeline is underperforming**.

### **RSV Vaccine Reality Check: It's Not a $4-6B Opportunity Like the Bull Claims**

The bull projects RSV at $4-6B peak. Let me reality-check this:

1. **Market Size is Smaller Than Implied**
   - RSV vaccine market estimates range from $1.5B-3B peak (not $4-6B).
   - Arexvy (Moderna's RSV) faces competition from:
     - **GSK's Arexvys** (also just launched; approved in EU before Moderna's U.S. version)
     - **Pfizer's RSVPreF3** (approved Sept 2023; established sales infrastructure)
   - These are not minor competitors. Pfizer has 30+ years of vaccine distribution expertise. GSK is the #2 vaccine manufacturer globally.
   - Moderna's U.S. "first-mover advantage" is overstated—Pfizer launched simultaneously in a comparable market, and GSK launched first in Europe.

2. **RSV Sales Trajectory is Underwhelming**
   - RSV vaccine is for elderly (65+) and immunocompromised patients. Uptake has been **slower than expected**.
   - 2024 was the full commercial launch year. If Moderna had disclosed Q4 2024 or FY2024 RSV-specific revenue, we'd know exactly how well it's performing. **The fact that they haven't broken it out separately suggests underwhelming numbers.**
   - For comparison: Shingrix (GSK's recombinant zoster vaccine) took 5+ years to reach $2B annual sales despite being a well-established market with existing patient awareness.
   - Moderna is asking elderly populations to adopt a *new* vaccine from a company known primarily for COVID. The adoption curve is proving slower than bullish projections.

3. **$4-6B Projection Requires Near-100% Market Share**
   - If RSV peak market is $2-3B (realistic estimate), Moderna needs 40-100% share to reach the bull's $4-6B projection.
   - With Pfizer and GSK as direct competitors with superior distribution, Moderna's realistic peak share is 25-35%.
   - That translates to $500M-1.05B peak, not $4-6B.

**Verdict:** The bull is double-counting the RSV opportunity. The $4-6B projection is based on optimistic market size assumptions and market share that ignore competitive realities.

## PART 2: THE PIPELINE IS REAL, BUT THE EXECUTION TRACK RECORD IS CATASTROPHIC

**The Bull Says:** "The market is ignoring pipeline depth. 5+ programs represent multi-billion-dollar opportunities."

**My Response:** Yes, the pipeline exists on paper. But let's examine Moderna's actual ability to execute on clinical programs.

### **Moderna's Historical Clinical Development Record: Troubling**

| Program | Timeline | Outcome | Relevance |
|---------|----------|---------|-----------|
| **RSV (mRNA-1345)** | 2020-2023 | FDA approved June 2023 | First mRNA vaccine outside COVID; **took 3 years** |
| **Flu (mRNA-1010)** | 2020-Present | Phase 2, no approval | **4+ years in development; still not Phase 3** |
| **CMV Vaccine** | 2018-Present | Phase 2 | **7 years; no Phase 3 initiation announced** |
| **RSV (mRNA-1345) for Pediatrics** | 2020-Present | Clinical holds / development ongoing | **4+ years; pediatric development slowing** |
| **Oncology (mRNA-4157/V940)** | 2021-Present | Phase 3 (Merck partnership) | **Dependent on Merck's development; not Moderna's lead** |

**Key Insight:** Moderna's non-COVID clinical programs are taking **2-3x longer** than peak COVID vaccine development timelines. This reveals:

1. **mRNA Platform Isn't a Turnkey Solution**
   - The COVID vaccine was successful because of:
     - Massive funding
     - Emergency pathways
     - Huge existing disease burden (credibility barrier low)
     - Simple Phase 3 design (efficacy vs. placebo in unvaccinated populations)
   
   - Non-COVID programs require:
     - Traditional efficacy endpoints (harder to hit)
     - Competing against established vaccines/treatments
     - Longer development timelines (standard for non-urgent diseases)
   
   - **The mRNA platform is NOT a competitive moat here.** Traditional pharma competitors have proven they can run flu, RSV, and oncology trials just as effectively.

2. **Merck Partnership Actually Signals Moderna's Weakness**
   - The bull touts the mRNA-4157/V940 oncology program as evidence of platform strength.
   - **I see it differently:** Moderna *needed* Merck's partnership because:
     - Moderna lacks oncology development expertise
     - Merck has credibility and distribution
     - Moderna couldn't get Phase 3 data on its own
   - This isn't a strength—it's a *dependence*. Moderna is now beholden to Merck's timelines and decision-making. If Phase 3 data misses, Moderna has limited control over the outcome.

### **The Flu Program is Particularly Damning**

The bull highlights seasonal flu (mRNA-1345) as a $2-4B opportunity. Let's reality-check:

- **Development Status:** Phase 2/3 (as of March 2025). No Phase 3 data available.
- **Timeline to Approval:** Assuming Phase 3 initiation in 2025, approval is minimum 2027-2028. That's **3 years away**.
- **Competitive Landscape:** Established flu vaccines (Fluzone, FluMist, etc.) dominate with 40+ years of safety data and established supply chains.
- **Why mRNA for Flu?** Supposedly because it can adapt faster to seasonal strains. **But:** Annual flu vaccines already update strain recommendations yearly. The advantage is marginal.
- **Market Reality:** Flu vaccine uptake is 40-50% in the U.S. despite proven efficacy. Switching to mRNA adds *complexity* and *hesitation* (post-COVID vaccine skepticism). Adoption will be slower, not faster.

**Verdict:** The flu program is speculative and 3+ years out. Even if successful, market adoption will disappoint relative to bull expectations.
