## THE CASH FLOW CONTRACTS AREN'T WHAT THE AGGRESSIVE ANALYST THINKS THEY ARE

Here's a crucial point the aggressive analyst glossed over: IQV's contracts are sticky for the customer, but IQV still bears the delivery risk.

In the CRO business, you sign a contract to run a certain number of trials or provide certain services over a specified period. But if your operational costs rise—due to inflation, labor pressures, supply chain issues—you can't always pass those costs through immediately. There's typically a lag between cost increases and contract repricing.

We're currently in a 3.3-3.5% inflation environment (per the macro report). That means IQV's operational costs are rising 3-3.5% per year. But are their contract repricing mechanisms flexible enough to capture that immediately? The aggressive analyst assumes yes, but they don't have evidence.

Moreover, the current ratio of 0.71x tells us that working capital is tight. Tight working capital + rising input costs = margin compression. And when you're already at 8.1% net margin with 13% operating margin, margin compression is dangerous. That forward earnings growth of 79.8% starts looking a lot less certain.
