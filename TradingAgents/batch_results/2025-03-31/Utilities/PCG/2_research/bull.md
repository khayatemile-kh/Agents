The **debt-to-equity ratio of 189.45** will make any analyst nervous. But let's be precise:

This isn't a bug; it's a feature. Here's why:

**Regulated utilities like PCG are DESIGNED to use debt financing because:**
- Their cash flows are guaranteed by regulators
- Interest expense is passed through to ratepayers
- Debt is cheaper than equity (pre-tax cost of debt ~4-5% vs. cost of equity ~9-11%)
- Leverage amplifies the regulated return on equity

**Example:** If PCG's regulated ROE is 10% and cost of debt is 5%, using leverage compounds returns:
- At 60% equity / 40% debt capital structure → levered returns increase
- This is *optimal* for a regulated utility

### **The Right Comparison: Debt-to-Capitalization, Not D/E**

When analyzing utilities, **debt-to-total capitalization is more meaningful** than D/E. Most utilities run:
- 40-50% debt / 50-60% equity split on a capitalization basis

PCG's leverage profile is elevated but not unprecedented for a utility mid-capex cycle.

While the full interest coverage ratio isn't provided, PCG's **$10.45B EBITDA can easily cover debt service** (typically 2-3x EBITDA for utilities). As FCF improves post-capex, leverage ratios will compress naturally.
