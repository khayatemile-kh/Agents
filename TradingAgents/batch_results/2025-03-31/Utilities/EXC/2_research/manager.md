**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: EXC trades at 14.27x forward P/E, carries heavy leverage (5.6x debt/EBITDA, 177x D/E), has a 3.84% dividend yield, and sits in service territories with real data-center demand exposure. The debate hinges on how to interpret those facts.

The bull's strongest points: EXC's leverage is standard for regulated utilities backed by durable rate-based assets; interest coverage (~2.06x) is thin but not alarming for a utility; the data-center/AI demand story is real and geographically relevant (PJM/Illinois); technical momentum (breakout above upper Bollinger Band on strong volume) is genuine; and valuation is at a discount to peers (NEE, DUK, SO, AEP) despite comparable growth.

The bear's strongest points land harder on scrutiny: (1) interest coverage is deteriorating (2.17x→2.06x) while debt keeps growing, and refinancing risk in a higher-for-longer rate environment is a legitimate concern that the bull didn't fully address beyond dismissing it; (2) the bull's own data-center EBITDA math was shown to conflate EBITDA with operating margin, and the bear correctly notes regulated utilities can't selectively charge premium rates to data centers — this is a real structural constraint, not just skepticism; (3) allowed ROE compression and lengthening regulatory lag (12-18mo → 24-36mo) is a concrete margin-erosion mechanism that the bull's "regulation as moat" framing doesn't rebut — it only argues capex volume grows, not that margins/returns on that capex are compressing.

Net assessment: the bear successfully picks apart the two most aggressive bull claims (data-center EBITDA upside and unqualified "leverage is fine") with specific, verifiable numbers already in the record (deteriorating interest coverage, ROE compression, margin math). The bull's fallback case — cheap valuation, safe/growing dividend, real but modest capex-driven rate base growth — still holds, but it supports a "steady/attractive income name" thesis rather than the "exceptional shareholder returns" growth story the bull opened with. There isn't enough here to justify a full Sell; the balance sheet is still investment-grade utility infrastructure with covered interest expense and a sustainable dividend. But the growth catalysts (data center EBITDA upside, momentum breakout) are overstated and the margin/refinancing risks are understated by the bull, so this isn't a conviction Buy either.

Given the discount valuation and dividend support versus real margin-compression and leverage-refinancing risk that the bear substantiated with harder numbers, the appropriate stance is a modest, constructive-but-cautious one: maintain exposure for the yield and defensive utility characteristics, but don't add aggressively into a rally that priced in overly optimistic data-center EBITDA assumptions and ignored rising refinancing costs and ROE compression. That's a Hold, tilting toward trimming if the rally extends further without confirming fundamentals (Q1 earnings, rate case outcomes).
