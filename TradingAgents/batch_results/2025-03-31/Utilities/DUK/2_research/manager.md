**Rationale**: Both sides make fair points, but weighing them against each other, the bear case lands more decisively on the metrics that actually matter for a regulated utility: cash flow durability and balance-sheet trajectory. The bull's earnings and margin story is real but the bear correctly notes the 2023 base was depressed, so the 59% YoY jump overstates the underlying trend—normalized growth looks like a mid-single-digit utility, not an inflection story. That undercuts the bull's valuation argument that 16.66x forward P/E is 'cheap'; at the high end of the utility peer range for a low-growth regulated business, it's a fair-to-rich multiple, not a bargain.

The more important issue is the cash flow and leverage picture. The bull frames negative FCF (-$4.48B TTM) as a harmless feature of the utility model, but the bear is right that the growing reliance on debt to plug a ~$4B annual funding gap (CapEx $13.12B vs OCF $12.33B, plus a $3.2B dividend) is a genuine structural concern, not just an accounting quirk. Net debt has risen ~14.5% over three years to $85B+, debt/EBITDA near 5.7x is elevated versus typical utility norms of 3.5-4.5x, and interest coverage near 2.9x is tight. That combination — leverage climbing while FCF stays negative — is the kind of setup that eventually forces either equity issuance, capex deferral, or dividend growth deceleration, none of which are bullish catalysts even if an outright near-term cut is unlikely.

The AI/data-center demand tailwind is a legitimate long-term structural positive for a Carolinas/Florida utility, but it's a multi-year embedded rate-base story, not something that changes the next 12 months of cash generation or de-levers the balance sheet quickly. It doesn't offset the near-term financial strain the bear identified.

On technicals, the bull's reading of "healthy pullback" signals (negative MACD histogram, price parked at the upper Bollinger Band, RSI in the 60s) is a reasonable interpretation, but it's equally consistent with a stock that has run up too far, too fast and is due for consolidation or mild pullback — not a basis for aggressive buying at/near resistance.

Netting it out: this isn't a "sell everything" bear case—DUK is still a regulated utility with a well-covered near-term dividend (26% of OCF) and legitimate long-term demand tailwinds. But the combination of rising leverage, negative FCF, a forward multiple that's already pricing in more good news than the normalized growth rate supports, and a technical setup that's extended rather than fresh, tips the balance toward caution rather than adding exposure here. The bear's leverage/cash-flow argument is the stronger, more falsifiable thesis in this debate round, so I'm recommending a cautious stance: trim/underweight rather than a full sell (the dividend and regulated cash flows are still real and the AI demand story has some validity as a multi-year offset), and rather than a hold, since the debate materially favors one side.
