## PILLAR 1: THE FORWARD EPS GROWTH STORY IS BUILT ON SAND

### The Critical Problem: Where Are These Earnings Coming From?

The bull claims forward EPS will grow 39% from $7.77 to $10.78. **This is a projection, not a guarantee—and the underlying assumptions are highly suspect.**

Let's think about what would be required to deliver this:

**Scenario 1: Oil Price Assumptions**
- The 2024 net income of $33.7B was generated at an **average Brent price of approximately $85-90/barrel**
- To achieve $10.78 EPS (vs. $7.84 in 2024), XOM would need either:
  - Oil prices sustained at $90-100+ per barrel, OR
  - Meaningful volume increases from new projects, OR
  - Significant operational leverage gains

**The problem:** None of these are reliably achievable:

1. **Oil prices at $90-100+ are at the high end of current market expectations**, not the base case
   - Market consensus for 2025-2026 Brent is closer to $75-85/barrel
   - Forward oil futures are trading in a range that **does not support $100+ Brent**
   - The bull is implicitly betting on higher commodity prices without stating this assumption explicitly

2. **New project ramp-ups (Guyana, etc.) face execution risk and cost inflation**
   - The 36.9% increase in net PPE to $294.3B is massive capex spending
   - Capex inflation in oil & gas is running 15-20% above historical norms
   - **Will these projects deliver expected returns, or will cost overruns erode margins?**
   - Guyana projects are ramping, but production growth is decelerating relative to initial estimates

3. **Operational leverage gains are being offset by cost inflation and energy transition headwinds**
   - Labor costs are rising
   - Renewable energy mandates are increasing compliance costs
   - Energy transition regulations are imposing stranded asset risks on existing production

### The Earnings Cliff Risk: Commodity Cycles Always Reverse

Here's what the bull refuses to acknowledge: **XOM's earnings are fundamentally cyclical and mean-reverting.**

Look at the data:
- **2022 Net Income:** $55.7B (abnormal cycle peak)
- **2023 Net Income:** $36.0B (normalizing)
- **2024 Net Income:** $33.7B (continuing to normalize)
- **Forward EPS projection:** $10.78 (requires reversal of this trend)

**This is not an inflection point—it's a normalization phase within a declining cycle.**

The bull claims 2022 was an anomaly. **Correct—but we're now on the downslope of that anomaly.** Normalized earnings are not $10.78 EPS; they're closer to the $7-8 range we're seeing now, or potentially lower if oil prices soften to the $70-75 range that many analysts project for 2026.

**Key concern:**

- To fund this capex AND the dividend, XOM is drawing down cash reserves
- In a low-oil-price environment, this model breaks

- Cut the dividend (breaking a 37-year streak of increases)
- Reduce capex

## PILLAR 3: ENERGY TRANSITION IS NOT PRICED IN

### The Structural Headwind the Bull Completely Ignores

XOM's business model is built on fossil fuel production and refining. **The world is systematically de-carbonizing.**

Consider the facts:

1. **EV adoption is accelerating**
   - EV penetration in new vehicle sales reached 14-18% globally in 2024 and is tracking toward 30%+ by 2030
   - This **directly reduces transportation fuel demand**—XOM's largest cash-generation driver
   - By 2035, IEA base-case scenarios project liquid fuels demand to be 10-15% lower than 2024 levels

2. **Renewable energy and electrification are replacing fossil fuel power generation**
   - Natural gas power plants are being retired globally
   - XOM's power and LNG projects face structural headwinds

3. **Corporate capital allocation is moving away from fossil fuels**
   - Asset managers representing $60+ trillion are divesting from fossil fuels
   - Insurance companies are withdrawing coverage for new oil & gas projects
   - This **reduces the market value of XOM's reserves and productive assets**

4. **Regulatory risk is accelerating, not decelerating**
   - Carbon taxes and emissions caps
