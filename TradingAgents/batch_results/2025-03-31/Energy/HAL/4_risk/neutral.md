## HERE'S WHAT BOTH SIDES ARE MISSING: THE MAGNITUDE OF THE CAPEX CYCLE MATTERS MORE THAN TIMING

Let me reframe this debate around something neither analyst fully addressed: **the capex cycle is real, but it's not binary**.

And here's the critical data point: prediction markets show sticky inflation around 3.3-3.4% through August 2025. 

Now, how does that impact capex decisions? The conservative analyst argues sticky inflation = higher discount rates = lower project IRRs = capex cuts. But that's incomplete analysis. Here's the complication:

**Oil majors today are sitting on record cash flows AND elevated cost structures.** When inflation is sticky, their *production costs* are also elevated, which means their *breakeven economics on new projects* is actually supported by the current inflation environment. A deepwater project breakeven at $55/barrel oil looks *better* when inflation is sticky at 3.3% than when it drops to 2%. Why? Because the inflation assumption is baked into their cost estimates. If inflation suddenly drops to 2%, they face *margin compression* on projects they've already sanctioned.

What this means: majors are likely to *continue sanctioning projects* at current inflation levels, because the real cost of *not* developing those reserves (given current basin economics) exceeds the cost of executing in an inflationary environment.

The aggressive analyst got close to this logic but didn't articulate it clearly. The conservative analyst dismissed it entirely as "wishful thinking."
