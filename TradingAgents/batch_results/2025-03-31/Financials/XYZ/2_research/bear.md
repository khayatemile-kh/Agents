## 2. THE LEVERAGE CRISIS: THIS IS NOT A "CAPITAL STRUCTURE PROBLEM" IT'S A SOLVENCY CONCERN

The bull casually dismisses the 32.58x debt-to-equity ratio as "alarming on its face" but then claims it's "not unsustainable."

**Let me show you why that's dangerously wrong:**

### The Leverage Reality Check

**Current situation:**
- Debt-to-Equity: 32.58x (Block's equity base is *minuscule* relative to debt)
- Interest Paid (Q1 2025): $116.42M quarterly = **$465.7M annualized**
- EBITDA (TTM): $1.55B
- **Interest Coverage Ratio: 3.33x** (EBITDA / Annual Interest)

That 3.33x coverage sounds acceptable until you realize:

1. **This is razor-thin for a technology company.** Investment-grade debt typically requires >5-6x coverage. At 3.33x, Block is *below* investment-grade thresholds.

2. **EBITDA is inflated.** EBITDA of $1.55B on $25B revenue = 6.2% margin. After depreciation, taxes, and reinvestment requirements, actual free cash flow is *negative* (-$27M). That means Block is servicing $466M in annual interest *while burning cash operationally.*

3. **Refinancing risk is acute.** If interest rates remain elevated or if Block's creditworthiness deteriorates (which a 35% stock price crash signals to credit markets), refinancing existing debt becomes more expensive. Higher rates on new debt = higher interest expense = **more pressure on already-thin earnings.**

### The Deleveraging Fantasy

The bull claims: "If Block refinances or pays down debt, EPS accretion can be enormous."

**Here's the problem: With negative free cash flow, where does the cash come from to pay down debt?**

**Block's options:**
1. **Sell assets** – No indication of major asset sales
2. **Raise equity** – Would massively dilute existing shareholders (not happening at $54.33)
3. **Cut costs** – Could improve margins, but no evidence this is planned
4. **Refinance at lower rates** – Requires credit markets to offer better terms, unlikely in "higher for longer" rate environment (per macro report)
5. **Revenue/EBITDA growth** – Possible, but not embedded in current negative FCF trajectory

**The bull is betting on deleveraging that has zero visible funding mechanism.** Meanwhile, Block is *actively burning cash* while servicing massive debt. This is not a "capital structure problem"; it's a **liquidity trajectory problem.**

### What This Means for Debt Service

None of these are sustainable in a high-leverage environment. **Negative FCF + 32.58x leverage = financial distress trajectory, not a temporary capital structure issue.**
