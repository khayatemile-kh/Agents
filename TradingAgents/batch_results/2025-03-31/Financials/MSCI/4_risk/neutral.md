When price makes a new near-term high but momentum oscillators fail to confirm, that's neither bullish nor bearish in isolation—it's *preparatory.* It's telling us that conviction is lacking. And in a stock that's already fragile, lacking conviction isn't a bullish signal. It's a warning that we need to see something else before committing capital.

Because the fundamental picture was deteriorating even while technicals were improving. The technical signal was real; the business signal was more real.

Let me address the macro picture that both analysts have referenced but haven't fully integrated. The world affairs report shows 56% probability of a soft landing, 41% probability of moderate overheating, and only 4% stagflation risk. For MSCI, what does this actually mean?

But here's what they're not acknowledging: soft landing scenarios also typically result in *stable or declining multiples* for already-expensive companies because the tail risk premium evaporates. If we hit a soft landing, growth stocks don't suddenly become more valuable—they often become *less* valuable because investors can rotate into lower-risk assets.

The Aggressive Analyst also mentions that rising yields (40% probability of 5.0% on the 10-year) increase demand for risk analytics. That's technically true, but it's also incomplete. Rising yields compress equity valuations. MSCI's forward P/E of 24.51x already assumes stable yields. If yields rise to 5.0%, the risk-free rate becomes more attractive, and capital allocates away from growth stocks with modest single-digit dividend yields (like MSCI at 1.43%).

So the macro environment isn't as supportive as the Aggressive Analyst suggests. It's more like *neutral to slightly negative* for a stock already priced for growth.
