The bear's strongest points expose real cracks the bull didn't adequately rebut: (1) FCF growth (12.5% CAGR) is outpacing OCF growth (8.7% CAGR) — meaning cash generation is being flattered by working-capital/capex timing or buyback-driven share count reduction rather than organic expansion, which is a legitimate quality-of-earnings flag; (2) rising capex intensity (2.8%→3.9% of revenue) suggests the platform requires more reinvestment just to stay competitive, undercutting the 'low capex compounder' framing; (3) competitive erosion from API-based unbundling, open-source/LLM tooling, and in-house insourcing by large institutional clients is a credible structural threat to the switching-cost moat — even the bull concedes FactSet's core clients are large institutions who are exactly the ones with resources to insource; (4) the current ratio of 0.683 combined with decelerating organic growth is a legitimate liquidity/quality concern even if operating cash flow could theoretically cover it — the bull's rebuttal here is reasonable but doesn't fully neutralize the concern about revenue growth deceleration underlying the whole debt-paydown narrative; (5) the technical picture is genuinely mixed — MACD remains negative and below the signal line, meaning the 'recovery' bull citation is real but not yet confirmed, and framing it as either 'early momentum' or 'dead cat bounce' is speculative in both directions.

Neither side provided actual revenue growth YoY figures, which is the single most important number missing from this debate — the bear repeatedly notes its absence and that omission itself is telling. Without confirmation of accelerating (or even stable) organic growth, extending premium multiples on moat/network-effect arguments alone is risky. The valuation (14.66x forward PE, 5.09x P/B) is not deeply discounted enough to force a Buy call when growth durability is contested and competitive dynamics (AI/API disruption, insourcing) are legitimate emerging risks specific to 2024-2025 that didn't exist to the same degree historically.

Given this, I don't think the evidence supports a strong conviction Buy — the bull thesis leans heavily on balance sheet quality and moat durability while glossing over the FCF/OCF divergence and rising capex intensity that the bear flagged concretely. But I also don't think this warrants Sell/Underweight — the fundamentals (margins, cash generation, capital returns, manageable leverage) are still objectively strong, and the technical setup, while unconfirmed, isn't broken (price above 200-day MA, RSI recovering). This is a case where the debate is genuinely balanced: real quality fundamentals versus real emerging competitive/growth-quality concerns, with the decisive data point (organic revenue growth rate) never actually surfaced by either side. That gap in evidence is itself a reason for caution rather than a directional bet.

I'm calling this a Hold: maintain existing exposure, don't add aggressively into an unconfirmed technical bounce and an unresolved growth-quality debate, but don't exit a business with clear balance-sheet and margin quality either.
