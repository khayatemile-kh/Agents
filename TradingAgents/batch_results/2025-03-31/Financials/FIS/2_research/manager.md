**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides use the same underlying numbers but reach very different conclusions, so let's weigh which argument actually holds up.

Bull's strongest points: the 6.06x trailing / 5.88x forward P/E is genuinely low for a company throwing off $2.65B FCF and 27.6% net margins with a 22%+ ROE. Even generous haircuts to those margins leave a business priced well below what a mission-critical, high-switching-cost financial infrastructure provider should command. The RSI/MACD recovery after the Feb selloff also suggests the panic-selling phase may be over, so there's a tactical case for the stock not falling further from here.

Bear's strongest points are more structural and, frankly, harder to wave away. The forward P/E sitting *below* the trailing P/E (5.88 vs 6.06) is a real signal — it means consensus estimates are not pricing in earnings growth, they're pricing in flat-to-declining earnings, which directly undercuts the bull's PEG-ratio argument (a PEG of 0.22 is meaningless if the "growth" denominator is illusory). The D/E of 132.51 is legitimately alarming in absolute terms, and the bull's move to Debt/EBITDA, while a fair lens, doesn't erase the fact that EBITDA multiples compress fast in a downturn while debt principal doesn't move — the bear's stress-test math (D/EBITDA jumping from ~2.5x to ~3.3x under a mild recession scenario) is a reasonable, evidence-based scenario, not fear-mongering. The current ratio of 0.535 is a real red flag on liquidity, and bear correctly notes that FCF being strong today doesn't guarantee capital-market access if credit conditions tighten — that's a legitimate risk vector the bull hand-waves away. The bear's point about the $402M swing in discontinued-operations cash flow between Q4'24 and Q1'25 is a concrete, evidence-based reason to distrust the headline FCF number without more clarity on run-rate operating cash generation.

Net: the bull's valuation argument is compelling on its face, but the bear successfully shows that the "cheapness" is at least partially explained by real balance-sheet and earnings-quality risk, not pure market irrationality. The forward P/E being below trailing P/E is the single most damning data point against the bull's PEG/growth narrative — you can't call a PEG of 0.22 a screaming buy signal if the market's own forward estimates say earnings are flat or shrinking. That said, the bear also overreaches in spots (comparing FIS's D/E to bank capital ratios is an apples-to-oranges comparison, and invoking Lehman Brothers is a rhetorical stretch not supported by the actual numbers here), so I'm not fully buying the "screaming sell" framing either.

Given the balance — real cash generation and margin quality on one side, but leverage, liquidity, and earnings-growth uncertainty (as evidenced by the forward P/E inversion) on the other — this doesn't warrant a full Buy/Overweight call, nor does it warrant an outright Sell. The stock is cheap for identifiable, evidence-based reasons (leverage, current ratio, flat forward estimates), and until there's clarity on FCF quality (ex-divestiture) and leverage trajectory, I'd rather have the trader hold size where it exists and use the technical recovery to trim into strength rather than add aggressively. This tilts slightly bearish of pure "Hold" because the forward P/E inversion and current ratio are concrete, quantifiable risk signals rather than speculative concerns, but it's not a high-conviction Sell because the FCF and margin profile are real and provide a cushion.
