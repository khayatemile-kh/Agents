### **Regulatory Environment: A Tailwind, Not a Headwind**

The bear case often flags: "Mastercard faces regulatory scrutiny on interchange fees."

Let me reframe this: **Regulation is a moat, not a threat.**

Why? Because:
- **Regulatory barriers protect incumbent networks** from new entrants
- **Interchange fee ceilings are already set** in most jurisdictions—this creates predictability, not volatility
- **Mastercard's diversified revenue** means interchange is now <40% of net revenue; the company has built alternatives

More importantly: **Governments *want* Mastercard to succeed.** Why? Because payment networks are critical infrastructure. Regulatory agencies understand that hamstringing Mastercard means pushing transactions to less regulated channels (crypto, unmonitored P2P transfers). 

*Translation:* Mastercard will never be heavily regulated into irrelevance because it's too critical to the financial system. It's a feature, not a bug.
