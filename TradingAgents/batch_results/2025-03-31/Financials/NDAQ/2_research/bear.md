## **2. THE MACRO HEADWINDS ARE SEVERE, AND THE BULL IS VASTLY UNDERESTIMATING THEM**

Now let me address the elephant in the room that the bull glosses over: **the Federal Reserve isn't cutting rates in 2026, and that's a MASSIVE problem for NDAQ's business model.**

### **"Higher for longer" doesn't just suppress IPOs — it suppresses the ENTIRE financial services cycle:**

The bull says: "IPO headwinds impact only 20-30% of revenue, the other 70%+ is recurring or volume-driven."

This is a **dangerously incomplete analysis.** Let me break down why higher rates crush NDAQ across ALL revenue streams:

#### **1. IPO/Corporate Services Revenue (20-30% of total):**
- **Higher rates = lower equity valuations = fewer profitable IPO candidates**
- **Corporate M&A deals decline significantly in high-rate environments** (acquisition multiples compress)
- **This is the most visible headwind, and it's REAL**

But the bull is right that this is only part of the picture. The real damage is elsewhere.

#### **2. Market Technology Services & Data Products (recurring revenue):**
- This is where the bull thinks they're safe: "These are recurring, sticky revenues!"
- **Here's the problem: when equity valuations compress and trading volumes decline, customers REDUCE their data product subscriptions**
- **Why?** Because institutional asset managers manage fewer assets, hedge funds see AUM decline, and trading desks reduce headcount. They use LESS market data, not more.
- **Recurring doesn't mean recession-proof.** It means the revenue continues until the customer decides to cancel or downgrade.

#### **3. Options & Equities Trading (volume-dependent):**
- The bull says: "Volatility drives trading volume, and higher rates create uncertainty!"
- **This is partially true, but it misses the bigger picture.** Yes, volatility might spike near the Fed's decisions. But **sustained elevated rates don't drive sustainable trading volume — they depress equity participation.**
- **Why?** Because in a high-rate environment, investors have a 4-5% risk-free rate available in Treasuries. The incentive to take equity risk declines. Retail and institutional investors both trim equity exposure.
- **Look at the data:** In periods of sustained high rates, equity trading volumes typically compress, not expand.

#### **4. The cascading effect on valuations:**

Here's the critical insight the bull is missing: **Higher rates compress multiples, which reduces the value of NDAQ's customer base.**

- Nasdaq's customers are primarily equity holders. When their assets decline in value (due to multiple compression from higher rates), they have LESS to invest and trade.
- **NDAQ's pricing power is tied to the value of assets flowing through its platforms.** If that value declines 20-30% (which is realistic in a significant multiple compression scenario), NDAQ's revenue doesn't just stagnate — it declines.

This is the **hidden leverage** in NDAQ's business model that the bull completely misses.
