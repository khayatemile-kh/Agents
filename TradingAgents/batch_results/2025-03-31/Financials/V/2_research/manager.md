Even backing out buyback effects, 14% underlying net income growth on high-90s incremental margins is still a good business. On valuation, the bear's argument that a 24x forward PE only 'implies' 16-17% growth is a modeling assumption, not a fact, and the peer comparison actually undercuts the bear case: Visa trades at a discount to Mastercard, its direct duopoly peer, despite comparable structural advantages, which argues Visa is not stretched relative to the closest comparable. The bear's regulatory and fintech-disruption threats were asserted but not substantiated with evidence in this debate, whereas the bull directly addressed why fintech/blockchain volume actually flows over Visa's rails rather than replacing them. Given the moat, margin trajectory, and reasonable relative valuation versus the one true peer, the bull's arguments are more evidence-based and decisive. This warrants a constructive Buy stance, while acknowledging position sizing should respect the fact that the stock is near 52-week highs and deceleration risk in headline revenue growth is real and should be monitored.
