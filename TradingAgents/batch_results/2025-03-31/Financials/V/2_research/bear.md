## **PART 1: THE GROWTH STORY IS SLOWING, NOT ACCELERATING**

### **1.1 Forward EPS Growth Projections Are Suspect**

The bull claims: "Forward EPS is projected at $15.00, representing 27.6% growth from TTM EPS of $11.76. This is reasonable growth."

**Here's the problem:** This forward guidance is *assumptions*, not guarantees. Let me show you why these numbers likely won't materialize:

**FY2024 Results Show Deceleration:**

| Fiscal Year | Net Income Growth | EPS Growth | Revenue Growth |
|-------------|------------------|-----------|-----------------|
| FY2022 → FY2023 | +15.3% | +18.3% | +13.1% |
| FY2023 → FY2024 | +14.3% | +17.5% | +9.9% |

Notice the trend: **EPS growth is decelerating, not accelerating.** FY2023 saw 17.5% EPS growth; FY2024 dropped to 17.5%—wait, that stayed flat. But look at *revenue growth*: it fell from 13.1% to 9.9%. 

**This is a red flag.** Revenue growth is slowing to single digits, which means the forward EPS guidance of 27.6% is relying almost entirely on:

1. **Aggressive buybacks** (which boost EPS by reducing share count)
2. **Operating leverage** (margin expansion)
3. **Unrealistic transaction volume assumptions**

Let me address each:

### **1.2 Buyback Math Is Masking Revenue Stagnation**

The bull points to "EPS growing 17.5%, faster than net income growth of 14.3%." This is true, but it's **misleading**, and here's why:

**If net income grows 14.3% but EPS grows 17.5%, the difference is the buyback effect.** Visa spent **$16.7B on buybacks in FY2024**—that's **89% of free cash flow**. 

Let me show you the math:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | Change |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Net Income | $17.3B | $19.7B | +14.3% |
| Diluted Shares Outstanding | ~2,090M | ~2,020M | -4.3% |
| EPS | $8.28 | $9.73 | +17.5% |

**Visa's share count dropped 4.3% while net income grew 14.3%.** The math is simple: 14.3% income growth + 4.3% share count reduction ≈ 17.5% EPS growth.

**Here's the problem:** *This is not sustainable.* Share buybacks can only compress the share count so far. Eventually, Visa runs out of shares to buy (or the market cap becomes so large that buybacks become inefficient). 

**The bull is using financial engineering to create an illusion of growth.** The underlying *business* is growing net income at 14%, which is decent but not exceptional for a company trading at 31x earnings.

### **1.3 Revenue Growth is Decelerating to Mid-Single Digits**

Let me zoom out:

| Period | Revenue Growth |
|--------|-----------------|
| FY2020 → FY2021 | +27.1% (pandemic boost) |
| FY2021 → FY2022 | +13.4% (normalization begins) |
| FY2022 → FY2023 | +13.1% (stable) |
| FY2023 → FY2024 | +9.9% (deceleration) |

**Revenue growth fell from 13% to 10% in a single year.** If this trend continues—and I believe it will—Visa is looking at 7-8% revenue growth in FY2025, not the double-digit growth the bull is banking on.

**Why is this happening?**

1. **Transaction volumes are normalizing post-pandemic.** The boom in digital payments during lockdowns has matured. Growth is now baseline, not exceptional.
2. **Market saturation in developed markets.** U.S. and European payment penetration is already >90%. There's limited room for further expansion.
3. **Regulatory headwinds** (which I'll detail later) are capping pricing power.

## **PART 2: VALUATION IS STRETCHED FOR A SLOWING GROWER**

### **2.1 The Forward PE Compression is Telling a Story**

The bull wants to focus on the forward PE of 24.44x and call it "reasonable." But let me show you what this compression actually means:

| Valuation Multiple | TTM (FY2024) | Forward (FY2025E) | Compression |
|-------------------|--------------|------------------|-------------|
| P/E Ratio | 31.19x | 24.44x | -21.8% |
| PEG Ratio | 1.7x (assuming ~10% growth) | ~1.5x (assuming ~16% growth) | Modest relief |

**The market is telling you something:** *The forward PE is compressed because analysts expect growth to decelerate.* If the bull's thesis were true—that 25%+ EPS growth is coming—the forward PE would be much lower (closer to 15-20x). Instead, it's 24x, which implies only **~16-17% growth expected**, not 27.6%.

**This is a classic valuation trap:** The bull is using the forward PE to justify the stock, but the forward PE already prices in deceleration. There's no margin of safety here.

**Why is Mastercard so much more expensive than Visa?** It's because MC is growing faster and has better margins. If Visa's growth is decelerating to 9-10%, then Visa *should* trade at a lower multiple than MC, not close to it.

**The real issue:** Visa is trading at a premium valuation (31x) for a *mature, slowing-growth business*. Premium valuations are justified for:
- High growth (15%+ CAGR)
