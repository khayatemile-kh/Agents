## **1. THE EARNINGS GROWTH ASSUMPTIONS ARE BUILT ON SHIFTING SAND**

Let's start with the bull's centerpiece claim: **41% forward EPS growth to $6.15 in 2025.**

**Here's my problem with this:** Analyst forward estimates are notoriously unreliable, especially for cyclical consumer discretionary stocks trading through periods of macro uncertainty. And I can prove it.

**The Real Issue: Revenue Growth is Already Decelerating**

Look at the revenue trajectory:
- 2023: +18.03% growth ($9.9B → $11.1B)
- 2024: +11.99% growth ($11.1B)
- **TTM: $13.159B suggests a 18.5% run-rate, but this is inflated by COVID-comparison effects wearing off**

The bull celebrates the 770 bps margin expansion as "operational leverage firing." **But here's what's actually happening:** ABNB is achieving margin expansion by:

1. **Modest cost-cutting** (slower hiring, reduced R&D investment relative to revenue)
2. **One-time benefits** (leveraging fixed costs across a larger revenue base from 2024)
3. **Peak-season mix effects** (higher-margin peak seasons pull up blended margins)

None of these are sustainable indefinitely. When the revenue growth *starts* to decelerate—which it already is—that margin expansion reverses quickly.

**The Forward EPS Estimate Assumes What?**

For $6.15 EPS in 2025 (up 41% from TTM $4.38):
- Revenue needs to grow from $13.1B (TTM) to approximately $15.0B+ (assuming normalized margins)
- That implies **14-15% revenue growth in 2025**

**Does that seem reasonable in a macro environment where:**
- Consumer discretionary spending is tightening?
- The Fed is still holding rates at 5%+ (data uncertainty)?
- Forward bookings are showing early signs of softness?

I'm skeptical. And so should investors be. When ABNB reports Q1 2025 results, and forward guidance misses these aggressive EPS growth assumptions, the stock re-rates lower.

## **4. SEASONALITY IS REAL, BUT IT'S NOT A TAILWIND IN A RECESSION**

The bull argues: *Q2/Q3 are strong seasons, Easter bookings provide visibility, spring travel ramps.*

**I concede the seasonality is real. But it's not a tailwind—it's a headwind if macro conditions deteriorate.**

**Here's Why:**

Seasonal strength assumes **consumer discretionary spending remains healthy**. But what if it doesn't?

- **Credit card delinquencies are rising** (not falling)
- **Consumer savings rates are declining** as inflation eroded purchasing power
- **Credit card balances are near all-time highs** relative to disposable income
- **Mortgage rates remain elevated**, constraining disposable income for travel

In a recession or consumer spending slowdown:
- Easter 2025 bookings might be **flat or negative** vs. Easter 2024
- Peak season pricing power collapses (occupancy falls first, then prices)
- Nightly rates compress as hosts compete for demand

**The bull is betting on a benign macro environment where seasonality plays out normally. I'm betting on early signs of consumer stress.** In a downturn, ABNB's exposure to discretionary travel becomes a massive liability.
