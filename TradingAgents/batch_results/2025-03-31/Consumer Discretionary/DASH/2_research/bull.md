## THE CORE BULL ARGUMENT: Three Pillars

### **PILLAR 1: THE PROFITABILITY INFLECTION IS REAL AND SUSTAINABLE**

Let's start with the most important fact: **DoorDash has turned profitable.**

After years of losses—$(1.365)B in 2022, $(558)M in 2023—the company posted **$123M in net income in 2024** and achieved a positive **1.1% net margin**. But here's what really matters: look at the *trajectory*.

- **2022:** -20.7% net margin
- **2023:** -6.5% net margin  
- **2024:** +1.1% net margin

That's a **21.8 percentage-point swing in net margin in just two years**. This isn't noise—this is a fundamental business inflection.

And the operating margin? It improved from **-15.7% (2022)** to **-0.4% (2024)**. The company is *right on the edge* of sustainable operating profitability. One more solid year of execution and DASH should be in the 2-4% operating margin range, which is perfectly respectable for a high-volume marketplace business.

**Why does this matter?** It proves management can execute on unit economics. It proves the business model works when scaled efficiently. And critically, it means we're not looking at a perpetually-loss-making tech story—we're looking at a **company that has already solved its path to profitability**.

### **PILLAR 2: CASH GENERATION IS EXCEPTIONAL AND GROWING**

Here's a fact that rarely gets mentioned in bear arguments: **DASH generated $5.15B in free cash flow on $10.72B in revenue**. 

Let me repeat that: **48% FCF conversion on revenue.**

For context, that's exceptional. That's better than most mature payment processors. That's better than FedEx. That's *better than most enterprise SaaS companies*.

And here's the kicker—**operating cash flow was $6.10B in 2024**, which means the company is barely spending on capital expenditures ($945M). The delivery network scales with independent contractors (Dashers), not owned assets. This is a *capital-light model that generates enormous free cash*.

**Cash balance:** $4.02B, up 51% year-over-year. **Working capital:** $2.95B and improving. The company has fortress-like balance sheet strength despite carrying some capital leases.

Why does this matter? Because:
1. **It de-risks the stock.** A company generating $5B+ in FCF can weather recessions, invest in growth, return capital to shareholders, or make acquisitions without diluting shareholders.
2. **It proves the market is pricing in zero growth optionality.** If DASH maintains current margins and grows revenue just 10% annually, FCF scales proportionally. The market isn't pricing that in.
3. **It provides a valuation floor.** At $5.15B FCF, even at a conservative 15x multiple, DASH should be worth $77B in enterprise value. We're at $86.85B market cap. That's a *2-3% upside from DCF alone*, not counting growth.
