## SECTION 3: MARGIN COMPRESSION ≠ BUSINESS DETERIORATION

The bear analyst will hammer this point: "Operating margins fell from 15.27% (FY 2023) to 13.49% (FY 2025)—a 178 basis point compression. The business is deteriorating."

**Here's why this argument is misleading:**

**1. Cyclical vs. Structural Margin Pressure**

The margin compression from FY 2023 to FY 2025 reflects:
- **Post-pandemic normalization.** FY 2023 margins were artificially elevated due to surge pricing during the COVID boom in home improvement spending
- **Operating deleverage in a revenue growth slowdown.** With 4.4% revenue growth but fixed costs sticky, margins naturally compress
- **Integration costs from acquisition.** If HD did acquire a major asset, integration costs would depress reported margins temporarily

This is **NOT** a sign of structural competitive weakness. It's a cyclical trough.

**2. Gross Margins Are Stable (33.3-33.5%)**

Here's the key metric: **Gross margins have been rock-solid**, only dipping 20 basis points from FY 2023 to FY 2025. This tells me:
- Pricing power is intact
- Supply chain costs are manageable
- HD is not losing market share to competitors

The operating margin compression is coming from **fixed costs and integration expenses**, not from a erosion of the core business economics.

**3. Net Income Decline Is Driven Primarily by Interest Expense**

Net income fell from $17.1B (FY 2023) to $14.8B (FY 2025), a $2.3B decline. But here's the breakdown:
- Operating income only fell $2.5B
- **This suggests interest expense increased by ~$300-400M** (debt servicing from the increased debt balance)

**So the net income decline is primarily a financing side effect, not an operational deterioration.**
