## **REBUTTAL TO PILLAR 1: "OPERATIONAL EXCELLENCE"—THIS IS THE PEAK, NOT THE FOUNDATION**

### **The 55% Earnings Growth is a Classic Cycle Peak, Not Sustainable**

The bull is celebrating 2024's 55% net income growth—from $797M to $1.23B. This is precisely the moment I'd be *selling*, not buying. Here's why:

**2024 was the recovery year from pandemic suppression.** Let me show you the trajectory:

| **Year** | **Revenue** | **Net Income** | **EPS** | **Net Margin** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2022** | $11.67B | Unknown* | TBD* | ~8.5% est. |
| **2023** | $12.84B | $797M | ~$5.78 | 6.2% |
| **2024** | $13.69B | $1.23B | $8.95 | 9.0% |
| **Growth (2023-2024)** | +6.6% | +55% | +55% | +240 bps |

This massive earnings expansion happened on *only* 6.6% revenue growth. That's not operational excellence; **that's margin expansion from a depressed base.** When you grow earnings 55% on 6.6% revenue growth, you're extracting temporary operating leverage—the low-hanging fruit of cost-cutting and efficiency. That's *not* repeatable.

**The critical question:** Where does EXPE grow from here?

- **Booking volumes** are tied to consumer discretionary spending, which is under pressure
- **Commission rates** have already been normalized post-pandemic; there's limited room for further expansion
- **Unit economics** can only be optimized so much before you hit saturation

The bull is showing me 2024's *inflection point*—the peak of the recovery cycle—and calling it "sustainable operational excellence." **That's backwards. Peaks are where you sell, not buy.**

### **The Gross Margin Story Masks Pricing Pressure**

Yes, 89.5% gross margins are excellent. But let's dig deeper into what's driving them and whether they're sustainable:

**The Real Issue: Commission Rate Pressure**

Expedia operates on a commission model—typically 15-25% of booking value depending on the supplier. But here's what the bulls won't tell you: **the travel marketplace is becoming more competitive and commoditized.**

- **Direct booking**: Hotels and airlines are increasingly bypassing OTAs (online travel agencies) and driving customers directly to their websites with loyalty incentives
- **Metasearch competition**: Google Hotels, Kayak, and other free comparison tools are fragmenting the traffic pool
- **Supplier power**: Major hotel chains (Marriott, Hilton, IHG) have consolidated market share and are negotiating harder on commissions

The 89.5% gross margin reflects *current* commission structures, but those are under pressure. A 200-300 basis point compression in commission rates wouldn't be shocking in a more competitive environment—and that would obliterate EXPE's margin story.

**Real-world example:** In 2023-2024, did EXPE raise prices or improve commission rates? Or did they just benefit from pent-up travel demand post-pandemic? I'd argue it's the latter, and that's not a moat—that's a temporary tailwind.

## **REBUTTAL TO PILLAR 2: THE VALUATION IS A TRAP, NOT A GIFT**

### **Forward P/E of 11.3x Assumes Growth That Won't Happen**

Here's where the bull's logic collapses. They say: "11.3x forward P/E is cheap because EXPE is growing earnings."

**But cheap relative to what?** And based on what growth assumptions?

Let me deconstruct the forward P/E:

**Forward EPS = $24.38** (per the fundamentals report)  
**Current Price = $166.15**  
**Forward P/E = 11.26x**

Now, what does this $24.38 forward EPS assume? It assumes:

1. **Revenue growth continuation** in the 5-8% range
2. **Margin maintenance** at current elevated levels (89.5% gross, 18.98% operating)
3. **No recession or travel demand shock**
4. **No competitive pressure on commissions**

**But none of these assumptions are safe.** Let me challenge each:

#### **Assumption 1: Revenue Growth Slowing Materially**

The **93% prediction market probability of NO Fed rate cuts in 2026** is a critical data point the bull glosses over. Higher-for-longer rates will compress consumer discretionary spending. Travel is *the first luxury to cut* when consumers face financial pressure.

Real-world evidence:
- **Credit card delinquencies** are rising (Fed data)
- **Consumer savings rates** have normalized downward from pandemic highs
- **Auto loan delinquencies** are at 7-year highs (indicating broader consumer stress)

If consumer travel bookings slow to 1-2% growth (or turn negative) in 2025-2026, the forward earnings estimate of $24.38 is **fantasy**. Let's model it:

| **Scenario** | **Revenue Growth** | **Operating Margin** | **Net Income** | **EPS** | **P/E on Current Price** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull Case** | +5% | 18.98% | $1.65B | $24.38 | 11.3x |
| **Base Case** | +2% | 17.5% | $1.12B | $16.50 | 16.8x |
| **Bear Case** | -3% | 15.0% | $0.58B | $8.54 | 32.1x |

**If travel demand weakens just moderately, the forward P/E explodes to 32x, not 11x.** The bull is pricing in a best-case scenario and calling it a valuation discount.

#### **Assumption 2: Margin Compression is Inevitable**

I already touched on commission pressure. But let's add another layer: **higher labor costs and tech investment.**

Expedia's competitive position is eroding against:
- **Google's vertical integration** (Google Hotels with direct booking capability)
- **Booking Holdings** (competitor with 50%+ market share in some regions)
- **AI-powered travel assistants** (ChatGPT plugins, Gemini integration) threatening the need for dedicated platforms

To compete, EXPE is forced to spend on:
- **AI/ML capabilities** (R&D costs rising)
- **Marketing and customer acquisition** (CAC inflation)
- **Mobile and UX improvements** (competitive necessity)

Operating margins *compress* during intensifying competition, not expand. The 18.98% operating margin is likely peak, not sustainable.

#### **Assumption 3: No Recession**

The market is currently pricing in a "soft landing" scenario for the U.S. economy. But the 93% "no Fed cuts" probability suggests investors are *bracing* for extended tightness and potential stress.

Travel demand is **highly cyclical**. In 2020 (COVID), EXPE's revenue collapsed. In 2008-2009 (Financial Crisis), travel was decimated. If we get even a *mild* recession in 2025-2026, EXPE's earnings could fall 40-50%—making today's "11.3x valuation" look like a value trap at 25-30x on normalized
