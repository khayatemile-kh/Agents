**Rationale**: Both sides land real punches, and the honest conclusion is that the evidence is genuinely mixed rather than a clean win for either camp.

Enterprise AI transformation spend is a real multi-year secular tailwind, and ACN's breadth across strategy, implementation and managed services gives it more surface area to capture that spend than a pure play.

Bear's strongest points: The 25.04x debt-to-equity ratio is a legitimate red flag, not something to be waved away with "service companies lever more" — the bear's peer comps (IBM ~1.5x, Infosys/TCS <0.5x) are the more relevant benchmark for a services firm, and they correctly note that ROE inflated by a shrunken equity base isn't the same quality signal as Apple/Microsoft-style ROE. The bear also correctly flags that the market's 16.95% forward EPS growth assumption is unconfirmed — there's no recent guidance update, no fresh news, and a stock that fell 27% in eight weeks typically did so because the market is repricing growth expectations downward, not because of an irrational panic in a vacuum. The competitive-crowding argument (McKinsey/BCG on the high end, hyperscalers subsidizing AI consulting to sell cloud, boutique AI shops on the specialized end) is a credible structural risk to margin and share, even if overstated. Technically, the bear's point that RSI 36 is mid-range rather than panic-oversold, and that MACD is still deeply negative, tempers the bull's reversal call — this looks more like consolidation at a lower base than a confirmed bottom.

Net: the bull's valuation/cash-flow argument has merit and the sell-off does look overdone on a pure multiple basis, but the balance sheet leverage question and the total absence of confirming fundamental news to justify a rebound mean I can't commit capital aggressively here. Neither side has decisively won — this is a case where discipline says wait for confirmation (debt/leverage clarity, earnings/guidance news, or a cleaner technical reversal) rather than take a strong directional stance in either direction.
