**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull's case rests almost entirely on a single number: forward EPS of $15.51 implying a 295% earnings jump and a "cheap" PEG of 0.49. The bear's rebuttal is the more rigorous read of the same facts: that forward estimate is a consensus forecast, not a fact, and semiconductor consensus estimates have a documented history of being wrong at cycle inflection points. The bear also correctly deconstructs the 2024 margin expansion story—showing it's largely inventory normalization and mix-shift that has likely run its course, not a structural re-rating, while foundry (TSMC) costs are rising and Intel/Nvidia competitive dynamics argue for ASP compression rather than continued expansion. On competitive positioning, the bull's 30-40% AI-accelerator share thesis for AMD is not well supported: CUDA lock-in is a real, structural moat (retraining costs, code rewrites, validation cycles) that is fundamentally different from generic 'no one holds 90% share forever' arguments, and current AMD accelerator share is only 3-5% vs Nvidia's ~90%. The bull's balance-sheet and FCF points (fortress balance sheet, $2.4B FCF, de-leveraging) are legitimate positives and not really contested, but they don't resolve the core disagreement, which is whether the growth priced into forward estimates will actually show up. The technical picture is also genuinely mixed — down 37% from highs, RSI recovering from oversold, MACD turning positive — which is consistent with either a bottoming process or a bear-market bounce inside a broader downtrend; the bull's 'capitulation is over' read is optimistic but not obviously wrong, so I don't want to fully dismiss it. Given that the entire bull valuation argument depends on unverified forward estimates that the bear has given concrete, cycle-specific reasons to distrust, and given that the competitive moat argument (CUDA lock-in vs. an ambitious share-gain assumption) favors the bear, I lean toward the bear side but not to full Sell conviction — AMD still has real fundamental strengths (revenue growth, margin improvement already achieved, de-leveraging, FCF generation) that argue against outright exiting. The right call is a cautious, underweight stance: trim exposure into strength, don't add on the "cheap PEG" narrative, and wait for actual earnings prints to validate or invalidate the consensus forward EPS trajectory before re-engaging aggressively.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing AMD exposure by roughly 20-30% into any bounce toward the $110-120 range, rather than adding on the "cheap forward PE / low PEG" argument, since that thesis depends entirely on a consensus forward EPS ($15.51) that the bear has shown is exactly the kind of number that has been unreliable at past semiconductor inflection points. 2) Do not initiate new/incremental long positions until at least one or two quarters of actual results confirm the assumed acceleration in revenue growth (from 13.7% toward the 25-30%+ implied by consensus) and continued margin expansion beyond the 2024 inventory-normalization/mix-shift benefit. 3) Keep a reduced core position (do not go to zero) to retain exposure to the legitimate positives — de-leveraging balance sheet, improving FCF ($2.41B, 9.3% FCF margin), and real (if modest) AI data-center CPU/accelerator traction — in case the bull's margin/growth thesis is validated by upcoming prints. 4) Use options or tight stop levels below recent technical support (near the 37%-drawdown lows) to protect the remaining position against further downside if MACD/RSI stabilization fails to hold and the sell-off resumes. 5) Watch two concrete triggers to reassess: (a) any evidence of AMD gaining meaningful (>1-2 pts/quarter) AI-accelerator market share against Nvidia's CUDA-locked ~90% share, and (b) confirmation that gross/operating margins continue expanding beyond the one-time inventory-normalization boost — either would justify moving back to Hold/Overweight; continued margin flatness or ASP compression from Nvidia/Intel competition would justify moving further toward Sell.
