## THE FUNDAMENTAL BEAR THESIS: VALUATION DISCONNECT FROM REALITY

### **1. The Forward P/E Illusion: 89.7% EPS Growth Is Fantasy**

The bull analyst makes a critical sleight-of-hand argument here. Let me expose it:

**The Bull's Claim:**
- TTM EPS: $5.03
- Forward EPS: $9.54
- Implied growth: 89.7%
- Valuation: "Reasonable" at 29.77x forward P/E

**The Bear's Reality Check:**

Here's what the bull is asking you to believe: **CDNS will nearly *double* earnings in the next 12 months.** This isn't a modest acceleration—this is an earnings explosion that would require either:

1. **Revenue to surge 80%+ YoY**, OR
2. **Operating margins to expand dramatically**, OR  
3. **A combination of both**

Let's examine the probability of either scenario:

**Can Revenue Really Grow 80%+?**

The bull points to TTM revenue of $5.84B as evidence of acceleration. But let's be precise about what's actually happening:

| Period | Revenue | YoY Growth |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| 2022 | $3.56B | — |
| 2023 | $4.09B | +14.9% |
| 2024 | $4.64B | +13.5% |
| TTM (Q4'24 annualized) | $5.84B | +25.8% (calculated) |

**Wait. The bull is citing TTM growth of 25.8%, but this is misleading.** Here's why:

The TTM figure includes Q2, Q3, and Q4 of 2024 plus Q1 2025 (if available). If the company reported $5.84B annualized, that means recent quarterly run rates are running around $1.46B per quarter. But this doesn't mean the company is growing 80%+ to achieve $9.54 forward EPS.

**Let me show you the real math:**

If CDNS achieves a realistic **30-35% revenue growth** (a stretch, but let's be generous) in the next 12 months:
- Base 2024 revenue: $4.64B
- +32% growth: $6.12B (still below the TTM $5.84B annualized figure, which already reflects recent acceleration)

**Even with aggressive 32% revenue growth, we only reach $6.1B in revenue.** This is *incompatible* with the 89.7% EPS growth the market is pricing in.

**Can Margins Really Expand 50-80 bps to Drive EPS Growth?**

The bull argues that operating leverage and cost synergies will expand margins. Let's scrutinize this:

| Year | Operating Margin | Net Margin |
|------|------------------|-----------|
| 2022 | 30.1% | 23.8% |
| 2023 | 32.2% | 25.4% |
| 2024 | 31.7% | 22.7% |

**Notice what actually happened in 2024?** Operating margin *compressed* from 32.2% to 31.7% (a 50 bps decline). Net margin *collapsed* from 25.4% to 22.7% (a 270 bps decline). This is the opposite of the margin expansion story the bull is selling.

**Why did margins compress in 2024?**
- Gross margin fell from 89.4% (2023) to 86.0% (2024) — a 340 bps decline
- This suggests product mix shift, pricing pressure, or increased hardware/support costs
- Operating expenses as a % of revenue remained flat, so the margin compression came from gross profit deterioration

**The forward 89.7% EPS growth requires margin expansion that the company has been unable to deliver over the past year.** This is a credibility problem for the bull thesis.

### **2. The PEG Ratio False Comfort: 2.21x Is Not "Reasonable"**

The bull argues: "A PEG of 2.21x indicates modest premium pricing, but hardly bubble territory."

**Let me contextually destroy this argument:**

**First, what is a "normal" PEG ratio?**

A PEG of 1.0 means the stock trades at its growth rate (fair value). A PEG of 1.0–1.5 is considered "fair to slightly premium." A PEG above 2.0 **signals significant overvaluation relative to growth.**

**But here's the critical issue: the PEG calculation is only as good as the growth assumption.**

The bull's PEG of 2.21x is based on forward EPS growth of 89.7%. **But we just established that 89.7% growth is highly unlikely.** Let's recalculate the PEG with *realistic* growth assumptions:

**Scenario 1: Conservative 25% EPS Growth**
- Forward P/E: 29.77x
- Growth rate: 25%
- **PEG ratio: 29.77 / 25 = 1.19x**

That sounds reasonable! But wait...

**Scenario 2: Realistic 35% EPS Growth** (a meaningful acceleration from 2024)
- Forward P/E: 29.77x
- Growth rate: 35%
- **PEG ratio: 29.77 / 35 = 0.85x**

Hmm, that's *undervalued*! 

**Scenario 3: What Growth Rate Would Justify 29.77x?**
- Forward P/E: 29.77x
- PEG at 1.5x (fair value): 29.77 / 1.5 = 19.85% growth needed

**For the stock to be "fairly valued" at a forward P/E of 29.77x, CDNS needs to sustain 20% EPS growth indefinitely.** But the company's *revenue* growth is in the 13-26% range. For EPS to grow 20%+ consistently, margins would need to expand *every single year*—which they've failed to do in 2024.

**The PEG ratio is not your friend here; it's obscuring the reality that the market has priced in perfection.**

**Neither is true.** CDNS is a mature, profitable software company with predictable (not exponential) growth. The 48.6x FCF multiple is excessive.
