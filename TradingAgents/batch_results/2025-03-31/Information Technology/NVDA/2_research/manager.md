**Recommendation**: Buy

**Rationale**: Weighing both sides, the bull case rests on hard, already-reported financials: 114% revenue growth to $130.5B, gross margins expanding to 75%, operating margins to 62.4%, net income up 145% to $72.9B, ROE of 117%, and operating cash flow of $87.3B (66% of revenue)—all backed by a net-cash balance sheet with $43.2B in liquid assets and debt/equity of only 0.126. These are facts, not projections, and they demonstrate NVIDIA already monetizes the AI buildout at extraordinary efficiency with real pricing power (CUDA lock-in, 3.5M+ developers). The bear case, by contrast, is almost entirely forward-looking and speculative: it argues 114% growth can't continue (true, but a straight-line extrapolation to $450B by FY2027 is a strawman nobody is actually underwriting), it flags AMD MI300X and custom silicon as emerging threats (real but early-stage and unproven at scale—no hard share-loss data provided), and it reinterprets rising inventory and cash reserves as red flags without any concrete evidence (e.g., no DSI deterioration, no write-downs, no gross-margin miss) to support that spin over the more straightforward reading of building supply to meet demand. The bear's strongest point—that decelerating growth and margin compression are the natural next phase after this kind of hypergrowth—is a real risk worth monitoring, but it argues for tempering the pace of position growth, not for abandoning the thesis, especially given the company is trading with a ~3%+ FCF yield off a de-risked, high-quality balance sheet. The prediction-market argument cuts both ways and I'm not leaning on it as a primary driver either way. Net: the current 22.7% pullback looks more like a valuation/technical reset within a still-intact structural growth story than a broken thesis. Bulls win on the weight of verified fundamentals versus bears' largely speculative competitive-erosion narrative.
