## SECTION 2: THE BULL'S FINANCIAL METRICS ARE MISLEADING AND CONTEXT-FREE

Now let me directly challenge the bull's fundamental arguments. **The numbers sound impressive until you actually scrutinize them.**

### **EBITDA Growth ≠ Earnings Quality**

The bull celebrates 75.8% EBITDA growth and 131% EBIT growth. This sounds amazing. But let me ask the critical question: **Where did this growth come from, and is it sustainable?**

Here's what the bull conveniently glossed over:

**Revenue grew 22.4%. Operating expenses grew 16.6%. The margin expansion is real, but it's coming from *operating leverage*, not from core business improvement.**

What does that mean? It means:
1. **Operating expenses are becoming a smaller percentage of revenue** (good), BUT
2. **The company is *not* investing at the same growth rate as revenue** (could be a problem)

This is the classic "squeeze the golden goose" scenario. ServiceNow invested heavily in R&D and sales in prior years. Now those investments are mattering *less* because the company isn't accelerating R&D proportionally.

**Here's the risk: In a software market where AI is a competitive necessity, under-investing in R&D relative to revenue growth is dangerous.** When competitors like Salesforce, Microsoft, and Oracle ramp AI capabilities, ServiceNow needs to match that investment—which will *compress* margins again.

The 75.8% EBITDA growth is a *one-time* benefit from prior investments compounding. It's not a repeatable feature of the business.
