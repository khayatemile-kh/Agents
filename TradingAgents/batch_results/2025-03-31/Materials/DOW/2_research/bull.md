## **PART 1: THE FUNDAMENTAL RECOVERY NARRATIVE**

### **The TTM Loss Masks a Cyclical Rebound**

The bear case will point to DOW's negative TTM net income of -$1.30 billion. I get it—that's a red flag at first glance. But here's what's critical: **this TTM figure is backward-looking and heavily weighted by 2024's trough**. 

Let me break down what's actually happening:

| Year | Net Income | EPS | Margin |
|------|-----------|-----|--------|
| **2022** | $4.58B | $6.28 | 8.0% |
| **2023** | $0.59B | $0.82 | 1.3% |
| **2024** | $1.12B | $1.57 | 2.6% |
| **Forward Est. (2025)** | N/A | $1.84 | ~3.0%+ |

**See the trajectory?** The company *bottomed* in 2023 and is *recovering* in 2024. The TTM metric includes the worst of Q1-Q3 2024, but **Q4 2024 and Q1 2025 are showing green shoots**. Forward EPS of $1.84 represents a **17% improvement** from 2024's $1.57. This isn't a company in free fall—it's a company recovering from a cyclical downturn.

**Why the recovery?** Chemicals are cyclical. DOW operates in sectors tied to construction, automotive, and consumer goods. These industries faced headwinds in 2023-2024 but are stabilizing. The prediction markets show only an 8% recession probability for 2026—that's a bullish signal for industrial demand.

Now, let's talk about what matters most: **cash flow and capital discipline**.

Despite the temporary earnings dip, DOW's board maintained a **dividend of $1.97 billion in 2024** (4.76% yield). This is not a company in distress—this is management signaling they expect normalization.

Here's the critical insight: **In a true crisis, the dividend gets cut.** Yet DOW's board kept it steady. Why? Because they see the cyclical trough as temporary. And frankly, **I believe them more than I believe the bear thesis** because:

1. **They have skin in the game** (management compensation tied to shareholder returns)
2. **They have better forward visibility** than we do as external analysts
3. **The dividend is sustainable** on current FCF with modest improvement expected

At current price (~$32), this 4.76% yield provides a **compounding cushion**. If the company recovers to normalized earnings (which I'll argue below), the dividend likely grows, driving total returns.
