- **2022:** Net loss of -$1.87B
- **2023:** Net loss of -$2.59B (getting worse)
- **2024:** Net income of **+$263M** (turnaround!)
- **TTM (includes Q1 2025):** -$791M (one bad quarter)

Here's the critical insight: **2024 represents a fundamental inflection.** The company went from hemorrhaging money to profitability. That's not a typo—it's a transformation. And yes, Q1 2025 appears weak, but one quarter does not negate a structural recovery.

More importantly, look at **EBITDA**, which strips out non-cash items and restructuring charges:
- **2022:** -$110M
- **2023:** -$996M (massive operational deterioration)
- **2024:** +$1.63B 
- **TTM:** +$1.91B

**That's a $2.6 billion swing in EBITDA in just one year.** That's not accounting magic—that's operational improvement. The company has gotten its costs under control and is generating real operational cash flow.

| Metric | 2024 | 2023 | Trend |

I argue the opposite will happen:

1. **Restructuring is largely complete.** The 2023 impairments and restructuring charges should be behind us.
2. **Operating leverage will expand** as revenue stabilizes and cost synergies flow through.
3. **Working capital normalization.** The Q1 2025 working capital outflow of -$362M appears to be a one-time inventory/receivables issue, not structural.

**Conservative projection:** FCF grows to $1.2-1.5B annually by 2027 as operational efficiency gains materialize. That implies a **normalized FCF yield of 1.5-2.0%**, which is attractive for a specialty chemicals company.
