**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Weighing both sides, the bear case lands more punches on the data actually presented. The bull's core argument—that 2024 marks a durable inflection—rests heavily on a single profitable year sandwiched between two years of multi-billion dollar losses, and that inflection is already being called into question by the TTM figures (net income -$791M, implying a very weak-to-negative Q1 2025 after backing out the 2024 run-rate). The bull dismisses this as 'one bad quarter,' but the magnitude of the swing (roughly $1B+ negative delta) is too large to wave away as noise, and the bear correctly points out that if the recovery were structural, Q1 2025 should have continued the trend, not reversed it sharply.

On cash flow, the bear's read is more consistent with the actual numbers: FCF fell from $952M (2023) to $602M (2024), a 36% decline, and the bull's framing of this as 'still positive, about to accelerate' is unsupported by any evidence in the data—it's a forward-looking assumption, not a trend confirmed by the numbers. Coupled with dividends ($514M) consuming ~85% of that already-shrinking FCF, the coverage cushion is thin, and the TTM deterioration raises real (not hypothetical) questions about dividend sustainability.

On valuation, the bull's PEG argument depends on a forward EPS estimate ($3.96) that implies over 300% earnings growth from a depressed, impairment-affected base—an assumption the bear rightly stress-tests. A 25% miss on that EPS number pushes the effective forward multiple to over 25x, which is rich for a company with flat-to-declining revenue ($12.4B in 2022 to $11.5B in 2024) and margin pressure from input costs. The peer comparison (Symrise, DSM trading at lower multiples with better organic growth/ROIC) reinforces that IFF is not obviously cheap even under the bull's own framework.

The balance sheet argument is a genuine split: the bull is right that net debt and leverage ratios (Debt/EBITDA improving from 6.6x to 5.9x) show a de-leveraging trend, and the 44.8x D/E figure is indeed a distorted metric given negative tangible equity. This is a fair rebuttal and I don't fully credit the bear's use of that headline ratio. However, this balance sheet improvement doesn't offset the more immediate concerns about earnings quality and cash generation deteriorating right as the 'recovery' was supposed to be taking hold.

Net assessment: the bull thesis requires several optimistic assumptions to hold simultaneously (EPS estimate accuracy, FCF reacceleration, restructuring being 'done') while the bear thesis is grounded more directly in the trend data provided (declining FCF, negative TTM net income, thin dividend coverage). That asymmetry—more support for the bear's specific claims, more speculation embedded in the bull's—tips this toward a cautious stance. It's not a full Sell because the de-leveraging trend and non-cash nature of impairments are legitimate mitigants, so I'm not recommending an exit. But the deteriorating cash flow trajectory and questionable earnings quality argue against adding exposure or holding at full weight.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing IFF exposure modestly (10-20% of position) rather than adding at current levels; do not initiate new positions until Q1 2025 cash-flow and working-capital dynamics are confirmed to be transitory rather than structural.
2) Closely monitor the next 1-2 quarterly reports for: (a) whether FCF turns positive again and dividend coverage improves above 1.5x, (b) whether working capital outflows normalize, and (c) whether forward EPS estimates get revised down toward more conservative levels—any of these would be a trigger to reassess toward Hold/Overweight.

4) Set an upside re-entry trigger: sustained (2+ quarters) positive FCF with dividend coverage above 1.5x and confirmed EBITDA/leverage improvement (Debt/EBITDA below 5.5x) would justify moving back to Hold or Overweight.
5) Avoid using current 'recovery' narrative or PEG-based valuation as sole justification for increasing position size—treat the $3.96 forward EPS estimate as a risk factor, not a base case, until confirmed by actual quarterly delivery.
6) Size any residual position conservatively, keeping IFF as a smaller-than-benchmark weight in the portfolio until earnings quality and cash generation trends turn unambiguously positive for at least two consecutive quarters.
