**Recommendation**: Hold

**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points that roughly offset each other. The bull is right that ECL's underlying business quality is strong: normalized EBITDA growth of ~16.5%, expanding gross margins (42.6%→43.5%), a robust FCF margin of 11.6%, improving net debt/EBITDA (1.72x on normalized EBITDA), and a defensible market position in mission-critical hygiene, water treatment and food safety end-markets that carry high switching costs. The technical setup (price above 50/200-day SMAs) also supports near-term stability.

However, the bear correctly flags that a chunk of the headline 54% net income growth is inflated by a one-time $340M divestiture gain and a falling effective tax rate, meaning true operating profit growth is more like 15-18%, not 54% or even 27% EBITDA growth. The bear's point that extreme operating leverage (2.8% revenue growth driving 27% EBITDA growth) is more likely a sign of cost-cutting nearing its limits than a repeatable structural advantage is a fair challenge — this kind of leverage does tend to work in reverse when volumes soften. The debt picture is genuinely mixed: absolute debt has only fallen 8.3% over two years despite strong FCF, and the company continues to prioritize dividends/buybacks (~$1.65B/year) over faster deleveraging, while $11.2B of goodwill/intangibles (50% of assets) does represent real impairment risk if any acquired unit underperforms or growth stalls. That said, the bear's tangible-leverage math is somewhat alarmist — a specialty chemicals franchise with recurring, needs-based revenue isn't comparable to a distressed balance sheet, and the underlying net debt/EBITDA of 1.7x is still investment-grade territory.

Where I come down: the valuation argument is the crux. At 36x trailing earnings, the stock is pricing in continuation of double-digit EBITDA growth that itself is partly an artifact of a one-off gain and tax benefit — normalized growth in the mid-to-high teens is good but doesn't obviously justify a 64% premium to the market multiple, especially with revenue growth stuck at under 3%. At the same time, there's no evidence in this debate of an imminent catalyst for a sharp de-rating — no covenant stress, no confirmed margin reversal yet, and the technical trend remains intact. This is a case of a quality compounder that's priced for a level of perfection the bear has legitimately called into question, but without a clear deteriorating catalyst that would justify actively reducing exposure today. Given the balance of a strong franchise against a full valuation with earnings-quality caveats, the appropriate stance is to stay neutral rather than add or trim aggressively.

**Strategic Actions**: 1. Maintain current position size in ECL; do not add new capital at current levels given the premium valuation is partly supported by non-recurring items (divestiture gain, lower tax rate) that inflate the growth narrative.
2. Do not initiate a short or aggressively trim — the balance sheet, while carrying real goodwill/intangible risk, is not in distress (net debt/EBITDA ~1.7x, current ratio 1.84x, $1.26B cash), and the technical trend remains constructive above the 200-day SMA.
3. Set a watch-list trigger to reassess toward Underweight if: (a) revenue growth stays below 3% for another 1-2 quarters while EBITDA growth decelerates toward high-single digits (confirming the bear's 'peak margin' thesis), or (b) any goodwill impairment charge or debt covenant commentary emerges.
4. Set a trigger to reassess toward Overweight/Buy if normalized (ex-one-offs) EBITDA growth sustains above 12-15% for two more quarters alongside accelerating revenue growth, which would validate the bull's pricing-power/operating-leverage thesis as structural rather than cyclical.
5. Use any pullback toward the 200-day SMA (~$243-244) as an opportunity to add on a scaled basis if fundamentals hold, rather than chasing strength at current multiples.
6. Hedge idiosyncratic risk modestly (e.g., via sector ETF puts or a small collar) given the elevated multiple leaves limited room for multiple compression if growth normalizes to bear-case levels.
