The technical setup they're describing—positive MACD histogram, RSI moving from oversold—these are lagging indicators of capitulation, not leading indicators of sustainable recovery. They tell us that sellers have exhausted themselves, not that buyers have genuine reasons to accumulate. This is exactly when mean-reversion trades get punished. You catch the first bounce, thinking you're smart, and then the stock rolls over again because the original selling pressure returns.

Yes, Walmart delivered 25.3% net income growth year-over-year in fiscal 2025. Yes, EBITDA expanded 15.5%. But let me ask the hard questions: Is this sustainable? Or is this a one-cycle benefit from cost discipline that faces headwinds ahead?

Gross margin ticked up to 24.8% from 24.4%. These are real improvements. But here's the catch: they came during a period when wage pressures haven't yet fully hit retail, when supply chain costs were normalizing, and when Walmart had pricing power.

Now look at what's coming. Overheating probability is 41% and rising. That means:
- Wage inflation for store associates
- Consumer pressure on pricing (people will demand discounts as their real purchasing power erodes)
- Potential margin compression if input costs accelerate
- Inventory management risks if consumer spending softens

The forward 34% EPS growth projection that the aggressive analyst cited? That's analyst consensus. And analyst consensus frequently overestimates earnings growth in inflationary environments, especially for retailers with narrow margins where wage pressure is a direct hit to profitability.

I'm not saying Walmart's earnings will collapse. I'm saying the 34% growth rate embedded in current expectations is almost certainly too optimistic, and when those expectations reset—which they will—the stock will face a re-rating downward. We'd be buying right before that disappointment.

## THE STOP LOSS AT $83 IS SECURITY THEATER

The aggressive analyst kept emphasizing the discipline of a $83 stop loss, representing only 4% portfolio loss if triggered. I appreciate the intellectual honesty here, but I need to point out a critical reality: **stops don't always execute at the price you set them.**

In a market stress scenario—which is exactly when WMT could fall below $83—spreads widen, gaps occur, and your $83 stop might execute at $81 or $79 in a flash crash. We saw this repeatedly in 2020 and again in August 2024. You think you're risking 4%, but you're actually risking 5-6% in adverse market conditions.

More importantly, the stop-loss framing creates a false sense of security. It says "we have defined our risk." But risk in markets isn't just about the size of a loss—it's about the *probability* of hitting that loss and the *opportunity cost* of being wrong. If we take this 5-7% position and it does hit the stop, we've now underperformed the market while bearing execution risk. We'd have been better off waiting for more clarity.

The aggressive analyst is treating the stop as a guarantee of limited downside. In reality, stops are probabilistic tools that work best in trending markets and fail in whipsaw environments. In a flat-to-down market like we might enter if overheating concerns intensify, we could trade sideways between $83-90 for months, locking our capital into a low-return asset while missing opportunities elsewhere.
