## **PART 1: THE VALUATION ARGUMENT IGNORES EARNINGS COLLAPSE**

The bull argues EL trades at 15.6x EBITDA—a "discount" to historical 18-25x multiples for luxury goods companies. **This completely misses the point:** multiples compress when **earnings quality deteriorates and growth stalls.**

### The Multiple Should Be Lower, Not Higher

Let me show you why the 15.6x multiple actually **understates the weakness**:

**The bull's logic:**
- "EL trades at 15.6x EBITDA; luxury companies trade at 18-25x; therefore EL is cheap."

**The reality:**
- EL's **diluted EPS collapsed 61% year-over-year** (from $2.79 in FY2023 to $1.08 in FY2024)
- This is not a cyclical dip—this is structural earnings impairment
- Revenue is *flat* YoY ($15.61B in FY2024 vs. $15.91B in FY2023, a -1.9% decline)
- Yet the bull expects us to pay luxury multiples for a company with **declining revenue and collapsing earnings**

Here's the issue: **EBITDA is a backward-looking metric; earnings are forward-looking.** When a company's normalized earnings (net income, not adjusted EBITDA) have collapsed 61%, you don't value it at historical multiples—you apply a *haircut*.

### The Impairment Argument Is a Red Herring

The bull says the $471M impairment is "non-cash" and doesn't matter. **This is dangerously naive.** Here's why:

1. **Impairments signal deterioration in asset values:** When a company takes a $471M writedown in FY2024 and $207M in FY2023, it's admitting that **the assets it previously valued at higher levels are now worth less.** This is management saying: "We overpaid for these acquisitions/brands, or they're not generating the returns we expected."

2. **Impairments predict future losses:** Assets are impaired when they can't generate sufficient future cash flows. A $471M impairment on a $21.68B asset base (2.2% of assets) suggests management sees **reduced earning power ahead**—not just accounting adjustments.

3. **Non-cash doesn't mean irrelevant:** The bull conflates "non-cash charge" with "doesn't matter." But impairments indicate **real economic losses.** If you overpay for a brand and later write it down, you've destroyed shareholder value—it just took time to show up on the income statement.

### The "Normalized EBITDA" Shell Game

The bull points to "normalized EBITDA of $2.57B" to argue operational health is intact. **Let me expose this:**

- **TTM EBITDA:** $2.46B
- **Net Income (TTM):** $182M
- **Net Margin:** 1.21%

The gap between $2.46B EBITDA and $182M net income is **$2.28B.** That gap includes:
- $2.06B in **capital lease obligations** (cash outflow)
- Interest expense and debt service
- Taxes

The bull wants credit for EBITDA while ignoring the massive gap to **actual earnings**. This is intellectually dishonest. **EBITDA is not cash flow.** With only $182M in net income on $15B in revenue, EL's *true* profit margin is 1.21%—that's barely above breakeven for a $36.8B market cap company.

## **PART 2: THE "IMPROVING FCF" ARGUMENT IGNORES INVENTORY DISTRESS**

The bull celebrates the $3.0B free cash flow swing (FY2023: -$1.56B to FY2024: +$1.44B). **I need to show you what this actually means:**

### The FCF "Improvement" Is Inventory Liquidation, Not Operating Strength

The $1.44B FCF improvement breaks down as follows:

| Driver | Amount | Assessment |
|--------|--------|------------|
| Operating Cash Flow improvement | $0.63B | Genuine operational improvement |
| **Inventory Reduction** | **$0.77B** | **UNSUSTAINABLE—this is liquidation** |
| CapEx reduction | $2.37B | **CAPITAL STARVATION** |
| Total Swing | $3.77B | |

Here's what's really happening:

**Inventory Collapse Is a Distress Signal, Not Discipline:**
- FY2024 inventory: $2.18B
- FY2023 inventory: $2.98B
- **27% reduction in one year**

The bull frames this as "working capital management." **In reality, this signals:**

1. **Demand weakness:** Companies reduce inventory when they can't sell what they have
2. **Distributor pressure:** Retailers are reducing shelf space for EL products or demanding tighter inventory turns
3. **Channel stress:** The massive inventory reduction coincides with declining revenue (-1.9% YoY), suggesting EL is *pushing inventory out at discounts* to generate cash

This is **not a sign of health**—it's a sign of **forced cash generation in a weak demand environment.**

### CapEx Collapse Is Capital Starvation, Not Normalization

The bull dismisses the $2.37B CapEx reduction (from $3.29B to $919M, a 72% cut) as "normalization." **This is wrong.**

- **FY2023 CapEx:** $3.29B (19.6% of revenue)
- **FY2024 CapEx:** $919M (5.9% of revenue)
- **FY2022 (pre-data) context:** A 72% year-over-year CapEx cut is **NOT normalization—it's capital rationing**

When a company cuts CapEx by nearly 3/4 in one year, it's either:
1. Completing a major capstone project (unlikely for a diversified beauty company)
2. **Preserving cash due to financial stress** (most likely)

For a company in the beauty retail space, where DTC expansion and supply chain modernization are *essential*, a 72% CapEx reduction signals **management is prioritizing cash preservation over growth investment.** This is bearish.

### The $947M Dividend Is a Red Flag, Not a Sign of Strength

The bull doesn't mention the $947M in dividend payments in FY2024. Here's why this concerns me:

- **FCF in FY2024:** $1.44B
- **Dividend paid:** $947M (65.7% of FCF)
- **Debt reduction:** Near zero (company is actually refinancing, not deleveraging)

**EL is paying dividends while:**
1. Revenue is declining
2. Earnings are collapsing  
3. Debt levels remain elevated at $9.83B
4. Capital investment is being slashed

This is **unsustainable capital allocation.** The company is prioritizing dividend payments (to support the stock price) over investing in the business. That's a sign of **a mature, shrinking business**, not a healthy one.
