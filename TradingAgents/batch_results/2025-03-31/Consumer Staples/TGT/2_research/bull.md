## **SECTION 2: BALANCE SHEET STRENGTHENING — NOT DETERIORATION**

I anticipate the bear argument will focus on the elevated debt-to-equity ratio (107.58%) and negative working capital. **Fair concerns on the surface, but context matters enormously.**

Yes, TGT carries $19.88B in total debt. But:

3. **Debt-to-Equity context**: Target operates with capital-intensive real estate assets (PPE = $36.79B, or 63.7% of assets). This is not a tech company with intangible assets — this is a retailer that needs to finance physical stores. A D/E of 107% is *normal* and *appropriate* for the sector.

4. **Interest coverage is healthy**: With EBIT of $5.67B and interest expense of only $411M, **interest coverage is 13.8x** — well above the 2.5x minimum for investment grade. TGT's debt is *eminently serviceable*.

### **The Negative Working Capital Non-Issue:**

Yes, working capital is negative at -$1.35B. But this is a **feature, not a bug, for efficient retailers.**

- Retailers collect cash from customers *immediately* (credit card settlements in 1-2 days)
- Retailers pay suppliers on *Net 30-60* terms
- This timing gap creates naturally negative working capital, which is *free financing*

The trend is improving: Working capital deficit narrowed from -$1.8B (FY2024) to -$1.35B (FY2025). That's operational optimization in action.
