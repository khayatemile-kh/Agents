Growing cash hoards alongside rising absolute debt is more consistent with defensive positioning than the confident deleveraging story the bull tells. The bear's point that margin expansion coinciding with revenue contraction is more likely mix-shift/cost-cutting than pricing power is also plausible and unaddressed by the bull with hard evidence (no same-store sales, traffic, or unit volume data was presented to rebut it).

On technicals, the bear's read is also more consistent with the facts presented: a death cross, RSI in oversold territory sustained through multiple failed bounce attempts, and heavy capitulation volume (31M shares) are more typically associated with fundamental deterioration than with a clean technical-only overreaction. The bull's "oversold = buy signal" argument is a generic mean-reversion heuristic without confirmation of a reversal (bounces have occurred on declining volume, which the bull actually cites as bullish but could equally indicate lack of conviction from buyers).

Where the bull is right: this is not a broken business — profitability, cash conversion, and balance sheet servicing capacity remain intact today, and valuation is not demanding. That keeps this from being a high-conviction Sell. But the un-rebutted revenue deceleration versus sector, the cash-hoarding-vs-deleveraging distinction, and the technical death cross/oversold-without-reversal-confirmation combination are enough to tilt toward caution rather than accumulation. I don't see enough evidence of a re-acceleration catalyst to justify adding to or holding steady on this name right now.

Net: this leans bearish-of-neutral. Underweight is the appropriate call — trim/reduce exposure and wait for confirmation (either fundamental proof of stabilizing comps/market share, or a technical trend reversal with rising volume) before considering re-entry, rather than either loading up on the "value" narrative or abandoning the position entirely given the balance sheet is not in crisis.
