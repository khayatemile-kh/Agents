**Operating Cash Flow Trajectory:**

| Period | OCF | Change | Context |
|--------|-----|--------|---------|
| **FY 2022** | $823M | — | Peak capex cycle |

| **Q1 FY2025** | $396M (Q only) | Annualizes to $1.58B | Bull's hopeful read |

**Here's Why the Bull's Q1 Annualization Is Misleading:**

1. **Seasonal Bias:** Q1 (Dec-Jan) is traditionally a weak capex period in manufacturing. Annualizing a Q1 quarter overstates the year because Q2-Q3 (Mar-Aug) are typically stronger seasonally.

2. **FY 2024 Full Picture Shows the Real Trend:**
   - Q1 FY2024: ~$300-350M (estimated)
   - Q2 FY2024: ~$350-400M (estimated)
   - Q3 FY2024: ~$75-150M (weakness emerged)
   - Q4 FY2024: ~$75-150M (severe weakness)

   **The pattern is clear: ROK experienced a sharp OCF cliff in H2 FY2024 that shows no clear sign of reversal.**

3. **Free Cash Flow Tells the Worse Story:**

   | Metric | FY 2023 | FY 2024 | Change |
   |--------|---------|---------|--------|
   | **OCF** | $1,374M | $864M | -37% |

**This is Not Working Capital Timing—This Is Structural:**

- ROK's capex is *increasing* (not a sign of cost-cutting efficiency)
- Shareholder returns are *accelerating* (unsustainably relative to FCF)
- The company is funding the gap with **debt issuance of $674M in FY2024**

**The math is simple:** If FCF is $639M but the company pays out $1.17B in dividends and buybacks, *you're funding the difference with debt.* That's not a sustainable business model—that's financial engineering masking underlying weakness.
