## WHERE THE AGGRESSIVE ANALYST IS RIGHT—AND WHERE THEY OVERREACH

But here's where the Aggressive Analyst makes a critical error: they're treating technical bearishness as predictive of continued downside when what they're actually observing is *past* weakness that's already been priced in. The Aggressive Analyst claims institutional holders haven't finished liquidating, but the evidence contradicts this. A 14.8% decline over three weeks on *moderate* volume, followed by a failed bounce that lasted six days, is not the signature of ongoing institutional distribution—that's the signature of a stock that's repriced risk and is now consolidating.

More importantly, the Aggressive Analyst dismisses the RSI bullish divergence as noise because RSI hasn't crossed 50. This is where they're most dangerously wrong. They're waiting for a confirmation that might never come in the timeframe they expect. Bullish divergence in oversold territory followed by RSI stabilization at 39.87 is textbook early reversal setup. The fact that RSI doesn't need to cross 50 overnight to signal a bottom reversal is basic technical analysis that the Aggressive Analyst is overlooking.

The real problem with the Aggressive case is this: they're extrapolating one bad quarter and one tough fiscal year into a permanent structural decline. Yes, FY2024 was weak. Those aren't the numbers of a company in free fall—those are the numbers of a company experiencing cyclical headwinds.

By the time a death cross forms, much of the repricing has already occurred. More critically, the Conservative Analyst is arguing for holding and waiting for cyclical recovery, but they're not acknowledging the time cost of that thesis. If ISM stays sub-50 for another 18 months—which the Aggressive Analyst correctly notes is entirely possible—then holding ROK at 252.92 means your capital is tied up in a company that's delevering, cutting costs, and generating minimal FCF while the broader market potentially moves elsewhere.
