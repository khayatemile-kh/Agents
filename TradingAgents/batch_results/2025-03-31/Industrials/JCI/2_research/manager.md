Where I come down: the bear's cash-flow/capital allocation critique is the single most concrete, hard-to-dismiss data point in the whole debate — a company overdistributing versus FCF for 2 of 3 years while increasing net debt is a real leverage-trend concern, not merely "financial engineering." However, this isn't yet a going-concern issue: interest coverage remains solid at 5x, the shortfall margin isn't extreme, and the valuation discount to peers plus technical base-building offer real support for the stock at current levels. The deceleration in revenue growth and EPS decline are worth monitoring closely, but they don't yet constitute thesis-breaking evidence — they argue against aggressively adding here, but not against holding.

Net conclusion: this is a name with genuine give-and-take — real leverage/capital-allocation risk on one side, offset by peer-relative valuation support and stabilizing technicals on the other. That balance argues for a Hold rather than committing capital in either direction until the FCF-coverage trend either improves (validating the bull thesis) or worsens further (validating the bear's debt-funded-distribution warning).
