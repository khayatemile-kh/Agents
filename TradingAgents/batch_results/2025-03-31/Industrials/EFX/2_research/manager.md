**Recommendation**: Hold

**Rationale**: Both sides land real punches, and the disagreement is really about interpretation of the same numbers rather than one side having clearly superior facts. The bull is right that FCF conversion has improved dramatically ($132.6M→$813M) and that EV/EBITDA at 14.5x isn't crazy for a mission-critical data/analytics franchise with 30% EBITDA margins. The technical setup (RSI recovering from oversold, MACD histogram turning positive, support building near $232-235) does suggest selling pressure is exhausting, which argues against adding to a bear position aggressively here. But the bear's rebuttal on capital allocation is the most important fact in this whole debate and the bull never actually addressed it: of the $813M FCF generated in 2024, net debt only fell by ~$146M, meaning the bulk of cash went to dividends/buybacks rather than deleveraging. That directly undercuts the bull's core catalyst — the '2.5x leverage in 2 years' timeline — and pushes the real deleveraging path out to 5-6 years assuming no disruption, which is a much weaker near-term catalyst. The bear's point on margin compression is also well documented: EBITDA margin down from 32.8% (2022) to 30.3% (2024), operating income actually down while revenue grew, and 3-year net income down 13.2% despite revenue growth of 10.9% — that's real deterioration in unit economics, not just optics. The CapEx-cut argument is legitimate too: capex intensity fell from ~11% to ~9% of revenue in a business that is 69% intangible assets and needs to keep investing in security/AI/cloud — so some of the FCF 'improvement' is not fully sustainable and could reverse. On the macro side, neither side has decisively won that argument yet — higher-for-longer rates cut both ways for a credit-data business (more scrutiny needed for lending = good for EFX's product relevance, but also higher own interest expense which the bear flagged as compressing net income). Given that the bull's central re-rating catalyst (fast deleveraging) is empirically not happening at the pace claimed, but the company is still cash-generative, has a defensible moat in credit bureau data, and stock is showing early signs of technical stabilization rather than continued breakdown, the balanced call is to hold rather than take a strong directional bet. This isn't a 'set it and forget it' hold — it's a genuinely contested valuation situation where neither bull's optimistic re-rating math nor bear's value-trap thesis has been proven out yet, and the next 1-2 quarters of capex trend, leverage trend, and margin trend will resolve which thesis is correct.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Maintain current position size — do not add aggressively on the bull's re-rating story until there's clear evidence net debt reduction is accelerating beyond the ~$150M/year pace seen in 2024 (want to see two consecutive quarters of net debt reduction materially above that run rate before increasing exposure). 2) Do not cut the position purely on the bear's margin-compression argument either, since EBITDA growth (+9% in 2024) and FCF conversion (95%) are genuinely improving in absolute dollar terms even if margin percentage has compressed slightly — this is a quality business, not a broken one. 3) Set a tight watchlist trigger: if Q1/Q2 2025 capex reverts higher (back toward 11% of revenue) to defend competitive position, treat that as bear-thesis confirmation and consider trimming; if capex stays low AND net debt reduction accelerates meaningfully, treat that as bull-thesis confirmation and consider adding. 4) Use the technical support zone ($230-235) as a risk-management level — if price breaks decisively below $230 on volume, that invalidates the 'bottoming' technical case and would argue for reducing exposure; a confirmed MACD bullish crossover with RSI moving back above 55 would support modestly increasing exposure. 5) Track next earnings call specifically for management commentary on capital allocation priorities (buybacks/dividends vs. debt paydown) since that single data point is the crux of the disagreement and will do more to resolve the thesis than any macro narrative.
