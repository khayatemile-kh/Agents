## **TO THE CONSERVATIVE ANALYST: YOU'RE TREATING WORKING CAPITAL ACCOUNTING AS PERMANENT STRUCTURAL DECLINE**

Defense contractors operate on long-dated contract cycles with milestone billing, not recurring revenue like SaaS companies. When you have a multi-year development program that crosses calendar years, the timing of cash receipts and cash outflows creates natural working capital volatility. A company might generate $5.29B in FCF in Year 1, but $8B in Year 2 if a major development milestone completes and invoices are paid.

LMT's working capital deterioration in 2024 could reflect exactly this: they're in the middle of contract milestones where outflows precede inflows. This is a *timing* issue, not a structural issue. By 2025 or 2026, when those contracts hit deliverables or customer payment schedules normalize, working capital could swing back favorable.

So your conclusion that "LMT is borrowing from the future" might be correct, but the relevant question is: how far into the future? If it's a 1-2 year cycle before cash normalizes, that's not catastrophic. If it's permanent, you're right. But you haven't demonstrated that it's permanent—you've just shown that it's negative in a single year.

Moreover, the fact that LMT is comfortable returning $6.76B to shareholders while carrying $2.8B in net debt issuance suggests management believes the cash flow will stabilize. Are they confident? Maybe overconfident. But they're not panic-mode optimistic either. They're returning capital at a pace that's consistent with long-term dividend and buyback policy, not desperately trying to shore up the stock price.
