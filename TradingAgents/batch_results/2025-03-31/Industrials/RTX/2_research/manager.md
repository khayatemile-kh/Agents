**Recommendation**: Overweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: RTX rode a real, structural tailwind (higher defense budgets, NATO rearmament, commercial aerospace recovery) into a 2024 revenue and earnings surge, and the balance sheet (Debt/EBITDA ~2.7x, net ~2.2x) is manageable for the sector. Where they diverge is on durability of the 2024 inflection and on what's already priced in.

The bear's strongest point is a legitimate one: the 49% net income growth and 83.7% operating income growth on 17% revenue growth in 2024 is an unusually high incremental margin (~25%) that reflects a first-wave scaling/catch-up effect (supply chain normalization, backlog conversion, pricing capture) rather than a repeatable annual pattern. It's reasonable to expect incremental margins to normalize toward the high-teens as the company matures off this high base, which meaningfully lowers the EPS growth assumption baked into the bull's $165-175 target. The bear's PEG re-rating (from 1.2x to ~2.0-2.8x under a 6-8% growth assumption) is a fair sensitivity check, and the technical deterioration (RSI rolling from 65 to 54, MACD turning negative, distribution-style volume pattern near the recent high) adds a near-term timing caution even if it doesn't invalidate the long-term thesis.

That said, the bear case is mostly about degree, not direction. Even the bear's own "normalized" numbers still imply mid-single-digit organic growth plus margin stability, and using the bear's own fair-value bracket of $145-155, that's still meaningfully above the current $129.29 — an 12-20% implied upside before even counting the ~2.5% dividend yield. The bull's structural argument holds up because defense budget growth (NATO members moving toward 3% of GDP) is a multi-year appropriations process, not a one-quarter phenomenon, and the backlog visibility ($200B+) gives real revenue durability regardless of near-term margin normalization. The debt picture, FCF yield (~7.6%), and backlog-driven visibility are not seriously challenged by the bear — the rebuttal is about growth-rate degree and near-term technical wobbles, not about the health of the underlying business or the multi-year secular tailwind.

Net: the bear's caution is a reasonable check on overly aggressive EPS growth assumptions and argues for tempering position-sizing and entry timing, but doesn't overturn the fundamental bull case. This nets out to a constructive-but-not-maximum-conviction stance: I'm siding with the bull's direction while giving real weight to the bear's point that current levels already reflect much of the "supercycle" and that near-term technicals argue for a disciplined, staged entry rather than an aggressive full-size add right at $129.29.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Increase exposure gradually rather than in one lump sum — scale into the position over 2-3 tranches, using pullbacks toward the 50 SMA (~$125.41) or the $120-122 zone flagged by the bear's technical read as preferred add points, rather than chasing strength above $130.
2) Set a base-case fair value range of $145-165 over 12-24 months (blending the bull's optimistic $165-175 with the bear's more conservative normalized-growth $145-155 estimate), and treat a move toward the low-to-mid $160s as a point to start trimming/taking partial profits.
3) Monitor the operating-leverage trend explicitly each quarter: watch incremental margin (change in operating income / change in revenue) — if it holds above ~20% that supports the bull case for continued re-rating; if it falls toward the bear's 15-18% normalized estimate, temper further additions and reassess the multiple being paid.
4) Keep a close eye on near-term technicals (RSI, MACD, volume patterns) as a risk-management overlay — the recent momentum rollover and distribution-style volume near the highs argue against adding size aggressively into strength; use weakness, not strength, to build the position.
5) Maintain the current position at minimum (do not underweight/sell) given intact balance sheet health (Debt/EBITDA ~2.7x, Net Debt/EBITDA ~2.2x), attractive FCF yield (~7.6%), and multi-year backlog visibility (~$200B+) that underpins the structural defense-spending thesis; size the incremental add so total position reflects a constructive but not maximum-conviction stance (e.g., moving from a standard weight to a modest overweight, not doubling down).
6) Reassess the thesis at the next 1-2 earnings prints for signs of margin normalization or reacceleration — this will determine whether to move toward a full Buy (if incremental margins/backlog conversion stay strong) or back to Hold (if bear's margin-compression case plays out).
