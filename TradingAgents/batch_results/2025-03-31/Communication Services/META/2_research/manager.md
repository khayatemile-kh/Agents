**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: META is a highly profitable, cash-generative business (29.8% net margin, 34.8% operating margin, $54.1B FCF, PEG 0.82, forward P/E 17.55x) that is simultaneously showing a clear near-term technical breakdown (19.5% drawdown from February highs, widening negative MACD histogram, a failed bounce in late March followed by a fresh leg down, RSI in the high-30s without yet reaching capitulation extremes). The disagreement is really about time horizon and about how to interpret the capex/FCF/OCF deceleration.

The bear's strongest point is concrete and forward-looking: OCF growth decelerated sharply (41.0% -> 28.5% YoY) while capex accelerated (+38.1% YoY), and capex is now consuming a rising share of revenue (20.0% -> 22.7%) with no clearly articulated ROI framework yet. That is a legitimate reason to expect margin/FCF pressure over the next few quarters, and it lines up with the technical deterioration (accelerating MACD histogram, failed relief rally, price cracking through the 200-day SMA support zone). The NFLX 2021 comparison is a reasonable cautionary analogy for why a 'low' forward P/E built on decelerating growth assumptions isn't automatically a value signal.

The bull's counters are real but mostly backward-looking (trailing margins, trailing net income growth, PEG on consensus estimates that the bear directly challenges as overly optimistic). The bull doesn't actually engage with the OCF deceleration / capex acceleration math, which is the crux of the bear case — that's a meaningful gap in the bull argument. The bull's technical rebuttal ("RSI not yet at extreme, so this is where recoveries start") is speculative and contradicted by the bear's more granular price-action sequencing (failed March 25-27 bounce, renewed breakdown into April, widening negative MACD histogram signaling accelerating not stabilizing downside momentum).

Given that: fundamentals remain solid on an absolute basis (this is not a broken business), but the trend evidence — both the capex-vs-cash-flow math and the technical momentum structure — points to near-term downside risk that isn't fully priced out yet, and the bull did not adequately rebut the capex/OCF deceleration argument. That tips this to a cautious stance rather than a balanced Hold: this looks like a case for trimming/underweighting into strength or delaying new buying, without going so far as full Sell given the balance sheet strength, still-strong absolute margins, and reasonable absolute valuation (PEG 0.82, forward P/E ~17.5x) that keep this from being a broken thesis.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing META exposure modestly (e.g., reduce position size by roughly 15-25%) rather than adding on the current 'dip' — the technical breakdown (widening negative MACD histogram, failed March 25-27 relief rally, renewed collapse through the 200-day SMA test zone) suggests the downtrend has not stabilized and further downside toward the Bollinger lower band (~$561) is plausible before a durable bottom forms.

2) Do not initiate new full-size buys at current levels; if adding at all, wait for confirmation of a technical bottom (e.g., MACD histogram narrowing/turning positive, RSI basing and turning up from below 40, price reclaiming and holding above the 200-day SMA on volume) before redeploying capital.

3) Closely monitor the capex-vs-cash-flow trend flagged by the bear: capex grew 38.1% YoY vs. FCF growth of only 22.8% and OCF growth decelerated from 41.0% to 28.5% YoY. Treat the next 1-2 quarterly reports as the key fundamental catalyst — if operating margins compress or OCF growth decelerates further, that confirms the bear thesis and warrants further trimming; if capex begins translating into re-accelerating OCF/margin expansion, that would justify upgrading back toward Hold/Overweight.

4) Keep a core long-term position given genuinely strong absolute fundamentals (29.8% net margin, fortress balance sheet with 2.23 current ratio and 0.268 debt/equity, PEG of 0.82) — this is a trim/de-risk call, not an exit call, since the business itself is not impaired, only the near-term risk/reward skew given decelerating incremental returns on capex and confirmed negative technical momentum.

5) Use any bounce toward resistance (prior failed bounce zone near $620-624) as an opportunistic point to further lighten exposure rather than chase; reassess after the next earnings print clarifies whether the 31.7% forward EPS growth consensus is holding up or being revised down, as the bear argues is already underway.
