## 4. ADDRESSABLE MARKET EXPANSION: The Experiences & International Play

### Why Regulatory Headwinds Are Overstated and TAM Is Still Growing

The bears will cite New York City and Paris short-term rental bans as evidence of TAM compression. **This is myopic.**

**The reality of regulation:**

1. **NYC & Paris impact:** These cities represent ~3-5% of Airbnb's global bookings. Material, but not existential.

2. **Compliance creates competitive moat:** Smaller competitors will be driven out. Airbnb can absorb regulatory costs better than rivals.

3. **Higher prices in restricted markets:** Limited supply + regulatory friction = higher nightly rates, which *increases revenue per booking* even if booking volumes decline.

**But here's the bigger story—the bears ignore Experiences and Services:**

- **Experiences** (tours, activities, dining) are a $1.7+ trillion global TAM
- **High-margin**: Experiences carry gross margins approaching 80%+
- **Lower regulatory risk**: Tours and activities face far less short-term rental regulation
- Airbnb has been quietly expanding this offering and integrating it into bookings

**International expansion continues:**

- APAC (Asia-Pacific) represents ~15-20% of bookings today but has a $4+ trillion travel TAM
- Rising middle class in India, Vietnam, Indonesia offers multi-decade growth runway
- Airbnb's platform is already live in these markets; it's a "plant now, harvest later" dynamic

**The math on TAM:**
