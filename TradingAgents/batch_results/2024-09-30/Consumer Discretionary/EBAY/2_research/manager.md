**Recommendation**: Overweight

**Rationale**: The bull's case rests on three pillars: high margins (72% gross, 21.6% operating), an attractive forward PE/PEG (15.5x, 1.48 PEG) predicated on 41% forward EPS growth, and strong technical momentum (27.9% above 200-day SMA, bullish MA alignment). The bear's rebuttal is sharper on the fundamentals: (1) the 41% EPS growth figure is unsubstantiated by any evidence of GMV acceleration - the bull never actually shows GMV growth data, and the bear's point that this could be buyback-driven EPS growth rather than organic earnings growth is a serious, unaddressed concern; (2) the marketplace margin is under structural competitive pressure from Amazon, Facebook Marketplace, Mercari, etc., and fee cuts are a real historical pattern; (3) the balance sheet leverage (153x D/E, 1.02x current ratio) is a legitimate risk that the bull hand-waves with debt-to-EBITDA estimates that are not sourced from any actual disclosed debt figures - the bull literally says 'estimated from leverage metrics,' which is a red flag of speculation, not evidence. The Costco analogy the bull uses to defend the current ratio is weak and the bear correctly identifies the disanalogy (inventory-based business vs fee-based marketplace with escrow-like liabilities).

On the technical side, the bull's momentum argument is real but momentum alone doesn't justify a full conviction buy when fundamental growth assumptions are shaky and leverage is elevated. RSI near 68 approaching overbought, and the extreme prior RSI spike to 82.94 followed by continued rally, could just as easily presage a sharp mean-reversion pullback as continued strength - this cuts both ways and isn't a clean bull point.

Weighing this: the bull's valuation argument is entirely contingent on 41% EPS growth being real and sustainable, and the bear directly attacks the credibility of that number without the bull providing hard evidence to defend it (no discussion of buyback-adjusted growth, no GMV growth data cited). Given that PEG and forward PE assumptions are the crux of the 'cheap' thesis and that crux is unverified, I can't award full conviction to the bull. At the same time, the profitability metrics (margins, FCF, cash generation) are real and demonstrated, not illusory - so this isn't a strong sell case either. The debt metrics are concerning but the bear's own analysis also relies on the same 153x D/E headline number without independently verifying total debt levels either - both sides are working from limited data here.

Net take: this is a business with genuine profitability strengths, real technical momentum, but a growth story that is not well-substantiated and a leverage profile that's murky and worth caution on. That asymmetry - strong current profitability/cash generation but unverified growth narrative and capital structure risk - points to a constructive but not full-conviction stance. I'll land on Overweight rather than Buy or Hold: the cash-generative business quality and momentum warrant increasing exposure, but the unresolved growth/leverage questions mean this shouldn't be a maximum-conviction full buy, and position sizing should reflect that the growth assumption is the single largest swing factor for the stock re-rating higher or seeing multiple compression.
