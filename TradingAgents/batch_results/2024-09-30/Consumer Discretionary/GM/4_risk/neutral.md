Let me explain what I mean. The conservative analyst asks rhetorically: "In what environment does an auto manufacturer achieve 760 basis points of margin expansion when the Fed is holding rates at restrictive levels through 2026?" The implicit answer is: "Nowhere—it's impossible."

But this reasoning contains a hidden assumption: that margin expansion and macro headwinds move in lockstep with perfect correlation. That's not how corporate profitability actually works. Here's a concrete example: even in a high-rate environment, a company can expand margins if it gains market share, improves manufacturing efficiency, or achieves favorable product mix shifts. These are *operational drivers* that can move independently of macro conditions.

The key empirical question is: Is GM currently losing or gaining market share? Are they experiencing favorable or unfavorable product mix? Are manufacturing efficiencies materializing? The conservative analyst doesn't engage with these company-specific factors deeply enough. They're allowing macro pessimism to override the possibility of outperformance within a difficult environment.

So here's my push-back on the conservative case: **You're correct that the macro environment is challenging, but you're incorrect that it renders the margin expansion thesis "unachievable." It renders it conditional and probabilistically lower, but not impossible.** There's a difference.
