### 4. **The Debt Story is Overblown—Context Matters**

Now, the bear case will focus on the 230.27 debt-to-equity ratio. I agree it's high. But let me provide essential context:

**Why debt-to-equity ratios are misleading for cash-generative businesses:**

The D/E ratio alone is a static snapshot of the balance sheet. What matters is the *debt service coverage*—can Expedia service its debt from operating cash flow?

With **$3.43 billion in free cash flow**, even if Expedia has $7–8 billion in debt (which would be consistent with the D/E ratio), the company has approximately **2-2.5x debt-to-FCF coverage**. This is acceptable and sustainable, not a financial distress signal.

**Industry context:** Online travel platforms operate with high leverage as a **deliberate capital strategy**. They use debt to fund growth, marketing, and technology investments because the model is so cash-generative. It's not financial weakness; it's financial engineering.

Compare to traditional hotels (D/E often 1.5–3.0x) or airlines (D/E often 2.0–4.0x). Expedia's leverage is high but not anomalous for its industry.

**The acid test:** How many times has Expedia missed a debt payment or faced financial distress? **Zero.** The company has successfully refinanced multiple times and maintained investment-grade credit access. If the debt were truly dangerous, credit markets would price it accordingly. They haven't.
