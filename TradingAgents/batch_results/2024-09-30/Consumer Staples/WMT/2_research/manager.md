**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: WMT is a high-quality, high-ROE (22.3%) defensive retailer with genuine e-commerce/advertising margin drivers, but it now trades at a historically rich multiple (38.45x trailing / 32.85x forward PE) after a rapid 14%+ run in six weeks. The bear's math correction is the decisive point: the bull's PEG framing was internally inconsistent (claiming PEG=4.09 equated to '2.4x growth,' which doesn't follow from PEG mechanics), and once corrected, WMT's PEG using forward numbers is ~1.9-2.0x, rising to 2.7-3.3x if growth normalizes to a more realistic 10-12% (in line with WMT's historical 4-5% revenue growth and past pattern of consensus overforecasting retail earnings by 25-40%). That's a legitimate structural concern for a mature, low single-digit revenue-growth retailer trading at a 35-50% premium to its own historical 20-25x forward PE norm.

The bull's rebuttals on liquidity (sub-1.0 current ratio is normal for retail) and margin composition (advertising/Walmart Connect flowing to bottom line) are fair and reduce balance-sheet-panic concerns, but they don't resolve the central valuation risk — they explain why WMT is a good business, not why today's price offers a good entry point. The technical picture reinforces caution too: MACD histogram turned negative even as price made new highs, and RSI cooled from 82-84 down to 64.83 over 17 sessions while price held near highs — a classic bearish momentum divergence that often precedes consolidation or pullback, not confirmation of a fresh breakout.

Net: this is a quality company but a stretched entry. The risk/reward is asymmetric to the downside near-term — modest multiple compression back toward 25-28x forward PE (still a premium to historical norms) implies high-teens-to-20%+ downside, while the bull case requires flawless execution on growth just to hold the current price, with re-rating upside contingent on outcomes not yet confirmed by fundamentals. This doesn't rise to a Sell/exit call given the underlying business quality, balance sheet access to liquidity, and secular tailwinds (advertising, e-commerce), but it does argue against adding here and favors trimming into strength, especially with a bearish technical divergence forming. That points to Underweight rather than Hold or Buy.
