And that's where they lose me, because the data doesn't support that leap.

Let me be direct: if EL's fundamentals were actually broken in the way the Aggressive case argues, the company would not be generating $1.81 billion in free cash flow. It could mean management has no plan (bearish), or it could mean management is executing a quiet restructuring and doesn't want to telegraph intermediate steps that could spook investors. And on that 242.93x debt-to-equity ratio—yes, it's scary looking on a spreadsheet. The company is refinancing fine. Banks are lending to them. CDS spreads aren't widening. That's not the fingerprint of a company about to blow up; that's the fingerprint of a company with real operational challenges but adequate financial engineering to manage them.

So where do I land on the Aggressive case? The technical call is solid. The exit strategy is right. But the narrative around fundamental catastrophe is overcooked and not supported by the totality of the data.
