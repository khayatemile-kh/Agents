Recent guidance suggests Aetna is stabilizing with:

## 4. ADDRESSING THE BEAR'S INEVITABLE LEVERAGE ARGUMENT

Yes, the debt-to-equity ratio of 95.50x is *technically* alarming on a headline basis. Here's why it's a red herring for CVS specifically:

**Why Debt-to-Equity Ratios Are Misleading for Healthcare/Insurance Hybrids:**

1. **Industry Standard:** Healthcare companies—especially those with insurance divisions—naturally carry higher leverage because they have *stable, recurring, contractual cash flows*. Aetna's insurance premiums are as reliable as they come.

2. **Equity Value Suppression:** CVS's book value per share ($62.37) is suppressed by goodwill and intangible assets from acquisitions. The true tangible equity is higher.

3. **Comparable Peers:** Let me check how UnitedHealth Group (UNH) and Cigna (CI) compare:
   - **UnitedHealth:** Also carries significant leverage relative to book equity because insurance operations *require* leverage for capital efficiency
   - **The industry norm:** Healthcare plans operate with D/E ratios that would terrify investors in other sectors, yet these companies are investment-grade rated

4. **Interest Coverage:** What matters more than the ratio itself is whether CVS can cover interest payments. With $8.08B in FCF and manageable debt service, it absolutely can.

**Real Risk or Paper Tiger?** It's a **paper tiger**—a ratio that looks scary but reflects industry norms, not financial distress.
