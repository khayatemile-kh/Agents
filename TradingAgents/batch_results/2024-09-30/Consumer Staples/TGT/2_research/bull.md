## **2. THE CURRENT RATIO "CONCERN" IS ACTUALLY A SIGN OF OPERATIONAL EXCELLENCE**

The bear case will point to the 0.988 current ratio and say "liquidity crisis imminent." 

**I respectfully disagree.** Let me explain why this is a feature, not a bug:

### **How Retail Liquidity Actually Works**

Target's current ratio of 0.988 reflects an optimized working capital model:

- **Inventory converts to cash continuously.** Retailers operate on rapid inventory turnover—typically 6-8 turns annually. This means inventory that sits on the balance sheet today becomes cash within weeks or months.

- **Accounts payable is strategically extended.** Suppliers extend terms (30, 60, 90 days) which shows up as a liability. But this isn't a liquidity crisis—it's negotiating power. Target has enough clout with suppliers that it can extend payment terms, effectively using supplier financing.

- **Cash-on-cash operating model.** Many transactions are cash or card-based instantly, so the company collects revenue in real-time while holding inventory and extending payables. This is **a feature of working capital efficiency**, not a weakness.

**Real-world validation:** Target is investment-grade rated and has easy access to capital markets. If there were genuine liquidity concerns, credit spreads would widen, borrowing costs would spike, and the company would face covenant violations. None of that is happening. The bond market—which is far less forgiving than equity analysts—is pricing TGT as a healthy credit.

The current ratio of 0.988 is **tighter than ideal for my comfort** (I'd prefer >1.1), but it's **operationally sustainable** for a retail business with the turnover profile Target has.
