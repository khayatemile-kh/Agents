1. **Oil prices** (which remain elevated and stable ~$70-80/bbl)

The majors—Chevron, ExxonMobil, Shell—are in disciplined capex mode: *high dividends and buybacks, steady-state capex*. They're not slashing exploration budgets due to rate concerns; they're optimizing returns. BKR benefits from steady-state capex spending on equipment, maintenance, and digital services.

The prediction markets show 46% probability of 2.0-2.5% GDP growth. Bear analysts will say: "Weak growth = weak oil demand = weak energy services spending."

- **Weak growth actually favors large, efficient operators**—which are BKR's customers. Smaller competitors get squeezed; majors consolidate and optimize. BKR is the preferred vendor for optimization and digital efficiency.

**The bear will argue macro headwinds. The reality: we're in a goldilocks scenario for steady energy services demand.**
