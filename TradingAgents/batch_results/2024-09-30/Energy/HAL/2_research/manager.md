Net: the bull's valuation argument is entirely conditional on the forward estimate holding, and the bear directly attacks the two soft spots in that estimate (rate/capex sensitivity and oil-price durability) with concrete, hard-to-dismiss logic — zero probability of cuts through Sept-2026 is restrictive, not supportive, and geopolitical price floors are by nature not permanent. Given cyclical earnings risk plus a technical picture that remains below both major moving averages, I don't think this is a genuinely balanced 50/50 debate — the bear's case for caution is more evidence-grounded than the bull's case for imminent re-rating. That said, the balance sheet and FCF quality are real strengths that argue against outright selling a name with no distress signals.
