## **THE MACRO & CYCLE HEADWINDS: WHY THEY'RE OVERSTATED**

Now, let me address the elephant in the room. The bearish narrative relies on:
- **Higher interest rates dampening capex** 
- **Geopolitical uncertainty**
- **Energy transition headwinds**

Fair points. Let me counter each:

### **On Interest Rates & CapEx Discipline:**

Yes, rates are elevated. **But here's what matters:** The prediction market data shows only a **55% probability of a 25bps hike in September 2026**, and **zero probability of rate cuts by September 2026**. Translation: **rates are expected to stabilize, not continue rising**.

When rates stabilize, energy companies don't immediately slash capex—they *optimize* it. And crucially, **higher oil prices offset higher financing costs**. Oil & gas companies evaluate projects on IRR (internal rate of return). A $100M deepwater project with a 25% IRR remains attractive even at 5% cost of capital. Energy companies cut projects at single-digit IRRs, not double-digit ones.

**What's happening right now?** E&P firms are spending prudently but *steadily*. HAL's services businesses benefit directly from this. Drilling fluids, wellbore construction, production enhancement—these are not discretionary when a well is being drilled. They're *mandatory inputs*.

### **On Geopolitical Risk & Oil Prices:**

The report notes Middle East tensions are supporting crude prices. Let's be honest: **oil prices in the $75–$85/bbl range are more than sufficient to justify current capex cycles in most global basins**. Breakeven costs for many fields range from $40–$60/bbl. Current prices offer healthy margins.

And geopolitical support is *sticky*. Red Sea shipping disruptions, Iran sanctions, Yemen dynamics—these aren't resolving in Q4 2024 or Q1 2025. This provides a **floor under oil prices** for the next 12+ months, which *directly benefits* HAL's revenue and margins.

### **On Energy Transition:**

Yes, renewables are growing. But here's the reality check: **Global oil demand forecasts still show growth through 2030 and plateau, not decline, through 2040**. Even the IEA—no friend to fossil fuels—projects oil demand at 100+ million barrels per day through 2030. 

More importantly: **HAL isn't just an oil & gas play anymore**—it's increasingly a *midstream & LNG* play. These segments have **decades of contracted cash flows** and are essential to the energy transition (LNG enables natural gas displacement of coal). HAL's 2024 positioning in these higher-margin segments is under-appreciated by the market.
