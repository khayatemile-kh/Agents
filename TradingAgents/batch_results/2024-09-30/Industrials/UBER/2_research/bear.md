
Bear Analyst: # **BEAR CASE FOR UBER (UBER): The Growth Mirage and Structural Headwinds**

---

## **Opening Statement: The Bull Case is Built on Sand**

Look, I appreciate the bull's enthusiasm, but let's strip away the narrative and look at what's actually happening here. Yes, Uber is profitable now—but that profitability is fragile, built on a foundation of unsustainable unit economics, regulatory uncertainty, and intensifying competitive pressures. The 16.7x forward P/E isn't a "discount"—it's fair value for a company with **slowing growth, margin compression risks, and a labor cost crisis they haven't solved.**

At $75.16, Uber is trading at **fair value or slightly expensive** relative to its real risks. The bull case relies on extrapolating recent momentum and ignoring the structural headwinds that are about to hit this business hard. Let me show you why.

---

## **1. THE "PROFITABILITY INFLECTION" IS AN ILLUSION—HERE'S THE REAL PICTURE**

### **The Bull Claims: "TTM Net Income of $9.58B Proves Structural Profitability"**

Let me break down exactly what's happening with those numbers:

**First, the 2023 Manipulation:**
- 2023 Net Income: $1.89B
- **2023 Investment Gains: $7.17B** (this is the "one-time" the bull dismisses)
- **Adjusted Net Income (2023): -$5.28B** (operating loss when you remove the gain)

The bull says "normalize to EBITDA," but that's dodging the real issue: **Uber had to book a $7.17 billion gain on its investment in Uber Technologies itself to show profitability.** That's not operating leverage—that's financial engineering.

**Now, the TTM Numbers Don't Add Up:**

The bull touts $9.58B TTM net income, but let's look at the trajectory:
- 2023: $1.89B (boosted by $7.17B one-time gain)
- 2024 YTD (through Q3): The TTM figure of $9.58B implies ~$7.69B of net income in the first 9 months of 2024

That's an **extraordinarily sharp acceleration** that requires scrutiny. Without seeing Q1-Q3 2024 earnings reports in the data provided, we're being asked to trust TTM figures that may include:
- Additional investment gains
- One-time operational improvements
- Unsustainable cost-cutting

**The Hard Truth About Normalized EBITDA:**

The bull touts $7.47B TTM EBITDA as proof of "core operational profitability." But here's what EBITDA doesn't capture:

1. **Stock-based compensation** (~$2-3B annually, growing as Uber retains talent)
2. **Depreciation on assets** (as the company builds infrastructure, D&A will increase)
3. **Taxes** (Uber will face meaningful tax bills as profitability persists)
4. **Driver and merchant acquisition costs** (amortized over time, not in EBITDA)

**Real, spendable profitability?** Much lower than the bull is claiming.

## **2. THAT "48% TTM GROWTH" IS A MIRAGE—THE REAL STORY IS DECELERATION**

### **The Bull's Sleight of Hand:**

The bull says: *"The 17% 2023 figure is stale—it's now 48% TTM."*

**That's mathematically dishonest.** Here's what's actually happening:

| Period | Revenue | YoY Growth |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| 2023 Full Year | $37.28B | +17% |
| TTM (implies ~$55B annualized) | $55.23B | +48% |

The bull wants you to believe the company went from 17% growth to 48% growth. In reality, here's what likely happened:

- **2023 Q4**: ~$9B revenue (weak quarter, often true for consumer discretionary)
- **2024 Q1-Q3**: Strong quarters, but here's the catch—**they're being compared to weak 2023 comps**

This is a **comp comparison artifact**, not accelerating growth. When you're comparing a weak Q4 and Q1-Q3 of 2023 to strong early 2024, you'll see inflated growth rates. But **the annualized growth rate** is likely in the 25-35% range, not 48%.

### **The Competitive Pressure Reality:**

Here's what the bull didn't mention:
- **DoorDash (DASH)**: Growing faster than Uber Eats in U.S. food delivery (larger share)
- **Lyft (LYFT)**: Still competitive in ride-sharing; undercutting on price in key markets
- **Regional competitors**: Didi in China, Grab in Southeast Asia—fragmenting global market share

**The margin expansion story crumbles when you account for competitive pricing pressure.** Uber's gross margin went from 38.3% to 39.8%—a 150 bp improvement. But that's **on a higher revenue base with intense competition**. How sustainable is that?

Answer: **Not very.** When margins expand amid industry-wide competition, it's usually temporary—the result of prior pricing increases that haven't been competed away yet. Once competitors match pricing (and they will), margins compress.

## **3. THE VALUATION "DISCOUNT" IS A TRAP—HERE'S WHY IT'S ACTUALLY EXPENSIVE**

### **The Bull's Faulty Comparison:**

The bull cherry-picks comparables:

| Company | P/S Multiple | Bull's Claim |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| UBER | 2.7x | "Cheapest growth platform" |
| DASH | 4.2x | More expensive |
| ABNB | 8.1x | Way more expensive |

**But this is apples-to-oranges trash.** Let me show you:

**1. DASH is More Expensive for a Reason:**
- **DoorDash**: Improving unit economics, gaining share, higher EBITDA margins (15-18%)
- **Uber Eats**: Lower EBITDA margins, losing share to DASH in core U.S. market
- DASH's premium valuation **is justified**; UBER's discount reflects lower-quality earnings

**2. ABNB Isn't a Comparable:**
- Airbnb is a marketplace with **40%+ EBITDA margins**, higher asset-light model
- Uber is a **labor-intensive logistics business** with structural 20% EBITDA margins (best case)
- Comparing 2.7x P/S for Uber to 8.1x for ABNB is comparing a taxi company to a software company

**3. The Real Valuation Benchmark:**

Compare UBER to actual logistics and transportation comps:
- **FedEx**: 0.4x P/S (but declining margins, aging business)
- **XPO**: 0.8x P/S (struggling with labor costs)
- **Saia**: 1.2x P/S (niche player, stable but not growing)
- **Uber Freight**: ~1.5-2x implied (standalone)

**Uber at 2.7x P/S for a low-margin logistics/labor-intensive business is not cheap—it's fairly valued to slightly expensive.**

### **The PEG Ratio Myth:**

The bull cites a PEG of 0.35, claiming it's "trading like a value stock." Let's break this down:

- **Assumed Growth Rate**: The PEG of 0.35 assumes Uber is growing at ~48% (based on forward P/E of 16.7x and a 0.35 multiple)
- **Reality Check**: We established that 48% growth is a comp artifact; real growth is likely 25-35%

**If real growth is 30%, the PEG is actually 0.56x—still reasonable, but not the bargain the bull suggests.**

More importantly: **A 30% PEG is not cheap for a mature, labor-intensive business.** That's the PEG of a high-growth SaaS company, not a logistics platform dealing with driver cost inflation.
