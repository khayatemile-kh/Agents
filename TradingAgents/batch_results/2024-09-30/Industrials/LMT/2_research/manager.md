**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points, but weighing them carefully tips the balance toward a constructive-but-not-aggressive stance. The bull's structural argument is real: LMT sits in an irreplaceable position in the defense industrial base (F-35 program), has multi-year government contract visibility, strong FCF ($5.56B), and a PEG near 1.0x that isn't obviously expensive for a company still compounding double-digit EPS growth. The bear's pushback on valuation history (16.37x vs 14-16x 2015-2019 baseline) is fair context, but it doesn't prove the stock is overvalued outright—it just tempers the bull's 'discount' framing. Similarly, the bear's point that EPS growth (27.2%) outpaces net income growth (20.8%) due to buybacks is accurate and worth flagging, but buybacks funded by genuine FCF are a legitimate capital allocation tool, not inherently value-destructive, especially with margins roughly flat rather than deteriorating (12.6% vs 12.7% op margin — a rounding-level compression, not a red flag).

Where the bear lands stronger punches: the negative tangible book value and the fact that deferred revenue is a liability/obligation rather than a cash cushion are valid balance-sheet nuances the bull glossed over. The pension obligation growth and refinancing-rate sensitivity are legitimate tail risks worth monitoring but are not acute solvency threats given 8.51x interest coverage. On technicals, RSI at ~69.6, price above the upper Bollinger Band, and a freshly negative MACD histogram do suggest the stock is short-term overbought after a 26% run, even though the longer-term moving-average structure (10 EMA > 50 SMA > 200 SMA, golden cross) remains constructive.

Netting it out: the long-term structural bull case (defense industrial moat, contract visibility, bipartisan spending support, healthy FCF, reasonable PEG) outweighs the bear's valuation-history and balance-sheet nitpicks, which are real but not thesis-breaking. However, the extended technical overbought condition and the bear's fair point that much of the geopolitical premium is already reflected in the 26% rally argue against pressing new money aggressively right now. This is a 'stay invested and lean in on weakness, but don't chase strength' situation — that maps to Overweight rather than an outright Buy or a defensive Hold/Underweight.
