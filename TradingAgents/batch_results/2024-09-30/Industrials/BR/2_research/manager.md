**Recommendation**: Hold

**Rationale**: Both analysts agree on the underlying facts—7.4% revenue growth, margin expansion of low double-digit bps, ~26% FCF conversion, $943M FCF, heavy capital return (~90% of FCF), and roughly flat-to-slightly-worsening leverage (D/E ~1.65x, net debt down modestly). The disagreement is about interpretation, not data.

Bull's strongest points: margins are expanding across gross/operating/net/EBITDA lines simultaneously while revenue grows, which is a genuine sign of operating leverage in a capital-light, highly recurring/sticky revenue model; buybacks and dividends are well covered by FCF (28% payout for the dividend alone); balance sheet risk is front-loaded away from current debt into long-term, reducing refinancing risk in the near term; forward multiple (~14.5x) is not demanding for a company with these characteristics.

Bear's strongest points: the TTM "acceleration" claim is unsubstantiated speculation not backed by disclosed quarterly data—this is a real analytical overreach by the bull and should be discounted. The OCF/NI growth gap (28.3% vs 10.7%) is a legitimate flag for working-capital-driven cash flow that may not repeat, so the cash flow "excellence" framing is overstated. CapEx as % of revenue is low and declining, which could indicate underinvestment. Total debt is basically flat (-2.1%) and leverage ratio is actually ticking up (1.63x -> 1.65x), so the "improving balance sheet" framing is spin — it's really just debt-maturity extension, not deleveraging. Revenue growth of 7.4% is unremarkable for the multiple being paid, especially against faster-growing SaaS peers.

Weighing it: the bear successfully punctures the bull's most aggressive claims (TTM acceleration, cash-flow-as-pure-quality-signal) using the same dataset, which suggests the bull oversold the growth/quality narrative. But the bear doesn't disprove the core defensive characteristics — margin expansion is real, FCF is real and growing, dividend is safe, debt is not growing, just not shrinking much either. This isn't a broken business; it's a moderately-growing, high-quality, cash-generative compounder that is fairly valued rather than exceptionally cheap or dangerously levered. Given the bear neutralizes the more speculative bull claims but doesn't present a genuine deterioration case (revenue isn't declining, margins aren't compressing, dividend isn't at risk), the right call is a measured, constructive-but-not-aggressive stance: maintain exposure, don't chase it aggressively on the back of overstated acceleration claims, and don't panic-sell on the leverage story since leverage is stable not increasing meaningfully. That points to Hold rather than Buy or Sell — this is the textbook case of genuinely balanced evidence: real quality metrics on one side, real overstatement/complacency risks on the other, with no side landing a knockout blow.
