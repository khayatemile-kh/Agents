The Conservative Analyst also argues that 2.0-2.5% GDP growth kills capex cycles. But that's not empirically true. Industrial capex doesn't require 3%+ growth to stabilize—it requires *certainty* that growth won't turn negative. We have 46% probability of 2.0-2.5% growth and only implicit low probability of recession. That's exactly the setup where manufacturers stop panicking and start planning refresh cycles.

Here's where the Conservative Analyst overreaches: they're treating OCF collapse as disconfirming evidence of a rebound. But OCF collapses in cycle troughs precisely because customers delay payments (extended terms), inventory sits in warehouses (cash tied up), and order backlogs clear out. This is trough behavior, not permanent behavior. Once orders re-accelerate, receivables collections tighten, and inventory turns improve, OCF can snap back faster than revenue because it benefits from working capital releases.

So the Conservative Analyst has correctly identified the trough conditions. But they're confusing trough with permanent structural break. Those are different things.
