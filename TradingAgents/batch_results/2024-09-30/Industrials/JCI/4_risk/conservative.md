Here's why that matters: **if the company's book value of equity is genuinely that low relative to debt, that's still a serious red flag.** It means the company has taken on a debt load that has eroded shareholder book value to an extreme degree. 1. **Limited capacity for new debt issuance** without triggering rating downgrades
2. **Vulnerability to earnings misses** that could trigger covenant violations
3. **Refinancing risk** if short-term debt matures in a higher-rate environment

I'm supposed to be managing this firm's downside. The Aggressive Analyst is arguing we should ignore a structural leverage warning because the company "seems fine now." That's precisely the wrong risk management posture.
