## Opening Statement

I'm bullish on **RTX (RTX Corporation)** and here's why: we're looking at a company positioned at the intersection of three powerful tailwinds—**elevated geopolitical risk, structural defense spending growth, and operational recovery**—trading at a valuation that already prices in much of the bad news while offering substantial upside from margin expansion and cash flow generation. ## I. THE MACRO TAILWIND: GEOPOLITICAL RISK = DEFENSE DEMAND

Let's start with what's actually happening in the world right now:

**Prediction markets are pricing in meaningful escalation risk:**
- **86% probability** of Israel striking 4+ countries in 2026 (trending UP +9 percentage points in just one week)
- **91% probability** of zero US-Iran diplomatic meetings by Sept 2026 (meaning sustained adversarial positioning)
- **16% probability** of direct US-Iran military conflict before 2027

This isn't speculation—this is market-implied risk. And historically, when geopolitical tensions spike, three things happen immediately:

1. **Congress fast-tracks defense appropriations** (no political party opposes "national security")
2. **Procurement timelines accelerate** (military urgently wants inventory replenishment)
3. **Defense contractors see order backlogs extend and convert faster to revenue**

RTX operates in the **sweet spot**: missile systems (Raytheon), avionics, and defense electronics. When you're sitting on $268 billion in market cap with contracts across every major US defense program, **escalation directly translates to higher backlog conversion and pricing power**.

The beauty here is that this geopolitical environment is *not priced into the stock as aggressively as it should be*. Why? Because the market hasn't yet seen the earnings revision cycle that typically follows geopolitical repricing. That's coming.

## II. THE FUNDAMENTAL SETUP: PROFITABILITY RECOVERY IS ALREADY UNDERWAY

Now, I know what the bear will say: *"Look at 2023—net income crashed 38.5%, margins compressed everywhere, and debt exploded."* Fair point. Let me address it directly.

**But here's the critical nuance the bear is missing:**

### **2023 was a trough year—transition year—not the new normal**

The margin compression in 2023 reflects a **one-time operational burden**:
- **Post-pandemic supply chain normalization:** Aerospace & defense is still working through inflation, component shortages, and labor cost pressures from 2021-2023
- **Raytheon integration costs:** The 2020 merger added complexity; systems integration takes time
- **Deferred revenue conversion timing:** Defense contracts often have lumpy recognition patterns

But look at the forward guidance embedded in the market: **Forward P/E of 25.3x vs. trailing 35.0x—a 27% compression**. Why? Because the market is *already expecting* earnings to expand to **$7.86 per share in 2024** from the depressed 2023 base of $2.23.

**That's a 253% earnings recovery implied by current market pricing.**

This isn't aggressive—this is actually *conservative* given:

1. **Free cash flow remains robust:** $4.7B in 2023 despite margin pressure—that's 6.8% FCF margin on $69B revenue. This is *sustainable* and proves the underlying business is healthy.
2. **Operating cash flow grew despite lower earnings:** $7.9B in 2023 vs. $7.2B in 2022. When operating cash flow *grows* while net income *falls*, it signals accounting/timing issues, not operational deterioration.
3. **Supply chain normalization is accelerating:** The 290 bps gross margin compression from 20.4% to 17.5% is *reversing* as component costs stabilize. Industry commentary in Q3 2024 already reflects margin recovery momentum.

### **The Buyback Question: Not Reckless, Strategic**

The bear will hammer the $12.9B buyback in 2023 paired with rising debt. Let me flip this:

**RTX is using cheap debt to buy back shares at a reasonable valuation, capturing long-term value.** Here's why this works:

- **Weighted average cost of capital (WACC) on RTX debt:** ~4.2-4.5% currently
- **Forward P/E of 25.3x = Forward Earnings Yield of ~3.95%**
- **Spread:** 25-50 basis points *positive*

The math says: *"Borrow at 4.2%, buy shares with 3.95% yield, wait for margin expansion to push earnings higher."* That's textbook accretive capital allocation.

And the share count reduction? It's supporting EPS even *during* a margin trough. Once margins recover (which they are), that EPS accretion compounds.

## III. THE VALUATION ISN'T STRETCHED—IT'S FAIR GIVEN THE SETUP

This is where the bull case gets really interesting.

**Current valuation multiples:**
- **Trailing P/E:** 35.0x (looks high in isolation)
- **Forward P/E:** 25.3x (reasonable for a defense contractor)
- **EV/EBITDA:** ~16.8x (in line with peers like LMT)

**But here's what this misses:**

1. **Peer comparison:** Lockheed Martin (LMT) trades at 28.3x forward P/E. Boeing's defense division would trade at a significant premium if separated. RTX at 25.3x forward is *not expensive* for the quality and positioning.

2. **Growth visibility:** With geopolitical tensions rising and defense budgets locked in through mid-2025+, RTX has **5-7 years of high-visibility cash flow**. That justifies a *premium* multiple, not a discount.

3. **The real earnings power:** The $7.86 forward EPS estimate is *conservative*. If margins recover to 8-9% operating margin (historically normal for RTX), and the geopolitical environment drives 3-5% revenue growth, we're looking at **$9.50+ EPS by 2025-2026**. That would put the current stock at 12x forward earnings—a significant undervaluation.
