**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull leans heavily on a geopolitical-escalation narrative and a "2023 was just a trough" story to justify paying 25x forward earnings and a premium multiple to peers. The bear's rebuttal is stronger on the fundamentals that actually matter for a position decision: (1) prediction-market escalation odds are tail-risk pricing, not earnings pricing, and defense budgets are largely fixed/reallocated rather than incrementally boosted in the near term; (2) RTX is already trading at the top of its peer group (25.3x vs LMT ~22-28x and GD ~20x depending on source), so the "cheap relative to peers" argument is shaky and contested; (3) the margin story is the crux — gross margin fell 290bps and operating margin fell 300bps in 2023, and the bull's explanation (supply chain/integration/timing) is asserted, not evidenced, especially four years post-merger, which should be enough time for integration synergies to show up, not vanish; (4) the OCF > net income divergence that the bull spins as "health" is at least as plausibly explained by working-capital squeeze (payables stretched, receivables compressed) as by genuine operating strength — this is a real red flag on earnings quality; (5) the leverage buildup (Net Debt/EBITDA 2.34x, debt up ~35% YoY financing a large buyback) is a legitimate risk to flag even though coverage ratios are currently adequate — it reduces the margin of safety if the margin-recovery story is delayed.

Where the bull is on solid ground: technical setup shows a genuine uptrend with dip-buying, and the company does have multi-year cash-flow visibility and a real macro tailwind, so this isn't a broken business. But the trade here is about entry point and expectations, and the current valuation already embeds a fairly aggressive earnings recovery ($7.86 EPS, expanding out to $9.50+) that the bear correctly flags as unproven — margins actually got worse in 2023, not better, and the bull hasn't shown hard evidence that the trend has reversed yet, just cited "industry commentary."

Net: this isn't a broken-thesis sell candidate — defense fundamentals and cash generation are real — but the stock is priced for a recovery that hasn't been demonstrated in the data, sits at a peer-group premium multiple, and carries rising leverage funding buybacks rather than deleveraging. That combination argues against adding here and argues for trimming into strength / waiting for confirmed margin recovery before increasing exposure. I'm not going full Sell because the cash flow generation, backlog visibility, and technical uptrend are real support factors that argue against a large short or full exit — this is a valuation/quality mismatch rather than a broken business. Underweight better reflects "trim exposure, don't chase the premium multiple until margins actually recover" than either Hold (which would ignore the valuation/premium and unproven margin story) or Sell (which would ignore the genuine cash generation and demand backlog).

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing RTX exposure into current strength rather than adding — do not chase the stock at 25x+ forward earnings while it trades at the top of the peer group (vs. LMT/GD in the low-to-mid 20s) without confirmed margin recovery.
2) Do not initiate or grow new positions until at least two consecutive quarters show operating margin expansion back toward the 7-8%+ range (from the 2023 trough of 5.2%) — treat the bull's $7.86-$9.50 EPS path as unconfirmed until data shows it.
3) Set a valuation-based re-entry trigger: revisit Overweight/Buy if forward P/E compresses toward peer-average levels (~20-22x) while margin trend is simultaneously improving, or if margin recovery is confirmed even at the current multiple.
4) Monitor balance-sheet trend closely — Net Debt/EBITDA at 2.34x and debt up ~35% YoY funding buybacks (rather than deleveraging) is a risk amplifier if the margin recovery is delayed; any further leverage increase without margin proof should be treated as a negative catalyst.
5) Watch working-capital metrics (receivables/payables trend) each quarter as a check on earnings quality — continued compression of working capital alongside OCF > NI divergence should be treated as a red flag, not confirmation of health.
6) Use technical levels as tactical guide only: reduce on strength above the 50-day SMA (~$113) toward resistance, and reassess only if price holds well above the 200-day SMA with margin data confirming the bull case; a break below the 200-day SMA on weak earnings would justify further de-risking.
7) Position sizing: reduce RTX weighting modestly (e.g., trim by roughly a quarter to a third of current position) rather than exiting entirely, keeping optionality to re-add if fundamentals confirm the bull thesis.
