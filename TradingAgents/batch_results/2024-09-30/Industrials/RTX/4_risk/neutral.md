## Where the Aggressive Analyst's Case Collapses

Listen, the TTM margin story is clever framing, but it's ultimately evasive. The aggressive analyst is using a statistical artifact to paper over an operational concern. Here's the honest truth: we don't actually know if RTX's margins have recovered. We have full-year 2023 data showing 5.2% operating margin. We have forward guidance suggesting things will improve. We have TTM data that purports to show 12.7% margins. But TTM is backward-looking—it's pulling in partial 2024 data mixed with 2023 weakness. It's not a reliable operational signal; it's an average of a deteriorating period and an improving one. The aggressive analyst is asking us to have confidence in a recovery that hasn't been fully reported in audited financials yet.

Here's the problem with that: when you're making capital allocation decisions on a company with deteriorating operational performance, you should be waiting for *proof*, not predictions. The aggressive analyst keeps saying "margin recovery is coming," but every quarter that comes in without delivering that recovery weakens the case. We're now in late September. If margins were truly normalizing, we should see it in Q3 preliminary indicators. Guidance should be crisp and confident. Instead, we're relying on forward-looking statements from management that could easily miss.

And let me be direct about the geopolitical tailwind argument: the aggressive analyst is committing a classic error. They're treating prediction market probabilities as if they're certainties. Yes, there's an 86% probability that Israel strikes 4+ countries by 2026, and yes, that spiked 9 points in a week. That's interesting. But here's what matters: that probability is already priced into defense sector multiples. RTX isn't trading at a 10x P/E. It's trading at 35x TTM. The market has *already* priced in defense sector strength. So the question isn't whether geopolitical risk exists—it's whether RTX specifically is going to outperform *within* that strengthening sector. That's a much higher bar.

And there's another problem the aggressive analyst glossed over: geopolitical catalysts don't always flow through to the specific contractors you own. Yes, if there's military escalation in the Middle East, defense spending will rise. But who captures that spending? It depends on contract portfolio positioning, which systems are prioritized, and frankly, which contractors have relationships with decision-makers. RTX does have strong positions, but they're not the only player. And if Lockheed Martin or Northrop Grumman have better-positioned systems for the anticipated scenarios, RTX could underperform even in a rising-tide scenario.

Finally, the leverage argument. The aggressive analyst says 2.34x net debt to EBITDA is "normal" and "manageable" for defense contractors. Technically true. Either RTX is heading higher because geopolitics and margin recovery, or it's heading lower because leverage and operational execution. The real risk here isn't that RTX collapses. The real risk is that it stagnates or consolidates sideways for 6-12 months while the firm's capital is sitting in a position that's not delivering alpha. In that scenario, the optimal strategy is *not* a full sell and it's *not* a full hold. It's a tactical trim.
