**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull case leans heavily on a single, aggressive forward-EPS estimate ($9.10 vs. $4.38 trailing, i.e. +108%) and a PEG of 0.79 to argue CBRE is a screaming value/growth buy, backed up by clean moving-average alignment and FCF generation. The bear case attacks the credibility of that earnings number directly and, importantly, does so with real math: CBRE's operating margin (~3.6%) and profit margin (~3.0%) are thin and variable-cost-heavy (brokerage commissions, property management staff, investment management comp), so doubling EPS without a commensurate revenue surge is very hard to justify through 'operating leverage' alone. The bear also correctly flags that the macro backdrop cited (elevated-for-longer rates, muted CRE transaction volumes, refinancing pressure) is not the environment in which transaction-driven brokerage/services earnings typically double. On leverage, the bear's arithmetic is more rigorous than the bull's: a D/E of ~118x implies a debt load that dwarfs the $1.64B FCF; even devoting 50% of FCF to debt paydown barely moves the needle, and rising refinancing rates on tens of billions of debt could add hundreds of millions in interest expense, disproportionately hitting a net income base that's already thin. The bull's claim that leverage is 'operational, not financial' doesn't hold up once you look at the actual FCF-to-debt-service math the bear lays out. On technicals, the bull's momentum/MA-alignment story is real but the bear correctly notes the RSI has been declining even as price marginally rises (72.42, down from 74.59 and 73.78) — a mild bearish divergence that at minimum argues against chasing the stock at these levels, even if it isn't a decisive top signal on its own.

Net assessment: the bull's valuation math (15.65x forward PE, PEG 0.79, implied $150 target) is entirely contingent on that 108% EPS growth forecast being real. The bear makes a credible, math-backed case that this forecast is unlikely to be met given current margins, revenue mix, and the macro/rate backdrop, and separately shows that the leverage genuinely constrains the company's flexibility if that growth disappoints. That combination — an unrealistic-looking earnings bridge plus a fragile, highly-levered balance sheet exposed to higher-for-longer rates — outweighs the bull's technical and cash-flow arguments. This isn't a slam-dunk short case (the RSI/MA setup and absolute FCF generation aren't nothing, and CBRE is a real, diversified franchise, not a broken business), so I'm not going to full Sell. But the risk/reward is skewed enough that maintaining or adding to a full position isn't warranted either. I'm calling this Underweight: trim exposure, tighten risk management, and wait for either (a) confirmation that the forward EPS number is achievable via actual reported margin expansion, or (b) a real de-rating in price that reflects the leverage/rate risk, before considering re-adding.

Any disclosed refinancing at materially higher spreads should trigger further de-risking.
5) Retain a small residual position (rather than zero) to stay exposed to the bull scenario (FCF generation, technical uptrend, diversified global platform) in case management does deliver on margin expansion and deleveraging — but size it so that a disappointment on the earnings bridge does not meaningfully impair the portfolio.
6) Re-evaluate toward Hold/Overweight only if: (a) reported margins show clear expansion toward levels consistent with the forward EPS bridge, (b) rate expectations shift dovish reducing refinancing risk, or (c) leverage metrics (D/E, interest coverage) show tangible improvement from actual FCF deployment, not just guidance.
