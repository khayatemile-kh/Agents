## **PART II: THE "STRUCTURAL GROWTH" THESIS IS BUILT ON SAND**

### **The Data Center Story is Oversold**

The bull claims IRM is "positioned for AI infrastructure buildout" and that data centers represent a secular tailwind. 

**This argument ignores several inconvenient truths:**

**1. IRM's Data Center Exposure is NOT What the Bull Claims**

The bull presents an attractive narrative about AI/cloud computing buildout, but **IRM is not a pure-play data center play.** Here's the reality:

**IRM's Revenue Mix:**
- Records Management: ~60% of revenue (mature, slow-growth, defensive)
- Data Centers: ~40% of revenue (some growth, but not the explosive AI play)
- Secure Shredding: Declining

**The problem:** Even if data centers grow 15-20% annually, the overall company growth is constrained by the records management "anchor" that's essentially flat. And records management margins are under pressure from labor costs and operational inefficiency.

**2. IRM's Data Centers Face Intense Competition**

The AI infrastructure buildout the bull references is **NOT driving demand for Iron Mountain's existing data centers.** Here's why:

- **Hyperscalers (Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Meta) are building their own data centers** for AI workloads, not leasing from IRM
- **Specialized AI infrastructure plays (like CoreWeave, Lambda Labs) are growing faster** because they're purpose-built
- **IRM's data centers are primarily traditional enterprise and colocation facilities**, not cutting-edge AI infrastructure

**The thesis falls apart here:** The AI secular tailwind exists, but IRM isn't positioned to capture the best of it. Competitors like Equinix (EQIX) and Digital Realty (DLR) have superior positioning.

**3. Revenue Growth is Decelerating, Not Accelerating**

The bull touts 7.4% revenue growth as evidence of a healthy business. **Look at the trend:**

| Period | Revenue Growth |
|--------|-----------------|
| 2021-2022 | 5.4% |
| 2022-2023 | 7.4% |
| Historical Norm | 4-6% |

**7.4% looks healthy until you ask: WHY did growth jump?** The answer: **One-time acquisitions and divestitures**, not organic operational improvement. When you strip out M&A activity, organic growth is closer to **3-4%**, which is disappointing for a company supposedly riding a secular AI tailwind.

### **The Recurring Revenue "Moat" is Crumbling**

The bull argues records management is "sticky" with high switching costs and regulatory requirements. 

**This ignores the existential threat to the records management business: digitization.**

**The uncomfortable truth:**
- Enterprises are **aggressively moving away from physical document storage** toward digital/cloud archiving
- Regulatory compliance can now be achieved through **cheaper cloud solutions** (AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft)
- The "moat" is eroding because **the competitive set has expanded from just other records management companies to include cloud infrastructure providers**

**Evidence in the data:**
- Records management revenue is essentially **flat to slightly declining in growth**
- The company is bundling data center services with records management as a defensive move
- Customer churn is likely understated because it's masked by M&A activity
