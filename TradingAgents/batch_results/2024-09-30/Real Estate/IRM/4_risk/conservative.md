## THE "SELL INTO STRENGTH" FRAMEWORK IS BACKWARDS FOR THIS SITUATION

The Aggressive Analyst frames this as a textbook "take profits before momentum exhausts" scenario. I fundamentally disagree with this narrative.

Selling into strength works in two contexts:

**Context 1: When you have genuine conviction that a fully-valued business is about to disappoint.** You sell before the market discovers the disappointment. Example: a software company with 40x earnings that's about to guide lower on user growth.

**Context 2: When you've held through a strong rally and want to rebalance portfolio risk.** You trim a 30% position that's become 45% of your portfolio because it's overweight, not because the business is broken.

IRM doesn't fit either context clearly—but it should be triggering a third decision: **hold or liquidate fully, but don't trim and hope.**

Here's why: IRM carries refinancing risk that doesn't just disappear in a pullback scenario. The company is spending more capital ($1.345B capex in 2023, up 52%) while generating less cash (-$231.5M FCF). At some point—and the "point" is when existing debt matures and needs to be rolled over—the market is going to demand a higher interest rate or a covenant adjustment.

When that happens, common shareholders find out whether the company can absorb the additional cost, or whether it has to dilute equity, cut the dividend, or restructure.

Trimming 25-35% doesn't protect you from that outcome. It just means you're still exposed to 65-75% of the capital loss when the repricing happens. **You're taking the tail risk without the upside hedging.**
