### **Why Forward EPS Growth Is a Mirage**

The bull cited three drivers:

#### **1. "Occupancy Recovery Post-COVID"**

The bull's claim: Facilities are still below 90%+ peak occupancy, so margin expansion is ahead.

**The Reality:**
- Post-COVID recovery peaked in **2022-2023**. We're now in late 2024. The "recovery" narrative is two years old.
- Healthcare facility occupancy rates are **already normalized** at or near historical levels. Occupancy data for senior living and nursing facilities shows we're already at 85-90% levels.
- **The low-hanging fruit has been picked.** If there was easy 10%+ operating margin expansion from occupancy recovery, it would have already happened. The fact that forward EPS is only growing 43% *despite* 16+ months of post-COVID normalization suggests the recovery is largely complete.
- **Any further occupancy gains will be marginal** (1-2%), not the transformational jump the 43% forward growth suggests.

This is a **tired narrative** that ignores the current state of the business.

---

#### **2. "Rent Escalation Clauses Will Drive Growth"**

The bull's claim: CPI-tied rent escalators will ensure consistent revenue growth.

**The Reality—And Here's Where It Gets Problematic:**

The bull just contradicted the macro guidance. The same data showing **55% probability of rate *increases* through September 2026** also suggests:
- **Inflation probability >5% in 2026:** Only 8%
- **Inflation probability >4.5% in 2026:** Only 10%

So the market is pricing in **low inflation (~3-4%)**, which means rent escalators will grow revenue by roughly 3-4%—in line with historic healthcare inflation.

**But here's the kicker:** If WELL was already growing at 3-4% annually from escalators *before*, that's not **new** growth driving the 43% forward earnings expansion. **The bull is counting the same revenue growth twice.**

Moreover, **rent escalators apply to incoming cash, not net income.** WELL's operating costs—property maintenance, staffing, utilities, property taxes—are also inflating. In a 3-4% inflation environment, WELL's cost base rises *nearly in line* with rental revenue. **Net operating margin expansion from escalators is minimal.**

This is a **textbook accounting sleight of hand** the bull is using.

#### **3. "Portfolio Optimization Improves Cap Rates"**

The bull's claim: Culling underperforming assets and deploying to higher-yielding properties improves returns.

**The Reality:**

This is happening *slowly*, if at all. Here's why:
- **Capital redeployment requires selling assets.** In a high-interest-rate environment, real estate buyers face higher cap rate requirements. WELL can't sell its mediocre assets *without taking significant losses* or delivering marginal economics.
- **The portfolio is already quite good.** A $171B healthcare REIT isn't sitting on a bunch of junk it can easily swap for better assets. The incremental yield improvement is measured in basis points (5-10 bps), not meaningful percentage gains.
- **This is also slow.** Real estate transactions take 3-6+ months. The 43% forward earnings growth is supposed to materialize in the *next 12 months*. Portfolio optimization is a multi-year story, not a near-term catalyst.

This is a **secondary factor**, not a driver of the 43% growth claim.

### **The Real Driver of Forward Earnings Growth (That the Bull Won't Admit)**

Here's what's actually happening: WELL's forward earnings are growing because the market is assuming **that the company will cut its dividend significantly or reduce share buybacks.** This is how you get 43% EPS growth from a business with stable/slow revenue growth:

**The formula:**
- Revenue: +3-4% (from escalators)
- Operating costs: +3-4% (inflation)
- Net income: +2-3% (minimal margin expansion)
- **EPS: +43%** ← Only if the company reduces shares outstanding *dramatically* or cuts distributions

**But the bull didn't mention this**, because it undermines the thesis. If WELL cuts its distribution, the current 1.44% yield evaporates, and the stock re-rates lower as it transitions from "income play" to "growth play."

**The bottom line on valuation:** WELL is trading at an extraordinary premium based on forward earnings that are built on **assumptions about occupancy recovery (largely done), rent escalators (not new), and portfolio optimization (slow process).** The 74.6x forward PE leaves **zero margin of safety** for disappointment.
