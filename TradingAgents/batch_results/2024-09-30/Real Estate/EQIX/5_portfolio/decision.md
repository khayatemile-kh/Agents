**Executive Summary**: Maintain existing EQIX position with no new capital added at current levels; do not chase the breakout above the 50 SMA ($793.57) given the stock is already 7.2% extended and only 2.5% from the upper Bollinger Band ($872.12). **Investment Thesis**: This is a genuinely two-sided debate where neither side rebuts the other's strongest point, which is exactly why Hold is the correct calibration rather than a compromise for lack of conviction. The bull case (Aggressive Analyst) is real: EQIX is in a confirmed technical uptrend (price above 10/50/200 EMAs, MACD +16.74 and positive histogram, RSI moderating from 71.39 to 66.89 rather than breaking down), operating income grew 28.8% against 12.7% revenue growth, and EBITDA margin sits at a best-in-class 45.8%. The AI/hyperscaler demand backdrop and regulatory moratoria (73% Polymarket odds) plausibly confer scarcity value on EQIX's existing 260+ data center footprint.

However, the Conservative and Neutral analysts land the more damaging, harder-to-dismiss point: the 28.8% operating income growth diverges sharply from 15.3% EBITDA growth and 8.6% operating cash flow growth, which the Neutral Analyst correctly identifies as a possible accounting artifact (declining D&A as a share of earnings) rather than true operating leverage. Critically, FCF fell 36.3% YoY to $436mm while CapEx rose 22% to $2.78B — if the AI capacity buildout were already monetizing, FCF should be stabilizing or inflecting upward even amid heavy investment; instead it is deteriorating faster than CapEx is rising, which is the "signature of a company deploying capital and not yet seeing the return," per the Neutral Analyst. Dividend coverage at ~32% of FCF, funded by debt issuance, combined with Debt-to-EBITDA at 3.84x, leaves limited cushion if EBITDA growth decelerates on a quarterly basis. Valuation (67x TTM P/E, 55.77x forward, PEG 2.92) prices in near-certainty that this capex converts to durable revenue growth — a thesis, not yet a fact, as the Hold recommendation itself concedes by waiting for "FCF inflection confirmed."

Technically, the Conservative Analyst's point that the $68 cushion to the lower Bollinger Band ($782.27) is thin relative to a $14.52 ATR is a fair tactical risk flag, even though the Neutral Analyst reasonably counters that a positive (if flattening) MACD histogram and RSI in the high-60s are more consistent with healthy consolidation than imminent breakdown. Neither the fundamental cash-flow quality concern nor the bullish technical/operating-leverage momentum is dismissible, so sizing should stay flat: protect the existing position with a hard stop at the 50 SMA, but do not add into a 67x multiple while FCF and dividend coverage are visibly weakening. This mirrors the lesson from the NVDA precedent — prospective risks (here, capex-to-revenue conversion) should be treated as monitoring triggers requiring one or two quarters of confirmation before escalating to a directional downgrade or upgrade, rather than acted on preemptively in either direction.

**Time Horizon**: 1-2 quarters (pending FCF/EBITDA inflection confirmation)
