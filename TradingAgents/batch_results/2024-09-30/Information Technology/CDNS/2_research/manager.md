**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: CDNS is a high-quality, high-margin EDA software franchise (85.8% gross margin, 28.6% operating margin, 23.6% net margin, $1.61B FCF) trading at a demanding valuation (56.5x trailing P/E, 29.8x forward P/E, PEG 2.21) with above-peer leverage (D/E 38.66 vs. single-digit/teens for software comps) and a technical picture that is only tentatively turning up (fresh MACD crossover, RSI back to neutral 50.6, price still below the 200-SMA, inside a tight Bollinger squeeze).

The bull's strongest points are the durability of CDNS's competitive moat (EDA tools are mission-critical, sticky, non-discretionary spend tied to multi-year AI/semiconductor design cycles) and the sheer quality of the cash-generation engine, which does provide real cushion. The bear's strongest points are that (1) the forward P/E of ~30x is only 'reasonable' if the Street's ~89% EPS growth lands almost exactly on target — leaving zero margin for error in a name priced for perfection; (2) EDA/semiconductor capex is historically cyclical, and the bull's 'this time is different' argument hasn't been falsified by evidence, only asserted; (3) the leverage argument only works in the good scenario — D/E at 2-4x software peers is a real structural difference, and the bear's downturn math (margin compression + FCF decline + D/E spiking toward 60) is a legitimate tail risk that the bull didn't actually rebut, just asserted away; and (4) the technical setup is genuinely fragile/early — a crossover after a sharp 25% drawdown with price still below the 200-SMA and RSI fading back to neutral is exactly the kind of setup that fails as often as it confirms, so it's not a green light on its own.

Netting this out: the fundamental quality of the business is not in dispute, but the bear's critique of valuation asymmetry is the more rigorous argument here. At 56x trailing / ~30x forward with PEG north of 2, the stock has very little room for the kind of estimate revision that's common when a cyclical hardware-adjacent demand driver (semiconductor capex) even modestly disappoints. The bull's rebuttal to the leverage and cyclicality concerns was more assertion ('software companies can carry leverage,' 'AI is different') than evidence, whereas the bear grounded the same points in comparable peer data and historical capex cycles. The technical picture is genuinely mixed/early, not a decisive tiebreaker either way, so it doesn't rescue the bull case.

Given that we have a great business at a valuation that is priced for near-flawless execution, with leverage that amplifies the downside if that execution falters, I don't think this rises to a Sell — the franchise quality, gross margins, and FCF generation are too strong to justify an aggressive underweight/exit call. But I also can't get to Buy or Overweight when the risk/reward is this asymmetric at current levels and the bull failed to substantively counter the leverage-in-a-downturn and 'priced for perfection' arguments. This calls for a cautious, trim-into-strength stance rather than accumulation.
