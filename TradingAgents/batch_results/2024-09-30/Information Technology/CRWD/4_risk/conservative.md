## The RSI Mean Reversion Argument Confuses Recovery Momentum With Sustainability

The Aggressive Analyst says the pullback from RSI 69 to RSI 54 is "healthy consolidation" and "textbook accumulation behavior." Okay, but let's be precise about what that means in the context of a crash recovery.

Here's the problem: RSI oscillators are *mean-reverting by design*. They tell you when a move has gotten stretched. The fact that RSI ran from 15.93 in early August to 69.11 in late September is not evidence of structural strength—it's evidence of a *technical bounce* in an oversold market after a panic sell-off.

When an asset crashes 43% in a week, RSI gets crushed to 15. When it bounces 33% over six weeks, RSI naturally runs to 65-70. **That's not bullish divergence. That's a normal mean reversion bounce in an oversold condition.** And here's the critical part: **Mean reversion bounces frequently retest or break below the previous lows when the fundamental catalyst doesn't materialize.**

The Aggressive Analyst says "if RSI breaks below 45, yes, that signals weakness." But that's the wrong threshold to watch. The real risk is if RSI fails to hold above 50 when tested. Here's why: RSI at 50 is the neutral midpoint. If CRWD consolidates in the 45-55 range for weeks without breaking above 60 again, that's not bullish—that's *distribution*. That's smart money quietly exiting into rally attempts.

And here's the part that keeps me up at night: **We're only three weeks into this consolidation.** RSI at 54 today doesn't tell us much about where it will be if Q3 guidance disappoints or if churn metrics come in hot. If the company reports that customer additions were flat or that NRR declined, RSI will crack below 45 within a day, and we'll see a retest of the $64-$65 level—the very stop loss the trader wisely placed.

The Aggressive Analyst is reading the tea leaves of a technical recovery and calling it proof of fundamental recovery. Those are not the same thing.
