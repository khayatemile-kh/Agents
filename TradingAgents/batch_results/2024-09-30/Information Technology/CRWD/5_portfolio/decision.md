**Investment Thesis**: The debate converges on one central fact: the 33% rally off the panic low occurred entirely in a data vacuum. As the Conservative and Neutral analysts both stress, the FY24 profitability inflection ($183M loss to $72M profit, EBITDA to +$276.7M, gross margin +210bps) was booked before the July 19 outage and therefore cannot be used—as the Aggressive analyst does—as proof that post-outage fundamentals are intact. No Q3 NRR, churn, customer-count, or RPO data exists yet to confirm the bull's recovery narrative.

The technical picture is genuinely ambiguous, not bullish, which undercuts the Aggressive case for adding here. Price sits between the 50-day SMA ($64.92) and 200-day SMA ($76.11), still 44% below the pre-crash high; RSI has round-tripped from 15.93 panic-capitulation to 69 and back to 54 (neutral, not breakout-confirming); MACD's move from -8.55 to +1.33 is, per the Conservative analyst, a normal reversion from extreme panic rather than a directional signal, and can flip negative in a single session on a disappointing print. The Neutral analyst's key insight is that Bollinger Band compression is directionally agnostic pre-earnings — it can resolve violently either way — and that the risk-reward of adding fresh capital now is symmetric (21-28% upside to $85-90 vs. 20-30% downside to $50-55), which does not justify incremental buying, only maintaining existing exposure.

The Conservative analyst's structural point carries the most weight for position sizing: a security vendor's entire value proposition is operational trust, and a global outage with no evident staged/canary rollout is a governance failure that historically (SolarWinds, Microsoft patch precedents) takes multiple quarters — not six weeks — to fully reflect in churn and competitive win/loss data. Litigation exposure remains unquantified, and competitive diversification (Microsoft Defender, Palo Alto Cortex XDR) is a credible, not hypothetical, risk to growth durability even if it manifests as margin/pricing pressure rather than franchise collapse, as the Neutral analyst notes.

Against this, the bull case (large structural EDR market, 75%+ gross margins, genuine operating leverage) is not dismissed — it is why this is Underweight and not Sell. But the valuation argument (~44x-130x on a still-unproven $1.60 forward EPS) is not corroborated by hard data and should not be treated as attractive until at least one clean post-outage quarter is reported. Trimming into strength while retaining a core position and defined risk triggers reflects the asymmetry: confirmed operational/governance risk and unconfirmed recovery narrative on one side, versus a real but unvalidated structural growth story on the other.
