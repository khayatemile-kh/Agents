**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: AMD trades at a rich valuation (129x trailing, 32.6x forward P/E) that is entirely dependent on a ~295% forward EPS ramp materializing, carries elevated leverage (6.36x D/E) versus peers, and sits in a bullish but decelerating technical pattern (contracting ATR, rolling-over volume into the 200 SMA/resistance zone). The bull's strongest points are real: AMD has genuine AI-driven demand (EPYC/MI accelerators), a 21%+ FCF margin that comfortably services debt today, 55.8% gross margins reflecting real pricing power, and a technically constructive MA stack (10EMA>50SMA>200SMA) with the 200-day now acting as support. The bear's strongest points are also real and, on balance, cut deeper: the valuation leaves zero margin for error—even a partial miss on the forward EPS guide (e.g., 60-70% delivery) implies a stock trading at 45-65x forward earnings, which is not defensible for a cyclical semiconductor business, especially with the "no Fed cuts through 2026" backdrop keeping discount rates elevated and multiples compressed. The bear also correctly flags that AMD's leverage, while serviceable in the current bull scenario, is asymmetric to the downside: if growth disappoints, margins compress, or NVIDIA/Intel take share, the earnings-adjusted leverage deteriorates quickly with no cushion. The technical read is genuinely ambiguous — the bull's "healthy consolidation" and the bear's "distribution/exhaustion" (declining ATR, rolling volume) are both plausible from the same data, and I don't have follow-through price action to adjudicate that fight, so I'm not leaning on technicals to break the tie. What tips this debate for me is asymmetry of the fundamental argument: the bull needs near-flawless execution across growth, margins, and competitive positioning to justify current multiples, while the bear's downside case only requires a partial, plausible shortfall to trigger meaningful multiple compression given the elevated discount-rate environment and above-peer leverage. That's a lopsided risk/reward at current levels, even though the long-term AI secular story for AMD is legitimate and not something to abandon entirely. This argues against outright Sell (the underlying business and AI positioning are real, not broken), but strongly against adding risk at these prices, so Underweight is the appropriate stance: trim/lighten exposure and de-risk the position size given how much perfection is priced in, while acknowledging the thesis could work if execution is flawless.
