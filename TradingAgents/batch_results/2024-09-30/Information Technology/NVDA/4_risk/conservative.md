## The 15% Government Stake Risk Is Actually More Serious Than It Looks

This is where I think the aggressive analyst makes his biggest error. He dismisses the 15% probability of government involvement by saying it's "already priced in" and that government backing would actually *help* shareholders.

That's wishful thinking masquerading as analysis. Let me explain why.

First, a 15% probability of material government intervention is NOT trivial. That's a one-in-six-and-a-half chance of a significant adverse event. In portfolio management, we don't dismiss one-in-six events. We hedge them or we avoid them.

Second, the framing of "government backing helps shareholders" is dangerously naive. Government stakes in semiconductors historically come with *conditions*: export restrictions, domestic content requirements, price controls, mandatory share-outs with competitors, or outright forced corporate restructuring. Look at what happened with Intel when government subsidies got involved—the company ended up agreeing to massive capex commitments and government oversight that destroyed capital efficiency.

If the US government takes a stake in NVIDIA, it's not because they want to be passive shareholders. It would be because policymakers have concluded that NVIDIA's dominance in AI poses a strategic concentration risk. The "solution" would almost certainly involve dilution (new shares issued to government entities), governance constraints (board seats for government reps), and operational restrictions (which markets NVDA can sell to, which partners are acceptable, etc.).

That's not a feature for equity holders. That's a threat.

And here's the thing: the market is *not* pricing in this threat correctly. NVDA's valuation assumes unencumbered market access and operational autonomy. The moment government involvement becomes more than a theoretical tail risk, the market reprices dramatically.
