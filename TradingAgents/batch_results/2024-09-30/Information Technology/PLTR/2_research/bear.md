## **THE CORE BEAR THESIS: THREE CRITICAL FRACTURES**

### **FRACTURE #1: THE "EXCEPTIONAL PROFITABILITY" NARRATIVE IS MISLEADING AND UNSUSTAINABLE**

Let me directly engage with the bull's Pillar 1 argument, because this is where the entire thesis lives or dies.

**The bull claims:** "49% net margins and 47% operating margins justify the valuation because these are 'durable' and superior to Microsoft and Google."

**The reality is far more complicated:**

#### **A) Margin Sustainability is Being Overstated**

The bull cites PLTR's historical consistency on margins. But look at the actual context:

**PLTR's revenue composition is artificially skewing margins upward:**
- Government contracts (~60% of revenue) carry *higher margins* because they're cost-plus or fixed-price contracts with limited price competition
- These contracts scale less aggressively than true commercial SaaS
- As commercial revenue becomes a larger percentage of the mix (required to justify 99% EPS growth), **gross margins will compress**

Why? Commercial software customers demand competitive pricing. They have alternatives. They negotiate. Government buyers don't—they accept what's offered or go through 18-month procurement cycles. 

**The math doesn't work:** If government revenue is $3.7B at 60% of total, and commercial is $2.5B at 40%, the company needs *commercial revenue to nearly double* to justify 99% EPS growth. That requires price cuts and margin compression.

**Historical precedent:** Look at Palantir's own history. When they shifted from pure-play government work toward commercial products (Gotham, Apollo), what happened to margins in those segments? The company doesn't break this out, which is itself a red flag. If commercial margins matched government margins, why wouldn't management trumpet that?

#### **B) The Margin Comparison to Microsoft and Google is Apples-to-Ostriches**

The bull wrote: "PLTR's 49% net margins are superior to Microsoft's 30% and Google's 22%."

This is technically true but wildly misleading. Here's why:

| Metric | PLTR | MSFT | GOOGL | Why PLTR's Margins Are Misleading |
|--------|------|------|-------|-----------------------------------|
| **Revenue Scale** | $6.16B | $240B+ | $280B+ | Massive scale enables efficiency for peers |
| **Geographic Reach** | US-Centric | Global | Global | PLTR lacks international diversification |
| **Segment Mix** | Govt Heavy | Diversified | Ad-Driven | PLTR depends on government stickiness |
| **R&D as % Revenue** | Lower | Higher | Higher | Peers invest more in innovation |
| **Total Addressable Market** | Constrained | Massive | Massive | PLTR's market is smaller |

**The real insight:** PLTR's margins are high *because it operates in a less competitive market segment*. Microsoft and Google operate in highly competitive markets where they've *earned* their premium valuations through decades of scale and innovation. PLTR operates where it has fewer competitors because the barriers are different (government relationships, domain expertise, customer switching costs).

**The danger:** When competition increases—and it will—those margins compress. AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud all have data analytics capabilities. As they invest in this space, PLTR's competitive moat weakens, and pricing pressure increases.

#### **C) The $2.16B Free Cash Flow Claim Needs Scrutiny**

The bull points to $2.16B in FCF as validation of "real cash being generated."

**Let me ask critical questions the bull didn't address:**

1. **What's the capital intensity trend?** Is capex increasing as PLTR scales? If the company needs more infrastructure to support commercial growth, FCF margins will decline.

2. **What's happening to working capital?** Is accounts receivable growing faster than revenue (sign of collection risk)? Is deferred revenue (a credit) being normalized away?

3. **Stock-based compensation:** How much of that "net income" is SBC-adjusted away? If PLTR is burning $500M annually in SBC (not uncommon for growth tech), then real economic earnings are 17% lower than reported.

**The bull didn't provide this level of detail, which tells me the narrative depends on not looking too closely.**

### **FRACTURE #2: THE 99% EPS GROWTH PROJECTION IS NOT CONSERVATIVE—IT'S A HOUSE OF CARDS**

The bull claims: "99% forward EPS growth is conservative relative to the business's potential."

**This is where the bull's argument becomes most dangerous, and I need to interrogate it ruthlessly.**

#### **A) The Math Required for 99% EPS Growth is Extreme**

Let's work backwards from the claim:

- **Current TTM EPS:** $1.17
- **Forward EPS (projected):** $2.33
- **Implied growth:** 99%

For this to happen, one of three things must be true:

1. **Revenue grows ~25-35% AND margins are maintained**
2. **Revenue grows modestly (10-15%) AND margins expand significantly**
3. **Profitability rises through cost cuts (operating leverage)**

Let's test each:

**Option 1: Revenue growth of 25-35%**
- TTM revenue: $6.16B
- Required revenue (at 99% EPS growth): ~$7.7B-$8.3B
- That's growth from $6.16B to $8.3B = 35% growth *in one year*
- Does this align with 99% EPS growth? Only if margins are held flat or expand
- **Is 35% revenue growth realistic in 2025?** Possible, but not guaranteed. Macro headwinds (higher rates, potential recession) make this aggressive.

**Option 2: Modest revenue growth + margin expansion**
- Revenue grows 15%, reaching $7.08B
- Margins expand from 49% to ~60%+ net margins
- **Is margin expansion realistic?** This requires either: (a) pricing power increases, (b) cost structure improves, or (c) mix shift to higher-margin products
- None of these are highly likely simultaneously

**Option 3: Operating leverage through cost cuts**
- Revenue is flat or modest growth, but EPS doubles through margin expansion
- **This requires halving the cost base, which isn't realistic without layoffs or abandoning initiatives**

**The real risk:** The market is pricing in Option 1 (35% revenue growth). If PLTR delivers 20% revenue growth instead, EPS growth drops to 45-50%, not 99%. At that point:
- Forward P/E doesn't compress to 43x as the bull suggests
- It compresses to 90-100x on the new lower EPS
- **The stock still crashes**

#### **B) Market Headwinds Are Being Ignored**

The bull cites AI infrastructure demand as a structural tailwind. But the macro data shows:

**From the world affairs report:**
- Fed rates staying higher for longer (0% probability of cuts by Sep 2026)
- GDP growth expected at 2.0-2.5% (modest, not robust)
- Enterprise IT spending will be cautious, not aggressive

**Translation:** When CFOs are managing tight margins in a 2% GDP growth environment, they don't approve 35% increases in big-ticket software spending. They approve 5-10% increases on mission-critical systems.

**PLTR's commercial business is NOT mission-critical for most customers.** It's an optimization layer. When budgets tighten, optimization gets cut first.
