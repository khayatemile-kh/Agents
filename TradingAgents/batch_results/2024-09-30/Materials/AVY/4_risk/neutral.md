## **WHERE THE AGGRESSIVE ANALYST GOES OFF THE RAILS**

The Aggressive Analyst keeps returning to this idea that the September 18 washout was "capitulation" followed by "institutional accumulation." Here's my problem with that framing: **they're retrofitting a narrative onto price action after the fact.** This is classic hindsight bias dressed up as technical wisdom.

Yes, the move was sharp. Yes, volume was elevated. But sharp moves on volume happen constantly in equities—they don't automatically mean mean reversion is coming. I could just as easily argue that the September 18 washout was a sign that institutional investors were getting out, and the modest recovery we've seen is short-covering by day traders. The volume data alone doesn't resolve which narrative is correct. The Aggressive Analyst is presenting as certainty what is actually ambiguity.

And here's the part that really bothers me: they keep pointing to RSI at 55.36 as "neutral with room to run." But neutral doesn't mean it's going up. Neutral is exactly where you'd expect RSI to be in a consolidation phase, which is exactly what the data shows. They've interpreted "momentum has normalized" as "momentum is about to accelerate," which is a logical leap not supported by the technical setup.

The Bollinger Band argument is similarly oversold. Yes, price is near the upper band. But the upper band isn't a promise of breakout—it's a statistical boundary. And the fact that we tested it on September 27 and couldn't hold above it is actually more bearish than bullish. That's price saying "I can't sustain these levels." To then argue that this represents "textbook pre-breakout positioning" is cherry-picking the data that fits the thesis while ignoring the data that contradicts it.

Now let me address the more serious problem: **the Aggressive Analyst's handling of leverage is dangerously glib.** They're essentially saying, "Look at the ROE, look at the free cash flow, therefore the debt-to-equity ratio of 158.65x is fine." But they're doing this while simultaneously ignoring the Conservative Analyst's entirely valid point: **we don't actually have the absolute debt figure or the maturity schedule.** 

The Aggressive Analyst is defending unknowns by pointing to ratios that assume continued normalized operations. That's backward risk management. You don't defend a leveraged position by saying "well, the company has demonstrated it can service debt in good times." Of course it can. The question is what happens if it can't. And a 158.65x debt-to-equity ratio means that even a modest operational deterioration creates serious refinancing risk.

The comparison to Amazon is particularly weak here. Amazon operates on low current ratios because Amazon has secular tailwinds that make its cash flow predictable and growing. Avery Dennison is in packaging—a consumer cyclical business where demand fluctuates with economic cycles. Those aren't comparable situations, and the Aggressive Analyst knows that but presents the comparison anyway.

Here's what really concerns me: the Aggressive Analyst is treating the forward PE of 15.3x as evidence that the market has already priced in the risk correctly. But the market doesn't price in tail risks efficiently. A company can trade at 15x forward earnings and still carry significant leverage risk if macroeconomic conditions deteriorate. The multiple doesn't prove safety; it's just what people are willing to pay today.

If the Conservative Analyst actually believed AVY was dangerously leveraged, they should be recommending a sell or a short, not a hold pending earnings. If you're bearish on AVY because of recession risk, you should probably be bearish on every cyclical stock.
