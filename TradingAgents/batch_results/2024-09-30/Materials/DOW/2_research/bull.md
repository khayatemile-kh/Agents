## II. THE BEAR CASE HINGES ON ETERNAL PESSIMISM—BUT CYCLES DO TURN

Before the bears jump in, let me preempt their core argument: *"DOW lost $1.3 billion last year, leverage is through the roof at 111.91x debt-to-equity, and the dividend is unsustainable."*

Fair points on the surface. But let's dig deeper into what's *actually* happening:

### A. The Earnings Crisis Appears to Be Bottoming

**This company didn't become insolvent overnight**—it's been caught in a cyclical downturn. The chemical industry is notoriously cyclical, and when you have high fixed costs (which DOW does), downturns are severe. But here's the critical insight:

The forward EPS estimate of **$1.84** represents a **massive swing from -$1.83 TTM**. That's a **$3.67 earnings turnaround** baked into analyst expectations—and the market is beginning to price this in (hence the 8.9% rally we've seen in two weeks).

**The question isn't whether DOW is profitable today—it's whether it will be profitable soon.** The technical recovery combined with forward EPS projections suggests the market is anticipating exactly that.

### B. The Leverage Story Is More Nuanced Than It Appears

Yes, 111.91x debt-to-equity is alarming on a standalone basis. But here's what bears won't tell you:

- **DOW's EBITDA is $3.96 billion**—that's real cash-generative capacity. In the chemicals industry, EBITDA multiples matter more than accounting net income because non-cash charges distort GAAP earnings.
- **The company is still generating positive free cash flow ($237.6M)**, despite the TTM net loss. That's the proof in the pudding—operations are generating cash even when accounting losses are negative.
- **The extreme leverage is *already priced in*.** The market hasn't been kind to DOW; it trades at only 1.35x book value and a forward P/E of 16.11x—not expensive for a cyclical recovery story.

The bears will argue leverage is still dangerous. I agree—but **it's danger that's already reflected in valuation**. We're not being asked to pay premium prices for hidden risk.

This is a **soft-landing environment**, not a cliff. What does soft-landing mean for DOW?

2. **Feedstock costs (oil/gas) likely remain elevated but stable**, not spiking further. This helps DOW's margins—the worst-case scenario (energy spikes) is off the table.
3. **The worst cyclical outcome is already priced in.** If the market were expecting recession in 2025, DOW wouldn't have recovered 8.9% in two weeks. The rally IS the market saying "we've bottomed."

**The absence of negative catalysts is itself a catalyst.** No company-specific bad news for the past week, neutral macro backdrop—this is exactly when beaten-down cyclicals explode higher as rotation begins.

## IV. THE DIVIDEND YIELD IS A FEATURE, NOT A BUG

Bears will scream: *"A 4.76% yield on negative earnings is unsustainable!"*

True. But consider the logic:

1. **If DOW cuts its dividend, it frees up cash** that was going to shareholders. That cash goes to deleveraging—which is exactly what the company needs to do.
2. **A dividend cut is often the *capitulation moment* for cyclical stocks**—after which they recover sharply.** We may have already seen it, or it may be imminent. Either way, once that uncertainty is resolved, the stock re-rates higher.
3. **The market is already pricing dividend risk into valuation.** We're not being asked to pay for a safe, stable dividend; we're being asked to hold through a restructuring that creates value.

In cyclical turnarounds, **the dividend cut is the painful transition—but it's *temporary pain that leads to permanent gain*.** Once DOW stabilizes earnings and begins deleveraging, the yield will look incredibly cheap on a forward basis.
