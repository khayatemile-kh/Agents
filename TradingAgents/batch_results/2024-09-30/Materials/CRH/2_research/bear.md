## **I. THE VALUATION TRAP: "CHEAP" IS ONLY CHEAP IF EARNINGS HOLD UP**

The bull argues CRH trades at a "discount" with a 13.72x forward P/E. **But here's the problem: that forward multiple assumes 16.8% EPS growth to $6.60/share, and those assumptions are baked into a deteriorating macro environment.**

### **The Forward Earnings Are Highly Questionable**

Let me break down why I'm skeptical:

**1. Forward EPS assumes normalization that won't materialize:**
- The market is pricing in EPS of $6.60, up from $5.66 TTM (+16.8%)
- But this growth assumption **contradicts the macroeconomic signals** the bull himself acknowledged:
  - **No Fed rate cuts through 2026** (only 7% probability of cuts)
  - **Mortgage rates sticky at 7.0-7.2%** (suppressing residential demand)
  - **28% recession probability by 2027** (elevation from current baseline)

**If the macro worsens in 2025—as the data suggests—those forward earnings evaporate.** We'd be looking at a 16x multiple on $5.50 EPS (flat to down), not $6.60.

**2. Comparing to peers obscures the real problem:**

The bull says CRH trades at 16x vs. MLM at 20-24x or VMC at 22-25x, implying CRH is cheap. **But let me ask: why are peers trading at higher multiples?**

- **MLM (Martin Marietta):** ~23x P/E, but MLM's aggregate business has **better pricing power and less cyclical exposure** than CRH's diversified (but lower-margin) portfolio
- **VMC (Vulcan Materials):** ~24x P/E, but VMC is **aggregates-pure play**, not exposed to struggling cement and ready-mix concrete segments

CRH trades at 16x **because the market is already pricing in margin compression** from its exposed cement and construction products divisions. The discount isn't an opportunity—it's a warning signal.

**3. The 5-year P/E average is misleading:**

The bull cites CRH's historical 18-20x average. **But 2017-2019 multiples (when that average was established) reflected a pre-COVID, low-rate environment with strong residential demand.** We're not going back to that regime.

In cyclical industries, cutting capital investment is often a precursor to demand deterioration. Management conserves cash when they see headwinds on the horizon.
