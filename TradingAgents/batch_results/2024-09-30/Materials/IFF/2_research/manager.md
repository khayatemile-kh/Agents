**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides have real points, but weighing them, the bear case is more grounded in hard evidence while the bull case relies heavily on optimistic assumptions about reversal timing. Key facts that tip the scale: (1) EBITDA is negative even before the impairment add-back, meaning core operations are currently unprofitable, not just optically ugly due to a one-time charge. (2) This is the second consecutive year of massive impairments ($2.4B in 2022, $2.7B in 2023 - $5.1B cumulative), which undercuts the bull's 'one-time cleaning house' framing and instead suggests a pattern of value-destructive acquisitions and a genuine risk of further write-downs if performance doesn't improve. (3) Tangible book value is negative $4.4B with 61% of the balance sheet in goodwill/intangibles - a fragile capital structure exposed to further impairment risk. (4) The bear's competitive read - that Givaudan and Symrise have held up better on volume/pricing while IFF specifically lost share - suggests the revenue and margin weakness may be idiosyncratic/structural (execution and integration problems) rather than purely macro/destocking driven, which the bull hasn't rebutted with company-specific data. (5) The FCF improvement, while real in cash terms, is flagged by the bear as substantially working-capital driven (inventory drawdown) rather than a sustainable operating inflection - a swing that is not fully repeatable once inventory normalizes. The bull's counterpoints are reasonable in isolation (impairments do reset the depreciation base, FCF actually was generated, technicals are constructive, industry structure is favorable long-term), but they depend on execution catalysts (cost savings materializing, pricing holding, volumes recovering) that are not yet confirmed in the data and are somewhat speculative. Given negative core EBITDA, a two-year impairment pattern, a fragile balance sheet, and evidence of company-specific share loss versus peers, I don't think this qualifies as a balanced 'Hold' - the bear thesis has the stronger evidentiary footing right now, even though the technical setup and cash generation numbers offer some support for the bulls. I'm leaning cautious: trim/underweight rather than full sell, because there is real FCF being generated and debt is being paid down, which limits near-term downside from a liquidity/solvency perspective, and the technical trend is still constructive short-term. But conviction in aggressive accumulation is not warranted until we see actual organic revenue/volume stabilization and clean (ex-impairment) EBITDA turn durably positive.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing IFF exposure modestly rather than adding on the technical breakout - do not chase strength until fundamentals confirm the bull thesis. 2) Wait for at least two consecutive quarters of (a) organic/ex-FX revenue growth or stabilization and (b) EBITDA positive on an ex-impairment, ex-one-time basis before re-adding risk. 3) Watch Q3/Q4 2024 earnings specifically for: gross margin trajectory (needs to show sequential improvement, not just YoY comps), evidence that price increases are sticking without further volume/share loss versus Givaudan/Symrise, and whether FCF strength persists once inventory drawdown normalizes (i.e., check if operating cash flow ex-working-capital changes is actually improving). 4) Monitor balance sheet risk closely - given $19B of goodwill/intangibles against a negative tangible book value, treat any further guidance cuts or soft volume prints as a high-probability trigger for another impairment cycle; size position to reflect this tail risk. 5) Keep a tight stop/review level tied to the $101 technical support zone - if price breaks back below the 50/200 SMA convincingly, treat it as confirmation of the bear thesis and reduce further. 6) Do not treat the debt paydown or potential divestiture rumors as a green light to add until an actual signed transaction or credit-rating upgrade materializes - these are speculative catalysts, not confirmed fundamentals. Overall stance: reduce weighting now, keep a small residual position to participate if the turnaround catalysts (cost savings, volume recovery) prove out, but do not increase exposure until core operating profitability (ex-impairment EBITDA and organic growth) inflects positively for multiple quarters.
