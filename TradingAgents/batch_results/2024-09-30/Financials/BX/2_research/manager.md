**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points, but the bear's arguments are more grounded in verifiable mechanics of asset-manager economics, while the bull leans on extrapolation and favorable framing of ambiguous technicals. The bull's macro case (rates staying elevated, low recession odds) is a double-edged sword: the bear correctly notes that higher rates also raise portfolio companies' cost of capital and widen cap rates, which marks down existing real estate/PE holdings and directly compresses the AUM base fees are calculated on—this is a more precise mechanical argument than the bull's assertion that unlevered yields simply improve. On the critical valuation debate, the bull's 67.6% forward EPS growth and 1.28 PEG are the crux, and the bear's fact-check is compelling: to hit that growth via management fees alone would require roughly $700B of net new inflows in a single year versus BX's realistic $80-150B annual pace—a 5-9x acceleration that isn't supported by disclosed fundraising pipeline. The bull doesn't rebut this specific math, instead pivoting to multi-year normalized growth (30-50% CAGR), which is a much softer, less falsifiable claim than the headline 67.6% figure driving the "undervalued on forward PE" argument. The bear's point that performance/carry fees are lumpy and tied to a specific vintage harvest window (not structurally recurring) also directly undermines the bull's "harvest phase compounding" framing—carry realized now doesn't repeat until the next fund matures 5-7 years out. On technicals, both sides are essentially interpreting the same ambiguous MACD/RSI signals (RSI cooling from 79 to ~60, MACD histogram flipping negative while MACD line still above signal) through their own lens; I'd call this roughly a wash rather than a deciding factor, though the bear's point about a 15.4% one-month rally increasing vulnerability to any earnings disappointment or macro re-rating is a reasonable risk-management consideration layered on top of the valuation stretch. Putting it together: BX is a high-quality, structurally advantaged franchise (scale, fundraising brand, operating leverage are real), but at ~30x TTM earnings the stock is pricing in a continuation of an unusually favorable performance-fee harvest and fundraising pace that the bear's inflow math shows is very hard to sustain. That combination of a rich multiple resting on optimistic, not-yet-proven growth assumptions plus a recent sharp rally argues for trimming into strength rather than adding here or simply holding at current levels. I'm not moving to outright Sell because the underlying business quality, AUM scale, and structural shift toward alternatives are genuine long-term positives that the bear doesn't dispute—this is a valuation/timing call, not a business-quality call. That combination points to an Underweight: acknowledge the franchise strength but recommend reducing exposure given the disconnect between the priced-in growth trajectory and the more conservative, evidence-based fundraising/carry-realization math the bear laid out.
