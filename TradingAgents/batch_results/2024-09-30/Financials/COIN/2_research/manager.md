**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points, but weighing them, the bear case is more grounded in the actual risk/reward math. Technically, the bull tries to spin the death cross and oversold bounce as bullish, but a death cross with price below all three moving averages is still a classic distribution/further-downside signal until proven otherwise — the burden of proof for reversal hasn't been met, it's speculative pattern-reading. The macro setup argument (rate cuts driving rotation into risk assets) is speculative and cuts both ways — high-for-longer rates plus a hostile-to-uncertain regulatory backdrop (SEC lawsuits, election uncertainty) skew risk to the downside near-term. Given the extreme valuation sensitivity to multiple compression, the historical unreliability of forward estimates for this name, and the real dilution cost from SBC that the bull dismisses too easily, the risk/reward is skewed unfavorably at current levels. This doesn't mean the business is broken — gross margins are genuinely strong and FCF is real cash generated — but the price already embeds near-perfect execution and sustained multiple, leaving little margin of safety. That combination — good business, bad entry point, asymmetric downside risk from multiple compression — argues for trimming exposure rather than adding, but stops short of a full sell call given genuine FCF generation and a legitimate scarce/regulated-market position domestically.
