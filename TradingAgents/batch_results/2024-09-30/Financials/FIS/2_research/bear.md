## **II. THE BALANCE SHEET IS DISQUALIFYING: The 132.51x D/E Ratio Isn't "Context-Dependent"—It's a Siren**

Here's where the bull's argument completely falls apart. The bull dismisses the **132.51x debt-to-equity ratio** as "misleading without context" and claims FIS's debt is "investment-grade."

**Let me call this out: that's an evasion, not a rebuttal.**

**Equity = $153M / Debt = $20.3B**

That's not "leverage for growth." That's **financial distress territory.** Here's why:

1. **Equity Cushion Is Minimal:** With only ~$153M in shareholder equity against $20.3B in debt, **any significant earnings miss or asset write-down vaporizes shareholder value.**

2. **The Bull's "Context" Doesn't Help:** Yes, FIS generates $2.65B in FCF. But let's do the math:

3. **Covenant Risk Is Real:** A company carrying $20B in debt this highly leveraged will have **restrictive covenants limiting M&A, dividend capacity, and financial flexibility.** Any economic downturn forces deleveraging actions that pressure margins.

### **Why Investment-Grade Rating Doesn't Matter**

The bull claims FIS is investment-grade rated, so the debt is "safe." **That's backwards logic.** Investment-grade means it's *barely* not junk—not that it's safe. Here's what investment-grade companies actually do:

- **When customers defer spending,** there's no financial cushion to absorb the impact

And FIS is **particularly exposed** to all three of these risks.
