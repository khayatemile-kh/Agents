## PROBLEM 1: REVENUE GROWTH IS DECELERATING INTO A WALL

Let's start with what the bull hopes you'll ignore: **the growth deceleration is real and accelerating.**

| Period | Revenue Growth |
|--------|-----------------|
| FY2022 | +13.1% |
| FY2023 | +13.1% (flat year-over-year) |
| FY2024 | +5.6% |
| **Implied FY2025 (consensus)** | ~3-4% (estimated) |

**This isn't maturation. This is deceleration into stagnation.**

A company that grew 13% two years ago and now grows 5.6% isn't "hitting its stride"—it's hitting a wall. And the bull's argument that AI and emerging markets will re-accelerate growth is **speculative storytelling without a single concrete datapoint.**

Here's what's really happening:

### **The Subscription Model Isn't the Moat the Bull Thinks It Is**

The bull argues: *"Switching costs are astronomical. Clients can't leave because they have 500+ workflows built on FactSet."*

**This is backwards logic.** High switching costs cut both ways:

1. **Existing customers have ZERO reason to upgrade or expand** — They've already invested. The incremental value proposition of new features diminishes exponentially. Why pay 15% annual price increases for marginal AI features when your legacy setup works fine?

2. **New customer acquisition is expensive and slowing** — FDS can't grow its customer base at double-digit rates because: (a) the addressable market is relatively small (large institutional asset managers, banks), (b) they have existing relationships with Bloomberg, Refinitiv, etc., and (c) switching to FDS requires wholesale operational changes.

3. **Churn is real, even if not disclosed** — The company doesn't break out churn rates, but when a firm consolidates desks or reduces headcount (increasingly common), they cancel redundant subscriptions. In a recession, *expect this to accelerate.*

### **The Math Doesn't Support a Turnaround**

Let me challenge the bull directly: If FDS is positioned for AI-driven growth, **where's the evidence in the numbers?**

- **FY2024 Revenue:** $2,203.1M
- **AI modules introduced:** Multiple ("Insight Platform," generative AI tools, etc.)
- **Revenue acceleration from these initiatives:** **Zero. None. Nada.**

Revenue *decelerated* from 13% to 5.6% *after* rolling out AI. That's the opposite of a catalyst. The bull is essentially arguing: *"Trust us, the next AI initiative will work, even though the last three didn't move the needle."*

**That's not investment thesis. That's hope.**

## PROBLEM 2: THE MARGIN EXPANSION STORY IS A TEMPORARY MIRAGE

Now here's where the bull really bends the facts. He highlights:

> *"Operating margin expanded 160 bps. EBITDA margin expanded 180 bps. Operational leverage in action!"*

**Correct. But he conveniently ignores WHY.**

### **Margins Expanded Because of Headcount Discipline, Not Pricing Power or Product Mix**

Let's look at the details the bull glossed over:

1. **The company is *not* hiring at the rate of revenue growth** — In a slower-growth environment, FDS can't justify aggressive headcount expansion. So fixed costs are being spread over existing employees, which mechanically expands margins.

2. **This is not sustainable** — You can only squeeze productivity gains for so long before product quality, customer satisfaction, and innovation suffer. The company *is* investing in R&D, but at declining rates relative to revenue.

3. **It's a rearview-mirror story** — The bull is arguing: "Look, margins improved!" But this improvement happened *while revenue growth collapsed*. In the next cycle—when growth stalls further—where does the margin expansion come from? You can't cut people indefinitely without consequences.

### **The Leverage Story Is Backward**

The bull celebrates:

> *"The company paid down $250M in debt while maintaining buybacks!"*

**Here's what that actually signals: management doesn't believe in reinvestment opportunities.**

If FDS saw high-conviction growth opportunities (new markets, new products, acquisitions), they would:
- **Invest in R&D** to capture those markets
- **Make acquisitions** to build scale
- **Expand the go-to-market organization** to accelerate growth

Instead, they're:
- Paying down debt
- Buying back stock

**This is the playbook of a mature, cash-harvesting business**, not a growth story. It's saying: *"We've maximized our TAM, we don't see big opportunities ahead, so we're returning cash to shareholders and de-levering."*

There's nothing wrong with that if you're buying FDS as a cash-cow dividend play. But the bull is marketing it as a *growth* story. **Pick one. You can't have both.**

## PROBLEM 3: THE MACRO ENVIRONMENT IS TURNING AGAINST FDS

Let me pivot to macro headwinds, which the bull almost completely ignores.

### **Fed Rate Cuts → Lower Trading Volume → Lower Analytics Demand**

The Fed is in a rate-cutting cycle. This typically correlates with:

1. **Reduced market volatility** — When rates are falling, there's less financial stress, less portfolio rebalancing, less trading.
2. **Lower transaction volumes** — Fewer trades = less demand for real-time data, research terminals, and analytics.
3. **Client consolidation** — Asset managers facing headwinds cut research staff and consolidate spending.

FactSet's revenue is *partially* tied to activity levels. The bull argues the subscription model is recession-proof. **I'm arguing it's recission-resistant, not recession-proof.**

In a true downturn:
- Asset managers reduce headcount → fewer terminal licenses needed → revenue decline
- Trading volumes drop → clients use the platform less intensely (even if they don't cancel)
- New deal velocity slows → harder to grow customer base

### **The Institutional Client Base Is Under Pressure**

Look at the real-world evidence:

- **Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, and other major investment banks have been on hiring freezes** — This directly impacts FDS's customer base.
- **Asset management consolidation is ongoing** — Fewer, larger firms = fewer terminal licenses needed.
- **Passive investing growth** — Passive managers use *less* FactSet than active managers. As passive AUM grows, overall industry demand for analytics declines.

The bull's counter-argument would be: *"FDS is essential infrastructure; they'll keep spending."*

**My response: They will. But they'll spend less, and growth will slow further.**
