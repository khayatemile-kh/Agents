## **I. THE RATE WINDFALL IS ALREADY EXHAUSTED—NOT STRUCTURAL**

The bull argues: *"Elevated rates are structurally intact and represent 'free money' for AXP."*

**Let me challenge this aggressively:**

Yes, NII surged 32.7% in 2023 ($9.90B → $13.13B). But here's what the bull conveniently glossed over: **this was a one-time benefit from rapid Fed tightening, not a sustainable structural advantage.**

Here are the real dynamics:

**1. Interest Expense Is Growing Faster Than Interest Income**

Look at the 2023 data:
- Interest income: +58.0% YoY
- Interest expense: **+148.1% YoY**

The bull says "AXP captured the bulk of the rate benefit," but the numbers don't support that. Interest expense *more than doubled* while interest income grew 58%. **This is the narrowing margin pattern that should worry investors.**

Here's why: **Deposit competition is intensifying.** Money market funds and high-yield savings accounts now offer 4.5-5.0% yields with zero credit risk. AXP has to compete for deposits by raising rates on savings products. As competition intensifies, the bank's ability to keep deposit rates artificially low erodes rapidly.

**The bull's "free money" claim assumes deposit costs stay low. They won't.**

**2. Prediction Markets Are Pricing Rate Stability, Not Rate Cuts—But That's the Problem**

The bull cites the 7% probability of rate cuts by December 2026 as proof rates stay elevated.

**That's backwards logic.** If prediction markets price an 93% probability of NO rate cuts by December 2026, that means they're also pricing a very realistic scenario: **rates don't fall, but they also don't rise further.** 

Here's the issue: **AXP's NII boost came from the *slope* of rate increases, not the absolute level.** Every time the Fed raised rates 25 bps in 2022-2023, AXP immediately repriced loan yields higher while deposit rates lagged. That *spread expansion* is what created the 32.7% NII growth.

**But once the Fed stops raising rates—which we're already past—that repricing cycle ends.** The company goes from expanding spreads to maintaining flat spreads. That's not a windfall; that's normalization.

And critically: **If we get even one or two 25 bps rate cuts in 2025-2026, NII compresses by $400-600M annually.** The bull is betting that prediction markets are correct and cuts don't happen. But prediction markets have been wrong before, and the base case economists are watching for rate cuts in H2 2025.

**Bottom line: The bull is treating a temporary repricing cycle as permanent structural earnings power. That's dangerous.**

## **II. THE "NEAR-ZERO RECESSION" THESIS IS DANGEROUSLY OVERCONFIDENT**

The bull leans heavily on the 8% recession probability and argues this means credit quality stays pristine.

**I call foul here for three critical reasons:**

**1. Prediction Markets Have a Terrible Track Record on Recessions**

Remember 2022? Nobody priced a 2023-2024 recession—yet the economy nearly tipped multiple times. In fact, prediction markets *systematically underestimate recession probability* because:
- They're heavily influenced by momentum and consensus
- They don't account for tail-risk scenarios (geopolitical shocks, policy errors, financial stress)
- They're backward-looking, not forward-looking

**An 8% recession probability is laughably low.** Real economists—not prediction market gamblers—estimate 15-25% recession probability through 2025-2026, depending on the timeframe and model.

**2. Even Without a Recession, Credit Deterioration Is Already Starting**

Here's the critical data point the bull ignored: **AXP's credit quality is already showing stress signals.**

From the macro report: Unemployment is *expected* to remain benign at 5.0% or below. But we're already seeing:
- Delinquency rates rising across the credit card industry
- Consumer savings rate declining
- Credit card balances hitting record highs
- Personal bankruptcy filings rising

**AXP's affluent cardholder base is not immune to these dynamics.** A high household income ($150K+) doesn't protect you when:
- Student loan repayment restarted in Q4 2023 (major budget shock)
- Rental prices are still elevated
- Healthcare costs are surging
- Childcare costs are at record highs

**The bull assumes affluent = immune. History says that's wrong.** During the 2008 crisis, high-income cardholders had *higher* delinquency rates than the general population because they had more debt to carry.

**3. Transaction Volume Growth Will Slow—Not Accelerate**

The bull argues: *"50% of revenue comes from merchant discount fees, so resilient spending = higher revenue."*

**Two problems with this logic:**

**First, merchant volume growth is already moderating.** Year-over-year growth in card transactions has slowed from 8-10% in 2022-2023 to likely 3-4% in 2024. Why? Because the easy spending comps are behind us. The post-pandemic revenge spending cycle has normalized.

**Second, competition in the premium card space is intense.** Visa and Mastercard are seeing similar volume moderation. But AXP has a unique problem: it owns and operates its own network, unlike Visa/Mastercard, which means it has **higher fixed costs and less pricing power.**

If transaction volumes slow to 2-3% growth (from historical 7-8% growth), AXP's discount fee revenue only grows 2-3%. That's a material deceleration from the bull's "mid-single-digit growth" expectation.

**Bottom line: The bull's recession thesis is built on prediction market data that's historically unreliable. Real credit stress is already visible, and spending growth is already decelerating.**

- Analysts haven't yet seen Q3 results
- They're extrapolating Q1-Q2 trends, which benefited from elevated rates

- They assume the bull case (no recession, continued strong spending)
