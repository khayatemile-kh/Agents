## ADDRESSING THE OBVIOUS CONCERN: THE DEBT RATIO

I know what the bears are going to say: **"That debt-to-equity ratio of 93.97x is insane!"**

They're right to flag it. It *sounds* alarming. But let's dig into what this actually means:

**Context matters enormously here:**

1. **Post-Spinoff Capital Structure:** GEHC was spun out from General Electric in February 2023. The elevated D/E ratio reflects the capital structure assigned during the spinoff, not operational deterioration. The company inherited debt that GE allocated to it.

2. **What the Ratio Actually Reflects:** This D/E ratio is influenced by how debt and equity are calculated in the capital structure. What matters more is **debt service capacity**, which is clearly strong:
   - $1.57B in free cash flow easily covers interest payments on the debt
   - The company is profitable at a 9.3% net margin
   - Current ratio of 1.26x shows adequate liquidity

3. **Peer Context:** Mature healthcare companies with similar business models often carry higher absolute debt levels because the cash flows justify it. The real question is: **Can GEHC service this debt?** The answer is **clearly yes.**

4. **Trending Improvement:** As a newly independent company, GEHC has an opportunity to optimize its capital structure. Many spinoffs reduce leverage in the first 24-36 months post-separation. Watch for debt reduction initiatives in upcoming earnings calls.

**The D/E ratio is a caution flag, not a stop sign.** It warrants monitoring but does *not* invalidate the investment thesis when you examine cash flow coverage and profitability.
