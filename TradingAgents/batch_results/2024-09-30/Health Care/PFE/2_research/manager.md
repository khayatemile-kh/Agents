The bull's FCF-per-share math actually supports the bear here: after dividend payments, only ~$3.28/share of FCF remains for debt service on a balance sheet with extreme leverage — that's a genuine constraint, not a strength.

On technicals, the bear's reading is more granular and more recent: a break below the 50-SMA, a 90% MACD decline, and a 16-point RSI decline in 14 days collectively indicate deteriorating momentum and distribution, not healthy consolidation. The bull's dismissal of these signals as 'coiling' is speculative and lacks technical rigor compared to the bear's point-by-point rebuttal.

Weighing all this, the bear's case is more grounded in verifiable data (peer leverage comparisons, historical yield context, granular technical deterioration) while the bull's case leans heavily on optimistic extrapolation from a single forward EPS estimate with no confirming catalyst. This tips the recommendation toward caution rather than balance — this is not a genuine Hold since the bear's rebuttals were substantively stronger and mostly unaddressed by the bull (especially on debt peer comparisons and technical deterioration). I'm landing on Underweight rather than outright Sell because Pfizer still generates real FCF ($12.5B), maintains investment-grade credit, and the dividend, while at elevated risk, has not been cut yet — so there's no confirmed catalyst for a full exit, but the risk/reward skews negative enough to justify trimming exposure.
