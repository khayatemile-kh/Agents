**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides raise legitimate points, but the bear case exposes a critical, unresolved data inconsistency that the bull never adequately addresses: the debt-to-equity ratio of 201.86x implies a scale of leverage that is mathematically incompatible with the "20x EBITDA interest coverage" claim used to dismiss it. That's not a minor quibble - it's a fundamental question about whether we actually understand BMY's balance sheet risk. When a bull thesis rests partly on "debt is fine, trust the coverage ratio" and the bear shows the coverage math doesn't reconcile with the leverage math, that's a red flag for underwriting a Buy/Overweight call.

On growth: the bull's 44.5% forward EPS growth argument is the crux of the valuation thesis (cheap multiple despite fast growth), but the bear correctly points out this implies ~55% net income growth on a company with already elite 71.7% gross margin and 34.42% operating margin, in a business growing revenue only mid-single-digits historically. Margin expansion of that magnitude with no clear catalyst identified is not credible without company-specific evidence (e.g., a major new drug launch, LOE cliff already priced out, or big cost cuts) - none of which was provided in evidence. That makes the "cheap forward P/E" argument much weaker than presented, since forward P/E is only cheap if you accept an unsubstantiated EPS jump.

On technicals: the golden cross / RSI setup is real and constructive short-term, but the bear rightly frames this as a bounce within a stock still ~31% off its 52-week high, i.e., a recovery, not confirmed trend reversal on the daily chart with no longer-duration confirmation given. That tempers, but doesn't eliminate, the bullish momentum picture.

Valuation-wise, the dividend yield (3.77%) and FCF generation ($8.11B) are genuinely attractive and provide real income/downside support - I don't dismiss the bull case entirely. FCF/NI conversion at 87% is still respectable in absolute terms even if the bear's "gap" framing has some merit.

Net: the bear did more to unsettle the core pillars of the bull thesis (unexplained EPS growth math, unreconciled debt figures) than the bull did to defend them within this round. Given real cash flow and income support, but real doubts about growth assumptions and leverage risk, this is not a "Sell" - the company isn't showing distress signals like negative FCF or covenant breach evidence - but conviction to add or hold full-size exposure isn't warranted either. The right call is to trim / de-risk this position modestly while keeping some exposure for the dividend and cash generation, until the debt/EPS growth questions get clarified in subsequent reporting.
