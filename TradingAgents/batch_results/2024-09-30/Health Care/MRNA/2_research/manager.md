**Rationale**: The bull case rests on three pillars: (1) framing the ~89% revenue collapse from COVID peak as a "deliberate strategic transition" rather than a demand cliff, (2) a pipeline (RSV, flu, mRNA-4157 cancer vaccine) that could generate $3-8B in future revenue, and (3) oversold technicals (RSI ~36, prior readings near 28) suggesting a bounce is imminent. On the financial side, the bear's math is more grounded in verifiable figures (TTM net income -$3.15B, negative gross profit and EBITDA, high cash burn) versus the bull's more speculative narrative that R&D spend will "flip a switch" into profitability. The bull's one strong point—positive FCF of ~$299M despite the net loss—is worth noting as a genuine mitigant, but it doesn't offset the scale of the revenue collapse or the multi-year gap before pipeline assets can plausibly generate offsetting cash flow. The technical oversold argument is a tactical, short-term consideration (mean reversion) and doesn't change the multi-year fundamental picture; oversold conditions can persist or worsen when the underlying growth story is broken, which is what we're seeing here (death cross, broken support).

Weighing this: the bear's rebuttal is more rigorously tied to disclosed figures (actual Q2 revenue, approval status of competing products, partnership economics) while the bull leans heavily on optimistic multi-year projections without near-term evidence of traction. Given the size of the revenue base problem (a company priced at ~$56B market cap generating under $1B in sustainous non-COVID revenue currently, with the largest pipeline catalysts still years from commercial scale and shared economics with partners), the risk/reward skews negative for accumulating a position now. This isn't a "double down on hope" situation; it's a name where the market is still working through re-rating the business post-pandemic, and the pipeline optionality, while real, is not yet substantial enough to justify current multiples or to buy into weakness. I'm not recommending an outright Sell/exit because there is real optionality (RSV/flu diversification, Merck-partnered oncology asset, positive FCF, oversold technical setup that could produce sharp relief rallies), but the weight of evidence favors trimming exposure and waiting for confirmed commercial traction (actual RSV/flu sales prints, flu approval, Phase 3 data on mRNA-4157) before adding capital.
