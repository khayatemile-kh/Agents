**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make points worth weighing, but the bear case lands harder on the two issues that actually matter for risk-adjusted returns: balance-sheet fragility and the fragility of the growth assumption baked into the price.

On leverage: the bull's defense ("industry standard," "FCF covers it") doesn't survive contact with the numbers the bear laid out. A D/E of ~258x is not comparable to peers cited at 1-4x — even if the peer figures are rough estimates, the order-of-magnitude gap is too large to wave away as "capital structure." The bull never actually reconciled the $2.15B FCF figure against a plausible debt-service estimate; the bear's back-of-envelope (interest expense eating 58-77% of FCF) may be imprecise, but it's directionally the kind of stress-test the bull thesis needed to rebut and didn't. A current ratio below 0.7 combined with that much leverage means very little margin for error if trial delays, pharma budget cuts, or a re-financing at higher rates hit.

On the growth story: the 80% forward EPS jump is the crux of the valuation. The bull describes secular tailwinds (GLP-1, oncology, RWE, AI) but never connects them to a specific, sized revenue bridge that gets you from $8.03 to $14.46 EPS. The bear's scenario table is the more useful piece of analysis here — it shows that the stock's fair value is extremely sensitive to hitting a growth number that hasn't been evidenced with contract wins or guidance. When a stock's TTM P/E (32x) sits so far above its forward P/E (18x), the market has already front-run the growth; that's a asymmetric setup skewed to the downside if growth disappoints, not a value gift.

On technicals: this is genuinely closer to a wash. The bull's squeeze/reward-risk framing is reasonable, but the bear correctly notes the MACD deterioration has been persistent (13 sessions negative) and the RSI/MACD trend is more consistent with distribution than accumulation. Neither trend is decisive, so this factor doesn't rescue the bull case, but it also isn't the primary reason to turn bearish.

Net: the bear's structural balance-sheet argument plus the unsubstantiated growth assumption behind the valuation multiple are the more rigorous, evidence-grounded points in this debate. The bull's rebuttals to leverage concerns were qualitative and didn't survive quantitative scrutiny. That tips this to a cautious stance — not a full conviction "sell into the ground" call, because IQV still has real recurring-revenue characteristics and the technical picture isn't a confirmed breakdown yet, but the risk/reward here favors trimming exposure rather than adding or holding steady.
