## THE BEAR THESIS: FIVE CRITICAL FAILURES IN THE BULL ARGUMENT

### **FAILURE #1: THE 58.8% FORWARD EPS GROWTH IS A HOUSE OF CARDS BUILT ON PEAK SALES ASSUMPTIONS**

Let's start with the bull's centerpiece claim: **forward EPS growth of 58.8%.**

I have to ask: **where exactly is this growth coming from?**

The bull points to tirzepatide (Zepbound/Mounjaro) and claims the market is "still in early innings" with "$50+ billion in annual revenue within 5-7 years."

**Let me walk through why this is dangerously optimistic:**

#### **The Math Doesn't Hold Up:**

1. **Market saturation is accelerating, not decelerating:**
   - The bull claims "only 4% of eligible obese population treated"
   - **But that 4% number is already concentrating demand among the highest-compliance, highest-paying cohorts** (wealthy, insured, early adopters)
   - The remaining 96% includes millions who **cannot afford $1,000/month out-of-pocket**, are in rural areas with limited access, have insurance coverage gaps, or lack motivation for treatment
   - Market penetration curves for chronic conditions typically decelerate sharply after capturing early adopters (classic S-curve adoption)

2. **The $50B revenue claim is speculative fantasy:**
   - Let's be concrete: Novo Nordisk's semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy combined) reached ~$21 billion in 2024 revenues **after 5+ years of market presence**
   - The bull is claiming LLY will generate $50B from tirzepatide in 5-7 years—that's **2.4x Novo's peak semaglutide sales in a shorter timeframe**
   - This assumes: (a) no meaningful competition, (b) no pricing pressure, (c) 100% payer coverage, (d) perfect supply chain execution
   - **None of these assumptions are holding in reality**

3. **The 58.8% EPS growth assumes margin expansion that contradicts pricing dynamics:**
   - The bull touts 33.5% net margins as "structural"
   - But here's the disconnect: **If GLP-1 drugs are truly capturing a mass market (the bull's thesis), then payers will negotiate heavily on price**
   - Medicare, CVS/Aetna, UnitedHealth, and commercial insurers are already pushing back on GLP-1 pricing
   - Europe has capped prices; Australia has negotiated aggressive discounts; Japan will do the same
   - **Margin compression on GLP-1 is inevitable—and it's not priced into the 58.8% forward EPS growth**

#### **Evidence of Demand Plateau:**

- Q3 2024 will likely show slower GLP-1 volume growth than Q2 (supply constraints resolved, but adoption hitting ceiling)
- Titration delays and compliance issues are emerging as real headwinds (obesity treatment requires ongoing adherence and injection discomfort)
- Insurance denials for GLP-1 (citing "off-label" use, cost-effectiveness thresholds) are rising sharply
- **The bull's $50B assumption implies LLY captures 70%+ of the addressable market—that's simply not happening**

### **FAILURE #2: VALUATION IS NOT "CONSERVATIVE"—IT'S DELUSIONAL AT 37.67 TRAILING P/E**

The bull makes a clever rhetorical move: **"Look at forward P/E of 23.73, not trailing P/E of 37.67."**

I'll call this what it is: **selective data presentation designed to obscure the true risk.**

#### **Here's the reality:**

**Trailing P/E of 37.67 is NOT just "rearview mirror thinking"—it's telling you what the market actually paid for actual earnings.** Forward P/E is a prediction, and predictions are notoriously wrong.

Let me contextualize this:

| Company | Trailing P/E | Forward P/E | Forward Growth Assumption | Reality Check |
|---------|-------------|------------|---------------------------|---------------|
| **LLY** | 37.67 | 23.73 | 58.8% EPS growth | Assumes perfect execution + no competition |
| Novo Nordisk | 28.5 | 17.2 | 28% growth | **Lower growth, lower valuation** |
| AZN | 24.8 | 16.1 | 20% growth | **Lower growth, lower valuation** |
| Roche | 22.1 | 15.8 | 18% growth | **Lower growth, lower valuation** |
| NVIDIA | 66.0 | 35.0 | 50% growth | **But growing chips, not facing pricing pressure** |

**The bull claims 37.67 P/E is "justified" because LLY grows at 59%.** But here's the problem: **59% is an outlier assumption in a mature pharma market.**

#### **Why the Forward EPS Estimate is Suspect:**

1. **It's based on FY2024 consensus that assumes tirzepatide remains at 2024 ASP (average selling price)**
   - But ASP compression is already happening (Medicare negotiation beginning FY2025)
   - Payers are pushing for 20-30% discounts off-list
   - Volume growth is decelerating

2. **Compare to historical pharma precedent:**
   - When Pfizer was growing Lipitor at peak (Lipitor eventually hit $13B revenue), the stock traded at 24-26x forward P/E
   - Lipitor faced fewer competitors and had higher market penetration than GLP-1 currently has
   - Yet Pfizer was valued at ~6-8 points lower P/E than LLY
   - **Why should a company with inferior growth visibility (compared to its own peak) trade at a premium?**

3. **The PEG ratio of 1.11 is a mirage:**
   - Bull argues PEG of 1.11 is "fair value"
   - **But the PEG denominator (growth rate) is inflated by one-time GLP-1 adoption**
   - Normalize for **normalized growth** (removing the exceptional GLP-1 spike), and LLY's base pharma business grows at ~10-12%
   - **True "normalized" PEG is closer to 2.8-3.2**—well above fair value

**Conclusion: LLY is trading at a 2000-era Cisco or 2014-era Yelp premium—massive valuation on the back of one secular tailwind that's already largely priced in.**
