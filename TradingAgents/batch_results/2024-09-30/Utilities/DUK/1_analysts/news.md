## 5. INVESTMENT IMPLICATIONS FOR DUK

### Risk Factors
1. **Interest Rate Risk (HIGH)**: With 93% odds of no rate cuts through 2026, DUK faces ongoing pressure on its cost of capital. New debt issuance will be expensive, and refinancing existing debt could be costly.
2. **Inflation Pass-Through Risk (MEDIUM)**: While utilities are typically inflationary hedges, regulatory lag and cost-of-capital recovery disputes introduce execution risk. If inflation remains elevated while rate increases lag, margins compress.
3. **Capital Expenditure Demands (MEDIUM)**: The energy transition requires significant CapEx. In a high-rate environment, this pressures returns on equity and cash flow available to shareholders.

### Supportive Factors
1. **Dividend Stability**: Regulated utilities typically maintain dividends even in challenging environments, providing downside support.
2. **Essential Service**: Electricity demand is relatively inelastic, providing stable revenue.
3. **Rate Recovery Mechanisms**: Regulatory frameworks typically allow utilities to recover prudent costs, including financing costs, though with lag.
4. **Inflation Hedge**: Long-duration, regulated cash flows can benefit from persistent moderate inflation if rate recovery mechanisms function.
