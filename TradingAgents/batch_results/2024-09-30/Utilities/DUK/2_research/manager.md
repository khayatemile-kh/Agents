**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the core facts: DUK is a regulated utility with 2023 EBITDA of $13.87B (+12.2% YoY), net margin expanding to 9.79%, but debt growing 7.9% YoY (outpacing 1% revenue growth), interest expense up 23.6% to $3.01B, debt/equity at 162%, debt/EBITDA at 5.8x, and flagged negative free cash flow. The bull's thesis leans heavily on the assumption that regulators will reliably grant offsetting ROE increases to cover rising financing costs, and that current leverage is "strategic" rather than stressed. The bear's strongest counter is that this regulatory-offset assumption is exactly the untested load-bearing pillar: debt is growing faster than revenue, interest expense is compounding at >20% YoY, and there's a real lag between cost incurrence and rate recovery approval, which mechanically compresses margins and cash flow in the interim regardless of eventual outcomes. Critically, the bear correctly flags negative free cash flow as an explicit, undisputed data point in this record — that is a factual near-term red flag the bull never rebuts with a counter-figure, only with qualitative reassurance about "regulatory triage." The bull's peer-multiple argument (18.26x vs NEE/SO/D) is reasonable and shows DUK isn't dramatically overvalued versus utility peers, which tempers outright "Sell," but it doesn't offset the fact that leverage metrics are moving in the wrong direction and cash generation is not currently self-funding the dividend or debt service. Given essential-service demand inelasticity and regulated cost-recovery mechanisms provide a floor against a full bearish collapse thesis, but the trajectory of debt growth outpacing revenue, rising interest burden, and negative FCF are real, currently-observable deteriorations rather than hypothetical risks — the weight of evidence tips toward caution rather than balance. This isn't a genuinely 50/50 debate: the bear's use of concrete, already-realized negative-trend data (debt growth vs revenue growth, interest expense trajectory, negative FCF) outweighs the bull's forward-looking, regulatory-goodwill-dependent narrative that lacks any hard confirming data point (no cited rate case win, no cited approved ROE increase). Therefore I'm leaning cautious/underweight rather than a full Hold, while acknowledging the essential-service defensiveness and peer-relative valuation keep this short of an outright Sell.

2) Do not add to the position or average up until there is concrete evidence of (a) free cash flow turning positive, or (b) a completed rate case showing an ROE increase that demonstrably outpaces the recent 23.6% YoY interest expense growth.
