**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull case rests on three pillars: (1) accelerating EPS/operating income growth, (2) technical momentum confirming fundamentals, and (3) a regulatory framework that guarantees cost recovery, making rate hikes and heavy capex a non-issue. The bear's rebuttal is sharper on the fundamentals: the 21% operating income jump and 16.6% revenue growth in a single year is not typical of a regulated utility and is very likely a one-off from simultaneous rate case approvals rather than a repeatable growth rate—regulated utilities structurally grow EPS in the 4-7% range, not 12-17%, so extrapolating the recent print into a durable growth thesis is the weakest link in the bull argument. The bear also correctly flags the regulatory lag problem (12-24 months between cost increases and rate case approval) and the increasing political/consumer pushback on full cost-of-capital pass-through, which is a real and underappreciated risk the bull simply assumed away with 'customers pay for it.' On negative free cash flow, the bull's 'it's by design' argument is too hand-wavy — not all capex is recovered at full allowed ROE, and capex has been accelerating (7.15B to 7.41B to ~7.6-7.8B TTM) while being increasingly funded by debt issuance in a higher-for-longer rate environment (93% probability of no cuts through 2026), which directly pressures the cost of capital on that growing debt load (D/E 1.71x, $44B debt). That said, the bull's technical picture is real support in the near term — bullish EMA/SMA alignment, positive MACD, RSI not yet overbought, institutional volume — and the dividend is unlikely to be cut given the regulated model's cash generation ($4.70B operating cash flow). So this isn't a full sell case; it's a story of near-term price support colliding with a fundamentally decelerating, capital-intensive growth narrative that's overearning right now and will normalize. Weighing it: the bear's structural argument about unsustainable growth rates and regulatory lag/political risk in a higher-for-longer rate regime outweighs the bull's reliance on a single unusually strong year and short-term technical momentum. I'm not going to full Sell because the downside case isn't a collapse thesis — dividend and cash flow are intact, and technicals show no breakdown — but the risk/reward skews toward trimming ahead of an expected growth normalization and margin compression from rate lag and debt-funded capex.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing EXC exposure by roughly 20-30% into current strength (stock near monthly highs at $37.69, RSI 66.34, approaching overbought) rather than adding on the bull's momentum case.
2) Do not initiate new full-size positions at 14.47x forward P/E if underwriting requires the 12-17% EPS growth rate to persist — instead underwrite forward estimates using a normalized 4-6% regulated-utility revenue/EPS growth trajectory and re-price the fair multiple accordingly; only add back if the stock corrects to reflect that normalized growth.
3) Monitor upcoming rate case filings and PUC decisions closely (18-24 month recovery lag flagged by the bear) — treat any signs of partial (vs. full) cost-of-capital pass-through or political pushback on bill increases as confirmation to reduce further.
4) Watch capex-to-financing trend (capex accelerating from $7.15B to $7.41B to ~$7.6-7.8B TTM funded increasingly by debt issuance, e.g., $5.83B LT debt issuance in 2023) against the 93%-probability higher-for-longer rate backdrop through 2026; rising financing costs without full recovery would be a further negative catalyst.
5) Retain a smaller residual position for the dividend income/cash-generation quality and to stay long the technical uptrend as a hedge, but size it as a core-only holding, not an overweight growth bet.
6) Set a technical stop/trim trigger if price breaks below the 50 SMA (~$35.49) with rising volume, which would confirm the fundamental deceleration thesis is being priced in.
