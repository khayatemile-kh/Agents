The bear's strongest points are more persuasive on balance for a 'growth already priced in' argument: (1) the rate-cut tailwind cuts both ways — refinancing savings are a modest 2-3% EPS benefit, while utility equities are yield-sensitive and can see multiple compression if the 10-year moves, so the net benefit is ambiguous, not clearly positive; (2) valuation at 18.8x forward P/E and 3.06x P/B is above sector norms, so the market has already substantially priced in the AI-demand/electrification story — this caps the risk/reward skew even if the story plays out; (3) the -$17.77B FCF gap is real and must be funded continuously via debt and equity issuance, which is fine in benign credit markets but is a genuine vulnerability to capital-markets stress, credit downgrades, or regulatory pushback on allowed ROE — the bull's dismissal of this as merely 'a feature' understates the tail risk; (4) execution risk on megaprojects and increasing competition for corporate PPAs could compress the premium returns bulls are underwriting.

Weighing these: the bull's macro and secular-growth arguments are sound but largely reflect the consensus/priced-in case, and the technical setup supports staying invested rather than adding aggressively. The bear's critique of valuation stretch and liquidity/leverage fragility is the more discriminating argument here — it doesn't refute the quality of the business, but it argues convincingly that the risk/reward at 18.8x forward earnings and thin equity cushion is no longer asymmetric to the upside. This tips the balance toward a constructive-but-not-aggressive stance: the underlying utility franchise and regulated cash flow support holding/maintaining exposure, but the valuation and financing-risk concerns argue against adding fresh capital or treating this as a high-conviction buy right now. Since the technical trend remains intact and the long-term regulated cash-flow support for the dividend is real, this isn't a sell candidate; but with valuation full and FCF/leverage risk unresolved, it doesn't merit an overweight/buy upgrade either. Genuinely balanced evidence on the near-term risk/reward supports a Hold.
