Let me put this differently: if I told you I had a $680M quarter but warned you that $147M of that came from a one-time working capital swing, would you annualize the full $680M and extrapolate it forward? ## **THE BOLLINGER BAND ARGUMENT REVEALS A FUNDAMENTAL MISUNDERSTANDING OF RISK**

The aggressive analyst claims that being 1% below the upper Bollinger band means "consolidation is over and the next leg up is beginning." But this fundamentally misunderstands what Bollinger bands measure.

Bollinger bands are volatility-based support and resistance levels. When price approaches the upper band, it means price is extended relative to the moving average—in other words, the stock has moved significantly away from its average price. That's exactly when reversions tend to occur.

Here's the historical reality: in **trending markets**, Bollinger band touches are often followed by reversions toward the middle band. The upper band is not a trampoline; it's a boundary that indicates extension. When you're 1% away from that boundary, you're in an extended position.

The aggressive analyst says "stocks regularly break through upper Bollinger bands during strong uptrends." Yes, they do—occasionally. But much more frequently, they touch the band and revert. The risk-reward at $187.40, just 1% below the upper band, is asymmetric to the downside. You have maybe 1-2% of upside to the band, but 7-10% of downside to the 50-day moving average at $175.58.

That's not a compelling setup to add. That's a setup that screams "let this thing pull back before adding more."
