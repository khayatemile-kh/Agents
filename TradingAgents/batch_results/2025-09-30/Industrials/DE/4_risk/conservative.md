## The Technical Argument Conflates Momentum With Direction

The Aggressive Analyst made a clever observation about RSI trending *into* oversold rather than bouncing out of it. And yes, they're right that this suggests downside continuation rather than reversal. But here's the logical flaw: **just because downside momentum is strong doesn't mean the price target is lower.**

Technical breakdowns are signal generators, not price forecasters. An RSI at 35 trending lower on a stock that's already down 12% from August highs tells me one thing: **capitulation is in process, and the final sell-off is likely underway.** But capitulation phases are finite. They end. And when they end, they often end abruptly—with V-shaped reversals that punish shorts and burn out bear positions.

The Aggressive Analyst cited the volume spike to 1.95M as evidence of "continued capitulation." Actually, I'd argue it's the *opposite*. In a true capitulation spike, volume should *accelerate* on follow-through days. If we don't see 1.8M+ volume on the next down day or two, then what we're looking at is a **capitulation tail**—the final exhale of sellers who held on hoping for a bounce. That's textbook reversal setup.

Here's the practical reality: the stock is trading below the lower Bollinger Band at $453.18 vs. $454.83. That's a 0.37% gap. The Bollinger Band is *designed* to act as dynamic support/resistance. When price trades just barely outside it on elevated volume after a 12% drawdown, it's typically a reversal setup, not a breakdown confirmation.

**The Aggressive Analyst's error:** They're using *momentum indicators* to justify a *directional position*. But momentum is mean-reverting. The more deeply oversold you get (RSI at 35), the higher the probability of reversal within the next 5-10 trading days. This isn't theory—it's empirical fact in equity markets. A stock doesn't go from RSI 35 straight to RSI 10 without at least a 2-3% bounce in between.
