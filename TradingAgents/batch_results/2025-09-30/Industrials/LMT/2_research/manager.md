**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points, but weighing them carefully tips the balance toward caution rather than aggressive buying. The bull's core argument—stable defense demand, low recession risk, and Q3 profitability—is real, but the bear correctly identifies that the Q3 'explosion' is largely a comparison against an anomalously weak Q2 (negative FCF, depressed net income) rather than evidence of a structural step-change. The tax rate normalization driving much of the EPS beat is a one-time item, not a repeatable operational improvement, and the bull never adequately rebuts this. On leverage, the bear's debt-to-FCF framing (~4x) is a more meaningful risk metric than the bull's debt-to-revenue framing, especially in a rate environment where cuts are not expected until late 2026 at the earliest—meaning refinancing risk and interest expense drag are real headwinds the bull glosses over. On technicals, both sides actually agree on the facts (RSI 80.83, price essentially at the Bollinger Upper Band, MACD histogram plateauing)—the bull frames this as 'accelerating trend' while the bear correctly notes the technical report itself flags this as a textbook overbought setup ripe for a pullback, and that 7+ day RSI>80 with price at the band has historically preceded consolidation. The unexplained July crash (-10.8% on no visible news) is a legitimate lingering risk that the recovery doesn't erase. That said, the bear doesn't present evidence of actual demand destruction, contract losses, or margin deterioration going forward—this is a valuation/technical/leverage caution story layered on top of a fundamentally intact defense franchise with government-backed revenue visibility, a reasonable forward P/E (16.2x) and PEG (~1.0), and continued capital returns (dividend + buybacks). Given a stock that has run up 22%+ off a crash low, sits at extreme overbought technical extremes, and whose recent 'profitability explosion' is partly non-repeatable (tax rate), while carrying leverage that's more strained than the bull suggests, this is not the moment to add aggressively into strength. But the underlying business (backlog, government demand stability, margin trajectory) doesn't support an outright sell/exit either. The appropriate stance is to trim/de-risk the position size at these technical extremes and reassess entry after the RSI/Bollinger extreme resolves or Q4 tax-rate-normalized earnings confirm the profitability trend is durable, rather than chasing the rally or holding full-size exposure into an overbought, leverage-sensitive setup.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing LMT exposure by roughly 20-30% into current strength rather than adding at these levels—RSI 80.83 and price pinned at the Bollinger Upper Band signal elevated pullback risk in the near term. 2) Do not initiate new/full-size positions here; wait for either (a) a pullback toward the 50 SMA (~$437) / 10 EMA (~$473) zone, or (b) Q4 earnings confirming the profitability improvement persists with a normalized tax rate (18-19%) rather than the favorable one-time rate seen in Q3. 3) Monitor the debt-to-FCF ratio (~4x) and any disclosed interest expense figures next quarter—if debt servicing costs are rising materially due to the higher-for-longer rate environment (0% probability of cuts through Sept 2026), that reinforces the underweight stance. 4) Reassess working capital swings next quarter; if the Q3 improvement reverses back toward negative FCF territory as seen in Q2, treat it as confirmation of lumpy/non-structural cash generation and maintain reduced exposure. 5) Keep a residual core position given intact long-term demand visibility (backlog, geopolitical tailwinds, dividend yield ~2.63%), but size it as underweight relative to prior conviction until technical extremes normalize and earnings quality (ex-tax-benefit) is confirmed.
