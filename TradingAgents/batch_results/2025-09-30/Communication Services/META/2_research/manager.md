**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make points that partially hold up, but when I weigh them, the bear's forensic dissection of the cash flow and CapEx math is more rigorous and harder to dismiss than the bull's narrative framing.

On cash flow: the bull's headline 58.6% OCF conversion and $45B annualized FCF story leans heavily on a single quarter that included a ~$19.87B deferred tax adjustment and ~$5.56B in SBC add-backs. The bear is right that these are largely non-recurring/non-cash-in-substance items that inflate the reported figure; a 'clean' run-rate closer to high-single-digit % conversion is a meaningfully different picture than the bull's framing, and the trader needs to size positions off the more conservative number, not the flattering one.

On valuation: the bull's PEG (0.82) and forward P/E (18.4x) argument assumes multiple expansion is warranted, but the bear correctly flags that the implied earnings growth embedded in that multiple (~16-18%) is roughly double current revenue growth (7.9% YoY), meaning the market/bulls are underwriting significant margin expansion that hasn't shown up yet — R&D is up 17% QoQ while revenue growth is flat-to-modest. That's a real disconnect the bull didn't adequately resolve, and it directly cuts against the 'value price for a growth company' thesis.

On CapEx/PP&E: the bear's point that ~$75B annualized CapEx run-rate could consume more than the 'clean' FCF is important — this is a capital intensity risk, not just a moat-building story. If ad demand growth doesn't accelerate to justify the infrastructure buildout, this becomes a drag on returns on capital, and the bull didn't quantify a payoff timeline or offer a scenario where the ROI shows up in numbers yet.

On AI positioning: I find this argument more balanced — Meta is genuinely monetizing AI in ad-targeting/recommendation today (this is real, not purely speculative), so the bear's 'defensive spend only' framing is somewhat overstated. This is the one area where the bull's case is stronger.

On macro: I'll give partial credit to both — no recession + Democratic control is a genuine tailwind for ad spend and reduces near-term regulatory tail risk, but the bear is correct that in a 'sticky higher for longer' rate environment, a company behaving like a growth/capex story (not a mature cash cow) faces multiple compression risk, and the technical setup (below 50 SMA, bearish MACD) is more consistent with that than the bull's 'contrarian entry' narrative.

On balance sheet: the bull's clarification about capital leases vs true debt is fair and I accept it — leverage concerns are overstated by the raw D/E ratio, and liquidity is solid. This doesn't offset the valuation/cash flow concerns though.

Net: the fundamental cash-generation story is real but weaker than the bull's headline framing, the valuation is not as clearly 'cheap' once you interrogate the embedded growth assumptions, and CapEx intensity is a genuine risk that needs to show ROI in coming quarters. This isn't a full bear/sell case — Meta still has a strong core ads business, low net debt, and real AI monetization — but the balance of the debate tilts toward caution on adding exposure at current levels until (1) the cash flow quality normalizes and is visible without one-off tax adjustments, and (2) CapEx-to-revenue-growth ratio shows signs of convergence (i.e., revenue growth accelerating or CapEx moderating). I'm landing on a cautious/underweight stance rather than a full sell, since the balance sheet and core ad business are still solid and this isn't a broken thesis, just an overpriced/overhyped one at the moment relative to its clean fundamentals.
