In technical analysis, there's a principle: volume should expand on the direction of the primary trend. If we're truly in a healthy uptrend that's going to continue, we should expect volume to pick up as price stabilizes and the market gets ready to make the next leg up. Instead, volume is drying up. Institutions are not re-entering. Retail traders are sitting on the sidelines. That's not discipline; that's selling at the worst possible time.

## The Macro Headwind Is Not "Baked In"—It's About to Become Active

The Aggressive Analyst argues that the 93% market probability of zero Fed rate cuts in 2026 is already priced into valuations at 22.21x forward PE. I fundamentally disagree. Here's why:

**The tech sector and growth stocks *in particular* are still adjusting to the reality of persistent high rates.** We've had a relief rally since August (GOOG up 21.8%, broad tech up 15-18%), partly because markets briefly priced in rate cuts in late summer. Now that the Fed has pushed back and markets have repriced to zero cuts in 2026, we're entering the *second phase* of adjustment—where growth multiples compress further.

The forward PE of 22.21x is not cheap, and it's certainly not a "screaming bargain" for a company facing 12-24 months of elevated rates. Compare this to the historical median forward PE for GOOG, which has ranged from 18-20x during "normal" rate environments. At 22.21x forward PE in a high-rate regime, we're paying a *premium* for the privilege of owning a growth stock in a period of monetary constraint.

**Moreover, elevated rates have real, immediate effects on Alphabet's advertising business, which represents 80%+ of revenue.** When rates are high, consumer spending on discretionary goods and services declines. Small and mid-sized businesses (critical advertisers on Google) pull back on marketing budgets. CPM (cost per mille) pressures emerge. We haven't yet seen the Q4 2025 results, but if advertising weakness materializes (which is likely given consumer spending slowdowns), you're going to see multiple compression far more severe than any modest upside from cloud growth.

The Aggressive Analyst argues that high rates benefit Alphabet because they reduce VC-funded competition. That's true at the margins. But the macro headwind of reduced consumer and business spending on advertising is far more material to Alphabet's revenue and earnings than any slight advantage from reduced venture funding.
