## THE CRITICAL FLAW IN THE BULL'S MACRO THESIS: HIGHER RATES DON'T HELP VZ, THEY BURY IT

The bull opens with a seductive argument: "93% probability of zero rate cuts in 2026 means VZ's 5.69% dividend yield is competitive with bonds." This is **dangerously misleading**.

Let me explain why:

### **The Dividend Yield Trap**

Yes, VZ yields 5.69%. But here's what the bull conveniently ignores:

**When rates stay "higher for longer," the discount rate applied to future cash flows RISES.** This means:

- **The present value of VZ's future dividend stream compresses.** A stock yielding 5.69% that's expected to grow dividends 2-3% annually is worth *less* in a 5%+ risk-free environment than in a 1-2% environment.
- **The bull claims VZ's yield is "competitive" with Treasury bonds.** False. A Treasury bond has ZERO credit risk and is backed by the U.S. government's taxing power. VZ has **$170.5B in debt and faces cyclical business risks**. You're being compensated for that risk with only 1.7-2% additional yield (5.69% vs. ~4.0% Treasury). **That's insufficient risk premium.**

**Mathematical proof:** If the 10-year Treasury is at 4.0% and VZ trades at $41.14 with a 5.69% yield:
- **Risk premium: 5.69% - 4.0% = 1.69%**
- For a cyclical telecom company with $170.5B debt, a 1.69% risk premium is **criminally low**.
- In a recession scenario (6-10% unemployment), VZ's revenue would decline 3-5%, yet you'd be locked into a fixed 5.69% "yield" while the stock price collapses 20-30%.

The bull is essentially arguing: "Get paid an extra 1.7% to take on leverage, cyclicality, and regulatory risk." That's a *bad trade*, not a defensive position.
