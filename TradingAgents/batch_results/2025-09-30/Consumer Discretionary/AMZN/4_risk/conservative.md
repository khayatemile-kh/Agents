When you see +12.5 percentage points of movement in one week on the $210B capex threshold, that's not "accelerating institutional conviction"—that's classic trend-following behavior. Traders see other traders moving, so they move. This is how bubbles form.

The aggressive case wants us to "front-run institutional inevitability." That's not investing—that's momentum chasing. And momentum works until it doesn't.

## Volume Compression Is Not Bottoming—It's Indecision

The bull thesis says volume compression means "selling has exhausted" and "buyers realize the dip is a gift." I need to push back hard on this interpretation because it reflects dangerous wishful thinking.

Volume compression in a downtrend typically means one of two things:
1. **The sellers have temporarily stopped, but there's no conviction among buyers yet** (indecision, not bottoming)
2. **Both sides are waiting for a catalyst or clearer direction** (consolidation, not recovery)

Volume compression is NOT evidence of bottoming. Bottoming is evidenced by volume EXPANSION on upside moves. When we see buyers coming in aggressively with volume, pushing price back above the 50 SMA and 10 EMA, *then* we talk about recovery.

Right now, we have declining volume on declining price. That's called "capitulation in slow motion." The aggressive analyst interprets this as "buyers seeing value." I interpret it as "nobody's confident enough to commit capital right now."

And frankly, that makes sense. We're in earnings season. We have unresolved questions about capex ROI. We have macro uncertainty (Fed policy, interest rates). In that environment, volume compression is traders waiting for clarity, not evidence that the bottom is in.
