## DECONSTRUCTING PILLAR 1: "TOWERING PROFITABILITY" IS A MIRAGE

The bull points to a 17.44% net profit margin and celebrates Amazon's "solved profitability equation." This is precisely the kind of misleading analysis that gets investors into trouble.

### **The Earnings Quality Problem: $10.19 Billion in One-Time Gains**

Let's look at Q3 2025 closely. The bull celebrates $21.19 billion in net income. But here's what they conveniently gloss over:

**Q3 2025 net income included $10.19 billion in gains on sale of securities.** That's 48% of reported earnings from *non-operating activities*.

When we strip out these unusual items, Q3 operating net income was only $13.49 billion—a 36% decline from the reported figure.

**Why does this matter?** Because:

1. **These gains are non-recurring.** Amazon won't report $10B in security sales every quarter. When that accounting boost disappears, we're left with normalized earnings that are far less impressive.

2. **It masks deteriorating operational performance.** If operating earnings had been strong, Amazon wouldn't need to offset them with asset sales. This is a classic sign of earnings manipulation—not intentionally fraudulent, but strategic timing to mask operational weakness.

3. **The TTM margin figure (17.44%) is artificially inflated.** Normalize for the $10B gain across four quarters, and Amazon's true operating margin is closer to 12-13%—still good for retail, but nowhere near the 17.44% the bull cited.

### **Margin Expansion Is Already Decelerating**

Looking at the quarterly progression:

| Period | Gross Margin | Operating Margin | Net Margin (Adjusted) |
|--------|--------------|------------------|----------------------|
| Q1 2025 | ~49.2% | ~12.1% | ~11.0% |
| Q2 2025 | ~50.1% | ~12.8% | ~10.8% |
| Q3 2025 | **50.8%** | ~13.8%* | **7.5%*** |

*Operating margin inflated by unusual gains; normalized closer to 8-9%  
**Adjusted for one-time items

**The critical observation:** Operating margin expanded from Q1 to Q2 to Q3, but only because of cost-cutting and operational leverage—*not* from pricing power or structural margin improvement. And when we normalize Q3 for the $10B gain, margins actually *contracted* sequentially.

**This is the opposite of what the bull suggests.** They're pointing to headline profitability while ignoring the deteriorating quality of earnings and the acceleration of one-time items needed to prop up reported figures.

### **Return on Equity: The Denominator Trap**

The bull cites 30.56% ROE as evidence of "capital efficiency." But let's parse this:

- **ROE = Net Income / Stockholders' Equity**
- **Net Income (TTM): $135.28B** (inflated by the $10B gain we discussed)
- **Stockholders' Equity: $369.63B** (includes $229.34B in retained earnings)

**Here's the problem:** Amazon has been retaining 100% of earnings, so equity is compounding every quarter. A high ROE can actually be a sign of *capital trap*—the company is generating returns, but those returns are declining because the equity base is growing faster than earnings.

**Normalized ROE (adjusting for the $10B one-time gain) = ~24% instead of 30.56%.** Still respectable, but tells a different story: the company's incremental returns on deployed capital are *declining*.

## DECONSTRUCTING PILLAR 2: THE $200 BILLION CAPEX TRAP

This is where the bull's argument really falls apart. They frame Amazon's $200B+ capex commitment as a "moat-widening" strategic advantage. I see it as a **value-destroying disaster in the making.** Here's why:

### **1. The Capital Intensity Death Spiral**

The bull points to historical precedent: "AWS capex peaked in 2014-2016, then margins expanded."

**That's a fundamentally flawed comparison.** Here's the actual timeline:

- **2014-2016:** AWS capex was 17-19% of *AWS revenue* (~$2-3B absolute spend) as AWS scaled from $5-8B in revenue
- **2026:** Amazon is committing $200B capex on a $720B revenue base

**That's 27.8% of total company revenue, not AWS revenue alone.**

More importantly:

| Period | AWS Revenue | AWS CapEx | CapEx % of AWS Revenue |
|--------|-------------|-----------|----------------------|
| 2015-2016 | ~$12B | ~$2-3B | 17-19% |
| 2026 (Projected) | ~$100B (est.) | ~$150-170B (AWS portion) | **150-170%** |

**Amazon is planning to spend 10x more capex as a percentage of AWS revenue than it did during the previous scaling phase.** The bull is citing precedent from a fundamentally different era of cloud infrastructure.

### **2. Capex-to-Revenue: A Divergence That Spells Trouble**

Let's look at what the financials actually show for capex trends:

| Period | Revenue | CapEx | CapEx % of Revenue |
|--------|---------|-------|-------------------|

**CapEx is accelerating as a percentage of revenue, not stabilizing.** This is the opposite trajectory of what happened in 2017-2019 (when capex as a % of revenue declined as infrastructure matured).

**What does this mean?** Amazon is entering a phase of sustained, elevated capex that will suppress free cash flow for years, not just 2-3 years as the bull suggests.
