The bull's technical argument (RSI bounce, MACD convergence) is weak support for a fundamental thesis - it's short-term noise, not evidence of a structural turn. The bull's DCF range ($31-60) is so wide it's not actionable, and by their own admission the current price sits at the "lower end" of a normalized range built on optimistic EBITDA assumptions ($1.6-1.8B) that aren't supported by the deteriorating cash flow and margin trend actually observed in the data.

Weighing both sides: the bear's arguments are grounded in the actual reported trend (declining OCF, compressing margins, rising intangible risk, short-term debt reliance) while the bull's case requires assuming away the bad quarter's charges and hoping for margin mean-reversion that hasn't been evidenced yet. Fundamentals are deteriorating in the areas that matter (cash generation, tangible equity, margin durability), and technicals are unambiguously bearish (below all major moving averages, negative MACD). This isn't a "true toss-up" - the bear thesis has the stronger, more evidence-grounded argument, but it's not a full-blown "sell everything" collapse either since there's no news of imminent covenant breach or actual impairment yet.
