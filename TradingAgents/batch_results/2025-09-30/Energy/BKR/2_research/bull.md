## PILLAR 4: POSITIONED IN A SECTOR WITH STRUCTURAL TAILWINDS

Now let's talk about the *why*—the fundamental demand drivers supporting BKR.

### Global Energy Demand Resilience

The energy transition narrative is nuanced. Yes, renewables are growing—but global energy demand continues to expand:
- **2024-2030:** Global energy demand projected to grow 1.5-2% annually
- **Oil & gas** still expected to represent 75%+ of primary energy through 2030
- **Energy poverty:** 750M+ people lack reliable electricity access; energy accessibility drives long-term demand

**This means:** Energy companies and their service providers (like BKR) will face sustained demand for the next 5-10+ years.

### Capex Cycle Tailwinds

Major oil & gas operators (Shell, Chevron, ExxonMobil, Saudi Aramco, etc.) have collectively increased upstream capex:
- **2021-2022:** Depressed capex (post-COVID, energy transition uncertainty)
- **2023-2025:** Robust capex recovery driven by:
  - Higher commodity prices creating cash generation
  - Depleting reserves requiring replacement production
  - Geopolitical instability (Russia sanctions, Middle East tensions) supporting energy security rationale

**Geopolitical Risk Premium (from macro analysis):** The prediction market data shows **16% probability of U.S.-Iran conflict by 2027, up +1.0pp weekly**. This geopolitical tail risk typically **supports crude oil prices**, which in turn drives operator capex budgets—and BKR benefits directly.

### Technology & Deepwater Complexity

As fields deplete and operators move to more challenging environments (deeper offshore, harsher climates), they increasingly rely on sophisticated oilfield services and advanced equipment—BKR's core competency. This creates a **structural pricing power advantage** as complexity drives switching costs and technology premiums.
