## **1. PROFITABILITY EXPANSION IS EXPLOSIVE—THIS ISN'T A REVENUE STORY**

Here's what bulls need to understand: **WMT's net income jumped 25.3% year-over-year to $19.44B in FY2025.** That's not a revenue bump—that's **earnings leverage**, and it tells us something critical is working.

**The Numbers Don't Lie:**

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2024 | Growth |
|--------|--------|--------|---------|
| Net Income | $19.44B | $15.51B | **+25.3%** |
| Operating Income | $29.35B | $27.01B | **+8.6%** |
| EBITDA | $42.01B | $36.38B | **+15.4%** |
| Revenue | $680.99B | $648.13B | **+5.1%** |

Do you see what's happening here? **Revenue grew 5.1%, but earnings grew 25.3%.** That's a *multiplier effect*. This means:

- **Gross margin expanded** from 24.39% to 24.85%—they're selling the same stuff at better prices
- **Operating leverage is working**—fixed costs are being spread over slightly higher revenue
- **Cost discipline is tight**—the company is running lean

This is the hallmark of a business hitting peak operational efficiency. For a retailer of Walmart's scale, this level of earnings acceleration is *exceptional*. The bears will tell you "retail is mature, growth is limited"—but earnings don't lie. **If management can continue expanding margin while growing revenue even modestly, EPS growth will outpace revenue growth for years.**

## **2. THE CASH GENERATION ENGINE IS HUMMING**

Let's talk about what really matters: **cash**. You can massage accounting all day, but cash flow is truth.

**WMT generated $36.44B in Operating Cash Flow (OCF) in FY2025**, up 2% from FY2024. More importantly, **Free Cash Flow of $12.66B** provides a war chest for:
- Shareholder returns (dividends + buybacks)
- Strategic growth investments (capex is up to $23.78B YoY—a strategic *increase*)
- Debt paydown
- Flexibility for M&A or pivots

**Here's the clincher:** While FCF appears flat YoY ($12.66B vs. $15.12B), that's *by design*. Management is **intentionally reinvesting more in capex** ($23.78B vs. $20.61B), signaling confidence in future returns. This is not weakness—it's capital deployment discipline.

The FCF/Net Income ratio of 65% is *high-quality*. For every dollar of earnings, 65 cents becomes real cash. Try finding that in many growth stocks.

## **3. BALANCE SHEET IS FORTRESS-LIKE**

The bears will point to rising debt ($65.01B in Q3). Fine. Let me address that head-on:

**Net Debt-to-EBITDA of 0.81x is *pristine* for a retailer.** For context:
- Target's net debt/EBITDA: ~1.5x
- Amazon's net debt/EBITDA: ~0.3x (but they also have zero debt practically)
- Costco's net debt/EBITDA: ~0.4x

At 0.81x, **WMT has fortress-level balance sheet safety while maintaining financial optionality.** The company can refinance easily, access capital markets at favorable rates, and weather downturns without stress.

Moreover, **stockholders' equity grew 8.5% to $91.01B** in FY2025. The company is building its equity base faster than it's accumulating debt—that's *accretive* capital structure management.

The negative working capital of -$21.5B? **That's a feature, not a bug.** Walmart collects cash from customers faster than it pays suppliers. It's a self-financing machine. Retailers *dream* of this dynamic.

**This is a Walmart goldmine:**

**Higher-For-Longer Rates:**
- WMT's low leverage (0.81x net debt/EBITDA) means rising rates don't hurt it
- Competitors with higher debt loads face refinancing pressure—WMT steals share
- Discount retail *thrives* when consumers get squeezed by rate pressure

**Low Recession Risk:**
- Consumer discretionary gets hit first in downturns
- **WMT's focus on essentials (groceries, daily necessities) is recession-proof**

- This is a secular tailwind for WMT

**The secular narrative:** When the economy is good, people have money and go to Walmart. When it's bad, people *have to* go to Walmart. **It's the ultimate barbell play.**
