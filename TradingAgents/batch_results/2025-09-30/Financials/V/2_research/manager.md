The bull's technical argument (symmetrical triangle, neutral RSI, negative MACD as 'coiling spring') is speculative and not decision-driving either way — technicals are genuinely mixed/neutral here, which cuts against high-conviction action either way.

Weighing it: Visa remains a structurally excellent business with a durable network-effect moat, elite margins, and low fundamental risk of business deterioration — this argues against a Sell or Underweight. But the bear's valuation and growth-quality critique is the stronger, more numerically grounded argument in this specific round: the premium multiple assumes growth rates that aren't clearly corroborated by the underlying quarterly cadence, and heavy reliance on buybacks for EPS growth is a real sustainability concern at current payout levels relative to FCF. Given quality-of-business is intact but the multiple leaves little room for error and the bull's growth assumptions look stretched under scrutiny, the appropriate stance is a measured, cautious one rather than aggressive buying or selling — this is genuinely a case where the fundamentals justify holding the position (don't exit a compounder) while the valuation/growth mismatch argues against adding aggressively.
