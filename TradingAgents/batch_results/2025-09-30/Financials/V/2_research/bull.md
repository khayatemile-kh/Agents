## 4. COMPETITIVE MOAT: Network Effects are Deeper Than Ever

Let me address a potential bear angle head-on: "Payment processing is commoditizing; competition is intensifying."

**This fundamentally misunderstands Visa's moat.** Here's why:

### Network Effects Create Durable Advantage
- **Merchants** adopt Visa because consumers use it (network effect)
- **Consumers** use Visa because merchants accept it (positive feedback loop)
- **Financial institutions** integrate Visa's rails because the network is mandatory (switching costs)

The bear might cite: "But PayPal, Square, fintechs, and BNPL platforms are eating Visa's lunch!"

**My Rebuttal:** Most of these companies *use Visa's network*. PayPal settles through Visa/Mastercard. Square's payments run on legacy networks. Even Buy Now, Pay Later platforms have to settle somehow—and they often use Visa's infrastructure.

Visa isn't a payments originator losing share to new competitors; **it's the rails those competitors run on.** That's a fundamentally different business.

### Pricing Power Remains Intact
Visa's service fee revenue grew 14.2% YoY in recent quarters *without* transaction volume growth faltering. This indicates merchants and banks are accepting price increases, which typically happens only when:
1. Network effects make switching impossible
2. The service is genuinely valuable
3. Alternatives don't exist or are inferior
