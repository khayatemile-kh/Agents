The aggressive analyst is asking us to sell into that bounce window. That's precisely backwards.

## II. The MACD "Collapse" Argument Confuses Momentum Normalization with Trend Death

The aggressive analyst presents a 73% collapse in MACD (from 4.63 to 1.20) as evidence of structural momentum death. But let's examine what's *actually* happening here:

MACD moved from +4.63 (peak momentum during overbought rally) to +1.20 (normalized momentum during correction). The histogram turned negative (MACD below signal line), which is true. But here's what the aggressive analyst isn't saying: **MACD still remains positive in absolute terms** (1.20 > 0). That means the 10 EMA is still above the 26 EMA—the fundamental definition of MACD—which indicates that short-term momentum is still above intermediate-term momentum.

A true structural breakdown would be MACD crossing below zero, which would mean the 10 EMA has fallen below the 26 EMA. We're nowhere near that. What we have is a normalization from overbought MACD to neutral MACD during a healthy correction. That's *exactly* what you expect after a 11% rally.

Moreover, the aggressive analyst is treating the bearish crossover (MACD < Signal) as if it's destiny. But MACD crossovers are *lagging indicators*. They confirm trends after they've already started moving, not before. By the time MACD generates a bearish crossover signal, many intermediate reversals have already occurred. The fact that we got a capitulative flush on high volume *after* the MACD crossover suggests the worst selling is already behind us, not ahead.

If MACD momentum were truly broken, we'd expect to see accelerating downside volume and progressively lower lows. Instead, we're seeing *declining* volume as price approaches support (2.6M shares on Sept 29, then a spike flush on Sept 30, but no follow-through deterioration). That's the opposite of trend death—that's exhaustion.
