## Where the Aggressive Analyst Is Right—And Where They Overreach

RSI at 77.44, price 2.4% above Bollinger bands, and 12% above the 10-EMA—these are real signals. The historical precedent they cite (80% of RSI >75 episodes preceded pullbacks) is directionally sound. I don't dispute the technical exhaustion signals.

But here's where the aggressive analyst commits a logical error: they treat "overbought" as synonymous with "imminent reversal." That's not what the data shows. Overbought means elevated probability of near-term consolidation or pullback, not certainty of a crash. There's a material difference between "likely to pull back 5-10% over 2-3 weeks" and "this is a climactic top and you must sell immediately."

The aggressive analyst models a 12-15% downside move to $125-130 as "high probability in the next 1-3 months." That might be correct. But they're also assuming this pullback represents a major inflection point rather than what it likely is: a healthy consolidation within a broader uptrend. When you're 49.5% higher in a month on an improving fundamentals backdrop, a 12-15% pullback isn't a vindication of the bear thesis—it's normal market behavior.

More problematically, the aggressive analyst is conflating "margin normalization will happen eventually" with "margin normalization will happen soon." Yes, I agree HOOD's 47.8% operating margins are elevated relative to normalized 29% levels. That's not one quarter of anomaly—that's two consecutive quarters of elevated margins with improving operational leverage.

## The Margin Question: Neither Analyst Has the Full Picture

Let me address the fundamental disagreement on margins directly, because this is where the analysis can be sharpened.

The conservative analyst is correct that HOOD's normalized margins should be lower than current 47.8% levels. But let me challenge the assumption that they should compress all the way back to 29%. Here's why:

First, the company has demonstrated operational improvements that aren't purely cyclical. Look at the data:
- Q2 2025 operating margin: 44.4%
- Q3 2025 operating margin: 49.8%

The 540 basis point improvement quarter-over-quarter is partially volume-driven (yes), but it's also partially driven by the company's ability to scale operations without proportional cost increases. That's structural improvement, not pure leverage.

Second, the industry has shifted. Competition from zero-commission brokers means HOOD's competitive moat is increasingly its user experience and product efficiency, not commission pricing. This allows for higher net margins even on normalized volumes compared to historical periods.

Third, revenue diversification has improved. The company isn't entirely dependent on trading volumes anymore. Net interest income from deposits has grown. This provides margin stability that didn't exist in prior cycles.

So here's my view: normalized margins are probably 35-40%, not 29%. That's higher than historical pre-2024 levels but lower than current 47.8%. The conservative analyst is directionally correct (margins will compress) but overestimates the magnitude.
