The real story? MDT is likely refinancing shorter-term tranches ahead of maturity (smart capital management) or deploying debt strategically for M&A or buybacks (shareholder-friendly). The 123% spike is *technical*, not *structural*.

A conservative analyst sees -$5.34 billion and sells. An aggressive analyst sees a proven track record of converting intangible assets into real cash flows and buys.

## COUNTER 3: Elevated Inventory is a TIMING Issue, Not a Quality Issue

MDT's inventory-to-revenue sits at ~69%, vs. a peer norm of 40-50%. Another headline that screams caution.

**But let's ask the right questions:**

1. **When was this inventory built?** Medical device supply chains were severely disrupted post-pandemic. If MDT built inventory in anticipation of demand recovery (which happened), that's strategic foresight, not mismanagement. The inventory ratio *should* decline as demand is fulfilled and supply chains normalize.

2. **Is it moving?** The fact that Q1 FY2025 revenue came in at $8.58 billion and showed positive momentum suggests demand is absorbing supply. If inventory were stuck, we'd see margin compression from obsolescence write-downs. We don't.

3. **What's the peer context?** Boston Scientific, Abbott, and Zimmer Biomet all ran elevated inventory during 2023-2024. The entire sector did. It's normalizing. MDT's 69% ratio is *high* but not pathological.

4. **Cash conversion cycle:** MDT's working capital is $11.69 billion and *positive*. That means the company isn't trapped in inventory hell; it's generating more cash from operations than it's tying up in working capital. The 69% ratio is a snapshot, not a trend.

**The aggressive perspective:** Elevated inventory at a growing company with strong cash flow is a *working capital timing issue*, not a structural problem. It will normalize in the next 2-3 quarters as supply chains fully stabilize and demand continues. When it does, management will have *one more lever to pull* for margin expansion and cash flow growth—exactly the kind of hidden operating leverage that drives stock outperformance.

A conservative analyst says, "Fix inventory before I buy." An aggressive analyst says, "Inventory is normalizing, and when it does, there's upside surprise in the cards."
