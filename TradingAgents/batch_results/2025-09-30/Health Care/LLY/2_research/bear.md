## **PART 1: THE VALUATION TRAP IS REAL—AND DANGEROUS**

### **1. The P/E Multiple Doesn't Pass the Smell Test**

My colleague says "37.7x P/E is justified because the PEG ratio is 1.11." Let's examine this claim carefully, because **it's doing a lot of heavy lifting to make this stock look cheap**.

**Here's the problem with the PEG argument:**

The PEG ratio assumes LLY will maintain **30%+ earnings growth indefinitely**. But let me be blunt: *that's not happening*. Here's why:

- **Law of large numbers:** LLY just crossed $79.7B in annualized revenue. Maintaining 30% growth means hitting $100B+ by 2027. That's adding $20B+ in new revenue annually. The entire obesity treatment market was only $50B *total* before GLP-1. Even if LLY captures the entire expanded market, they'll face **inevitable deceleration as they penetrate deeper into the population**.

- **Saturation is coming faster than bulls admit.** Once 30-40% of the obese population gets on GLP-1 therapy, *growth has to slow*. There's a mathematical ceiling. And that's probably 3-5 years away, not 10-15 years as the bull case implies.

**The real valuation story:**

Let's compare LLY to comparable growth pharma companies:

| Company | P/E (TTM) | Earnings Growth Rate | Implied Growth Justification |
|---------|-----------|---------------------|------------------------------|
| LLY | 37.7x | ~35% (reported) | Assumes 35% growth for 5+ years |
| NOVO (Novo Nordisk) | 21.8x | ~18% (conservative) | More modest growth expectations |
| JNJ | 18.2x | ~8% (stable) | Mature, dividend-focused |
| Broader Pharma Avg | 16-22x | 6-12% | Normalized pharma multiples |

**What this tells you:** LLY is trading at a **1.7x premium to Novo Nordisk, the other GLP-1 beneficiary**—despite both companies competing in the same market with similar growth tailwinds. That's not a sign of justified premium; that's a sign of bubble valuations.

**The forward P/E of 23.76x doesn't save the bull case**, either. That's based on analyst consensus—and pharma analyst estimates have a documented track record of **over-optimism on blockbuster drug cycles**. When LLY misses by 10-15% (which will happen), that forward multiple collapses.

### **2. The "Growth is Accelerating" Narrative is Already Priced In—And Fragile**

Bull point: "Q3 2025 revenue of $17.6B shows acceleration."

**Let me push back hard here:** Yes, revenue is growing. But here's what matters:

**The growth is *already in the stock price***. The market has priced in:
- 30%+ earnings growth for the next 3-5 years
- Continued GLP-1 market share gains
- Successful pipeline expansion into oncology/immunology
- Operating leverage improvements
- No major competitive disruptions

**If any ONE of these assumptions breaks, the stock resets 20-30% lower.** And historically, pharma companies fail to meet these assumptions constantly.

**Real-world example:** Pfizer in 2010-2012. After the blockbuster run on Lipitor, everyone assumed similar growth rates would continue. The stock traded at 15x P/E based on earnings growth expectations. What happened? Patent cliffs, pipeline disappointments, and the stock flat-lined for 8 years. Pfizer's premium valuation compressed dramatically once growth slowed.

**The same risk applies to LLY.** Once GLP-1 growth normalizes (and it will), the 37.7x multiple becomes indefensible.
