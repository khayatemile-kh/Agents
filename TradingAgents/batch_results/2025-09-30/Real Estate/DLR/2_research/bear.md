## **PART 1: THE AI TAILWIND IS REAL, BUT DLR'S LEVERAGE WILL CONSUME THE BENEFIT**

### The Bull's Critical Error: Confusing Demand Growth with Shareholder Returns

The bull says: *"AI capex is accelerating. DLR has $26 billion in data center assets. This is a supply-constrained play."*

Here's what the bull glosses over: **DLR is heavily leveraged.** With **$19.51 billion in debt**, every dollar of additional EBITDA growth gets partially consumed by servicing that debt. And here's the kicker—**interest expense is rising faster than EBITDA can grow.**

Look at the numbers from the fundamental report:

| Metric | Q1 2025 | Q3 2025 | Change |
|--------|---------|---------|--------|
| **Interest Expense** | $98.5M | $113.6M | +**15.3%** (+$15.1M) |
| **Normalized EBITDA** | $703.8M | $752.8M | +**6.9%** (+$49.0M) |
| **EBITDA Growth Rate** | — | 6.9% | — |
| **Interest Expense Growth Rate** | — | 15.3% | — |

**The problem is immediately obvious:** Interest expense is growing at **2.2x the rate of EBITDA growth** (15.3% vs. 6.9%). 

This is not a sustainable trajectory. As rates remain elevated—and the macro report showed **66% probability of Fed rate increases in September 2026**—DLR's debt service will continue accelerating while revenue growth plateaus. Eventually, this math breaks.

### The "Supply Constraints" Argument Ignores Competitive Supply Dynamics

The bull leans heavily on the **72% probability of state data center moratoriums** as evidence of supply constraints favoring DLR.

Let me expose this logic:

**First, moratoriums are *regional*, not global.** If California or Virginia restricts new data center buildouts, hyperscalers don't just accept capacity constraints and pay DLR premium rates. They:
- Build in jurisdictions that *don't* have moratoriums (Texas, Arizona, other states with permissive policies)
- Invest in edge computing and smaller, distributed facilities outside restricted zones
- Accelerate buildouts in international markets (DLR's Europe and Asia-Pacific presence helps, but so do competitors like Equinix)

**Second, moratoriums may reflect capacity constraints that are *cyclical*, not secular.** Energy-constrained regions imposing buildout freezes often do so *after* saturation, not before. That means the constraint already hit the marginal operator's profitability. DLR doesn't escape this; it just faces lower growth in those regions.

**Third, and most critically: Competitors exist.** Equinix, CyrusOne, Digital Bridge, and hyperscalers building their own data centers (Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta) are *also* positioned in supply-constrained markets. The moratorium doesn't favor DLR uniquely—it affects the entire ecosystem. What it *doesn't* do is create pricing power for anyone, because long-term contracts are already locked in and the hyperscalers have pricing discipline.

## **PART 2: THE MARGIN COMPRESSION IS REAL, AND THE BULL'S "OPERATIONAL LEVERAGE" STORY DOESN'T HOLD UP**

### The Bull Claims Margin Compression is a Feature, Not a Bug. The Data Says Otherwise.

The bull argues: *"Gross margins declining from 56.9% (Q1) to 54.9% (Q3) is fine because absolute gross profit is rising and operational leverage will kick in."*

This is wishful thinking. Let me explain why:

#### **Issue #1: Margin Compression Suggests Rising Input Costs, Not Capex Timing**

The claim that margin compression is due to "ramping high-capex expansion" doesn't match reality. Here's why:

- **Capex expenditures don't reduce gross margin**—they appear as capital spending on the balance sheet or as depreciation/amortization over time
- **Gross margin compression reflects the *cost of revenue* rising faster than revenue** itself
- For a data center REIT, that means: power costs, cooling costs, labor, maintenance, and related operating expenses are inflating faster than lease rates

The macro report explicitly flagged that **energy costs and power availability** are critical constraints for data centers. If DLR's cost of revenue is rising 10%+ while revenue rises 5.6%, that's not a feature—**that's a sign of eroding unit economics.**

Here's the actual math from the financials:

| Metric | Q1 2025 | Q3 2025 | Growth |
|--------|---------|---------|--------|
| **Total Revenue** | $1,407.6M | $1,577.2M | +5.6% |
| **Cost of Revenue** | $605.3M | $710.3M | +**17.3%** |
| **Gross Profit** | $802.3M | $867.0M | +8.1% |

**DLR's cost of revenue is growing 3x faster than its revenue.** That's the opposite of operational leverage. That's operational *pressure*.

#### **Issue #2: "Operational Leverage Kicks In" Is Not an Investment Thesis—It's Hope**

The bull says utilization rates will climb, fixed costs will be amortized over fuller buildings, and margins will expand.

**Questions for the bull:**

1. **What utilization rate is DLR currently at?** The data doesn't specify. If it's already 85-90% (typical for mature data centers), there's no room for the lever to extend.

2. **Who's filling the empty capacity?** If hyperscalers are building their own data centers (Microsoft with OpenAI, Amazon with proprietary facilities, Google with Infra buildout), DLR's utilization may actually *compress*, not expand.

3. **At what lease rate?** If supply constraints force tenants to build their own capacity at lower all-in costs, DLR's new leases will likely be at lower rates than historical contracts. That's margin *erosion*, not expansion.

The bull is betting on utilization expansion without evidence. The data shows margin compression. **I'd rather trust observed deterioration than projected recovery.**
