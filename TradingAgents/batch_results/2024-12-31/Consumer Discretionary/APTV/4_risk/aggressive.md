## SG&A Rising While Revenue Falls: This Is Cost Structure Deterioration

Let me highlight something the bears will obsess over, and rightfully so: **SG&A expenses increased 2.0% to $1.47B while revenue *declined* 1.7% to $19.71B.**

In a disciplined, well-run operation facing revenue headwinds, you'd expect SG&A to decline or at least remain flat. Instead, it *rose*. Why? Because the company is committed to maintaining infrastructure, headcount, and organizational spending despite lower revenue. 

In a recession—and we should remember that the prediction markets price only 8% recession probability by 2026, but that's *still* 8%—this inflexible cost structure becomes a liability. If revenue falls another 5-10%, SG&A won't automatically decline. The company will be forced into aggressive restructuring, which means write-downs, severance charges, and potentially more debt.

The EBITDA growth is real, but it's happening in a context of rising debt. That means the EBITDA is going to *service debt*, not generate shareholder returns or fund growth. That's a structural problem.
