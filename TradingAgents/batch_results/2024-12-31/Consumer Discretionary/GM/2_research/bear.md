## I. THE "FORWARD EARNINGS STORY" IS BUILT ON FANTASY, NOT FUNDAMENTALS

Let me address the elephant in the room that the bull glossed over: **the 562% earnings collapse from TTM to forward guidance.**

Here's the uncomfortable truth the bull won't admit:

**When the market prices in a $12.60 swing in forward EPS (from $2.24 to $14.84), it's not optimism—it's acknowledging extreme uncertainty.** This isn't "market consensus"—it's consensus *assumption* that could evaporate within quarters.

Let me break down why this earnings recovery is predicated on shaky assumptions:

### A. The "Supply Chain Normalization" Argument Is Exhausted

The bull claims thin margins (1.05%) reflect "production disruptions that have normalized." **This argument is now 18+ months stale.**

- Supply chains normalized in 2022-2023
- We're now in Q4 2024—two full years post-supply crisis
- Yet margins remain razor-thin at 1.05%

**This tells you something critical: thin margins are structural, not cyclical.** The automotive industry operates in a low-margin, high-volume paradigm. Competitive intensity hasn't eased; if anything, it's *intensified* with EV entrants (Tesla, BYD, EV startups) crushing pricing power.

If normalization alone could restore margins, we'd see it by now. We haven't.

### B. "Pricing Power in Trucks/SUVs" Is Already Reflected in Current Earnings

Here's the logical flaw in the bull's reasoning: **if GM still commands pricing power in its core segments, why isn't that reflected in the current 1.05% net margin?**

The answer: **pricing power is illusory.** What's actually happening:

1. **Rising Material and Labor Costs Offset Price Increases**: The recent UAW labor contract locked in substantial wage increases. Battery costs remain elevated. Steel and aluminum prices, while off 2022 peaks, haven't collapsed.

2. **Mix Pressure from EV Transition**: As EV sales mix increases (currently low-margin or loss-making in many OEMs), the company-wide margin floor *lowers*, not rises. The bull promises Ultium profitability by 2026, but current EV production is already diluting blended margins.

3. **Dealer Incentives Rising Again**: Auto manufacturers are increasingly offering rebates and incentives to move inventory. This is *margin destruction*, not pricing power. If pricing power truly existed, incentives would be unnecessary.

**Proof Point**: Ford and Stellantis—GM's direct peers—show similarly compressed margins despite operating in the same markets. This isn't a GM-specific opportunity; it's an industry structural reality.
