- RSI bouncing off oversold (<30). Instead, it's neutral and falling.

### **2. THE MARGIN COMPRESSION ISN'T "STRATEGIC"—IT'S A DEATH SPIRAL**

The bull argues that Tesla *chose* to compress margins to defend market share. That's gaslighting the financial reality. Let me walk through the actual numbers:

**Gross Margin Collapse:**
- 2022: 25.59%
- 2023: 18.24%
- 2024: 17.87%

That's a **30% decline in gross margins in just two years.** This isn't "strategic"—this is what happens when you face real competition and have limited pricing power.

**Here's the bull's critical mistake:** He claims 17.87% gross margin is "7-8x higher than legacy automakers like Ford and GM." Let me fact-check that:

- Ford's gross margin (2024): ~12-14% on vehicles (varies by segment)
- GM's gross margin (2024): ~10-12%
- Toyota's gross margin: ~16-18%

**So Tesla's 17.87% isn't 7-8x higher—it's 25-50% higher than competitors, and falling toward parity.** And here's the kicker: Ford and GM generate those margins while paying legacy pension liabilities and UAW wages. Tesla doesn't have those constraints. If Tesla is only 25% ahead on margin while paying half the labor costs, **that means Tesla's operational efficiency advantage is eroding.**

**Now let's look at the profit margin data:**
- 2022: 15.44%
- 2023: 15.51%
- 2024: **3.67%**

That's not a "cyclical trough"—**that's a 76% collapse in one year.** Operating leverage has inverted. The bull's argument that margins will "expand again when Cybertruck ramps" is unfounded because:

1. **Cybertruck is a low-volume, capital-intensive vehicle** with uncertain profitability (Tesla admitted first-gen Cybertruck isn't profitable)
2. **The competitive pricing environment persists.** BYD, XPeng, Li Auto aren't going to politely step aside. They're gaining market share precisely because they're undercutting Tesla on price.
3. **FSD monetization is unproven.** The bull assumes $99-200/month subscription revenue, but there's zero evidence current FSD V13 can command that premium. Most early adopters got FSD bundled at vehicle purchase or at deep discounts. Converting them to $2,400-year subscriptions is not a given.

**The brutal truth:** Gross margins will stabilize around 17-18%, not expand. That's parity with Toyota and 2-3x lower than the bull's implied 25%+ recovery scenario. And at 17-18% gross margins on a commodity vehicle (Model 3/Y), **net margins compress to single digits permanently.** The 3.67% net margin is the new normal, not a trough.

**What the bull missed:**

| Item | 2024 | 2023 | 2022 | Trend |

| **Operating Cash Flow** | $14.92B | $13.26B | $14.72B | Flat, not growing |
| **Capex** | $11.34B | $8.90B | $7.17B | **Accelerating +27.5% YoY** |

| **Capex as % of Revenue** | 11.6% | 9.2% | 6.8% | **Deteriorating** |

1. **Operating cash flow is barely growing (+12.6% in 2024 vs. 2023) while capex is soaring (+27.5%).** This is *unsustainable*. At current trajectory, capex will consume 100%+ of OCF within 2-3 years if OCF growth doesn't accelerate dramatically.

   - Cybertruck is ramping losses, not profits
   - Berlin and Texas facilities are operating below capacity
   - Mexico facility (starting late 2025) requires additional capex
   - Robotaxi infrastructure is speculative—zero revenue today
