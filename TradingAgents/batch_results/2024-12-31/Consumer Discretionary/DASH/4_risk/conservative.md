## THE "INFLECTION POINT" IS REAL, BUT FRAGILE—NOT PROVEN DURABLE

Now, the aggressive analyst argues this is "not living off interest income"—but that's precisely wrong. If interest income accounts for $199 million of a $123 million net profit, then **the core business is still operating at a loss on a net basis.** Interest income is *propping up* reported profitability, not the other way around.

This matters enormously for valuation. When a company achieves profitability through non-operating income—interest earned on a $4.02 billion cash balance—you're looking at a temporary tailwind, not a structural fix. That cash balance exists because:

1. The company raised capital at favorable valuations before profitability concerns emerged
2. Operational cash flow has improved but is still being deployed conservatively
3. **Interest rates are 4.25-4.50% right now.** If the Fed cuts rates to 2.5-3% in 2025 (which prediction markets are pricing in), that $199 million interest income could compress to $100-120 million.** That would erase half the company's current net profit.

The aggressive analyst didn't mention this at all. That's not oversight—it's a blind spot in their thesis.
