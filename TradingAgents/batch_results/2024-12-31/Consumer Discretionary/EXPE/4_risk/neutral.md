The aggressive analyst has identified three genuinely valid concerns that conservative defenders are glossing over:

The concern isn't irrational—it's just incomplete. Here's the distinction: elevated doesn't mean wrong. Meta trades at 22x forward earnings in a mature advertising market. Amazon trades at 40x in a mature e-commerce space. EXPE at 27x with improving operational leverage and consistent market share gains in a mature OTA market isn't automatically overvalued. It's *vulnerable to disappointment*, which is different from being fundamentally mispriced.

Second, the earnings guidance risk is legitimate. When a company posts massive profit growth primarily driven by margin expansion rather than volume growth, the natural market question is: "What's next?" That's a fair concern that deserves portfolio positioning adjustment. The aggressive analyst is right that this creates event risk.

However—and this is critical—the aggressive analyst makes a categorical error in their technical analysis that undermines their entire exit thesis.

They claim the MACD crossover to -0.43 represents "institutional capital rotating out." This is where conviction exceeds evidence. A histogram of -0.43 on an indicator that typically ranges from -10 to +10 is literally the *smallest possible* negative crossover. We're not looking at MACD at -3 or -5, which would indicate genuine momentum deterioration. We're looking at what the conservative analyst correctly identifies as "normal oscillation." The aggressive analyst is essentially using a lagging indicator's minor oscillation to justify exiting at peak valuation, which is backwards logic.

More problematically, they claim the RSI at 55 represents "exhaustion." This is simply incorrect. RSI at 55 after a peak of 72 is a textbook healthy consolidation, not exhaustion. Exhaustion would be RSI below 30 after breakdown or declining lower lows. The current setup—neutral RSI after overbought extremes—is actually the *prerequisite* for continued uptrend, not the signal of reversal. The aggressive analyst is misinterpreting normalization as deterioration.
