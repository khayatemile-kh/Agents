## **PART 1: THE OPERATIONAL TURNAROUND IS REAL AND ACCELERATING**

### **Operating Leverage is Working**

Look at the fundamentals: eBay just delivered a **22.5% operating margin in FY 2024**, up 330 basis points from 19.2% in FY 2023. This isn't a one-time fluke. Here's what's driving it:

- **Operating income grew 19.4% YoY to $2.318 billion** while revenue grew only 1.7% to $10.283 billion
- **SG&A expenses fell 5.3%** from $3.413B to $3.233B
- **G&A expenses collapsed 23.6%** from $1.196B to $914M

This is the hallmark of a company that has finally achieved operational discipline. They're not growing revenue fast, true—but they're being *surgical* about costs. The company is essentially saying: "We're going to optimize profitability, not chase vanity metrics."

**The bull implication?** When (not if) revenue growth accelerates even modestly—say, to 3-4% in 2025—that operating margin of 22-23% will drop $100-130M in incremental operating income. That's the beauty of operating leverage in a high-margin business.

---

### **Capital Allocation Screams Confidence**

Here's what management is doing with cash flow:

- **$3.149 billion in share buybacks in FY 2024** (buyback yield of ~5% at current prices)
- **Debt reduction of $750 million** on long-term obligations
- **Continued dividend payments** totaling ~$1.2 billion annually

CEO and management are taking cash flow and returning it to shareholders *while simultaneously deleveraging*. This isn't the behavior of a company worried about the business. This is a company confident enough to:
1. Buy back stock at reasonable valuations
2. Reduce leverage to a more conservative level
3. Still maintain shareholder distributions

**If the business were deteriorating, they would be hoarding cash and cutting buybacks.** They're doing the opposite.

## **PART 2: THE VALUATION IS ATTRACTIVE WHEN YOU LOOK AT THE RIGHT METRICS**

Let me address the bear case preemptively here: "eBay is mature, slow-growing, and trading at a fair multiple." 

**I disagree. Significantly.**

Here's the analysis:

### **Current Valuation Context**
- **Stock Price:** $60.46 (as of 2024-12-31)
- **Diluted EPS (FY 2024):** $3.94
- **P/E Ratio:** ~15.3x

### **The Bear Will Say: "That's Fair Value"**

But here's where they're missing the picture:

**1. Net Income is Depressed by One-Time Items**

In FY 2024, eBay reported net income of $1.975 billion, *but* this was significantly impacted by lower securities gains ($53M vs. $1.825B in FY 2023). 

**Normalized net income would be approximately $2.027 billion**, which puts the **normalized P/E at ~14.8x**.

Compare that to:
- **S&P 500 average P/E:** ~21-22x
- **Internet Retail sector average:** ~25-30x (including high-flyers like Amazon and Shopify)
- **eBay's 10-year historical average P/E:** 18-20x

**eBay is trading at a 25-30% discount to its own historical valuation.** If the market simply re-rates eBay to 17x normalized earnings (still below historical), the stock would be worth **$34.46 * 17 = $67-69 per share**—a 12-15% upside from current levels.

---

### **2. Free Cash Flow Yield is Compelling**

eBay generated approximately **$2.5+ billion in operating cash flow** in FY 2024. With modest capex needs (~$350-400M annually for a platform business), **free cash flow is in the $2.1-2.2 billion range**.

At $60.46/share with ~500M diluted shares outstanding (post-buybacks), the **FCF yield is approximately 7%**—**triple the risk-free 10-year Treasury yield of ~4.2%**.

For context:
- Apple's FCF yield: ~2-3%
- Microsoft's FCF yield: ~2.5-3%
- Amazon's FCF yield: ~1.5-2%

**eBay's FCF yield is substantially higher than large-cap tech peers**, and eBay has proven cash generation capability. This is a classic "cheap cash flow generator" opportunity.
