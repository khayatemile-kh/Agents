The Aggressive Analyst then argues that as investors "rotate out of pure-growth narratives and into cash generative businesses," EBAY will re-rate higher. But here's the critical issue: **that rotation has already happened.** The stock isn't trading at a growth multiple compressed to a value multiple—it's already trading *as* a value asset. The market has already done the work of repricing EBAY from growth to cash generative.

When the Aggressive Analyst says "multiple expansion alone, regardless of earnings growth" will drive gains, they're asking us to believe that EBAY will simultaneously:
1. Maintain its 7% FCF yield (remain a value asset)
2. Expand its P/E multiple (trade at growth valuations)

**This is mathematically impossible.** You cannot have multiple expansion without either (a) earnings growth or (b) yield compression, both of which require favorable catalysts. The Aggressive Analyst is essentially betting on re-rating that the market has already internalized.
