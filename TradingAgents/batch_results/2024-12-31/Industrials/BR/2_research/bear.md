**The Valuation Disconnect:**

## **REBUTTAL 2: THE "FORTRESS MOAT" IS NOT AS WIDE AS THE BULL CLAIMS**

### **Moat Problem #1: Switching Costs Are Overstated in a Cloud-First World**

The Bull claims: "Switching away from BR requires multi-year, multi-million dollar re-engineering efforts."

**This was true in 2010. It's not true in 2024.**

**Why?**
1. **Cloud standardization has commoditized post-trade infrastructure**
   - Modern cloud platforms (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud) provide standard APIs and microservices
   - Building a competing post-trade platform is no longer a 5-year, $500M project
   - It's a 2-3 year, $100M+ project (still expensive, but achievable for a well-funded competitor or consortium)

2. **Consortium/API-driven alternatives are emerging**
   - Financial institutions are increasingly adopting **modular, best-of-breed** solutions rather than monolithic platforms
   - Example: The emerging blockchain/DLT settlement platforms (JPM Coin, Fnality) are designed to be **interoperable** and reduce dependency on legacy intermediaries
   - These don't need to replace all of BR's functions at once—they can cherry-pick high-value services

3. **New Entrants Are Already Here**
   - **Broadridge's own data** (from the fundamentals report) shows **$4.78B in goodwill**—this is $4.78B in acquisitions that BR felt it needed to make to maintain competitive position
   - If BR truly had an impenetrable moat, why spend $4.78B acquiring competitors?
   - Answer: Because the moat is eroding, and BR is forced to consolidate to maintain market share

4. **Regulatory Changes Are Reducing Stickiness**
   - GDPR, MiFID II, and upcoming SEC rules are forcing financial institutions to audit and potentially **re-platform their infrastructure**
   - These regulatory events create **windows where switching becomes possible** without the existential risk BR claims
   - EU regulations, in particular, are explicitly designed to reduce vendor lock-in

### **Moat Problem #2: 95%+ Renewal Rates Are Unverified**

The Bull claims "95%+ renewal rates." Where's the evidence?

**This is not disclosed in the fundamentals data provided.** Without verified customer churn rates, we cannot assess the true stickiness of the business. If actual renewal rates are 90% (still excellent), the growth assumptions change materially.

**More Critically:** High renewal rates do NOT prevent pricing pressure. If BR raises prices aggressively (trying to grow earnings faster), large customers will be incentivized to invest in competing solutions. The bull hasn't addressed this.
