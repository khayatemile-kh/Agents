## Where the Aggressive Analyst is Partially Right (But Overextending)

The aggressive case correctly identifies that we're not in panic capitulation. RSI at 43.51 is indeed neutral—not oversold. That's an actual fact, not opinion. And yes, the bullish moving average alignment (price > 50 SMA > 200 SMA) is structurally intact. These observations are sound.

**However—and this is critical—they're extrapolating from partial data into a narrative of high-probability reversal.** Here's where the logic breaks down: the aggressive analyst says "MACD bearish crossovers after extreme overbought rallies are reversal signals." That's presentation of a *tendency* as if it were a law of physics. Yes, some MACD crossovers after overbought conditions *do* precede reversals. But many don't. Without additional confirmatory signals, this is probabilistic hand-waving.

The conservative analyst caught this correctly: **we lack the classic reversal divergence patterns.** Price made a lower low on December 30 ($415.02), and MACD made a *lower* low (more negative). That's not bullish divergence—that's confirmation of weakness. The aggressive analyst never addresses this. They just assert that MACD "normalizing" below signal line is bullish. But normalizing to -0.80 with a -2.79 histogram that's *still widening* is not normalization—it's continuation of downside momentum.

The aggressive analyst also claims ATR contraction creates "coiling spring energy" primed for upside breakout. The conservative analyst immediately flipped this: in Bollinger Band dynamics, consolidation *below* the middle band after coming off upper band often breaks downward. **Both claims have merit as technical statements, but neither is deterministic.** This is where the aggressive analyst fails most egregiously: they treat a probabilistic setup as if it has an 85%+ win rate. It doesn't.
