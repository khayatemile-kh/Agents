The bear case will likely hammer FY2024's 16.1% revenue decline from $60.25B to $50.52B as evidence of fundamental deterioration. **I'll concede the headline is ugly.** But let's examine the *context*:

**The Demand Cycle:**
- FY2023 saw +17.4% growth to $60.25B — this was the *peak* of a post-pandemic demand surge
- Farmers gorged on equipment purchases in 2021-2023 when commodity prices were elevated and credit was cheap
- FY2024's decline represents a *normalization*, not a structural collapse
- The 50.52B revenue run-rate for FY2024 is still **1.7% ABOVE** pre-pandemic FY2022 levels ($50.52B vs $51.28B)

**What This Means:** DE isn't in a demand crisis; it's in a **demand normalization**. The company is now lapping the easiest comparables from the pandemic supercycle. Forward growth will be more modest, but more *sustainable*.

- **56% probability of soft landing** (most likely scenario)
- **91% consensus for 1.5-2.5% GDP growth** in 2026

This is the *ideal* environment for DE to recover:

**Soft landings are cyclical plays' best friends.** DE's business thrives in exactly this scenario.

## 3. OPERATIONAL LEVERAGE WILL RE-ACCELERATE IN 2025

### The SG&A Expense Headwind Is Temporary

The bear case will point out: "SG&A expenses rose 4.6% while revenues fell 16.1% — that's operational deleverage!"

**Correct observation. Wrong conclusion about forward trajectory.**

Here's why:

**FY2024 SG&A Analysis:**
- Total SG&A: $4.51B
- As % of revenue: 8.9% (up from 7.1% in FY2023)
- R&D: $2.29B (up 5.1% YoY)

This isn't bloat — this is *strategic investment*:
1. **R&D is up 5.1%** at $2.29B. In a downcycle, management is *maintaining* innovation spending. This signals conviction in the recovery and positions DE for competitive advantage post-trough.
2. **SG&A as % of revenue** expanded due to the cyclical revenue trough. But fixed costs (salaries, facilities, admin) don't move proportionally to short-term demand fluctuations. 

**As revenues recover to $53-55B in FY2025-2026, these same SG&A and R&D bases will become highly leveraged to revenue growth.** Operating leverage is a *feature* of cyclical manufacturing — it cuts both ways. We're at the trough of the scissors now; the expansion phase is ahead.
