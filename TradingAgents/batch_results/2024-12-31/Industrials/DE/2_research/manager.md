**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Weighing both sides, the bear case lands more punches on the near-term numbers while the bull case leans heavily on optimistic forward assumptions that aren't yet supported by evidence. The bull's core thesis - that FY2024's 16% revenue decline is 'normalization' rather than continued deterioration - is a reasonable hypothesis, but it's just that: a hypothesis. The bear correctly points out there's no order-book or guidance evidence yet confirming a bottom, and the macro backdrop (only 56% soft-landing odds, with 42% overheating/tightening risk) is far more mixed than the bull's framing suggests. A 1.5-2.5% GDP growth soft-landing is also just barely supportive of equipment replacement cycles, not a robust tailwind. On valuation, the bull's forward EPS estimate of $19.80 requires a 5% revenue rebound AND margin expansion to 23-24%, but the actual FY2024 data shows the opposite trend already in motion: SG&A rose 4.6% while revenue fell 16%, R&D rose 5.1%, and interest expense spiked 36.8% to $3.35B. That's operating deleverage and a rising cost of capital happening in real time, not a management team aggressively cutting costs to protect margins in a downturn. The bear's more conservative EPS range of $15.50-16.50 for FY2025 seems better grounded in the trajectory actually visible in the reported financials, which implies a true forward multiple closer to 25-27x - not cheap for a company with declining profitability and rising leverage costs. The bull's technical 'capitulation washout' argument is speculative and was cut off without concrete confirmation signals (still bearish MACD, no reversal candle patterns cited). The FCF cushion (2.7x dividend coverage) is a genuine positive and rules out any solvency concern, so I'm not recommending a Sell - the balance sheet strength and cash generation provide a floor. But the interest expense trajectory, unclear demand bottom, cost stickiness, and the fact that guidance/orders haven't yet confirmed the bull's recovery scenario argue for trimming exposure rather than adding into strength or holding at current levels. This isn't a broken company, but the risk/reward skews toward waiting for confirmation of margin stabilization and revenue troughing before getting more constructive.

**Strategic Actions**: Trim existing DE exposure by roughly 20-25% into any near-term bounce, rather than adding at current levels. Do not chase the stock on optimism about FY2025 recovery until at least one or two quarters of data confirm: (1) revenue stabilization or sequential improvement rather than continued decline toward $47-48B, (2) SG&A growth decelerating relative to revenue (evidence of cost discipline), and (3) interest expense growth moderating from the 36.8% YoY pace. Maintain a core position given the FCF cushion (2.7x dividend coverage) and blue-chip balance sheet, but keep dry powder to re-add on confirmed evidence of margin inflection rather than the bull's forward-looking assumptions. Set a re-evaluation trigger around next earnings print - specifically operating margin trend and forward guidance on equipment orders - to decide between moving back to Hold/Overweight or extending the Underweight to a full Sell if deterioration continues.
