### Here's What Really Matters: Operating Leverage Is About to Kick In

Now, bear analysts will point out: "Operating margins are only 5.17%—that's thin!" 

**Exactly. And that's the opportunity.**

Let me break this down:

**2024 Operating Expense Structure:**
- R&D: $441.6M (21.2% of revenue)
- SG&A: $741.2M (35.6% of revenue)
- **Total OpEx: $1,182.8M (56.8% of revenue)**

**But here's the critical insight:** These are largely *fixed* costs—particularly SG&A. When a company scales from $2B to $4B+ in revenue (which the forward guidance and market expectations suggest), those expense bases don't double. They grow, but at a *significantly slower rate*.

**Realistic 2025-2026 Scenario:**
- Revenue reaches $2.7-2.9B (conservative 30% growth)
- R&D grows to $500M (assume 13% growth vs. revenue's 30%)
- SG&A grows to $820M (assume 11% growth)
- **Operating Income would expand to ~$400-450M**
- **Operating Margin: 15-16%** (vs. current 5.17%)

**This is textbook SaaS operating leverage.** And when you're running a business with 60% gross margins and increasingly recurring revenue (deferred revenue up to $994.3M), that's where the real profitability explosion happens.

**Forward EPS of $10.57 vs. TTM EPS of $2.42?** That's not pie-in-the-sky—that's the market correctly pricing in margin expansion as the company scales.

## **PILLAR #3: Valuation Is Actually Reasonable on Forward Earnings**

### Current Valuation Metrics

**Current Stock Price: $594.32**
- **Market Cap: $41.06 billion**
- **TTM P/E: 169.4x** (looks insane)
- **Forward P/E: ~56x** (based on $10.57 forward EPS)
- **Price-to-Book: 11.17x**

Now, bear analysts will scream: **"P/E of 169x is a bubble!"**

But that's comparing the *trailing* 12-month earnings to a stock that's in the middle of a margin expansion inflection. Let me reframe this:

### The Right Valuation Framework: PEG Ratio

**Price/Earnings-to-Growth (PEG) Ratio = P/E ÷ Revenue Growth Rate**

- **TTM Revenue Growth: 33.3%**
- **Current P/E (trailing): 169.4x**
- **PEG: 169.4 ÷ 33.3 = 5.08x**

For a high-quality SaaS company with 60%+ gross margins, a PEG of 5.0x is **not unreasonable**—it's actually in line with other scaled SaaS platforms like Salesforce, Adobe, or ServiceNow during their growth phases.

### Better Framework: Forward P/E with Conservative Growth

If we assume **25% revenue growth forward** (conservative relative to historical performance):

- **Forward P/E: 56x**
- **Forward Revenue Growth: 25%**
- **Forward PEG: 56 ÷ 25 = 2.24x**

A PEG of 2.24x for a business generating $408M in operating cash flow and expanding margins? **That's a fair valuation, possibly undervalued.**
