**Rationale**: Both sides make reasonable points, but weighing them, the bull case rests on firmer, more verifiable ground while the bear case leans heavily on rhetorical framing rather than data that actually contradicts the bull thesis. Let me walk through why.

On the macro/demand side, the bear's argument that "geopolitical risk is priced in" doesn't actually refute the bull's core point—that defense budgets are structurally elevated and NATO/Indo-Pacific spending is a multi-year tailwind, not a one-time re-rating. The bear's tariff argument is speculative ("if" tariffs hit 25%, "if" contracts are mixed-fix) without any evidence in the record that this is materializing at scale for RTX today. Meanwhile the backlog point cuts both ways: yes, execution risk exists, but the bear provides no data showing RTX is actually failing to convert backlog profitably—margins are expanding, not compressing, which is empirical evidence against the "backlog is a mirage" framing.

On fundamentals, the bear is right to flag that some 2024 revenue growth is inorganic (UTC-Raytheon merger lapping) and that the tax rate jumped from 11.9% to 19.1%, which is a legitimate headwind the bull didn't address. That's a fair, concrete critique and should temper enthusiasm. However, even acknowledging inorganic growth and a higher tax rate, the underlying operating margin expansion (+210bps) and EBITDA margin expansion (+140bps) are real, reported figures — the bear's claim that this is "unsustainable pricing power" is asserted, not demonstrated. Net income growing 49% even after absorbing a higher tax rate is a hard number that supports the bull.

On leverage, the D/E of 57x looks alarming in isolation, but the bear doesn't really engage with the bull's more relevant point: Debt/EBITDA of 3.42x and interest coverage above 3.5x are the metrics rating agencies and credit markets actually price off of for a company with RTX's cash-generative, contracted revenue base. The equity decline driving the D/E spike is a byproduct of merger accounting/goodwill and buybacks, not operational deterioration — the bear's "terrifying" framing is dramatic but not fully substantiated by the data in front of us.

On valuation, forward PE of 25.3x against 38% forward EPS growth (even if that growth rate is optimistic and gets trimmed) still leaves RTX at a reasonable PEG, and this is in-line with historical aerospace/defense multiples during strong-cycle periods, as the bull noted.

On technicals, the picture is genuinely mixed/neutral — price is below the 50-day SMA, RSI in the low 40s, but MACD showing early stabilization. This isn't a strong signal either way, so I'm not leaning on technicals for the call.

Net-net: the bear raises legitimate, specific flags (organic vs. inorganic growth mix, tax rate headwind, high D/E, execution/backlog conversion risk) that argue against treating this as a top-conviction outsized buy, but the bear doesn't overturn the core bull pillars — margin expansion is real, cash generation supports the debt load on the metric that matters (EBITDA-based leverage and coverage), and the structural demand backdrop (NATO expansion, bipartisan China stance) is durable multi-year support, not a one-off. Given that this is a genuine mix of strong bull evidence tempered by real but secondary bear concerns, I'm landing on a constructive-but-not-maximum-conviction call: Overweight rather than a full Buy, reflecting that the position should be added to but with awareness of the tax-rate/organic growth/leverage risks the bear flagged.
