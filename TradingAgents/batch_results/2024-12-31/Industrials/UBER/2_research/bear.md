**The bull mentions "normalized EBITDA of $4.18B in organic operational performance."** Let me trace that math:

- Unusual items impact: ~$1.2B
- Normalized: $4.18B

Okay, so the bull is acknowledging a $1.2B headwind in the unusual items analysis. But the problem is **this normalized EBITDA of $4.18B still represents significant margin expansion that needs scrutiny**. Why?

Because **we need to understand whether this margin improvement is sustainable or temporary.**

Let me show you what concerns me:

**Operating leverage claims don't align with cost structure reality:**
- Revenue growth: 17.9% YoY
- Cost of revenue growth: 18.7% YoY

**The company is growing costs *faster* than revenue.** This is the opposite of what the bull claims about "achieved scale" and "operational optimization." If UBER truly had operating leverage, we'd see cost of revenue growth *trailing* revenue growth. Instead, we see the inverse.

The bull hand-waves this away as "negligible." **It's not.** Here's why:

For a platform business, gross margin contraction while revenue grows is a *terrible* sign. It indicates:
1. **Pricing pressure** — The company can't maintain unit economics on new transactions
2. **Mix shift toward lower-margin segments** — If Rides (higher margin) is slowing and Delivery (lower margin) is growing, that's cannibalization

**Looking forward, if gross margin continues compressing while opex remains sticky, operating margin expansion has a finite runway.**
