**Recommendation**: Overweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make legitimate points, but weighing them carefully, the bull case rests on firmer ground. The bear's strongest arguments—margin plateau, rising SG&A as % of revenue, and regulatory/interchange pressure—are real but represent headwinds at the margin, not a broken business model. A 120bps margin expansion over three years plus 57-61% operating margins is still exceptional versus any peer set; 'plateauing' at elite levels is not the same as deterioration. The bear's attempt to dismiss 15% net income growth and 23% OCF growth as 'sugar rush' or 'financial engineering' is a stretch—working capital optimization and tax rate improvements are normal parts of a well-run business's cash conversion cycle, not red flags, especially when they persist alongside genuine revenue growth in the low double digits. The bear's rebuttal on TAM/emerging markets ('this opportunity has existed for 20 years, so it must be exhausted') is weak—it ignores the accelerating digitization of payments post-COVID and the genuine multi-year runway in cross-border and value-added services (data/analytics), which the bull correctly identifies as an underappreciated, higher-margin growth vector.

Where the bear does score real points: valuation at ~28.6x earnings is not cheap, and regulatory risk (EU interchange caps, antitrust) is a legitimate structural risk that could compress margins over time and deserves monitoring. Growth deceleration in revenue (12.2% vs 12.8%) is worth noting as a moderating trend, even if not alarming.

Net-net: this is a high-quality, cash-generative, shareholder-friendly business with double-digit growth, low leverage (0.82x Debt/EBITDA), and ~98% of FCF returned to shareholders via buybacks and dividends. The bear's case is more of a valuation-discipline and risk-awareness argument than a thesis for exiting the position. Given the strength of the underlying fundamentals and the fact that the bear didn't present a credible catalyst for a near-term de-rating (just a 'this is priced in' argument), I'm siding with the bull but tempering it slightly—not a full-throated 'growing position aggressively' Buy, given the rich valuation and margin plateau bear points, but constructive enough to lean Overweight and add opportunistically rather than aggressively chase strength at all-time highs.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Maintain and gradually increase MA exposure on any pullback of 5-10% from current levels (~$521), rather than chasing strength at all-time highs — valuation at ~28.6x TTM EPS leaves limited margin for multiple expansion near-term.
2) Size the incremental add moderately (e.g., scale into a full overweight position over 2-3 tranches) to respect the bear's valid point that margins have plateaued (57-61% operating margin, +120bps over 3 years) and regulatory/interchange risk (EU caps, antitrust scrutiny) could cap upside surprises.
3) Continue to hold core position given fortress balance sheet (0.82x Debt/EBITDA, ~6.9x interest coverage), best-in-class 46% net margins, and ~98% of FCF returned via buybacks/dividends — these support downside protection even if growth moderates.
4) Monitor next 2-3 quarters for: (a) SG&A/revenue trend (watch for continued rise above 15.2%), (b) regulatory developments on interchange fees in EU/US, and (c) revenue growth deceleration below ~10% — any of these would be a trigger to revisit thesis toward Hold.
5) Use options or dollar-cost-averaging to build the overweight position rather than a single lump-sum entry, given the bear's credible near-term valuation caution.
6) Do not add aggressively into strength; reserve dry powder for a market-wide pullback or MA-specific guidance disappointment to improve entry price.
