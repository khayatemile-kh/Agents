That said, the bull's dividend yield of 4.2%, genuinely low multiple (6x trailing), and real (not fabricated) debt paydown do provide a floor — this isn't a story of a broken business, more one of decelerating cash conversion and slower growth priced somewhat too cheaply. The technical picture (RSI 35.79, price near 200 SMA support, low ATR) suggests the stock isn't in freefall, just consolidating.

Given the bear's stronger, more specific rebuttal on cash flow quality/debt-reduction sourcing and growth-adjusted valuation, but tempered by the fact the bull's dividend yield and absolute cheapness offer some downside support, the evidence leans bearish/cautious rather than genuinely balanced. I don't have enough conviction that the business is impaired to justify a full Sell, but the cash-flow deterioration and asset-sale-driven deleveraging are red flags serious enough to warrant trimming exposure rather than adding or holding steady. This points to an Underweight rating rather than Hold or Buy.
