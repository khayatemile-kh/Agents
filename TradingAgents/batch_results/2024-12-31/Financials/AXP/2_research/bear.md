## THE BEAR THESIS: FOUR CRITICAL VULNERABILITIES

### **VULNERABILITY #1: THE ROE MIRAGE—RETURNS ARE ARTIFICIALLY INFLATED AND REVERSING**

The bull makes a compelling-sounding argument about 34.4% ROE, but this number **masks critical deterioration and relies on leverage dynamics that are unsustainable.**

Let me break this down:

**The Real Story Behind That 34.4% ROE:**

First, let's understand what's driving it:
- ROE = Net Income / Average Shareholders' Equity
- AXP's equity is $30.26B on a $271.46B asset base (11.1% equity ratio)
- By contrast, a "safer" bank like JPMorgan has a ~12-13% equity ratio

**Here's the problem:** AXP is operating with *lower equity ratios* than its peers. That's not evidence of efficiency—that's **evidence of leverage reliance**.

When you lever up, you artificially inflate ROE. The 34.4% ROE is partly:
1. **Leverage amplification**: Higher debt-to-equity (1.69x) than conservative peers
2. **Rate environment tailwind**: The 2023-2024 higher-rate environment inflated net interest margins **temporarily**
3. **Credit cycle tailwind**: Charge-off rates remain artificially low because we're still in an economic expansion

**Here's what the bull won't acknowledge:** This ROE is **cyclically peaked**. Consider:

| Dynamic | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | Implication |
|---------|------|------|------|-----------|
| **ROE** | 30.0% | 29.8% | 34.4% | Artificially high in 2024 due to rate environment |
| **Operating Margin** | 24.04% | 21.97% | 20.32% | **Declining for two consecutive years** |
| **Interest Expense** | $2.76B | $6.85B | $8.25B | Up **199% in two years** |
| **Interest Expense as % of NII** | 21.8% | 34.2% | **34.7%** | Compressing margins relentlessly |

**The operative metric is Operating Margin, which has declined 370 basis points from 2022 to 2024.** That's not a growth story—that's **deteriorating operational efficiency**.

The bull cites "margin expansion" of 230 bps in net profit margin, but **that's a misleading accounting artifact:**
- Operating margin contracted 165 bps YoY
- The "expansion" in net profit margin came from lower tax rates and other one-time items, not operational excellence

**If rates fall in 2025** (which many expect), the entire ROE thesis collapses:
- NII would compress
- But leverage and credit losses would simultaneously *expand*
- The result: ROE would likely fall toward 25-27%—a **20% decline**

### **VULNERABILITY #2: EARNINGS GROWTH IS DECEPTIVE—DRIVEN BY TEMPORARY TAILWINDS, NOT SUSTAINABLE GROWTH**

The bull trumpets 24.9% EPS growth as evidence of a "compounder." I disagree fundamentally.

**Let me deconstruct that 25% EPS growth:**

**The Components:**

| Driver | 2024 Contribution | Sustainability |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Net Interest Income Growth** | +18.3% | **Unsustainable—rate tailwind peaking** |
| **Loan Portfolio Growth** | +7.9% | **Sustainable but moderating** |
| **Discount Revenue Growth** | ~5-7% (implied) | **Dependent on spending, which is slowing** |
| **Operating Expense Growth** | +13.1% (contra) | **Accelerating, not decelerating** |

Here's the problem: **The company is a net interest income machine that benefited from one specific tailwind: rising interest rates from 2022-2024.**

Look at the data:
- **Interest income**: $23.80B (2024) vs. $19.98B (2023) = +19.0% growth
- **Interest expense**: $8.25B (2024) vs. $6.85B (2023) = +20.5% growth

**Interest expense is growing FASTER than interest income.** That's a **margin compression dynamic**.

The bull tries to spin this as "normal funding cost increases," but the reality is starker:
- **Interest Income as % of Total Revenue**: 36.1% (2024) vs. 33.0% (2023)
- **Interest Expense as % of NII**: 34.7% (2024) vs. 34.2% (2023)

We're at the **inflection point** where funding costs are consuming more of the NII benefit.

**What happens in 2025?**

If the Fed cuts rates (consensus expects 2-3 cuts in 2025):
- **Interest Income falls**: Lending rates reset lower immediately
- **Interest Expense falls slower**: Deposits are sticky, but funding costs eventually decline
- **Net effect**: NII contracts 5-10%, not expands

The bull assumes rates stay elevated or fall symmetrically. Neither is true. In a rate-cutting cycle:
- Loan rates fall first and fastest
- Deposit costs fall later and slower
- **Net interest margin compresses**

**Result: 2025 EPS growth will likely disappoint, falling to single digits or turning negative.**

**The prediction market says 8% recession probability by 2026.** But here's what that misses:

Consider:
- **Current charge-off rate**: Historically, AXP's charge-off rates range from 1.2% (recession, 2009) to 0.5% (strong expansion, 2023-2024)
- **Current rate is near cycle lows**: This means minimal room for deterioration before hitting consensus

If charge-offs rise from 0.5% to 0.8% (still well below historical average):
- **Additional charge-offs**: $203B × 0.003 = **$609M incremental loss**
- **Provision reserve increases**: Another **$300-500M contingency**
- **Pre-tax earnings impact**: ~**$1B swing**

That's **7-10% of 2024 net income erased by a *modest* charge-off increase.**

**Where is the evidence that credit is improving?** The bull points to "net loans grew $14.96B." But growing the portfolio *while charge-offs rise* is **not a sign of strength—it's evidence of deteriorating underwriting discipline.**

**Forward-looking indicator**: Delinquency rates (loans 30+ days late) are the precursor to charge-offs. If we see delinquencies rising in Q4 2024 or Q1 2025 (data to be reported in late January earnings), that's a major warning sign that charge-offs are about to increase.
