On technicals, both sides agree RSI/MACD/price-below-EMA show short-term weakness after a 54% rally; the bull's 'it's just profit-taking' explanation is plausible but not proven, and the bear frames it as confirming distributional pressure — I don't have enough technical follow-through to fully side with either, so I treat technicals as a modest tie-breaker in favor of caution rather than the deciding factor.

Net effect: the bull thesis requires taking the one-off-looking earnings swing at face value and assuming re-acceleration of growth and moat durability that the numbers don't clearly support. The bear more convincingly shows the profitability jump needs scrutiny, growth is decelerating not accelerating, and leverage/goodwill risk is real even if not imminent. Valuation (~17-18x EV/EBITDA, ~18x forward P/E) is fair-to-cheap only if growth reaccelerates and margins hold — a big 'if' the bear has legitimately challenged. This isn't a screaming sell (no data here suggests imminent liquidity distress, and cash position is genuinely large), but the balance of evidence leans bearish/cautious rather than balanced, so a Hold is too generous — I'm calling this Underweight: trim exposure, don't add, and wait for clearer evidence that revenue growth and normalized earnings quality improve before increasing exposure.
