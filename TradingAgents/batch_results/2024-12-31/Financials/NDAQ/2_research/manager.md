The bear's leverage framing (3.6x net debt/EBITDA being elevated versus mature-business peers) also has merit, even though the bull's absolute cash-flow coverage math is directionally fine.

On technicals, both sides are describing the same data (RSI ~40.5, bearish MACD cross, price above lower Bollinger Band and above 200-SMA but just below 50-SMA) — this is neutral-to-mildly-bearish momentum, not a screaming buy or sell signal, and it doesn't resolve the fundamental disagreement.

Netting it out: the valuation is not so stretched that it screams sell, and the moat/recurring-revenue argument is real, but the EPS decline and margin compression the bear surfaced are hard evidence of currently deteriorating per-share economics that the bull's growth narrative hasn't yet overcome. Until Q4 earnings and 2025 guidance (late January) clarify whether margin/EPS trends are inflecting positively — and whether dilution from recent M&A is stabilizing — there isn't enough of an edge on either side to justify adding or cutting the position aggressively. This is a genuine case for staying balanced rather than committing capital in either direction.
