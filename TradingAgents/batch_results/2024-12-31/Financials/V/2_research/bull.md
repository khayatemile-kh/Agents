## PART 2: THE MACRO BACKDROP IS CONSTRUCTIVE

### **Consumer Spending Remains Resilient**

Let me address the recession elephant in the room directly. The prediction markets analyzed in our macro research show a **56% probability of a "soft landing"** through 2026. That's not wishful thinking—that's market-implied pricing from thousands of bettors with real capital at stake.

But here's what matters for Visa specifically: **even in a recession, payment volume doesn't collapse by 50%.** Look at 2020—yes, volumes dipped during COVID lockdowns, but the recovery was V-shaped. Visa's revenue fell only modestly and bounced back sharply. Why? Because:

1. **Consumers still make essential purchases** — groceries, utilities, healthcare
2. **The shift to digital payments is secular and non-reversible** — cards and digital wallets now dominate in mature markets
3. **Visa's service fee structure is inelastic** — they collect fees on transaction volume, not profitability

So even in a slowdown scenario, Visa has enormous downside protection.

### **Interest Rate Environment Supports Margins**

The Federal Reserve has held rates at 4.25%-4.50%, and prediction markets show limited aggressive rate cuts in early 2025. This is **genuinely positive for Visa**:

- **Float revenue** (interest earned on settlement balances) is maximized at higher rates
- **Network fees** (as a percentage of transaction value) benefit from higher nominal transaction sizes driven by inflation persistence
- **Client incentive pressure** (the main margin headwind) is reduced when rates support client revenues

In a "higher for longer" scenario, Visa's margins actually expand. Yes, if the Fed cuts to 2%, that's a modest headwind. But our macro analysis shows that scenario has low probability given sticky services inflation. The base case is rates staying elevated through 2025—a Visa tailwind.

### **Cross-Border Travel & Digital Payments Accelerating**

The latest quarterly data (through Q4 2024 cycle, to be reported in January) is showing strong international activity:

- **Post-pandemic cross-border travel volumes** are normalizing at elevated levels
- **Emerging market payment adoption** continues accelerating, particularly in APAC and LATAM
- **Digital wallet penetration** (Apple Pay, Google Pay, Samsung Pay, etc.) is expanding the addressable market for Visa's infrastructure

These are **secular, multi-year trends** that will drive transaction volume growth well into the 2030s. This isn't about cyclical strength—it's about structural shifts in how payment infrastructure gets used globally.
