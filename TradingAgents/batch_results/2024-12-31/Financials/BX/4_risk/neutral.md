## Where the Aggressive Analyst Gets It Right—And Critically Wrong

And yes, the subsequent 1.5% recovery validates that institutional support existed at those levels. I don't dispute any of that.

But here's where the Aggressive Analyst commits a cardinal sin of risk management: **they conflate short-term technical validation with fundamental attractiveness, and then use that conflation to justify holding a position at a price that remains disconnected from normalized earnings power.**

The core argument is essentially: "The reversal signals are working, therefore the position should be larger, not maintained." This is backwards. Technical reversal signals tell you something specific and limited: they tell you that directional momentum may stabilize or bounce in the near term. They tell you absolutely nothing about whether the stock is fairly valued relative to its cash-generating capacity.

And this is where the Aggressive Analyst's reasoning becomes dangerous. They acknowledge that on normalized earnings (roughly $2.35 per share), the stock trades at 69-75x P/E, then argue this is justified because "we're not in a normalized environment." But that's the classic trap. The market doesn't price stocks based on whether we're in "normalized" or "exceptional" environments. It prices them based on expectations of future cash flows. And when those expectations reset—which they invariably do—stocks trading at 70x normalized earnings experience sharp multiple compression, regardless of how many bullish technical setups appeared beforehand.

Let me be specific about the risk here: If Blackstone's Q1 2025 results show even modest softening in net inflows, or if management guidance suggests that 2025 realizations may moderate toward historical averages, the market will recalibrate the multiple. A re-rating from 70x to 45x on $2.35 normalized EPS is a 35% downside move. That's not a bear-case fantasy—that's a rational repricing based on normalized assumptions. And such repricing happens *despite* technical support levels and *despite* MACD histograms narrowing.

The Aggressive Analyst is essentially advocating that we buy into a technical reversal pattern while hoping that the fundamental narrative never requires recalibration. That's not risk management; that's narrative dependence.
