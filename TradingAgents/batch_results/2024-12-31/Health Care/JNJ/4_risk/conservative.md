The aggressive analyst also points to negative tangible book value as a red flag. Let me be direct: tangible book value is a largely meaningless metric for a modern pharmaceutical company. Of course JNJ has substantial goodwill and intangibles—it's acquired *successful* biotech companies and therapeutic franchises. That goodwill isn't worthless; it represents the present value of future cash flows from acquired assets. 

When you acquire a company with a blockbuster drug franchise, the acquirer records goodwill equal to the purchase premium. That doesn't mean the asset is impaired; it means the market valued the acquired company's cash flows above book value—which is *exactly what we want* as acquirers. Writedown risk exists, yes, but it's only realized if the acquired assets fail to generate expected returns. Given that JNJ's acquisitions (Actelion, Galapagos, etc.) have largely performed, this risk is theoretical, not imminent.
