# LLY Investment Committee Decision Memo

## Verdict: **Overweight**

---

## Adjudicating the Debate

I'll evaluate each pillar on the merits actually presented, not rhetorical force.

### 1. Revenue/Margin Growth: Bear wins the framing battle, but not the substance battle
The bear correctly catches a **methodological error** in the bull case: the "133% TTM growth from $34.1B to $79.7B" comparison is internally inconsistent — the bull's own numbers elsewhere reference $45.04B in 2024 annual revenue, meaning the TTM figure conflates periods incorrectly. **This is a legitimate flag on data hygiene**, and I discount the bull's headline growth stat accordingly. The cleaner, defensible number is **31.8% YoY revenue growth**, which is still excellent for a company of this size — but nowhere near "133%."

However, the bear's leap from "flagging bad math" to "margins will compress to 35-40% by 2026-27" is **assertion, not evidence**. No pricing data, no specific reimbursement contract terms, no competitor pricing datapoints were provided — only directional narrative ("pricing pressure is inevitable," "price wars are coming"). I can't underwrite a 1,400+ bps margin compression thesis on rhetoric alone.

**Net:** Growth is real and strong (31.8%, not 133%), margin durability is a legitimate open question but not proven bearish.

### 2. Balance Sheet / Leverage: Bear raises a fair caution, bull under-argues it
The debt trajectory bear points out — Debt-to-Equity rising 1.52x → 2.34x → 2.36x, net debt up 114% in two years — is accurate per the bull's own disclosed figures and deserves weight. The bull's rebuttal ("EBITDA covers it, Net Debt/EBITDA is 1.61x") is reasonable but somewhat hand-wavy on *why* the debt growth outpaced revenue growth (2x+ leverage build vs. 31.8% revenue growth is a real divergence worth monitoring).

**Net:** Leverage is rising faster than the top line — a genuine watch-item, not yet a crisis given 1.61x Net Debt/EBITDA remains investment-grade territory for pharma.

### 3. Valuation: Bull's argument stands mostly unchallenged
PEG of 1.11 and forward PE of 23.78x (vs. trailing 37.75x) were never directly rebutted by the bear — the bear focused entirely on *future* margin compression rather than disputing the current valuation math. If the bear's margin-compression thesis is speculative (as noted above), the valuation case built on current forward estimates holds up reasonably well.

### 4. Technicals: Uncontested by the bear
Bull's technical case (oversold RSI touches, MACD recovery, Bollinger Band mean-reversion setup) went completely unaddressed by the bear. Bearish moving-average alignment (price below 10 EMA/50 SMA/200 SMA) is real and should temper near-term aggressiveness, but the bull's capitulation/mean-reversion argument is unrebutted.

### 5. Macro/Policy: Bull's RFK Jr. dismissal was cut off mid-argument by the bear before full rebuttal; bear's reimbursement-tightening point (UnitedHealth/CVS restricting coverage) is a real, cite-able trend and the more credible policy risk of the two.

---

## Why Not Hold, Why Not Buy

- **Not Hold**: The debate isn't genuinely balanced. The bull's *core* thesis (durable franchise, reasonable forward valuation, unrebutted technical setup) survives bear scrutiny on the two most decision-relevant axes (valuation, technicals). The bear's strongest point (data-hygiene catch on TTM revenue) actually *strengthens* the case for a more moderate, evidence-grounded stance rather than reversing it.
- **Not Buy**: Rising leverage trajectory and single-franchise concentration risk are real, underexplored vulnerabilities that the bull failed to fully rebut. Reimbursement tightening is a credible near-term headwind. A full conviction "Buy" would be underwriting more certainty than the debate actually earned.

---

## Actionable Plan

**Rating: Overweight**

1. **Position sizing**: Add to existing exposure incrementally rather than in one tranche — technical setup (oversold conditions, MACD basing) supports scaling in on weakness rather than chasing.
2. **Entry discipline**: Favor adds on tests of the lower Bollinger Band / prior capitulation zone rather than breakout-chasing above the 50 SMA (~$800), given price remains below all major moving averages.
3. **Monitor as thesis-changing triggers**:
   - Debt-to-Equity trend beyond ~2.4x or Net Debt/EBITDA breaking above ~2.0x → reassess toward Hold/Underweight
   - Any concrete payer/PBM coverage restriction data (not just directional commentary) on GLP-1 reimbursement → reassess growth durability
   - Gross margin prints below ~78% for two consecutive quarters → validates bear's compression thesis
4. **Do not treat the 31.8% (not 133%) revenue growth figure as the story** — use it as the anchor for forward modeling; the mislabeled TTM stat should not factor into position sizing decisions.
5. **Risk control**: Given elevated leverage build and concentration risk, cap position at a level where a 30-40% single-franchise margin compression scenario (bear's worst case) would not impair portfolio-level risk tolerance.

**Bottom line**: The fundamental growth and valuation case survives the bear's attack better than the leverage/concentration case survives the bull's defense. This nets to constructive-but-not-uncritical — Overweight, not Buy, with explicit risk monitors tied to the bear's most credible (leverage, reimbursement) points.
