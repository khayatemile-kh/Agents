Where I diverge sharply from the Aggressive Analyst is the interpretation of what fair value means right now. Fair value doesn't mean "buy immediately." Fair value is a price where risk and reward are balanced, not tilted in your favor. And here's the critical distinction the Aggressive Analyst glosses over: **we need to know whether we're trading at fair value because the market accurately priced in the risks, or because the risks haven't fully materialized yet.**

The Aggressive Analyst says the 19% correction reflects RFK concerns being "already priced in." But let me push back on that claim with data. The fact that it's declining suggests the market might be *underpricing* the probability he stays and implements actual policy changes.

Think about what that means operationally. If RFK stays (88% probability per the prediction market), and he begins initiating pricing reviews or accelerating biosimilar approvals in Q1 or Q2 2025, that's a *future catalyst for negative repricing*. The 19% correction might reflect uncertainty about RFK's tenure or actions, but it doesn't necessarily reflect the full cost of him actually taking concrete steps that compress LLY's margins or growth assumptions.

So when the Aggressive Analyst says "you're buying after the risk is priced in," I'd counter: **you're potentially buying before the risk is fully manifested in the numbers.** There's a difference between "the market knows RFK might be a problem" and "the market has repriced earnings assumptions for the case where RFK implements aggressive pricing policy."
