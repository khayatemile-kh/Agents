## Where the Aggressive Analyst Gets It Right—And Where That Confidence Becomes Dangerous

An RSI at 32 tells us the stock has been sold aggressively. It does NOT tell us it will bounce. History is littered with stocks that stayed oversold for extended periods because the market kept validating the bears' thesis with new information.

Your comment about "institutional capital returning post-January 2nd and finding this technical setup attractive" assumes something critical that the data doesn't support: that institutions *want* to own MDT at these levels. Institutions are disciplined. They don't buy oversold stocks—they buy undervalued stocks. Those are different things.

Here's why: medical device companies are inherently defensive. They serve non-discretionary procedures and chronic disease management. Revenue doesn't decline 40% in a recession—it slows to 0-2% growth. More likely, if macroeconomic conditions hold steady (which they appear to be), MDT faces a normalization period where:

- Revenue stabilizes around 3-4% growth (not 2.5%, not 6%)
- EBITDA margins recover slightly as the company laps tougher year-ago comps and integrates acquisitions more efficiently
- EPS ends up somewhere around $5.00-5.30 (not $4.40-4.50, and not $6.40)

Under that base case, the dividend is completely safe. The company generates sufficient free cash flow, and payout ratios remain reasonable. The risk of a dividend cut exists but is not the base case—it's a downside tail risk if everything goes wrong.

Because the market will have already adjusted to that outcome by the time it's reflected in earnings. We don't get a surprise bull run—we get a sideways to slightly positive grind.
