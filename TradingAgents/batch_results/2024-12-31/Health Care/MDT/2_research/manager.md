**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull case rests on three legs: (1) a "cheap" forward P/E of 14.3x implying 58% EPS recovery, (2) fortress FCF of $5.41B as a moat, and (3) durable secular demand from aging demographics. The bear dismantled each leg with hard numbers from the same dataset: FY2024 actuals show revenue growth decelerating to 3.6%, EBITDA down 5.7%, operating income down 7.4%, and diluted EPS down 2.1% YoY. Against that backdrop, consensus forward EPS of $6.40 versus TTM $4.06 requires a 57.6% jump with no stated mechanism—no revenue acceleration, no margin expansion (margins are compressing), and buybacks would have to be debt-funded. That's a real gap the bull never closes; pointing to a consensus number isn't the same as showing a credible bridge to it.

On cash flow, the bear's arithmetic is the more useful lens for the trader: $5.41B FCF against ~$719M interest, ~$3.13B dividends, and estimated $1.5-2B principal amortization leaves essentially no cushion for M&A, buybacks, or an earnings miss. The bull's response that interest coverage (7.73x) and debt-service coverage (3.6x) are "healthy" is true in isolation, but it doesn't rebut the bear's point that after dividends and debt service, discretionary FCF is thin — that's a real constraint on flexibility, not solvency risk per se, but it matters for the multiple the market is willing to pay.

On the demographic/structural argument, the bull is directionally correct that aging populations create durable long-term demand, but the bear's counter — that reimbursement pressure and stagnant-to-declining U.S./European procedure volumes are the near-to-medium-term reality — is more relevant to the 12-month investment horizon a trader cares about. Long-duration secular stories don't rescue a stock over the next few quarters if the earnings trajectory is decelerating now.

Technically, the bull's "oversold = buy" framing (RSI 32, MACD turning) is a legitimate short-term signal, but it's undercut by the bear's more important observation: 50 SMA above 200 SMA (a bearish dead-cross setup), price sitting right at the 10 EMA with resistance stacked at 50/200 SMA above, and a low-volume holiday drift that could just as easily resolve down as up. Oversold conditions in a name with decelerating fundamentals often stay oversold or get more oversold — RSI near 30 is not a reliable standalone reversal signal absent a fundamental catalyst.

Net: the bear's argument is fundamentally-grounded and uses the same reported financials to show deteriorating trends (EBITDA, operating income, EPS all down YoY) that directly contradict the bull's forward-looking optimism. The bull's rebuttal leans heavily on hope embedded in consensus estimates and a favorable reading of ambiguous technicals, without providing a credible operational bridge to the forward numbers. That imbalance — hard recent deterioration vs. speculative recovery — tips this decisively toward caution, though the yield, FCF generation, and interest coverage are real enough that I wouldn't advocate an outright exit. This lands as Underweight: trim, don't panic-sell, but stop adding until there's real evidence of margin/revenue inflection.
