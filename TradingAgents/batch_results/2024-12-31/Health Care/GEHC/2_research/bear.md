## THE MARGIN EXPANSION STORY IS A HOUSE OF CARDS

Now, let's address the bull's marquee claim: **"Earnings grew 43% while revenue grew 0.6%—that's operational leverage!"**

**I need to flip this narrative on its head, because it's dangerously misleading.**

**Yes, net margins expanded from 8.0% to 10.1% (210 basis points). Here's what that likely means:**

### **Where Did Those Margins Come From?**

**Possibility 1: One-Time Gains or Non-Recurring Items**
- Spin-off from GE in 2024 creates accounting adjustments and one-time charges in prior year comparisons
- Restructuring charges in 2023 (pre-separation) artificially depress that baseline
- **If you adjust for these items, the margin expansion evaporates**

**Possibility 2: Cost Cutting, Not Pricing Power**
- $19.67B revenue with only 0.6% growth = the company is **not selling more**
- If margins expanded on flat/declining revenue, it means **the company laid off workers, closed facilities, or deferred investments**
- This is **not sustainable margin expansion—it's financial engineering**

**Possibility 3: Mix Shift Toward Higher-Margin Products**
- Medical devices companies often see margin expansion when they shift toward service/software revenue (which is higher margin)
- But look at the gross margin trend: 41.7% (2024) vs 40.5% (2023) = only +120 bps
- **If the company were successfully shifting to high-margin software/services, we'd see gross margin expansion of 300+ bps, not 120 bps**

**The Critical Red Flag: This margin expansion is NOT sustainable.**

Here's why: When a company grows revenue 0.6% but grows earnings 43%, it's typically because:
1. **One-time items** are inflating earnings (not repeatable)
2. **Cost cuts** are running out of runway (can't cut to zero)
3. **Accounting benefits** from restructuring or separation (temporary)

**None of these support the bull's forward earnings estimate of $5.41 (24.7% growth).** In fact, I'd argue that forward guidance is **wildly optimistic** given the revenue headwinds we're already seeing.

### **Issue #2: Valuation Multiple Compression is Likely**

Even if we accept the bull's $5.41 EPS (which I don't), the **current P/E of 14.4x is not justified for a slowing company.**

Why? Because P/E multiples are partially determined by **revenue growth momentum:**

**Historical P/E Relationship for Medical Devices:**
- 15%+ revenue growth → 20-22x P/E
- 10-15% revenue growth → 16-19x P/E
- 5-10% revenue growth → 14-16x P/E

**GEHC is in the <5% growth bucket** (0.6% current, likely 2-3% forward). This suggests a **12-13x P/E is more appropriate, not 14.4x.**

**If GEHC re-rates to 12x P/E on realistic $5.00 EPS:**

The bull's $104-108 target assumes:

3. ✗ Current 16x P/E multiple is maintained (unlikely for a low-growth company)

**All three assumptions are aggressive to the point of being unrealistic.**
