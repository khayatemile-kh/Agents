**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull case rests on genuine structural tailwinds: DUK's service territory (NC, SC, FL, OH, KY, IN) is seeing real load growth from data centers, electrification, and Sunbelt population inflows, and regulated utilities do capture that growth as rate-base expansion with a contractual ROE. The balance sheet metrics (Baa1/BBB+/BBB, ~3.5x interest coverage) are investment-grade and leverage levels are consistent with utility peers. However, the bear case lands the more decisive blows on the near-term financial reality: the 2024 FCF 'improvement' to breakeven is a one-year capex timing dip, not a structural fix, and management's own guidance points to $13-14B annual capex through 2030 — well above the current OCF run rate. That means the dividend is currently being funded by net new debt issuance (net debt up ~$4.67B in 2024) rather than organic cash flow, and this pattern is set to continue or worsen as capex reaccelerates. The bear also correctly flags that the regulatory environment—the linchpin of the bull thesis—is showing signs of stress: approved ROEs trending down (9.5-10% to 8.8-9.2%), politically charged rate cases in FL/NC/OH, and disallowance risk on storm hardening and renewable transition costs. Since DUK is already at 162% D/E and 5.6x net debt/EBITDA, any compression in allowed ROE or disallowed capex recovery hits equity returns disproportionately given the existing leverage, and there is little margin for error. The bull's growth story is real but plays out over years/decades, while the debt-funded dividend and capex-outrunning-cash-flow dynamic is a here-and-now risk that could pressure the equity story before the AI/electrification tailwind fully monetizes. On balance, this isn't a Sell — DUK still has IG ratings, a covered (on a cash basis) dividend today, and long-term demand tailwinds — but the deteriorating FCF/leverage trajectory and regulatory uncertainty argue against adding exposure or treating this as a clear Buy. The stronger, more immediate argument favors caution over the bull's optimistic extrapolation, so I'm leaning toward trimming rather than holding steady or buying.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing DUK position modestly (10-20%) rather than adding on this pullback; do not treat the 7% dip from November highs as a buying signal until FCF trajectory is confirmed, not just capex-timing driven. 2) Withhold new capital allocation to DUK until at least one more quarter/annual report clarifies whether the 2024 capex dip to $12.28B was structural or a one-year timing anomaly versus the guided $13-14B run rate. 3) Monitor net debt issuance closely each quarter — if net debt continues rising materially (as in the ~$4.67B 2024 increase) to fund the dividend, that is a red flag confirming the bear thesis and would justify further de-risking. 4) Track regulatory outcomes in NC, FL, and OH rate cases over the next 2-3 quarters; a continued downward trend in allowed ROE (below ~9%) or meaningful capex disallowances should trigger a re-rating to Sell. 5) Keep the dividend on watch — it is currently cash-flow covered on paper (26% payout of OCF) but effectively debt-funded once capex is netted out; any dividend growth announcement inconsistent with FCF improvement should be viewed skeptically. 6) For investors who want utility-sector exposure to the AI/data-center demand story, consider rotating a portion of proceeds into utility peers with cleaner FCF profiles and less regulatory friction rather than concentrating further in DUK. 7) Revisit the rating in 2-3 quarters once there's clarity on 2025 capex actuals and rate case outcomes — an upgrade back to Hold/Overweight is plausible if OCF growth outpaces capex reacceleration and regulators approve rate increases without material disallowances.
