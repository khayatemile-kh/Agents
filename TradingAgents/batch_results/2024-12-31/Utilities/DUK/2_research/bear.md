### **"Regulatory Mechanisms" Won't Save DUK from Rising Rates**

The bull's core argument rests on this assumption: *Regulators will always approve rate increases sufficient to cover capex + ROE + dividends.*

**This assumption is breaking down in real time.**

#### **Regulatory Risk #1: Rate Shock Backlash**

In 2024-2025, utilities across the U.S. are facing unprecedented pushback on rate hikes:

- **North Carolina:** DUK faces scrutiny on rate cases; state regulators are increasingly skeptical of aggressive capex plans
- **Florida:** DUK faced public outcry on rate increases; state politicians are pressuring regulators to cap hikes
- **Ohio:** Regulatory environment turning hostile to utilities post-FirstEnergy scandal

The bull assumes regulators *want* to raise rates and that customers/politicians will simply accept them. **That assumption is no longer valid.**

Recent trend: **Regulatory approved ROEs have been declining, not rising:**
- 2020-2022 average approved ROE: 9.5-10%
- 2023-2024 average approved ROE: 8.8-9.2%
- Trajectory: *Downward*

If DUK's approved ROE drops to 8.5% (a real possibility), the economics of leveraged utility investing collapse.

#### **Regulatory Risk #2: Cost Disallowances and Political Pushback**

DUK has faced regulatory challenges on:
- **Storm hardening capex**: Not all grid hardening investments are recoverable; some regulators cap these
- **Renewable transition costs**: Not all costs related to retiring coal plants are passed through
- **Executive compensation**: Regulators increasingly scrutinizing overhead costs

**Result:** DUK's actual capex recovery rate is *lower* than the theoretical "cost-of-service + ROE" model assumes.

Example: If DUK spends $13B on capex but regulators only allow $12.2B to be recovered in rates (15% disallowance), the company eats the difference. With leverage already at 162% D/E, there's no margin for error.
