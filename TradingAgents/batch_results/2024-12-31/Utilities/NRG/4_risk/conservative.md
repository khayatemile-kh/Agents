The fact that institutional investors are still willing to hold NRG bonds and equity suggests the market believes refinancing is manageable.

1. **Rates rising another 100 bps is not the base case.** The Fed held steady through year-end. Market pricing suggests 2-3 potential rate cuts in 2025. The aggressive analyst is building their case on a tail-risk scenario, not a probabilistic base case.

2. **Even if rates do rise, NRG has $3.5B in EBITDA to service $11B in debt.** A $110M increase in interest expense reduces EBITDA coverage from 3.2x to 3.1x. That's still a comfortable cushion, not a solvency crisis.

3. **The company has demonstrated it can refinance.** The fundamental data shows net debt *declining* from $10.21B in 2023 to $9.84B in 2024, even with the current maturity increasing. This suggests NRG is actively managing maturities and taking advantage of refinancing windows.

**The conservative position: Balance sheet leverage is a legitimate risk, but it's a known, manageable risk, not a hidden catastrophe.** And it's already been priced into the 10.69x forward P/E. Selling now because of refinancing risk that's already been discounted is selling into strength for shorts.
