Look at the historical pattern: CapEx in 2022 was $7.15B. In 2023, it spiked to $7.41B. Now it's $7.10B. This isn't a clear downtrend—it's volatility around a $7.2 billion baseline. This is where I need to push back hardest, because the aggressive analyst is making an enormous assumption without acknowledging the risk. They're saying: "Operating expenses are growing, but these will be recovered through rate mechanisms in 2025 when rate cases come through."

The aggressive analyst dismisses this as a "snapshot," but it's actually a warning flag the industry uses precisely because it predicts financing stress.

Here's the thing about rate cases: they're not automatic. Regulators scrutinize cost recovery requests. They face political pressure to keep rates affordable. And critically, *there's a lag between when a rate case is filed and when it's approved*. EXC filed rate cases, sure. But have they been *approved* yet? The data doesn't tell me they have. And the fundamental report notes margin compression happened *already* in 2024—the operating expense burden is here *now*, while the rate recovery is promised for *later*.

The aggressive analyst says this is "standard operating procedure" for utilities. True enough. But standard doesn't mean risk-free. And it's certainly not a reason to be *accumulating* at current prices when the company is in a financing-dependent phase.
