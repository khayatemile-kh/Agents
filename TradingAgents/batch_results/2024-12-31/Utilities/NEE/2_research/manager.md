**Rationale**: Weighing both sides, the bear case lands more decisive blows on the specific numbers the bull used as pillars of the thesis. The bull's headline "171% FCF surge" is undercut by the bear's correct observation that the base year (2023) was itself a weak recovery from a negative FCF year (2022: -$1.48B), meaning 2024's $4.75B is better read as a partial normalization off a low base rather than a durable growth trajectory. That reframes the entire cash-flow narrative from 'inflection point' to 'still fragile.'

The debt argument is where the bear really wins the round. The bull's spin that $24.77B of gross debt issuance versus $11.64B net issuance represents 'strategic de-leveraging' doesn't hold up — net debt actually rose 14.7% YoY to $80.85B, and debt-to-equity at ~162% is genuinely high even by regulated-utility standards. The bear's point that FCF ($4.75B) minus dividends ($4.24B) leaves only ~$0.5B before even touching a very large implied interest burden is a legitimate solvency-quality concern, even if the precise interest-expense figure is an estimate rather than a hard number from the prompt. The bull never actually rebuts the leverage trajectory — it just reframes rising debt as 'prudent,' which isn't a real rebuttal, it's relabeling.

On the capex point, the bear's reframe — declining capex could reflect underinvestment in an aging, growing-demand grid (Florida population/EV/data-center growth) rather than 'capital discipline' — is a coherent alternative read that the bull doesn't address quantitatively; the bull simply asserts optimization without evidence distinguishing the two explanations.

On NEER/renewables, the bear raises a legitimate margin-compression/commoditization concern, and points out that consolidated revenue actually fell ~12% while margin expanded — that is consistent with cost-cutting rather than genuine growth, which the bull's segment narrative doesn't reconcile.

Where the bull is stronger: the technical picture (RSI recovery from oversold, MACD turning positive, price bounce off Dec lows) is factual and does suggest near-term technical stabilization, and the regulated-utility cash flow base (FPL) plus a >60-year dividend history are real qualitative strengths that shouldn't be dismissed outright. The FCF yield (~7%) and P/E being roughly in line with or below historical average are also fair, unrebutted valuation points. But these are more tactical/technical positives layered on top of a balance sheet that is deteriorating, not tactical positives that offset a fundamentally strengthening company.

Net verdict: the bear's critique of the balance sheet quality (rising net debt, high leverage, tight FCF-after-dividend coverage once financing costs are considered) and the FCF-trajectory reframing are the more rigorous, harder-to-dismiss arguments. The bull's rebuttal to these points was largely rhetorical ('strategic,' 'prudent') rather than data-driven. That tips this from a clean 'Hold' into modest bearish territory — not a full Sell, because the underlying regulated FPL business and long dividend history still provide a cash-flow floor and the technicals suggest the immediate downside may be limited — but conviction should lean toward trimming/de-risking exposure rather than adding on this dip, given genuine leverage and cash-flow-quality concerns that the bull failed to convincingly refute.
