## **PILLAR 1: THE MACRO SETUP IS IDEAL FOR BUILDING MATERIALS**

### **Interest Rates Are Now Tailwinds, Not Headwinds**

The Fed is entering an easing cycle. We've already seen three rate cuts in 2024, and the forward guidance suggests more flexibility in 2025. Here's why this matters *directly* to CRH:

**Mortgage Rates & Housing Demand:**
- The 10-year Treasury yield is likely to settle in the 4.0-4.2% range, compared to the 5.0%+ we saw in 2023-early 2024
- Lower mortgage rates unlock pent-up demand in residential construction—exactly CRH's bread and butter
- Millennials and Gen-Z entering peak household formation years are *waiting* for rates to moderate before buying
- CRH's Americas segment, which is a massive revenue driver, will see a meaningful uptick in residential volume starting Q1-Q2 2025

**The Data:** Housing starts have been volatile but remain around 1.1-1.2 million annualized. With 30-year mortgage rates potentially dropping 100+ basis points from 2023 peaks, expect starts to normalize toward the 1.4-1.5 million level—that's a **20-35% uplift in housing unit demand**, which translates directly to cement, aggregates, and ready-mix concrete demand for CRH.

### **Infrastructure Spending Remains a Structural Tailwind**

- Biden-era infrastructure policies (CHIPS Act, infrastructure bill) are rolling through project pipelines
- Europe's green transition initiatives (EU infrastructure projects) benefit CRH's strong European footprint
- These are *multi-year* programs that don't disappear with elections—they're embedded in budgets

CRH's non-residential segment, which includes infrastructure materials, should see steady demand, insulating the company against pure residential cyclicality.

### **Input Costs Are Moderating, Not Spiking**

One of the bear arguments I anticipate is: *"Input costs will crush margins."* But here's the thing—**we're past the commodity inflation peak.** Energy prices have moderated from the 2022 crisis levels. Freight costs are normalized. Wage inflation, while sticky, is no longer accelerating at 8-10% annually.

CRH has *pricing power*—they've already demonstrated this with gross margins expanding from 34.2% (2023) to 35.7% (2024). As input costs remain benign and demand picks up, the company can expand margins further without pricing customers out of the market.
