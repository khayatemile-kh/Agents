**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides make valid points, but the bear case ultimately lands more decisive blows on the issues that matter most for near-to-medium-term risk/reward. The bull's strongest points—gross margin improvement (280bps), net debt reduction ($860M YoY), and reasonable EV/EBITDA vs peers (13.2x)—are real, but the bear successfully undercuts the core 'turnaround' narrative by highlighting that normalized EBITDA actually declined ~1.9% YoY ($1,715M to $1,681M), meaning the reported margin/EBITDA improvement is more attributable to portfolio mix shifts from divestitures than genuine operational efficiency gains. That's a critical distinction the bull never adequately rebuts.

The debt picture is the decisive factor. A 5.22x Net Debt/EBITDA ratio is well above investment-grade thresholds (~3.5x) and specialty chemical peer averages (~2.5-3.0x), and none of the comparably-leveraged peers cited by the bull (HUN, ALB, EMN) carry negative tangible book value—IFF is the outlier here. The bear's math on deleveraging pace (7-8 years to normalize at realistic $300-400M/year debt paydown, not the bull's optimistic $500-700M) is more grounded, since asset-sale low-hanging fruit is already exhausted and FCF is constrained by capex/working capital needs in a capital-intensive specialty chemicals business. Interest expense consuming an estimated 26-33% of EBITDA leaves little room for error if EBITDA softens or rates stay elevated.

Revenue stagnation (essentially flat 2023-2024 at ~$11.48B, down from $12.44B in 2022) reinforces that this isn't a growth story riding operational leverage—it's a company treading water while carrying a distressed balance sheet. The bull's technical argument (RSI oversold, Bollinger compression) is a reasonable short-term trading signal but doesn't offset the structural balance-sheet concerns for a 12-18 month horizon.

That said, I'm not fully embracing the bear's most extreme framing (imminent covenant breach, structural insolvency)—the EBITDA base is still substantial and management does have some deleveraging trajectory, however slow. The stock's steep post-November decline and oversold technicals argue against adding to a full Sell/exit stance, but the weight of evidence—declining normalized EBITDA, elevated leverage well above peers, negative tangible book value unique among comps, and stagnant revenue—tips this toward a cautious, risk-reducing stance rather than a constructive one. This nets out to Underweight: acknowledge the balance sheet risk is real and underappreciated by the bull, without calling for full capitulation given the depressed valuation and possibility that oversold technicals produce near-term relief rallies.

**Strategic Actions**: 1. Trim existing IFF exposure by roughly 25-35% into any near-term technical bounce (the oversold RSI ~32 and Bollinger compression could produce a 5-10% relief rally toward $86-90; use that strength to reduce, don't chase further downside panic-selling).

2. Do not add to the position on the 'cheap valuation' argument alone — the 13.2x EV/EBITDA is not adequately discounted given IFF sits at 5.22x Net Debt/EBITDA vs. peer average of 2.5-3.0x, and is the only major peer with negative tangible book value (-$1,686M). Multiple compression risk remains skewed to the downside until leverage normalizes.

3. Set a hard risk checkpoint: watch for any covenant-related disclosures, credit rating agency commentary, or further guidance cuts. A downgrade to high-yield territory or covenant renegotiation would be a trigger to reduce further toward Sell.

4. Monitor next 1-2 quarterly reports specifically for (a) normalized EBITDA trend — reversal of the 2023-2024 decline would be a positive signal to reassess upward, (b) actual free cash flow figures and debt paydown pace vs. the $500-700M bull case, and (c) organic revenue growth (ex-divestiture) to confirm whether margin gains are structural or portfolio-mix driven.

5. Maintain a reduced core position (not zero) sized to reflect the genuine oversold technical setup and possibility that panic-selling overshot fair value — but keep position sizing conservative (max 1-1.5% of portfolio NAV) given the balance sheet fragility until Net Debt/EBITDA shows a credible path back toward 4x or below.

6. Avoid using IFF's ~4.2% dividend yield as a reason to hold — with negative tangible equity and high leverage, dividend sustainability itself is a medium-term risk if EBITDA softens further; do not treat the yield as a margin of safety.
