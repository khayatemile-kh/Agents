## **1. THE EARNINGS SPIKE IS A CYCLICAL PEAK, NOT SUSTAINABLE RECOVERY**

This is the critical point the bull wants you to ignore. Yes, AVY reported 40% net income growth year-over-year. But let's examine what's actually happening under the surface:

**Revenue growth is anemic—only 4.7% YoY** (from $8.36B to $8.76B). That's not recovery; that's stagnation with a paint job.

Here's the real issue: The earnings expansion came primarily from **margin recovery and cost management**, not from revenue growth. The bull triumphantly points to gross margin expansion of 170 basis points (27.2% to 28.9%), but **this is a return to normalized margins after depressed 2023 levels, not structural improvement.**

Let me illustrate why this matters:

**2022 Performance:**
- Revenue: $9.04B (higher than 2024)
- Net income: $757.1M (higher than 2024's $704.9M)
- Profit margin: 8.37%

**2024 Performance:**
- Revenue: $8.76B (down from 2022)
- Net income: $704.9M (down from 2022)
- Profit margin: 7.62%

**Translation:** AVY's 2024 earnings spike is simply a recovery back toward 2022 levels—levels the company had already achieved two years ago. This isn't "operational leverage working"—this is **cyclical normalization after a trough year**. And when cyclical businesses emerge from troughs, the next phase is typically **moderation as revenue pressures return.**

The bull wants to extrapolate this 40% YoY growth into perpetuity. But **margins have already normalized**. Where's the next growth driver? Revenue expansion? Let's talk about that.

## **3. MARGIN EXPANSION IS UNLIKELY TO CONTINUE—COMPRESSION IS MORE PROBABLE**

The bull makes a big deal of AVY's "pricing power" demonstrated by the gross margin expansion. But this argument falls apart under scrutiny.

**Here's what actually happened in 2024:**

The company faced input cost inflation (acknowledged in the macro analysis as "persisting into 2025"). Despite this, AVY maintained margins. How?

**Answer:** A combination of:
1. **Modest pricing increases** to customers (limited by competitive pressure)
2. **Operating leverage** from returning to normalized volumes
3. **Cost reductions** from supply chain stabilization

**None of these are sustainable at the current level.** Here's why:

**Pricing Power Limits:**
- AVY operates in highly competitive markets (labels, packaging)
- Customers have alternatives; excessive price increases lead to volume loss
- The company can't take pricing without accepting market share erosion

**Supply Chain Tailwinds Are Already Priced In:**
- Supply chains have normalized (it's now late 2024)
- The "excess margin recovery" from supply chain stabilization is already in the P&L
- Further cost improvements are unlikely

**Input Cost Pressure Persists:**
- The macro analysis explicitly states inflation will remain elevated through 2025
- Raw material costs (adhesives, films, resins) remain elevated
- Labor costs aren't declining

**What this means:** When AVY guides for 2025, don't expect margin expansion—expect **margin compression or flatlining** as:
- Pricing leverage weakens (customers resist further increases)
- Volume pressure emerges (lower consumer spending)
- Input costs remain stubborn (inflation not evaporating)

The 28.9% gross margin achieved in 2024 represents a **cyclical peak, not a new floor.** The bull is extrapolating margin trends that are inherently cyclical.

## **4. THE FREE CASH FLOW ARGUMENT IGNORES CYCLICAL DETERIORATION**

The bull cites $699M in free cash flow as evidence of business health. I don't dispute that number—but **the interpretation is dangerously flawed.**

Here's the critical issue: **Free cash flow in a recovering cyclical is always strongest at the beginning of the cycle, not the end.** AVY generated record cash flow in 2024 because:

1. **Volume recovery from depressed 2023 levels** = cash inflow
2. **Margin normalization** = higher cash conversion
3. **Working capital normalization** (not yet under pressure)

As we move into 2025-2026, what changes?

- **Volume growth decelerates** (consumer spending slows ahead of rate hikes)
- **Margins compress** (pricing power exhausted; cost inflation persists)
- **Working capital demands increase** (inventory builds ahead of weak demand; receivables quality deteriorates)

The bull wants to assume that AVY maintains $700M+ annual FCF and deleverages to 3.5x net debt/EBITDA by late 2025. **This assumes 2025 earnings don't deteriorate.** But if the macro headwinds materialize and revenue growth stalls or declines, FCF could compress to $500-550M range—immediately halting deleveraging progress.

And here's the really uncomfortable part: **Cyclical businesses have a tendency to over-invest in capex near cycle tops.** If AVY management believes 2025 will be strong, they may increase capital expenditures for "growth," which would further reduce FCF generation. The bull's assumption that management will maintain disciplined capex *while believing demand is strong* is unrealistic.
