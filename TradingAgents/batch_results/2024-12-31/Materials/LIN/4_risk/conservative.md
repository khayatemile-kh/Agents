- 2023 FCF: $5.5 billion  
- 2024 FCF: $4.9 billion

That's a **13.8% decline in free cash flow over two years**. Meanwhile, the company is simultaneously ramping capital expenditure from $3.2 billion in 2022 to $4.5 billion in 2024—a 40% increase in capex while free cash flow contracts. This is not the profile of a business that is "improving operational efficiency despite flat revenue." This is the profile of a business that is *investing heavily to defend market position while returns are deteriorating*.

The aggressive analyst asks, "If Linde's business is deteriorating, where is the evidence?" Here it is: **declining free cash flow per share, rising capex intensity, and flat revenue growth with no clear path to acceleration.** That's deterioration dressed up in margin expansion metrics.

And let's be precise about those margin improvements. The EBITDA margin expanded 170 basis points to 38.9%, yes. But operating cash flow actually *declined* $400 million year-over-year, from $9.8 billion to $9.4 billion. So which is the real story? Is EBITDA margin expansion real, or is it accounting treatment and cost-cutting masking underlying operational stress?

**The honest answer**: It's likely both. Linde has squeezed costs and improved pricing power in a tight market. That's admirable. But squeezing costs when revenue is flat and capex is rising is a defensive posture, not an offensive one. And defensive postures don't justify 30x P/E valuations when the underlying growth engine shows no signs of reaccelerating.
