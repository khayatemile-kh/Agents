**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both sides agree on the raw numbers, but the disagreement is about framing, and the bear's framing survives scrutiny better. The bull's core point is that 2024 net income (+82% YoY) and EBITDA margin expansion (335bps) show operational healing, and that rising capex/dividends signal management confidence. The bear's rebuttal is decisive on three counts: (1) the 82% earnings 'recovery' is measured against a depressed 2023 base — versus 2022 (the last normal year), net income is down 74%, EPS down 75%, and EBITDA margin is still ~430bps below the 2022 baseline of 16.71%, so this is mean reversion off a trough, not a structural inflection; (2) the balance sheet story is worse than the bull admits — cash fell 26.7% ($2.99B→$2.19B) at the same time debt rose, which is inconsistent with a benign 'refinancing-driven' explanation; net debt is up 17.8% to $13.22B and working capital contracted $1.36B, both consistent with genuine cash-generation stress rather than 'strategic deployment'; (3) FCF is negative (-$0.15B) yet the company paid out $2.46B in dividends and buybacks, meaning shareholder returns were funded by new borrowing and cash depletion, not organic cash generation — a leverage-increasing decision in a cyclical, capital-intensive business right as the cycle looks fragile. The bull's growth narrative (EVs, Asia-Pacific, circular economy) is directionally plausible but speculative and multi-year, offering no near-term offset to the deteriorating balance sheet and margin gap versus normalized levels. Given that leverage (~2.5x now, plausibly 3.3-3.8x if EBITDA reverts) is being flattered by a single good year, and the company is monetizing debt/cash reserves to sustain the dividend, the risk/reward skews negative until there's clear evidence of sustained (not one-year) EBITDA recovery and positive FCF. This isn't a full Sell because the earnings trajectory is genuinely improving in absolute terms and the company remains profitable with real long-term secular exposure (EV materials, circular economy, APAC demand) — but the balance sheet fragility and cash burn are real enough to warrant trimming exposure rather than holding or adding.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing DOW exposure to underweight sizing (e.g., reduce position by 20-30% from current weight) rather than adding on the pullback — the 26.5% decline reflects real fundamental risk, not just sentiment. 2) Do not initiate new long positions until DOW shows two consecutive quarters of positive free cash flow and stabilizing/declining net debt — the dividend is not currently self-funded and is being financed by debt/cash drawdown. 3) Monitor Net Debt/EBITDA closely each quarter; if EBITDA margin compresses back toward high-single digits (as in 2023) while debt stays near $17.6B, treat that as confirmation of the bear case and reduce further. 4) Set a re-entry trigger: revisit Buy/Overweight only if EBITDA margin sustainably closes the gap toward the 2022 16.71% baseline (e.g., above 14-15% for multiple quarters) alongside FCF turning durably positive without asset sales. 5) For income-focused holders, flag dividend sustainability risk explicitly — with FCF negative and payout funded by borrowing, a dividend cut or freeze is a real tail risk if the cycle doesn't turn quickly. 6) Use options or partial hedges (e.g., covered calls) on any residual position to generate income while downside balance-sheet risk plays out, rather than being fully unhedged long.
