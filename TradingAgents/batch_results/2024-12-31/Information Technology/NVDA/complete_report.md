I'll illustrate with a concrete example. Apple traded at a PEG below 1.0 for much of 2008 before the financial crisis. Was Apple a great company? Absolutely. Was it trading cheap relative to growth? Yes. Was it a good buy on September 15, 2008? Absolutely not, because the stock fell another 40% before bottoming. The valuation framework was correct. The timing was terrible.

The Aggressive Analyst keeps pointing to the 92% prediction market probability of new all-time highs by 2026, and I need to be very precise here: that data is being misinterpreted. A 92% probability of reaching new highs by December 2026 is not the same as a 92% probability that the stock goes up from today. It literally just means that within a 24-month window, the market believes there's a 92% chance the stock will touch a new peak at some point. This is totally consistent with a 25-30% drawdown in the next six months. You could buy NVDA today, watch it fall to $105 in March, and the stock could still hit $160 by November 2026 and satisfy that prediction market condition.

So when the Aggressive Analyst uses the 92% figure to justify buying scaled tranches into weakness, they're using the wrong data for that purpose. That prediction market is about long-term directional bias, not about near-term support levels or bounce probabilities.
