## **1. GROWTH DECELERATION IS REAL—AND THE BULL CASE DISMISSES IT TOO EASILY**

The bull argues that 22.5% growth is "exceptional for a $139B company." Let me respectfully disagree with the framing here.

### **The Deceleration Trend is Concerning**

| Period | Revenue Growth |
|--------|-----------------|
| **2022-2023** | 23.9% |
| **2023-2024** | 22.5% |
| **2024-2025 (guidance)** | Likely <20% |

**Yes, the bull is correct that 22% growth at scale is solid.** But here's what they're glossing over:

- **Deceleration is happening.** We've gone from 23.9% to 22.5%—that's a 140 basis point drop year-over-year. If this trend continues (and there's every reason to believe it will), we're looking at sub-20% growth by late 2025.
- **Enterprise spending is tightening.** We're not in a robust digital transformation phase anymore. CIOs are getting cost-conscious. Companies are consolidating vendor counts, not expanding them. The tailwinds the bull referenced are becoming headwinds.
- **Market saturation is real.** ServiceNow has been in market for nearly two decades. How much TAM expansion is really left? The low-hanging fruit (large Fortune 500 companies) is already captured. Growth now depends on:
  - Smaller mid-market customers (lower ASPs)
  - Expansion into adjacent modules (cannibalization risk)
  - Geographic expansion (execution risk in new markets)

### **The Bull Misses the Macro Backdrop**

The bull assumes enterprise automation demand is structural and immune to cycles. That's **demonstrably false:**

- **Enterprise IT budgets are under pressure.** In 2025, we're entering a period where companies are scrutinizing software ROI. "Nice-to-have" automation tools get cut before essential operational software.
- **Recession risk is material.** If we enter a recession in 2025-2026, enterprise spending on workflow automation—a *discretionary* technology—will decline. ServiceNow's customers will delay implementations, reduce seat counts, and renegotiate prices.
- **The Fed isn't cutting rates.** The bull's macro assumptions are optimistic. If rates stay at 4.25-4.50% (or even rise), that *directly impacts enterprise capex decisions.* Higher cost of capital = lower IT spending.

**Bottom line:** Growth is slowing, macro backdrop is uncertain, and the bull is extrapolating a 22% growth rate into perpetuity. That's not investing; that's wishful thinking.

## **2. PROFITABILITY METRICS ARE MISLEADING—GAAP EARNINGS ARE WHAT MATTER**

The bull makes a compelling argument about EBITDA margins expanding to 21.2%. But let me highlight the critical problem here: **EBITDA is not cash earnings.**

### **Reported EPS Tells the Real Story**

| Year | Diluted EPS | Change |
|------|-------------|--------|
| **2022** | $0.32 | — |
| **2023** | $1.68 | +425% |
| **2024** | $1.37 | **-18.5%** |

**The bull dismisses the 18.5% EPS decline as "just tax timing and dilution."** That's not credible.

Here's what actually happened:

1. **2023 had a massive tax *benefit* of $723M** (not normal)
2. **2024 had a $313M tax *expense*** (more normalized)
3. **This tax swing alone accounts for most of the EPS decline**
4. **Equity-based compensation continues to dilute shareholders**—shares outstanding increased from 1,020M to 1,040M, a 1.96% dilution

The bull wants us to ignore GAAP earnings and focus on EBITDA. Why? Because **GAAP earnings are flatlining while revenue grows.**

### **The Margin Expansion Story Has a Catch**

Yes, operating margins expanded 390 basis points to 12.4%. But look at the *composition*:

- **Gross margins were essentially flat** (78.3% → 78.6% → 79.2%). Minimal expansion.
- **Operating margin expansion came from SG&A leverage**, not from improving unit economics or pricing power
- **The company cut operating expenses as a % of revenue**, but this is a **one-time benefit** as they consolidate post-acquisition integration

Here's the issue: **There's limited room left for margin expansion through cost-cutting.** The next phase of margin growth has to come from:
1. **Pricing increases** (difficult in competitive environment)
2. **Improved gross margins** (flat recently)
3. **Further headcount reduction** (limited upside; risk to R&D)

**The bull is celebrating *realized* margin expansion while ignoring that the easy gains are behind us.** Forward margin expansion is much less certain.

**Real talk:** Paying 26.8x forward earnings for a company with **decelerating growth** and **execution risk** is speculative, not prudent.
