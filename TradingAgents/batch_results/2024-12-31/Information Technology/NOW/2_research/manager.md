**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Both analysts agree on the core facts: NOW is growing revenue in the low-to-mid 20% range, expanding EBITDA margins meaningfully, carrying a strong balance sheet (net cash, 0.24x D/E, $5.8B liquid assets), and holding a dominant ~40% ITSM share with high switching costs. The debate hinges on interpretation, not facts. The bear's strongest points are: (1) growth is decelerating (23.9%→22.5%, likely sub-20% next), (2) GAAP EPS actually fell 18.5% in 2024 even if partly tax-driven, and (3) the stock trades at a demanding valuation (83.9x TTM P/E, 12.6x P/S, 26.8x forward P/E) that requires sustained high growth and margin expansion to justify - leaving little room for error. The bull's rebuttal on valuation was essentially 'forward P/E is reasonable if margins keep expanding,' but didn't convincingly refute that the easy SG&A-driven margin gains are largely behind the company, nor did it address that gross margins are flat, meaning further margin expansion has to come from harder-to-achieve levers. The bull's competitive-moat and cash-generation arguments are solid and real advantages, but they don't change the valuation math - even a wonderful business can be a poor investment if you overpay, and paying 12.6x sales while growth is visibly slowing is a real risk. That said, the bear's macro/recession argument is speculative and not evidence-based from what's given, and the fundamental business quality (moat, cash flow, profitability trajectory) is genuinely strong - this isn't a broken business. Given decelerating growth is a real, data-confirmed trend and valuation is stretched on multiple metrics (P/E, P/S far above sector), while the fundamental quality argues against outright selling a category leader with net cash and expanding margins, the balanced call is to trim risk exposure rather than add aggressively at these prices or sell entirely. The technical setup (pulled back to near 50-day SMA, RSI reset to neutral from overbought) suggests the stock isn't in freefall, but valuation risk outweighs the incremental bull case for adding here.
