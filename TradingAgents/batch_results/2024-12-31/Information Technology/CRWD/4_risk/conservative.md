## THE FALLACY OF "PRICED IN": VOLATILITY ISN'T CERTAINTY

The aggressive analyst claims the July outage is "already priced in—and then some" because the stock recovered from $85.68 to $233.88. This is one of the most dangerous logical traps in investing, and I need to expose it clearly.

**Here's what "priced in" actually means:** It means the market's current price incorporates available information up to this moment. It does **not** mean all tail risks have been eliminated or all uncertainty resolved. It definitely does not mean we won't see a retest of those lows.

The recovery from $85.68 to $233.88 happened over several months under specific market conditions: broad tech recovery, Fed rate-cut optimism through November, sector rotation into mega-cap tech, and—critically—*before* Q1 FY2025 renewal data would actually flow through*. We are currently in the exact window where that data is *about to hit*, and the stock is already down 11.8% from its recent peak.

Think about the timeline:
- **July 19, 2024:** CrowdStrike outage hits
- **Late July - August:** Stock craters to $85.68
- **September - November:** Recovery to $97.05+ as markets assume "all-clear"
- **December 16 - December 31:** -11.8% pullback as reality sets in that renewal cycles don't work on a 5-month lag

The aggressive analyst is essentially arguing: "Because the stock went down and then back up, the risk is resolved." But that's confusing a relief rally with fundamental resolution. **The actual enterprise renewal data that will answer the question "Did customers churn?" hasn't arrived yet.** When it does in mid-to-late January, it could confirm the bull thesis—or it could shatter it.

This is the exact scenario where a conservative approach protects capital: we trim now at $85.54, we get clarity on Q1 data, and then we re-engage with actual information rather than hope.
