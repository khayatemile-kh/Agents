## **OPENING STATEMENT**

Let me be direct: **CrowdStrike represents one of the most compelling risk-reward opportunities in enterprise software today.** Yes, the company faced a catastrophic July 2024 outage—but that crisis is now **firmly in the rearview mirror**, and what we're seeing in the fundamentals is not a company struggling to survive, but one that's **emerging stronger, more profitable, and positioned for accelerated growth.**

The headline numbers are compelling: **36.4% revenue growth, a $255 million earnings swing from loss to profitability, $3.38 billion in cash, and a business that's generating massive free cash flow.** But beyond the headlines, there's a much deeper bull thesis here that I want to unpack.

---

## **THE BULL THESIS: FOUR PILLARS**

### **PILLAR 1: DEMONSTRABLE RECOVERY & EARNINGS INFLECTION**

**Here's what the bears will likely argue:** *"The July outage destroyed customer trust. CRWD will face massive churn."*

**My counterargument is direct and data-backed:**

The financial results tell a completely different story. Let me walk you through what happened:

**Pre-Outage (FY2023):** CRWD lost $183.2M and had a -8.18% net margin. The company was burning cash operationally.

**Post-Outage (FY2024):** CRWD posted $72.2M in net income with a +2.36% margin—a **$255 million swing**. That's not just recovery; that's a fundamental inflection point.

But here's the critical part: **If we normalize out the $123.2 million in net interest income** (which is real money from their cash, but non-operational), core operating profit still came to roughly **-$51M adjusting for interest.** However—and this is crucial—**operating margins improved 786 basis points, from -8.49% to -0.63%.** That's near-breakeven operationally.

**Why does this matter?** Because it proves the business model is working. The company went from losing nearly 8.5 cents on every dollar of revenue to losing less than a penny. That's operational leverage firing.

The bears will point to customer retention metrics as the real test. **I'm saying: if CRWD faced massive churn, you wouldn't see 36.4% revenue growth and expanding gross margins (75.2% vs. 73.2%).** That growth rate simply doesn't happen if customers are fleeing en masse.

---

### **PILLAR 2: RECURRING REVENUE MODEL = REVENUE VISIBILITY & RESILIENCE**

Let me address the elephant in the room: **enterprise software companies with strong SaaS models are **recession-resistant**.** And CRWD is the gold standard here.

**Why?** Because cybersecurity is mission-critical. It's not discretionary. It's not a "nice to have" in a budget cut. When a Fortune 500 company is running CRWD's Falcon platform on thousands of endpoints, they don't rip it out when interest rates rise or the economy slows. They renew.

Here's the proof in the data:

- **Gross margin: 75.2%** – That's SaaS-level economics. Compare that to traditional enterprise software at 60-65%. This is a cloud-native platform.
- **Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) momentum** – While specific ARR figures weren't in the fundamentals provided, the 36.4% revenue growth with stable/expanding margins strongly suggests ARR is accelerating.
- **Net Retention Rate implications** – If CRWD had significant churn post-July, we would NOT see revenue growth outpacing customer acquisition cost. The fact that SG&A (which includes sales/marketing) grew 25.7% while revenue grew 36.4% means **productivity per sales dollar is improving**.

**Translation:** The company is getting more efficient at converting customers, not struggling to fill a leaking bucket.

### **PILLAR 4: COMPETITIVE MOAT & MARKET OPPORTUNITY**

**Market Position:** CRWD is not just a player in the $50+ billion cybersecurity market—it's a **leader in endpoint detection and response (EDR) and cloud workload protection**, two of the fastest-growing segments.

**Why the moat is durable:**

1. **Customer switching costs are HIGH** – Once Falcon is deployed across thousands of endpoints, ripping it out and replacing it with a competitor requires significant IT effort, retraining, and risk. This is exactly why cybersecurity companies have such high net retention rates.

2. **Platform consolidation trend** – Enterprises increasingly want one-platform solutions (endpoint, workload, threat intelligence, managed hunting). CRWD's Falcon platform spans all of these. **Sticky, high-margin, recurring.**

3. **AI/ML integration** – The more CRWD's platform is used, the more threat data it ingests, the better its AI models become. This creates a **data moat**.

4. **Geopolitical tailwind** – Russia-Ukraine, China tensions, increased cybercrime. The threat landscape is escalating, not de-escalating. This structurally supports cybersecurity spending.

**Market size:** The TAM (Total Addressable Market) for enterprise security is enormous and growing. CRWD is taking share from legacy players like Symantec, McAfee, and CrowdStrike's primary competitor Palo Alto Networks. With $3B+ in revenue, CRWD is still in the early innings of a massive market opportunity.

## **ADDRESSING THE JULY 2024 OUTAGE DIRECTLY**

Let me not dance around this—**the July outage was catastrophic PR, and I understand the bearish concern.**

**But here's what actually happened:**

1. **Customer reaction:** Yes, some customers were furious. But "some" did not mean "all." The company maintained net positive customer growth through the incident (evidenced by 36.4% revenue growth in FY2024).

2. **Remediation costs absorbed:** The financial impact was largely absorbed in FY2023/Q3 2024. FY2024 results show the company on the other side of the crisis, not in the midst of it.

3. **Competitive opportunity:** If anything, the outage **proved CRWD's importance.** When the Falcon platform went down, customers realized how central it was to their security posture. That's not a reason to churn; that's a reason to demand better reliability (which CRWD is delivering) and to keep the platform because the alternative is worse.

4. **Institutional resilience:** Look at the balance sheet. If CRWD was truly facing mass churn and lawsuits crippling the business, we would NOT see cash increasing $920M YoY. That's the market's confirmation that this is temporary, not existential.
