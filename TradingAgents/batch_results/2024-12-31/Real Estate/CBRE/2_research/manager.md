**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: The bull's case rests on 2024 headline revenue growth (11.9%) and EBITDA growth (13.6%) as proof of durable operating leverage, plus a constructive rate-cut narrative that should compress cap rates and reignite transaction volumes, and a benign read of rising leverage as 'strategic' capital deployment. The bear's rebuttal is more evidence-grounded on the specific numbers presented in this debate: net income was actually flat-to-down (-1.8%) in 2024 despite the revenue surge, normalized EBITDA growth (~7%) trailed revenue growth once one-time items are stripped out, gross/operating/EBITDA margins have compressed for two straight years (op margin 4.3%->3.4%->3.6%, still well below 2022 levels), and interest expense grew 44% while net debt nearly tripled since 2022 (Net Debt/EBITDA 0.46x->1.30x->1.59x, +113% in two years). The bull's claim that cap rates will compress with rate cuts is also directly contradicted within the same debate by the observation that cap rates actually stabilized/widened in Q4 2024, meaning the catalyst the bull leans on hardest is not yet visible in the data.

The bear's argument is stronger because it engages directly with the same disclosed figures and exposes that the bull's 'operating leverage' story doesn't hold once one-time/non-recurring EBITDA add-backs and rising interest burden are backed out — net income obviously isn't growing, which is the ultimate test of margin expansion claims. The bull's rebuttal to the leverage buildup (comparing to Accenture/Aptiv, citing 8.3x interest coverage) is reasonable but doesn't address the bear's core point that debt has nearly tripled in two years while net income has stagnated, meaning incremental capital is not yet translating into bottom-line growth. That's a real red flag on capital allocation efficiency, not just optics.

That said, this isn't a full sell case — CBRE still has a large, sticky AUM base ($220.8B), a real (if decelerating) revenue growth engine, and investment-grade-level coverage ratios (8.3x interest coverage) that mean near-term distress risk is low. So this isn't a business in existential trouble, but the risk/reward at current leverage and margin trends looks unfavorable for adding exposure. Given the deteriorating margin trend, flat net income despite double-digit revenue growth, and the debt trajectory outpacing profit growth, I'm siding with the bear's more rigorously supported argument and recommending a cautious underweight stance rather than an outright sell, since balance sheet risk is manageable in the near term and there is optionality if rate cuts materialize and transaction volumes genuinely re-accelerate.

**Strategic Actions**: 1) Trim existing CBRE exposure by roughly 20-30% of the position size, redeploying capital into names with cleaner margin trajectories or lower leverage within real estate services/adjacent sectors, rather than exiting entirely — the balance sheet is not in near-term distress (8.3x interest coverage) so a full sell is not warranted.
2) Do not add to the position on the recent pullback; treat further weakness as confirmation of the thesis rather than a buying opportunity until margin data (operating margin, normalized EBITDA growth vs. revenue growth) shows clear reacceleration.
3) Set a watchlist trigger to revisit the rating upward (toward Hold/Overweight) if: (a) normalized EBITDA growth reaccelerates above revenue growth for two consecutive quarters, (b) net income growth turns clearly positive and outpaces interest expense growth, and (c) cap rates actually begin compressing (not just stabilizing) alongside confirmed Fed rate cuts.
4) Set a risk trigger to move toward Sell if Net Debt/EBITDA breaches ~1.8-2.0x or if transaction revenue declines materially (mid-teens or worse) in a slowdown scenario, given the thin 3.6% operating margin cushion.
5) Size any residual position conservatively (below benchmark weight) and hedge cyclicality exposure given the stated sensitivity to office vacancy trends and financing costs, which remain unresolved headwinds per the bear case.
