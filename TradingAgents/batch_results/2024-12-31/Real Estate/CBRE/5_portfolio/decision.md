**Investment Thesis**: The bear case is the more evidence-grounded read of the disclosed numbers and both the Aggressive and Conservative analysts converge on it even as they disagree on magnitude. Net income fell 1.8% in 2024 (-$18M) despite 11.9% revenue growth and 13.6% headline EBITDA growth, because normalized EBITDA growth (~7%) trailed revenue growth once one-time items are stripped out and interest expense rose 44% ($149M→$215M) on debt that nearly tripled (Net Debt/EBITDA 0.46x→1.30x→1.59x, +246% in two years, per the Aggressive Analyst). Operating margin compressed for two straight years (4.3%→3.4%→3.6%) and remains well below 2022 levels, and the Conservative Analyst adds a corroborating, previously under-weighted signal: working capital collapsed 52% ($1,423M→$683M) and the current ratio fell to 1.07x, tightening the cushion against any revenue shock. The bull's central catalyst — rate cuts compressing cap rates and reigniting transaction volumes — is directly contradicted in this same debate by the observation that cap rates stabilized/widened in Q4 2024, so the thesis rests on a catalyst not yet visible in the data.

The Neutral Analyst's pushback is useful for calibrating severity, not reversing direction: ~50% of revenue is recurring occupier-outsourcing work, EBITDA still grew in absolute dollars ($1.712B→$1.945B), and 8.3x interest coverage means near-term insolvency is not the base case. This argues against the Conservative Analyst's push for a full exit and against escalating to outright Sell. But the Neutral Analyst's scenario framework itself assigns only 40-45% probability to the soft-landing case where cap rates compress and margins recover — meaning more probability mass sits with scenarios where leverage and margin pressure persist or worsen. Combined with technical confirmation (bearish MACD cross Dec 19, price below the 50 SMA which now acts as resistance, light-volume rebound suggesting no institutional accumulation, RSI bearish divergence into the November/December highs), the weight of evidence favors continued de-risking rather than holding full exposure or adding.

Lesson applied from the prior CBRE Underweight cycle (Sept 2024, which underperformed by -3.2% alpha): a partial trim into strength that stalls out waiting for "earnings confirmation" can lock in opportunity cost if the market's growth extrapolation persists. This is mitigated here because the current setup is technically confirmed (bearish MACD, sub-50-SMA) rather than purely valuation-driven, and because the trim is smaller (25-30%, not a full exit) with explicit two-sided triggers rather than an open-ended wait. This is not a full Sell: coverage ratios (8.3x), a sticky $220.8B AUM base, and the Neutral Analyst's recurring-revenue argument mean near-term distress risk is low, so eliminating the position entirely is not warranted by the evidence presented.
