**The Bear's Inevitable Argument:** "Debt-to-equity of 162x! This company is a debt bomb!"

**The Bull's Reality Check:** That ratio is technically accurate but fundamentally *misleading* for REITs. Here's why:

**REIT Leverage Metrics That Actually Matter:**

| Metric | EQIX Value | Industry Context | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|------------------|------------|
| **Net Debt** | $12,130M | Reasonable for $102.7B market cap | Serviceable |
| **Net Debt / EBITDA** | 3.53x | 2.5-4.0x is normal for infrastructure REITs | **Healthy range** |
| **Debt / EBITDA** | 5.50x | Elevated but not dangerous | Requires monitoring |
| **Interest Coverage (EBITDA / Int. Exp.)** | 7.53x | >5.0x is solid | **Adequate** |
| **Total Assets** | $35,085M | Growing portfolio | Underpins debt |

**Why D/E Ratio Is a Poor Metric for REITs:**
1. **REITs use leverage strategically**—it's embedded in their business model. Equity REITs typically operate at higher leverage than industrial companies.
2. **Stockholders' equity on the balance sheet is suppressed** by conservative accounting and the fact that REITs return capital via dividends, reducing book value.
3. **The real metric is leverage against cash flow**, not accounting equity—and EQIX's 3.5x Net Debt/EBITDA is *textbook healthy* for the sector.

**Capital Structure Reality:**
- **Total Debt:** $18,961M
- **Cash on Hand:** $3,081M
- **Annual EBITDA:** $3,443M
- **Refinancing Runway:** Multi-year maturity ladder (not cliff)

**My Challenge to the Bear:** If leverage is truly dangerous, then name me a single REIT in the data center space that isn't levered at similar or higher ratios. You can't, because **leverage is the mechanism through which infrastructure REITs generate returns for equity holders.**
