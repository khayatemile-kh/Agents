In reality, lenders have substantial incentive to *work with* a borrower rather than trigger an immediate default. Why? Because if CoStar defaults, the lenders have to take a loss. If CoStar restructures or gets a covenant waiver, lenders recover at par (or close to it).

So yes, there's risk of covenant renegotiation—which would be messy and would likely require capital deployment or dividend cuts. But the jump from "covenant concern" to "equity value wiped out" that the conservative analyst implies is actually a *worst-case scenario*, not the base case.
