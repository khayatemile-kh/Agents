**Rationale**: The bear case lands the more empirically grounded punches in this debate. A real growth investment thesis needs to show revenue acceleration or improving close rates; instead we see the opposite, which is a much stronger signal than the bull's speculative narrative about 'building sales infrastructure to capture share.'

On margins and EBITDA, the trajectory is genuinely alarming: EBITDA margin has fallen from 27.0% (2022) to 15.9% (2023) to 5.6% (2024)—three consecutive years of severe deterioration, not a one-off dip. The bull's recovery case requires EBITDA to more than double back to ~$410M with no management guidance, no evidence of SG&A discipline, and no clear catalyst since CRE market stress persists. That's an assumption-heavy scenario, not a base case.

The leverage picture reinforces the caution: 7.57x Debt/EBITDA is far outside typical covenant thresholds for a company of this profile (usually 3-4x), and the bear rightly flags real covenant risk that could force restructuring or limit capital allocation flexibility. The bull's rebuttal—that this is 'cyclical, not structural'—is undermined by the fact that the EBITDA collapse has been persistent and worsening for three years running, not a single-quarter aberration.

I'll grant the bull some credit on the balance-sheet cushion ($4.7B cash) and the qualitative point that CoStar's SaaS/subscription mix offers some retention resilience, and that this argues against an outright Sell/panic-exit call. But the preponderance of hard evidence—decelerating revenue growth despite ballooning sales spend, sequential margin collapse with no guided path back, and dangerously high leverage—means the risk/reward skews negative until management demonstrates operating leverage is actually inflecting. This is not a "balanced" debate; the bear's quantitative rebuttals (the S&M efficiency math, the EBITDA trend, the covenant risk) are more convincing and more evidence-based than the bull's narrative-driven optimism about capex cycles ending and rate cuts saving the day.
