**Second, the dividend cut risk is overstated:**

The Aggressive Analyst claims the $1.44 billion gap between dividends and FCF is unsustainable and will force a cut. But let's look at the actual policy history and covenant structure. REITs are **allowed to finance dividends with debt**—that's structurally baked into the REIT model. The requirement is that at least 90% of **taxable income** be distributed, not that 90% of cash flow be distributed.

IRM's taxable income vastly exceeds its accounting net income due to depreciation benefits. So a dividend cut is not some inevitable forced event; it's a **policy choice management makes under duress.** And management has made zero moves toward that choice. There's been no guidance revision, no covenant waivers requested, no equity issuance to shore up the balance sheet. What that tells me is that **management does not believe the situation is in acute crisis.**

Now, is management always right? No. But they have deep operational knowledge of their refinancing pipeline, tenant contracts, and debt covenants. If they're not in panic mode, cutting the dividend or issuing equity at distressed valuations, then the situation is likely **manageable within current structures**, not an imminent blow-up.
