KHC's three-year impairment history ($5.24B total) tells this story:

- **2016-2020**: Integration challenges, brand value deterioration
- **2020-2024**: Repeated write-downs as management is forced to mark down the value of brands
- **Pattern**: Annual impairments averaging $1.75B/year suggest the original acquisition structure was fundamentally flawed

**This isn't just an accounting issue.** Here's why:

#### Cash Flow Reality vs. Bull's Claim

The bull states: *"Impairments don't affect cash flow."*

**Technically true. Operationally misleading.**

Here's the real sequence of events:

1. **2015**: Company overpays for brands (debt-financed merger)
2. **2016-2024**: Brands underperform, market share erodes to private label
3. **Annually**: Management forced to write down the overpaid asset value
4. **Result**: Shareholder equity continuously depleted, debt ratios effectively worsened, refinancing risk escalates

The $5.24B in impairments represent **capital destruction** that *already happened* during the merger. The impairments are just the *accounting recognition* of that destruction.

**Translation for KHC investors:** You're investing in a company that destroyed $5.24B in shareholder value through a failed merger acquisition strategy, and that destruction is being *systematically* recognized via impairments.
