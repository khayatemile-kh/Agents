## ✅ Headline result: no price fabrication this time
Unlike the llama3.1:8b run — which invented a fake $1500.45 price when it hit the same blank-OHLC data gap — this run's `get_verified_market_snapshot` tool correctly excluded the corrupted 2026-08-28 row and grounded every report in the real last valid close: **$340.65 on 2026-08-27**. That number stayed consistent across the Market Analyst, News Analyst, Trader, and Risk debate throughout the entire run. The framework's grounding tool did its job, and this model didn't override it with invented numbers.

## ⚠️ But a new reliability issue: fabricated indicator interpretation, not price
The **Neutral Risk Analyst** claimed *"The RSI at 44.24 is closer to overbought"* — this is simply wrong. RSI is 0–100; overbought is conventionally >70, oversold <30. A reading of 44.24 is near the neutral midpoint (50), and if anything is numerically closer to *oversold* territory than *overbought*. This fabricated interpretation (not a fabricated number — the number itself, 44.24, was correctly carried over) directly reversed the pipeline's trajectory: it's the stated justification the Portfolio Manager used to override the Trader's own "Hold" call into a final "Buy."

**Takeaway:** the fabrication risk didn't disappear with the model swap and the `max_tokens` fix — it moved. This run got the raw numbers right throughout, but hallucinated at the *interpretation* layer instead of the *data-retrieval* layer. Worth watching for on future runs.

## Why the printed decision ("Buy") differs from the Trader's own text ("HOLD")
This isn't a bug — it's the pipeline's designed structure, but it produced a confusing-looking outcome:
1. **Market Analyst** (technical-only view): leaned toward a self-issued "Final Transaction Proposal: HOLD" (unusual — proposing a transaction isn't really the Market Analyst's role, but it did so anyway)
2. **Research Manager** (bull/bear debate synthesis): **Buy** — sided with the bull case on cloud/AI growth despite noted regulatory/debt concerns
3. **Trader**: **Hold** — cited neutral RSI and proximity to the 200 SMA as reasons for caution
4. **Portfolio/Risk Manager** (final, authoritative stage): **Buy** — overrode the Trader, citing the RSI-overbought claim addressed above

The pipeline's *final* stage is the Portfolio/Risk Manager, and its output (`final_trade_decision`) is what `propagate()` returns as `decision` — so "Buy" is genuinely the framework's final answer, even though it contradicts what the Trader itself proposed a step earlier. That internal disagreement, left unreconciled and decided by a fabricated technical claim, is itself worth flagging rather than treating "Buy" as a clean, uncontested output.

## Additional data-completeness gap
In the risk debate, the **Risky Analyst and Safe Analyst produced no output at all** (`risky_history` / `safe_history` both empty in the saved state) — only the Neutral Analyst spoke. Whether this is a `max_debate_rounds=1`/`max_risk_discuss_rounds=1` configuration effect or a model/framework quirk isn't confirmed, but it means this "debate" was really a monologue, not the three-sided risk discussion the framework is designed to produce.
