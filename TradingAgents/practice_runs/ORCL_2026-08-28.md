## ⚠️ Final rating marked UNRELIABLE — real data, backward interpretation

Unlike TSLA (which invented brand-new facts), this run used **entirely real, correctly-quoted numbers** but built its bullish thesis on a **confidently wrong reading of what those numbers mean**, then reused that wrong reading as the core justification across three separate pipeline stages. This is arguably more dangerous than outright fabrication because every individual number checks out — only the conclusion drawn from them is backward.

### The core error: 50/200 SMA relationship read backward
Real verified values: **50-day SMA = 141.40**, **200-day SMA = 169.74**. That means the 50-day average is *below* the 200-day average — the standard "death cross" configuration, conventionally read as **bearish** structure. The Market Analyst instead wrote: *"The 200-day moving average (169.74) is much higher than the 50-day moving average (141.40), **indicating a long-term bullish trend**."* That's backward. This exact misreading was then repeated near-verbatim in the **Research Manager's** Overweight rationale and the **Portfolio Manager's** final decision ("the 200-day moving average is significantly higher... indicating a strong long-term trend") — it's the single most-cited piece of evidence for the bullish call, and it's wrong.

### A second, smaller factual error
The Market Analyst also wrote the price is *"moving between the middle band (146.98) and the lower band (138.73)"* — but the real close is $150.85, which is **above** the middle band, i.e. between the middle and **upper** band (155.24), not the lower one. Minor on its own, but another instance of real numbers being described incorrectly.

### Unsupported claims presented as evidence
`fundamentals_report` and `sentiment_report` are both **empty** in the saved state (no fundamentals analyst ran, no news was found) — yet the investment plan and final decision cite *"robust financial health," "diverse product portfolio," "large, diverse customer base,"* and *"resilient financial performance"* as supporting evidence. None of these have any tool-sourced basis in this run; they're generic assertions about Oracle stated as if backed by fetched data.

### Also worth noting: an unflagged claim during the News stage
Separately observed live during this run: the News Analyst stated *"the unemployment rate remains relatively stable"* as of 2026-08-28, in the same breath as admitting the FRED macro data **could not be retrieved**. That's a claim with no data behind it, though it didn't end up central to the final rating.

### What was NOT wrong this run
- No invented historical events, dates, or percentages (unlike TSLA's fake "2023 golden cross" claim)
- No nonexistent-report citations (unlike TSLA's fake sentiment report)
- MACD/RSI readings were correctly interpreted (RSI 56.10 correctly called neutral; MACD line above signal correctly called bullish)
- Stop-loss ($141.40 = the real 50 SMA value) was traceable and its implied ~6.3% downside is roughly reasonable — no repeat of TSLA's stop-loss percentage math error

## Final Decision: **Overweight** — treat as unreliable given the above
**Trader's own read was actually "Hold"** — the Market Analyst's report and the Trader both independently concluded Hold/consolidation, while the Research Manager and Portfolio Manager overrode to Overweight using the backward SMA interpretation as justification. This is the same "later stage overrides an earlier, more cautious stage" pattern seen on GOOGL and NVDA, compounding the interpretation error with an unreconciled internal disagreement.
